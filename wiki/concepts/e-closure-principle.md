# The e-Closure principle and true-discovery guarantees

## Definition
An e-collection is a family (e_S)_{S ⊆ [m]} of nonnegative statistics with E_P[e_{N_P}] ≤ 1 for every P in the model, where N_P is the set of true nulls; for example e_S an e-value for the intersection hypothesis H_S, or only for the partitioning hypothesis {N_P = S} [xuBringingClosureFalse2025, Def. 5, Sec. 9]. The e-Closure procedure for an expected-loss error rate E[f_{N_P}(R)] at level α rejects any set R with e_S ≥ f_S(R)/α for all S. The e-Closure principle states that a procedure controls such an error rate, simultaneously over a collection of rejected sets, if and only if it is contained in an e-Closure procedure [xuBringingClosureFalse2025, Thm 6]. Thresholding at 1/α recovers the classical closure principle for FWER exactly, and with FDP as loss it yields the first necessary-and-sufficient principle for FDR [xuBringingClosureFalse2025, Thms 12, 14]. The e-value translation of closed testing goes back to Vovk and Wang's discovery e-vector and e-matrix, which give simultaneous lower bounds on true discoveries for every rejection set under arbitrary dependence [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Sec. 6, App. C].

## Key results
- The canonical e-collection e_S = max_R f_S(R)/α proves necessity and is idempotent, so power gains require better local e-values [xuBringingClosureFalse2025, Prop. 8].
- Closed e-BH uses the arithmetic mean of the e_i in S as local e-value and uniformly improves e-BH in power and simultaneity under arbitrary dependence; it can reject all m hypotheses where e-BH rejects none and rejects hypotheses with e_i < 1/α; with compound e-values closed e-BH equals e-BH [xuBringingClosureFalse2025, Thm 17, Example 20, Thm 25]; see [e-bh](e-bh.md).
- Closed BY uses an |S|-dependent p-to-e calibrator; closed Su uses a Simes p-value with l_α = −W_{−1}(−α/e); e-collections built from Li–Zhang compound e-values only reconstruct BH and adaptive BH [xuBringingClosureFalse2025, Thms 28, 34, 37].
- Closed BH: the first uniform improvement of ordinary BH by e-closure, valid under PRDS and a weaker minimal sufficient condition E[e_N] ≤ 1, built from a family of BH-like step-up procedures with a horizon matrix; equals minimally adaptive BH whenever that rejects fewer than √m, gains when BH already rejects many; simultaneity is essentially nil for it [goemanUniformImprovementBenjaminiHochberg2026, Thms 8, 12, 13, 19, Props 15, 16, 18].
- Post-hoc flexibility: simultaneity equals post-hoc choice of the rejected set; the error metric may be chosen post hoc among losses controlled by the same collection; if the collection is α-free, α may be chosen post hoc, extending the e-BH result of the book [xuBringingClosureFalse2025, Lemma 4, Thms 39, 41, 44, 48; ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025, Thm 9.10]; see [post-hoc-validity](post-hoc-validity.md).
- Discovery e-matrix for nested rejection sets is monotone along rows, columns and diagonals and computable in O(K²) by a convex-hull slope argument; it is the exact e-translation of the Genovese–Wasserman / Goeman–Solari procedure, and the authors assert admissibility of the arithmetic-mean version as an e-procedure without proof [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Sec. 9, Algorithm 4, App. C].
- Computation: O(m²) subsets suffice for monotone local e-values; closed e-BH in O(m² log m), closed BY in O(m³); R package eClosure and Python eclosure [xuBringingClosureFalse2025, Sec. 10].
- Admissible procedures on arbitrary e-values have weighted affine local e-values; Sun and Wang 2026 prove a complete-class theorem for weighted-mean closed e-BH [xuBringingClosureFalse2025, App. B].
- FWER closure of e-merging functions, the precursor [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021, Thm 5.1].
- Complementary universality: e-BH on compound e-values (single rejected set, FDR) versus the e-Closure principle (simultaneous sets, any expected loss), with the dictionary e_S = K^{-1} Σ_{i ∈ S} ẽ_i [ignatiadisAsymptoticCompoundEvalues2024, Thm 4.4]; see [approximate-and-asymptotic-e-values](approximate-and-asymptotic-e-values.md). Weighted averaging as the only admissible dependence-free local e-value, justifying closed e-BH's arithmetic mean [wangOnlyAdmissibleWay2024, Thm 1].
- Origin of the FDR closure idea: closed e-BH with the arithmetic mean per intersection, restricted to top-k sets, FDR ≤ α under arbitrary dependence; e-BH and minimally adaptive e-BH are strictly contained (E = (60, 39, 11), α = 0.05 gives 1, 2, 3 rejections); BY is inadmissible even among deterministic procedures; every FDR procedure is e-closed via E_A = FDP_A(R)/α; an α-free e-collection from post-hoc FDP families can add power; general expectation-based metrics and post-hoc choice of the error metric; O(K³) dynamic programme; compound equality conjectured and later proved [xuBringingClosureFDR2025, Prop. 1–6, Thms 1–3, Example 1, Alg. 1].
- FWER-only e-closed testing: adjusted e*_i = min_{I ∋ i} e_I, everything inherited from the single e-value e_{H_0}; post-hoc strong FWER E[1{max e*_i ≥ 1/α̂}/α̂] ≤ 1 where p-Holm has infinite post-hoc risk; post-hoc anytime-valid and always-valid FWER, the latter needing adjusters on running maxima to be post hoc; weighted e-Bonferroni local tests dominate weighted p-Bonferroni on inverse-e p-values; running maxima of independent e-processes give FWER ≤ α + ((n_0 − 1)/n_0) α² log(1/α) via a Pareto coupling; e-Holm by an O(n) threshold rule adapting to the number of nulls; averaging is not consonant, so e-Fallback needs an O(n) stack and arbitrary DAGs an O(n|E|) dynamic programme; the authors concede optimal fixed-sample p-values can beat e-values by more than aggregation recovers [hartogFamilyWiseError2025, Lemma 2.1, Thms 2.3–2.5, 3.1, 4.2, 6.4, B.1, Algs 1–4, Secs 1.4, 8.4].
- Online closed testing: coherent online true-discovery procedures are dominated by online closed ones; admissibility forces products of sequential e-values; SeqE-Guard shortcut [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Thms 2.1–2.3, 3.1]; see [online-multiple-testing](online-multiple-testing.md). Closed knockoffs use the same sign-flip e-values, and since these are compound e-values closed e-BH collapses to e-BH, so derandomised knockoffs gain simultaneity but not power from closure [renDerandomizedKnockoffsLeveraging2022, Sec. 1; xuBringingClosureFalse2025, Thm 38].
- Adaptive graphical procedures (Klinglmüller, Posch and Koenig 2014) built on partial conditional error functions A^I_i = E_P[φ^I_i | D] for weighted Bonferroni local tests are the e-closed procedure with local e-values E^I = Σ_i w^I_i A^I_i/(αw^I_i): A^I = 1 iff E^I ≥ 1/α, so both reject the same hypotheses, hypotheses may be dropped or reweighted at the interim, and rejecting at the interim when A^I = 1 with every A^I_i < 1 uniformly improves the single-stage closed test; Hartog and Lei's shortcuts are the case E^I_i = E_i; the e-closure principle for post-hoc error metrics is recommended to the clinical-trial community [brannathAnytimevalidTestingEvalues2026a, Secs 4, 5.1]; see [conditional-error-functions](conditional-error-functions.md).
- The two-stage precursor is the flexible closed test: prespecify a conditional error function A_J with E_{H_J}[A_J] ≤ α for every intersection hypothesis H_J and reject H_i iff the second-stage p-values satisfy q_J ≤ A_J for all J ∋ i; treatments or endpoints may be dropped at the interim by an unspecified rule (dropped hypotheses are accepted, and q_{J∩I_2} is used for H_J), hypotheses may be rejected at the interim iff A_J = 1 for all J ∋ i, and new hypotheses may be added. With two treatments and only the first continued, H_1 is rejected iff q_1 ≤ min(A_1, A_{1,2}) [brannathMultiplicityFlexibilityClinical2007, Sec. 2.3]. Wiki's reading: A_J/α is a local e-value for H_J, so this is closed testing with one first-stage e-value per intersection, exhausted at the second stage.
