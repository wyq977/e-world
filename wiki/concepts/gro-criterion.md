# GRO, GROW, REGROW and log-optimality

## Definition
Growth-rate optimality (GRO) chooses, for a given alternative Q, the [e-variable](e-variable.md) maximising [e-power](e-power.md) E_Q[log E], the analogue of Neyman–Pearson power for e-values [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, Sec. 2, eq. 11]. The book calls the same object log-optimal: E is log-optimal against Q if E_Q[log(E′/E)] ≤ 0 for every e-variable E′ [ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025, Definition 3.18]. GROW is the maximin version over a composite alternative, sup_E inf_θ E_θ[log E] [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, Sec. 3], and REGROW maximises worst-case growth relative to the oracle GRO rate, which is needed when GROW trivialises (nested models) or nuisance parameters are present [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, Sec. 4].

## Key results
- Theorem 1 of Safe Testing: if Q has finite KL to the convex hull of the null, the GRO e-variable exists, is essentially unique, equals q/p*_0 with P*_0 the [reverse-information-projection](reverse-information-projection.md), and its growth rate is D(Q ‖ P*_0); it is the only e-variable of likelihood-ratio form with q on top, so GRO e-variables are Bayes factors with a forced null prior [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, Thm 1, Cor. 2].
- Without regularity conditions the log-optimal e-variable always exists and is unique; it is the [numeraire](numeraire.md) [ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025, Prop. 3.19, Thm 6.4].
- Simple vs simple: the likelihood ratio is log-optimal with e-power KL(Q, P) [ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025, Thm 3.20]; conditional and process-level log-optimality [Prop. 3.22, Thm 7.11].
- GROW under monotone likelihood ratio with a minimum effect size is the point-vs-point ratio p_{δ+}/p_{δ−} [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, Prop. 3, Example 4]; for a simple null REGROW is the redundancy–capacity problem, the prior tends to Jeffreys' and regret is (d/2) log n + O(1) [Example 5].
- Nuisance recipe: REGROW away the nuisance for each effect size, then GROW over effect sizes; in group-invariant settings GROW = REGROW, for the 2×2 table they differ [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, Sec. 4.2–4.3, Examples 6–7]; see [bayes-factor-e-variable](bayes-factor-e-variable.md).
- Uniqueness corollary: at most one prior W_0 on the null makes q/p_{W_0} an e-variable, and then it is GRO [haoEValuesExponentialFamilies2025, Lemma 1].
- Asymptotic log-optimality of mixture and plug-in methods [ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025, Sec. 3.7, Def. 7.13, Prop. 7.14]; see [mixture-and-plug-in-methods](mixture-and-plug-in-methods.md).
- Quantitative e-power gaps between GRO and its competitors for exponential families [haoEValuesExponentialFamilies2025, Cors 1–4]; see [exponential-family-e-values](exponential-family-e-values.md).
- Betting-side statement: Q/P maximises E_Q[ln S] by Gibbs' inequality, and Kelly's additivity of the logarithm is the stated reason to optimise expected log rather than E_Q[S] or Q(S ≥ 1/α); the betting form of the Neyman–Pearson lemma [shaferTestingBettingStrategy2021, Sec. 2.2].
- Log-optimal anytime-valid e-values (LOAVEV): maximise E_Q[ln E_σ] over e-processes for an alternative that includes a randomised stopping time σ; the optimum is a reverse information projection jointly over null elements and stopping times (primal-dual theorem for finite outcome spaces); deterministic σ collapses to batch GROW; the LOAVEV is a plain likelihood ratio independent of σ when the one-outcome RIPr lies in the null (point nulls, Gaussian scale, 2 × 2 tables, exponential-family intervals) [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022, Problem 3, Thm 5, Cor. 9, Thms 8, 10, 12].
- A simple e-variable (likelihood ratio against one null element) exists iff the RIPr is a point in the null; then it is unique, GRO at every n and stopping-time log-optimal; Theorem 1 gives eight equivalent conditions for exponential families, chiefly Σ_p − Σ_q ⪰ 0 [grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024, Prop. 1, Thm 1].
- The survey unifies REGROW (γ = 1) and GROW (γ = 0) in one formula and shows the right-Haar t-test e-process is GROW and REGROW for every γ and τ because the expected log of the RIPr e-variable is constant in σ; Jeffreys' prior is asymptotically REGROW [ramdasGametheoreticStatisticsSafe2023, Sec. 3.2.4, eq. 15, Sec. 4.1.2].
- GRO without a convex null: the block k-sample e-variable is GRO whenever the single-outcome model is convex; any valid 'mixture of means' ratio on a convex parameter set is GRO; Wald's SPRT product is not [turnerGenericEvariablesExact2024, Thm 1, Prop. 3, Sec. 8.2]; GRO for every closed convex null in the 2 × 2 table [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023, Thm 2]; conditional GROW for θ ≤ θ1 in survival data [terschureAnytimevalidLogrankTest2024, Sec. 3.1].
- Greenland's one-shot chi-square challenge (y = 3.84, p = 0.05): naive e-variables need p ≤ 0.0000077, a REGROW-style exponentiated-Cauchy prior gives E = 1.81 and needs p ≤ 0.0007, Johnson's UMP Bayes factor with a point prior at δ* = 7.378 needs p ≤ 0.007, the best among likelihood-ratio e-variables; REGROW is 'not the method of choice' for one-shot n = 1 tests [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 2]. GROW point priors break test and confidence-interval consistency (Ly); REGROW or nonlocal priors fix it; REGROW is the reference-Bayes-like variant with no efficient general construction [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 3].
- 'Regret' as the name for REGROW relative to the oracle point-alternative GRO e-variable; universal numerators give regret (d_1/2) log m + O(1) and ((d_1 − d_0)/2) log m for nested maximum entropy models; Jeffreys' prior is regret-suboptimal in the 2 × 2 canonical test [giuffridaTestingMaximumEntropy2025, eqs 12–22, 87, Fig. 5]; see [mdl-and-maximum-entropy-e-values](mdl-and-maximum-entropy-e-values.md).
- Existence and uniqueness of the GRO e-variable for every composite null and point alternative without any condition, with benchmarked log-optimality to handle infinite growth [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025, Prop. 2.4, Thm 2.6, Remarks 2.5, 3.8]; strengthens Safe Testing's Corollary 2 [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025, Thm 4.1].
- Anytime-valid Hunt–Stein: under amenability the invariant likelihood ratio is GROW among all e-statistics, and GROW = REGROW under invariance; composite δ handled by optimal or proper priors [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Thms 2, 4, Cors 3, 5, 8, 9]; see [group-invariance-e-statistics](group-invariance-e-statistics.md). Power is unusable as a criterion because a power-optimal e-statistic is zero with positive probability [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 2.4].
- Log utility is the only utility whose optimal e-value is preserved under conditioning; the SPRT arises as the limit of induced Neyman–Pearson tests with α_N = exp(−N D(Q ‖ P)) [koningAnytimeValidityFree2026, Thms 7–8, Prop. 6].
- For e-collections used in estimation GRO is not the design criterion: Savage–Dickey GRO collections assess worse than a two-point collection [grunwaldEposterior2023, Example 11]; the delay-optimal e-detector baseline maximises E_Q log L_1 [shinEdetectorsNonparametricFramework2023, eq. 20]; 'simultaneous REGROW regularity' and local REGROW witness bases drive optimal change-detection delay [sahaNonpartitionedEdetectorsNonparametric2026, Defs 6.6, 6.8].
- 'Strongest e-statistic' E ≻ E′ iff ∫ln(E/E′) dP ≥ 0, the first strict extension of GRO to infinite e-power; the RIPr ratio is strongest [lardyReverseInformationProjections2023, Def. 2, Thm 5, Prop. 6]. Log-optimality survives exactness and pivotality constraints and is still a likelihood ratio, now against a probability measure; the Gibbs reduction is specific to the logarithm; pivotality costs e-power [zhangExistencePowerfulPvalues2023, Thm 4.2, Example 4.16].
- GRO, GROW and REGROW as three instances of one utility–penalty objective inf_Q ∫U(h)dQ − χ(Q), with existence by compactness and uniqueness criteria; REGROW no longer needs bounded χ [larssonTestingHypothesesGenerated2025, Example 7.1, Thms 7.3–7.6]. GRAPA, the empirical maximiser of E[log(1 + λ(X − m)) | F_{t−1}], is 'a sequentially adaptive version of the worst-case GROW criterion' [waudbysmithEstimatingMeansBounded2020, App. B.2]. Log-optimal simple separable compound e-values are ratios of mixtures, with e-power at most the average per-hypothesis KL [ignatiadisAsymptoticCompoundEvalues2024, Thm 5.2]; see [approximate-and-asymptotic-e-values](approximate-and-asymptotic-e-values.md). Theorem 7 of the betting paper's admissible merging counterpart: weighted averaging is the only admissible merger of tests [wangOnlyAdmissibleWay2024, Remark 3].
- Log-optimality placed in the expected-utility family with Neyman–Pearson (x ∧ 1/α) and power utilities; the log-optimal e-value against N(μι, I) is not uniformly log-optimal in μ, so there is no unique 'log-optimal t-test', unlike the NP test [koningMeasuringEvidenceExchangeability2023, Lemma 2, Thm 6, Cors 2–4, Example 5, Remark 14]. Hedged GRO e-values 1 − λ_i + λ_i E_i with an estimated null fraction are growth-optimal under a two-groups mixture, and raw GRO products collapse when many nulls are true [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Prop. 3.4]. Restricted GRO within 1 − λ + λe over λ ∈ [0, γ] equals the unrestricted GRO for mean, variance, VaR and ES–VaR backtests [wangEbacktesting2022, eq. 12, Remark 6, Sec. 6]. GROW e-values for forecast dominance are likelihood ratios against the score boundary κ_ν, and the growth-optimal alternative does not maximise finite-sample rejection probability [henziValidSequentialInference2021, Thms 3.2–3.3, Sec. 4.1]. First-order optimality of predictive-recursion e-processes relative to their mixture model [dixitAnytimeValidAsymptotically2023, Thm 2].
- GROW and REGROW over the nuisance (β, σ) for the Bayesian and point alternatives in linear models via Pérez-Ortiz et al.; the mixture precision chosen to maximise worst-case growth over |ξ| ≥ ξ_MDE [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, Def. 3.2, Thm 4.1, eq. 13, Thm 4.5].
- Simple null, alternative given by a set of means M_1: if M_1 is convex the GROW e-variable is the exponential-family likelihood ratio p̄_{μ*}/p_0 against the KL-closest mean μ* on the boundary of M_1, the same for every H_1 between the family slice E_1 and all distributions with mean in M_1, with GROW = D(P̄_{μ*} ‖ P_0); this follows from Csiszár's Pythagorean property alone, which weakens Theorem 1's conditions and Topsøe's 1979 result [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a, Props 1–2]. For a surrounding M_1 (complement an open star-shaped set around 0) and d = 1 the GROW prior sits on the two boundary points with equalised growth, proved via variation-diminishing kernels; d > 1 is open [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a, Thm 3, Lemma 1, Sec. 5]. Relative GROW in the minimax individual-sequence-regret sense (Jang et al., Orabona–Jun) over a partition of M_1 is a Shtarkov/NML distribution over the boundary divided by p_0; redundancy and regret both cost ((d − 1)/2) log n + O(1) when Y is a sample mean and the boundary is smooth, and for a KL-ball complement GROW_n = nD_1 − ((d − 1)/2) log n + O(1) [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a, eqs 3.8–3.10, Thms 5–6, eq. 4.5, Prop. 3]; see [mdl-and-maximum-entropy-e-values](mdl-and-maximum-entropy-e-values.md).
- Recommended to the adaptive-design community as the principled choice among the many sequential tests that differ only by how they weight the stages, with existence for any null and likelihood-ratio form cited from Safe testing and the numeraire paper [brannathAnytimevalidTestingEvalues2026a, Sec. 5.1].

## Disagreements / open issues
- Ly's discussion: GROW point priors leave δ+ inside anytime-valid confidence intervals forever; the authors answer with REGROW or nonlocal priors [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, discussion and reply].
- Whether the right-Haar t-test Bayes factor is GROW under Jeffreys' Cauchy prior (no second moment) is open [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, Sec. 4.3].
- Which GRO criteria admit sequentially decomposable specifications is open [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, eq. 31]; see [optional-stopping](optional-stopping.md).
- Log-optimality is built on the premise of a potentially infinite number of stages; when practical constraints limit a trial to a few stages, "other criteria may be more appropriate", none proposed [brannathAnytimevalidTestingEvalues2026a, Sec. 5.1].
