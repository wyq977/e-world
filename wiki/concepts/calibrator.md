# Calibrators between p-values and e-values

## Definition
A p-to-e calibrator is a decreasing function f with ∫_0^1 f ≤ 1, so that f(P) is an [e-variable](e-variable.md) for any p-variable P; an e-to-p calibrator maps e-values to p-values, and e ↦ min(1, 1/e) is the only admissible one [ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025, Definition 2.3, Props 2.4–2.5]. Every e-variable is a calibrated exact p-variable [Prop. 2.7]. Shafer's calibrator 1/√p − 1 maps p = 0.05 to 3.47 and needs p ≤ 0.0023 to reach an e-value of 20 [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, Sec. 7].

## Key results
- Explicit calibrator families and the log-optimal calibrator E_P[dQ/dP | P] [ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025, eqs 2.1–2.5, Cor. 3.23].
- Randomised e-to-p calibrator U/E and conditional calibrators on subclasses [ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025, Cor. 2.10, Thm 11.5, Chapter 15].
- Calibrators are the route for admissible p-merging under arbitrary dependence [ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025, Thm 12.15]; see [e-merging](e-merging.md).
- Cost of calibrating: reaching 80% power through a calibrated p-value needs about 3.0 times the Neyman–Pearson sample size, against 2.2 for GROW e-variables [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, Sec. 7].
- Shafer's discussion frames p-values as dishonest bets and the authors reply with the lottery-ticket argument [grunwaldSafeTesting2024, discussion, reply Sec. 2].
- Full characterisation: p-to-e calibrators are the decreasing f with ∫f ≤ 1, admissible iff upper semicontinuous with f(0) = ∞ and ∫f = 1; the single admissible e-to-p calibrator is min(1, 1/e); explicit families κp^{κ−1}, the mixture (1 − p + p ln p)/(p(−ln p)²), and the incomplete-gamma family; the Vovk–Sellke bound is not a valid e-value [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021, Props 2.1–2.2, eqs 1–2, App. B].
- Shafer's 1/√p − 1: 1/p has infinite null expectation and must be shrunk to a payoff with expectation one; the choice is 'fundamentally arbitrary' but maps 0.10/0.05/0.01/0.001 to 2.2/3.5/9.0/30.6 and agrees with Jeffreys; any shrinking rule implies a heavy-tailed alternative; −ln p is valid, −log₂ p is not [shaferTestingBettingStrategy2021, Sec. 3, eq. 6, Example 4, reply].
- Calibrators preserve family-wise validity in both directions; cross-merging: average e-values then invert, or calibrate p-values then average [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021, Remark A.1, App. G].
- Survey uses: turn any confidence interval into an e-confidence interval at level f^{−1}(1/α), lookback calibrators, calibrate per-study p-values before multiplying [ramdasGametheoreticStatisticsSafe2023, Secs 2.4, 6.1, 6.3].
- Lottery-ticket argument: reporting p is claiming the payout of a ticket one never bought; calibration makes the p-value an honest bet; the authors concede that once p-values are calibrated, e-values do not need more data [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 1].
- BH, BY and every Blanchard–Roquain shape-function step-up rule are e-BH applied to calibrated p-values f(p_k) with f = ψ/α; calibrators and inversion preserve PRDS; boosting factors for λp^{λ−1} [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021, Prop. 4, Remark 4, Fact 1, Examples 2, 5, 7].
- Round-trip inefficiency: 1/VS(0.005) ≈ 0.072, VS(0.05) ≈ 2.456; Jeffreys (5% ~ √10, 1% ~ 10) and Good correspondences; the harmonic-mean p-value is the 1/e image of averaging [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Sec. 3, eqs 6–12, App. B].
- Two new |S|-dependent p-to-e calibrators for closed BY and closed Su; calibrate-then-average is weaker than tailoring the local e-value; calibrators with a level must keep it fixed for post-hoc α [xuBringingClosureFalse2025, Lemmas 27, 32, Remarks 31, 49]; the extreme calibrator 1_{[0,a]}/a with a random threshold [goemanUniformImprovementBenjaminiHochberg2026, eq. 9].
- Adjusters a with ∫ a(x)/x² dx ≤ 1 on running maxima of e-processes, equivalently calibrators under x = 1/p; growth-preserving example a(x) = 2x/(2 + log x)² [sahaNonpartitionedEdetectorsNonparametric2026, Def. 6.1, Lemma 6.2]; calibrating then applying the e-rule is the only known Type-I-risk-safe use of a p-value and is usually suboptimal [grunwaldBeyondNeymanPearsonEvalues2024, Sec. 1.2]; whole-process calibration both ways [ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022, Props 12, 14].
- Randomised e-to-p calibrator Q(U, E) = (U/E) ∧ 1 dominates the deterministic one; every p-to-e calibrator satisfies h(p) ≤ 1/p [ignatiadisEvaluesUnnormalizedWeights2022, Remark 3.2, S3.1]. Any nonincreasing continuous-from-below confidence interval becomes an e-CI via C(f^{−1}(1/α)); the step-function BY calibrator f^{BY(K,α)}(p) = (K/α)(⌈Kℓ_K p/α⌉ ∨ 1)^{−1} 1{p ≤ α/ℓ_K} with ∫f = 1 makes e-BY on calibrated intervals exactly assumption-free BY [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Thm 1, Prop. 3, Cor. 2].
- The reciprocal p = 1/e is argued to be a change of representation, not a calibration; the randomised improvement α ↦ αe ∧ 1 is post-hoc valid [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Sec. 7.4, Thm 11, Cor. 5]. Calibration commutes with compound, approximate and asymptotic relaxations [ignatiadisAsymptoticCompoundEvalues2024, Thms 2.3, 3.3, Prop. 3.16]. E = 2 − 2X maps an exact nontrivial p-variable to a pivotal exact bounded e-variable [zhangExistencePowerfulPvalues2023, Prop. A.5]. Adjusters applied to running maxima lock in wealth at a squared-log price [ramdasTestingExchangeabilityForkconvexity2021, Sec. 4.7].
- A possibility contour is a prior-relative p-value, so ρ = 1/γ(q) with an admissible calibrator γ is a regularizer [martinRegularizedEprocessesAnytime2024, Prop. 1]. Grid calibrator f_t(p) = (αγ_t ⌈(pℓ_t/(αγ_t)) ∨ 1⌉)^{−1} makes r-LOND an instance of e-LOND [xuOnlineMultipleTesting2023, Cor. 1]; a per-hypothesis calibration condition under which online e-BH on ψ_t(P_t) recovers BH, BY and r-LOND [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Prop. 2.5]; online-simple as a binary p-to-e calibrator and the martingale Stouffer calibrator [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, eq. 14, S.4]. Adjusters on running maxima as e-to-e calibrators [hartogFamilyWiseError2025, Thm B.1]. Lag-h forecast comparison converts a harmonic-merged p-process back to an e-process with (1 − p + p log p)/(p (log p)²) [choeComparingSequentialForecasters2021, Props 5–6]. κp^{κ−1} with κ = 1/2 turns conformal p-values into a conformal Shiryaev–Roberts e-detector [dandapanthulaMultipleTestingMultistream2025, Sec. 9.3].
- 1/E is a strictly conservative p-value because 1/U has infinite expectation; with a known least favourable P_0 and S_t(u) = P_0(1/E_t ≤ u | E_1, …, E_{t−1}), the p-value S_t(1/E_t) is p-clud and strictly smaller than 1/E_t with positive probability when S_t is continuous, which is what lets a recursive combination test uniformly improve an e-value test; the improvement costs optional continuation and data-dependent α [brannathAnytimevalidTestingEvalues2026a, App. A, Remark 5]; see [conditional-error-functions](conditional-error-functions.md).
- Wiki's reading of the adaptive-design catalogue: a conditional error function A(p_1) that is non-increasing in the first-stage p-value with ∫_0^1 A = α is, after division by α, a p-to-e calibrator capped at 1/α. Bauer and Köhne's Fisher product gives c/(αp_1) between the boundaries α_1 and α_0, equal to 1/α below α_1 and 0 above α_0; Lehmacher and Wassmer's inverse-normal design gives (1 − Φ(√2 z_{1−α_2} − Φ^{−1}(1 − p_1)))/α; Proschan and Hunsberger's circular function and Shen and Fisher's linear one are two more [poschAdaptiveTwoStage1999, Secs 2–5]. The cap is what makes them exhaust the level and what forbids multiplying further; see [conditional-error-functions](conditional-error-functions.md).
