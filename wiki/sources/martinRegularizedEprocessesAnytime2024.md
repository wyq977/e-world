# Regularized e-processes: anytime valid inference with knowledge-based efficiency gains

Single-author paper by Ryan Martin (NC State), written in the first person ("I" the author, "You" the investigator) and aimed at importing his possibilistic inferential-model (IM) programme into the e-process world. Main text: Secs 1–6 (30 pages); Appendices A (remarks), B (Choquet integration), C (other regularizers), D (why the product form), E (more efficiency plots), F (no-sure-loss), G (proofs).

## Summary
The paper asks what to do when an [e-process](../concepts/e-process.md) can grow no faster yet the investigator genuinely knows something about the quantity of interest Θ = f(Ω) before seeing data [martinRegularizedEprocessesAnytime2024, Sec. 1]. That knowledge is encoded not as a Bayesian prior but as a credal set Q of priors, summarised by an upper probability Q̄ (Sec. 2.3); a *regularizer* is any ρ : T → [0, ∞] with Q̄-upper expectation Q̄ρ ≤ 1 (Definition 1), and the *regularized e-process* is the product e^reg(·, θ) = ρ(θ) e_θ(·) (eq. 8). Nontrivial ρ exceed 1 where θ is implausible a priori, so e^reg is larger there and rejects such θ sooner. Anytime validity is then relaxed to hold under the induced upper joint distribution P̄ for (Z^N, Ω) formed by all pullbacks of all priors in Q (eq. 12), and the chief result is a *regularized Ville inequality* P̄(e^reg{Z^N, f(Ω)} > 1/α) ≤ α for all stopping times (Theorem 1), which reduces to ordinary [Ville's inequality](../concepts/villes-inequality.md) when the prior is vacuous and ρ ≡ 1 (Sec. 3.5). For possibilistic priors with contour q the regularizer is ρ = 1/γ(q) for any admissible p-to-e [calibrator](../concepts/calibrator.md) γ (eq. 10, Proposition 1), which the author reads as "q(Θ) is a p-value relative to Q̄, and a p-value must be calibrated before its reciprocal is an e-value" (Sec. 3.2). The second half flips 1 ∧ 1/e^reg into a possibility contour, giving an "e-possibilistic IM" that generalises Grünwald's capped e-posterior; it is strongly and uniformly anytime valid (Theorems 2–3, Corollaries 1–3), satisfies the likelihood principle, avoids sure loss and one-sided contraction (Theorem 5), and yields an oracle-relative decision guarantee (Theorem 4). Examples: Gaussian mean with a Savage–Dickey e-process (Sec. 3.6, 4.6), a nonparametric median via Dey, Martin and Williams' generalised universal inference (Sec. 4.5), and Ware's 1989 ECMO trial with Turner–Grünwald 2 × 2 e-variables (Sec. 5).

## Main results
- **Definition 1 (Sec. 3.1)**: ρ is a regularizer for upper prevision Q̄ if Q̄ρ ≤ 1. Any 0 ≤ ρ ≤ 1 qualifies but is useless; under a vacuous prior Q̄ρ = sup ρ, so ρ ≡ 1 is the only option and nothing is gained. A dominating ρ (pointwise larger) is preferred.
- **Eq. 8 (Sec. 3.1)**: e^reg(·, θ) = ρ(θ) × e_θ(·), applicable to any e-process construction; the product form is justified by Bayesian-style updating coherence (App. D.1: e^reg(z^{1:n}, θ) serves as the regularizer for later data) and by Proposition 3 (App. D.2): for a nontrivial, admissible regularizer (Q̄ρ = 1, Q̄{ρ > 1} > 0) no other "re-merging" function that treats the data as sovereign (e ≥ 1 ⇒ m(r, e) ≥ r) P̄-strictly weakly dominates the product, proven with a two-valued e-process in the style of Vovk and Wang's ie-merging result.
- **Proposition 1 (Sec. 3.2, eq. 10)**: for a possibility measure with contour q (sup q = 1) and an admissible calibrator γ (non-decreasing, ∫_0^1 1/γ = 1, Shafer et al. 2011 Sec. 6), ρ = {γ ∘ q}^{−1} is a regularizer; proof via the Choquet formula Q̄g = ∫_0^1 sup_{θ : q(θ) > s} g(θ) ds (eq. 6). Recommended γ: the Vovk–Wang beta-mixture calibrator γ(u) = u(−log u)^{1+κ} / (κ · igamma(−log u, 1 + κ)), chosen because it vanishes fast as u → 0 and so penalises implausible θ severely.
- **Eq. 7**: Q ∈ credal set of contour q iff Q{q(Θ) ≤ α} ≤ α for all α, i.e. q(Θ) is stochastically no smaller than uniform; this is the p-value analogy behind the calibration step.
- **Proposition 2 and eq. 12 (Sec. 3.3)**: for continuous f, the set R_Q of pullback laws for Ω with f(Ω) ∼ Q is a non-empty, convex, weakly closed credal set; the induced upper joint distribution is P̄g = sup_{Q ∈ Q} sup_{R ∈ R_Q} E^{Ω∼R} E^{Z∼P_Ω} g(Z^N, Ω).
- **Theorem 1 (Sec. 3.4, eqs 13–14), regularized Ville**: for any regularizer ρ and e-process family {e_θ}, P̄[e^reg{Z^N, f(Ω)}] ≤ 1 and P̄(e^reg{Z^N, f(Ω)} > α^{−1}) ≤ α for all α and all stopping times N. Proof (App. G.3): factor g = ρ(f(ω)) e_{f(ω)}, use E_ω[e_θ(Z^N)] ≤ 1 inside, then Q̄ρ ≤ 1; the probability bound is Markov inside the upper expectation. Related to Ramdas and Manole's uniformly randomised Markov inequality: regularisation divides the e-process by a no-smaller-than-uniform variable γ ∘ q(Θ).
- **Sec. 3.5 (statistical implications)**: the test "reject Θ ∈ H iff inf_{θ∈H} e^reg(z^n, θ) > 1/α" has P̄(Type I error) = P̄{f(Ω) ∈ H and reject} ≤ α; the region C^reg_α = {θ : e^reg(z^n, θ) ≤ 1/α} has non-coverage upper probability ≤ α (eq. 16). With a vacuous prior this collapses to sup_{ω : f(ω) ∈ H} P_ω{inf_H e_θ(Z^N) > 1/α} ≤ α (eq. 15, Remark 3: the sup over a vacuous credal set is attained at point masses), i.e. ordinary anytime validity. Monotonicity gives P̄(Type I error) ≤ Q̄(H), so control is automatic for a priori implausible H and effort concentrates on plausible ones.
- **Sec. 3.6 (efficiency, Gaussian mean, known variance 1)**: Savage–Dickey e-process e_θ(z^n) = (nv+1)^{1/2} exp{−(n/2)(θ − z̄)² + ½ (n/(nv+1)) z̄²} with ξ = N(0, v), v = 10 (eq. after Fig. 1). Possibilistic prior via probability-to-possibility transform of N(0, K): q(θ) = 1 − pchisq(θ²/K, 1), K ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8} (Remark 4: the smallest possibilistic credal set containing N(0, K)). Fig. 2: n = 5, z̄ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1}; smaller K gives larger gains, and the 95% set {log e^reg ≤ −log 0.05 ≈ 3} narrows. Fig. 3: average log e^reg(z^n, 0.7) over 1000 replications of N(0, 1) data grows fastest for K = 0.1, so a false "Θ = 0.7" is rejected with fewer samples. App. E repeats this for weaker priors (E|Θ| ≤ K gives q = 1 ∧ K/|θ|; "at most K/5 sure that |Θ| > 2K") with small gains.
- **Sec. 4.2–4.3, Theorem 2**: taking the ranking function r = 1/e_θ in Martin's possibilistic IM with the stopping rule treated as a nuisance parameter gives the contour bound π^e_{z^n}(θ) = 1 ∧ e_θ(z^n)^{−1}, "what Grünwald (2023) calls his capped e-posterior"; Π̄^e(H) = sup_{θ∈H} π^e(θ) is anytime valid: sup_{ω : f(ω) ∈ H} P_ω{Π̄^e_{Z^N}(H) ≤ α} ≤ α for all N, H (eq. 19); Π̄^e(H) ≤ α iff inf_H e_θ ≥ 1/α, so the derived test is the e-process test.
- **Corollaries 1–2**: strong anytime validity sup_ω P_ω{π^e_{Z^N}(f(ω)) ≤ α} ≤ α (eq. 20) and, equivalently (eq. 41), uniform-in-hypotheses validity sup_ω P_ω{Π̄^e_{Z^N}(H) ≤ α for some H ∋ f(ω)} ≤ α (eq. 22), which holds even for adversarially data-chosen H. The level set C^e_α = {θ : π^e > α} is exactly the unregularized e-confidence set. Remark 5: the possibilistic form is what makes the contour determine the whole upper probability and reach 1, so uniform validity is attainable without loss of efficiency (Lemma 1 of Martin 2022b).
- **Theorem 3 and Corollary 3 (Sec. 4.4, eqs 23–24)**: the regularized contour π^{e×ρ} = 1 ∧ 1/e^reg satisfies P̄(π^{e×ρ}_{Z^N}{f(Ω)} ≤ α) ≤ α for all α, N, and hence P̄(∃H ∋ f(Ω) with Π̄^{e×ρ}_{Z^N}(H) ≤ α) ≤ α; the level sets coincide with C^reg_α. Because e-processes are likelihood ratios, the IM depends on data only through the likelihood and so satisfies the likelihood principle while keeping these frequentist-like guarantees.
- **Sec. 4.5 (median example)**: nonparametric median θ = argmin_t P_ω|Z − t| with the Dey–Martin–Williams e-process e^n_θ = exp{−η Σ_i (L_{θ̂_{i−1}}(z_i) − L_θ(z_i))}, running sample median as plug-in; subgaussianity of the 1-Lipschitz loss difference gives η ≤ 2ε when the density at the median is ≥ ε (ε = 0.1 here). Prior q(θ) = {0.05·1(θ < 0) + 1(θ ≥ 0)}/(1 + |θ|) ("95% sure the median is positive", further values less plausible). Fig. 4: n = 25 from t_2, shifted so the sample median is −0.5, 0, 0.5; regularisation helps most when data are mildly incompatible with the prior.
- **Sec. 4.6, Theorem 4 (decisions)**: upper expected loss Π̄^{e×ρ}(ℓ_a) = ∫_0^1 sup_{θ : π^{e×ρ}(θ) ≥ s} ℓ_a(θ) ds, best action â = argmin_a Π̄^{e×ρ}(ℓ_a) (eq. 25). Guarantee: P̄{sup_{a∈A} ℓ_a(f(Ω)) / Π̄^{e×ρ}_{Z^N}(ℓ_a)} ≤ 1 for all N (eq. 26), with vacuous-prior form sup_ω E_ω{sup_a ℓ_a(f(ω)) / Π̄^e(ℓ_a)} ≤ 1, "generalizing a result in Grünwald (2023) for capped e-posteriors"; Markov gives the probability version (27) and the sup inside allows data-dependent actions, in particular â(Z^N). Proof (App. G.6) shows ℓ_a(Θ)/Π̄(ℓ_a) ≤ e^reg(Z^N, Θ) pointwise. Gaussian illustration: squared-error loss, â = z̄, Π̄^e ℓ_â = n^{−1}{2 + log(nv+1) + (n/(nv+1)) z̄²} (App. B); regularised risk curves shrink the minimiser toward 0 (Fig. 6).
- **Theorem 5 (App. F)**: the updating rule (Q̄, z^n) ↦ (Π̲^{e×ρ}, Π̄^{e×ρ}) avoids one-sided contraction, sup_{n, z^n} Π̄^{e×ρ}_{z^n}(H) ≥ Q̄(H) (eq. 40), hence avoids sure loss and two-sided contraction; proof uses inf_{n,z^n} e_θ(z^n) ≤ 1 (an average is never below the minimum) and {inf_H ρ} Q̄(H) ≤ Q̄ρ ≤ 1. Full temporal coherence in Walley's sense is not attained in general (Martin 2022a Sec. 3.3). Remark 6: the contour fails to be a possibility contour only if e_θ(z^n) > 1 for every θ, a rare event by Ville.
- **Sec. 5 (ECMO application)**: Ware 1989 data, CMT 10 patients 4 deaths, ECMO 9 patients 0 deaths, trial stopped before its planned fourth ECMO death (eq. 28). E-process: Turner–Grünwald 2 × 2 construction with β = 0.18, θ̂_cmt,β = (6+β)/(10+2β), θ̂_ecmo,β = (9+β)/(9+2β). Prior: 90% confident Θ_ecmo ≥ 0.8, 50% confident Θ_cmt ≤ 0.3, contours q_ecmo = 0.1 + 0.9·1(θ ≥ 0.8), q_cmt = 0.5 + 0.5·1(θ ≤ 0.3), product contour, credal set containing products of certain betas. Fig. 7: 95% sets tighten for Θ_ecmo and loosen slightly for Θ_cmt (prior mildly incompatible with the data, "exactly what should happen"); neither test rejects Θ_ecmo ≤ Θ_cmt at α = 0.05, consistent with Ware's Fisher exact p = 0.054. Marginal contour for ∆ = Θ_ecmo − Θ_cmt by the extension principle (Fig. 8). Utility = survival: unregularized [Π̲(∆), Π̄(∆)] = [−0.042, 0.739], regularised [0.086, 0.820], so only with the partial prior is ECMO demonstrably preferred, against Walley's 1996 generalised-Bayes interval [0.152, 0.5].
- **App. C**: finite-T characterisation Q̄ρ ≤ Σ_θ ρ(θ) sup_Q Q({θ}), regularizers ρ_η(θ) = η(θ)/sup_{Q∈Q} Q({θ}) for any sub-probability mass function η ("mimics the universal inference formulation"); contamination (linear–vacuous) classes give Q̄ρ = (1 − ε) Q_cen ρ + ε sup ρ; constant-odds-ratio model via a fixed-point equation; belief functions, 2-monotone capacities and p-boxes left to Troffaes and de Cooman.

## Setting and assumptions
- Data Z_1, Z_2, … iid from P_ω, model {P_ω : ω ∈ O} possibly nonparametric; quantity of interest Θ = f(Ω) with f continuous (Remark 1 gives a work-around, at an efficiency cost); composite "Θ = θ" handled by e_θ = inf_{ω : f(ω) = θ} e_ω, ignoring measurability of the infimum (Sec. 2.2, eqs 1–2).
- Stopping times are arbitrary throughout; [optional stopping](../concepts/optional-stopping.md) and continuation are the motivation, including the ECMO trial that did not follow its own design.
- Prior knowledge is an upper probability Q̄ from a credal set, mostly a possibility measure with contour q; the author insists this is the investigator's actual corpus of knowledge, not a default, and that a probability density with the same shape would add artificial information (Sec. 3.2). Guarantees hold under P̄, which mixes over priors in the credal set, so their force depends on the prior being justified (Sec. 3.5).
- Examples use Savage–Dickey Bayes-factor e-processes with a default mixing density ξ that is explicitly "not genuine prior information" (Sec. 3.6).
- Decision-theoretic results need ℓ ≥ 0; finiteness of upper and lower expected loss requires previsability, e.g. bounded loss (Sec. 4.6).
- Only fixed-dimensional Θ; dimension-adaptive regularisation is left open (Sec. 6).

## Concepts touched
- regularized-e-process (new): knowledge-based regularizer, product rule, induced upper joint distribution and regularized Ville inequality [Def. 1, eq. 8, eq. 12, Thm 1, Prop. 3].
- possibilistic-inferential-model (new): e-possibilistic IM contour 1 ∧ 1/e, strong and uniform anytime validity, likelihood principle, no sure loss, Choquet decision rule [Thms 2–5, Cors 1–3].
- [villes-inequality](../concepts/villes-inequality.md): Theorem 1 generalises it to an upper probability mixing over a credal set of priors; the vacuous case recovers eq. 2.
- [e-process](../concepts/e-process.md): regularisation as a way past the growth limits of an e-process; composite marginalisation by infimum.
- [calibrator](../concepts/calibrator.md): a possibility contour is a prior-relative p-value and ρ = 1/γ(q) with an admissible calibrator is the regularizer; Vovk–Wang beta-mixture calibrator in use [Prop. 1].
- [e-posterior](../concepts/e-posterior.md): capped e-posterior = e-possibilistic IM contour; Theorem 4 extends the e-posterior risk guarantee.
- [confidence-sets-and-sequences](../concepts/confidence-sets-and-sequences.md): regularized e-confidence regions C^reg_α with non-coverage upper probability ≤ α [eq. 16]; marginal sets via the extension principle [Sec. 5].
- [post-hoc-validity](../concepts/post-hoc-validity.md): uniform-in-hypotheses validity against adversarial H and data-dependent actions [Cor. 2, Thm 4].
- [e-merging](../concepts/e-merging.md): "re-merging" of a regularizer with an e-process, product not strictly weakly dominated [Prop. 3].
- [universal-inference](../concepts/universal-inference.md): generalised universal inference for risk minimisers, median e-process with η ≤ 2ε [Sec. 4.5]; ρ_η regularizer in App. C.
- [bayes-factor-e-variable](../concepts/bayes-factor-e-variable.md): Savage–Dickey e-processes with default mixing density as the running example.
- [k-sample-and-contingency-table-e-values](../concepts/k-sample-and-contingency-table-e-values.md): ECMO 2 × 2 analysis with the Turner–Grünwald e-variable [Sec. 5].
- [optional-stopping](../concepts/optional-stopping.md): stopping rule treated as a nuisance parameter in the IM; e-processes bound the sup over stopping rules [Sec. 4.3].

## Open questions / gaps
- Elicitation: how to turn qualitative knowledge into a possibility contour, and how different calibrators γ compare across models (Sec. 6).
- High-dimensional Θ: efficiency gains require regularisation that adapts to dimension, awkward when data stream under a dynamic stopping rule and when Q̄ is meant to be real knowledge (Sec. 6).
- Measurability of inf_{ω : f(ω) = θ} e_ω is ignored (Sec. 2.2).
- Full temporal coherence of the IM updating rule is unattained in general (App. F).
- Proposition 3 is a non-domination result only; the author invites strengthening (App. D.2).
- No growth-rate or optimality theory for the regularized e-process; efficiency is shown by simulation only (Secs 3.6, App. E).
