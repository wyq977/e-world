# The numeraire e-variable and reverse information projection

## Summary
The paper tests an arbitrary composite null P (any nonempty set of probability measures on a measurable space) against a point alternative Q with [e-variables](../concepts/e-variable.md), [0, ∞]-valued X with E_P[X] ≤ 1 for all P ∈ P, and asks which e-variable is optimal under Q. Its central result is that, with no assumptions whatsoever on P or Q, there exists a Q-a.s. unique, Q-a.s. strictly positive e-variable X*, the numeraire, with E_Q[X/X*] ≤ 1 for every e-variable X; this is equivalent to log-optimality E_Q[log(X/X*)] ≤ 0 [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025, Def. 2.1, Prop. 2.4, Thm 2.6]. The numeraire defines a sub-probability measure P* by dP*/dQ = 1/X*, which lies in the effective null P_eff (the bipolar of P) and satisfies every property classically attached to the [reverse information projection](../concepts/reverse-information-projection.md): it minimises relative entropy over P_eff, strong duality E_Q[log X*] = sup_X E_Q[log X] = inf_{P ∈ P_eff} H(Q|P) = H(Q|P*) holds, and when a reference measure and finite entropy or finite description gain exist it coincides with the RIPr of Li 1999 and Lardy, Grünwald and Harremoës 2024 [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025, Lemma 3.3, Def. 3.4, Thm 3.6, Thm 3.13, Cor. 4.10, Cor. 4.11]. Section 4 gives verification tools (the numeraire is the only e-variable that is a likelihood ratio against an equivalent element of P_eff; for generated nulls it suffices to check the numeraire inequality on the generating family plus E_Q[1/X*] = 1; with a reference measure it suffices to check E_Q[p/p*] ≤ 1 over P rather than P_eff) [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025, Thm 4.1, Cor. 4.2, Thm 4.7]. Section 5 computes numeraires for nulls without a reference measure (bounded mean, sub-Gaussian with nonpositive mean, symmetric distributions), Section 6 extends the theory to power utilities and reverse Rényi projections, and Section 7 proves that universal inference is inadmissible whenever the numeraire differs from it.

## Main results
- Definition 2.1: a numeraire is a Q-a.s. strictly positive e-variable X* with E_Q[X/X*] ≤ 1 for every e-variable X (conventions x/∞ = 0, ∞/x = ∞, ∞/∞ = 1). Unique up to Q-nullsets by Jensen. Terminology from the numeraire portfolio of mathematical finance (Long 1990, Karatzas and Kardaras 2007) [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025, Sec. 2].
- Example 2.2 / Example 3.12 / Corollary 3.15: for a point null {P_0} with Q ≪ P_0 the numeraire is dQ/dP_0; for arbitrary Q it is ∞ on a P_0-null set N carrying the singular part of Q and dQ*/dP_0 off N, and the RIPr is the Q-absolutely-continuous part of P_0 [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025].
- Lemma 2.3: if the numeraire for P is still an e-variable for a larger null P′ ⊃ P, it is the numeraire for P′.
- Proposition 2.4: X* is a numeraire iff it is log-optimal, E_Q[log(X/X*)] ≤ 0 for all e-variables X; the numeraire property is the first-order condition of log-optimality. A log-optimal e-variable is therefore unique even when E_Q[log X*] = ∞. Remark 2.5: benchmarked log-optimality, E_Q[log(X/X_b)] maximised by X* for any benchmark X_b, with finite optimal value.
- Theorem 2.6: a numeraire always exists. Equivalent: (i) X* is Q-a.s. finite; (ii) every e-variable is Q-a.s. finite; (iii) Q ≪ P in the Halmos–Savage sense (Q charges no event that is null under all P ∈ P); (iv) the set of e-variables is bounded in Q-probability. Proof via a Komlós-type lemma (Delbaen and Schachermayer 1994), bounded approximations of the logarithm (Lemma 2.9, first-order condition for a bounded concave utility), and the bipolar/decomposition result of Brannath and Schachermayer 1999 (Lemma 2.7). The authors read this as the e-variable analogue of the fundamental theorem of asset pricing.
- Definition 3.1 / Remark 3.2: the effective null P_eff = {P ∈ M_+ : E_P[X] ≤ 1 for all e-variables X} is the bipolar P°° of P, the largest family with the same e-variables; it consists of sub-probability measures and is solid. A nontrivial e-variable (E_Q[X] > 1) exists iff Q ∉ P_eff.
- Lemma 3.3 / Definition 3.4: dP*/dQ = 1/X* (zero where X* = ∞) defines P* ∈ P_eff with P* ≪ Q; P* is named the RIPr of Q on P_eff.
- Theorem 3.6 (Q ≪ P): for P* ∈ P_eff equivalent to Q, the following are equivalent: P* is the RIPr; E_Q[dP^a/dP*] ≤ 1 for all P ∈ P_eff; E_Q[log(dP^a/dP*)] ≤ 0 for all P ∈ P_eff (P^a the Q-absolutely-continuous part). Strong duality (3.3): E_Q[log X*] = sup_{X ∈ E} E_Q[log X] = inf_{P ∈ P_eff} H(Q|P) = H(Q|P*), possibly all +∞. Remark 3.7 reads the two sides as Gateaux first-order conditions of a convex dual pair. Remark 3.9: P* is the unique element of P_eff with zero maximum description gain in the sense of Lardy, Grünwald and Harremoës 2024. Remark 3.10: extends Zhang, Ramdas and Wang 2024 (finite P: a positive-e-power e-variable exists iff Q ∉ conv P).
- Remark 3.11: P* is in general a sub-probability measure (E_Q[1/X*] < 1 possible, seen in Sections 5.1, 5.4), and distinct elements of P_eff can share the same absolutely continuous part P*.
- Theorem 3.13 (general case): with λ* = Q(X* < ∞), the RIPr is equivalent to Q on {X* < ∞} and lies in λ*·P_eff; the characterisations of Thm 3.6 hold with indicators 1{X* < ∞}, and conditional strong duality holds for Q* = Q(·|X* < ∞) with numeraire X* and RIPr P*/λ*. Remark 3.14: {X* = ∞} is the singular set of the generalised Lebesgue decomposition of Q with respect to P (Lemma A.1), computable without the numeraire.
- Theorem 4.1 (Q ≪ P): a Q-a.s. positive e-variable X* is the numeraire iff the measure dP*/dQ = 1/X* belongs to P_eff. So the numeraire is the only e-variable that is a likelihood ratio dQ/dP against some P ∈ P_eff equivalent to Q; E_{P*}[X*] = 1 and P* is setwise maximal among equivalent elements of P_eff. Strengthens Corollary 2 of Safe Testing.
- Corollary 4.2 (generated nulls P = {P : E_P[X] ≤ 1 for X ∈ E_0}): if E_Q[1/X*] = 1 and E_Q[X/X*] ≤ 1 for all X ∈ E_0, then X* is the numeraire and the RIPr lies in P itself. Example 4.3 (one coin toss) shows the equality E_Q[1/X*] = 1 cannot be weakened to ≤.
- Example 4.4: with P all Dirac masses on [0, 1] and Q uniform, every function in [0, 1] is an e-variable, P_eff is all sub-probabilities, the RIPr is Q itself, and Thm 3.6(ii) restricted to P would be vacuous. Lemma 4.5: under a reference measure μ, P_eff is the smallest μ-closed convex solid set containing P (bipolar theorem of Brannath and Schachermayer 1999). Corollary 4.6: for finite P, P_eff ∩ M_1 = conv P.
- Theorem 4.7 (reference measure μ, Q ≪ P): for p* ∈ P_eff equivalent to Q, RIPr iff E_Q[p/p*] ≤ 1 for all p ∈ P, iff E_Q[log(((1−t)p* + tp)/p*)] ≤ 0 for all t ∈ (0, 1), p ∈ P. Example 4.9: Gaussian mixtures vs Cauchy, H(Q|P) = ∞ for all P ∈ P but P* = Q and H(Q|P*) = 0.
- Corollary 4.10 / Corollary 4.11: for convex P under a reference measure, a sequence with vanishing maximum description gain (Lardy, Grünwald and Harremoës 2024) or an entropy-minimising sequence with some finite H(Q|p) has log p_n → log p* in L¹(Q) with p* the RIPr of Def. 3.4; so Def. 3.4 agrees with Li 1999 and with Lardy et al. whenever those apply. Self-contained proof via the inequality (1/9)(|log t| ∧ |log t|²) ≤ log((2 + t + 1/t)/4).
- Section 5.1: null {N(1, 1), N(−1, 1)}, Q Cauchy (infinite KL to both): RIPr (P_1 + P_2)/2, numeraire 2q/(p_1 + p_2), strictly larger than the universal-inference e-variable q/max(p_1, p_2). Section 5.2 (bounded mean, P = {E_P[Z] ≤ μ} on [0, 1], Q uniform, no dominating measure): numeraire 1 + λ*(Z − μ) with λ* solving (1 + λ*(1 − μ))/(1 − λ*μ) = e^{λ*}, verified by Cor. 4.2 with E_0 = {Z/μ}; Remark 5.1 conjectures E = {X ≤ 1 + λ(Z − μ), λ ∈ [0, 1/μ]}. Section 5.3 (1-sub-Gaussian with nonpositive mean, Q = N(μ, 1), μ > 0): numeraire e^{μZ − μ²/2}, RIPr N(0, 1); Remark 5.2 conjectures E is dominated by mixtures of e^{λZ − λ²/2}. Section 5.4 (symmetric distributions, Q with density q): RIPr p* = ½(q(z) + q(−z))1{q(z) > 0}, numeraire 2q(Z)/(q(Z) + q(−Z)), the set of e-variables is exactly {X ≤ 1 + φ(Z), φ odd}; the same expression appears in Koning 2023. Section 5.5: one-dimensional exponential family with Θ_0 having smallest element θ* > θ_1 recovers Example 4 of Safe Testing: RIPr p_{θ*}, numeraire p_{θ_1}/p_{θ*}. Remark 5.3: for parametric nulls find the RIPr first, for nonparametric nulls find the numeraire first.
- Theorem 6.1 (power utility U(x) = x^{1−γ}/(1 − γ), γ > 1, no assumptions): sup_X E_Q[U(X)] = (1/(1 − γ)) exp((1 − γ) inf_{P ∈ P_eff} R_{1/γ}(Q|P)), both attained; the U-optimal e-variable X*_γ and the reverse Rényi projection P*_γ of order 1/γ are linked by dP*_γ/dQ = (X*_γ)^{−γ}/E_Q[(X*_γ)^{1−γ}]; the reverse Rényi projection of any order in (0, 1) always exists. Remark 6.2: the Lardy et al. e-variable (dP*_γ/dQ)^{−1/γ} is strictly smaller than X*_γ. Remark 6.3 asks whether all f-divergences can be covered.
- Section 7 / Lemma 7.1: with a reference measure, p* ≤ p_max = sup_{p ∈ P} p μ-a.s. (even though p* may lie outside P), hence X* ≥ X_UI = q/p_max; strict with positive probability for nondegenerate composite nulls, so universal inference is inadmissible.
- Appendix A, Lemma A.1: generalised Lebesgue decomposition Q = Q^r + Q^s with Q^r ≪ P and Q^s carried by an essentially unique P-negligible event N. Appendix B, Table 1: Q ≪ P gives finite numeraire, Q ~ P*, description gain H(Q|P* ⇝ P_eff) = 0 even when H(Q|P) = ∞ for all P ∈ P.

## Setting and assumptions
- Single observation on an arbitrary measurable space (Ω, F); null P any nonempty set of probability measures; alternative a single Q. No reference measure, no convexity, no finiteness of relative entropy [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025, Sec. 1].
- E-variables are [0, ∞]-valued; infinite values are allowed and essential (X = ∞·1_A is an e-variable for any P-negligible A). The only structural condition that ever matters is Q ≪ P (Halmos–Savage absolute continuity with respect to a family), which is equivalent to finiteness of the numeraire [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025, Thm 2.6].
- Fixed-sample, non-sequential: no filtration, no stopping times. The sequential extension (supermartingales, [e-processes](../concepts/e-process.md), filtrations) and the composite-alternative extension (mixtures, plug-in) are explicitly left to future work [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025, Sec. 8].
- Power-utility results (Section 6) likewise hold under no conditions; the utility must be bounded above, which is why the logarithm needs the approximation argument of Lemma 2.10.

## Open questions / gaps
- Composite alternatives (via mixtures or plug-in) and the sequential setting with filtrations and stopping times are stated as work in progress [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025, Sec. 8]; the exchangeability caveat raised in the Safe Testing reply belongs here.
- Remarks 5.1 and 5.2: the exact e-variable classes for the bounded-mean and sub-Gaussian nulls are conjectured, not proved.
- Remark 6.3: whether the duality extends to all f-divergences.
- Theorem 2.6 gives no rate or algorithm; computing the numeraire for a general nonparametric null still relies on guessing a candidate and certifying it with Cor. 4.2 or Thm 4.1.
- Missing from the bib: Lardy, Grünwald and Harremoës 2024; Zhang, Ramdas and Wang 2024; Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2024; Li 1999; Casgrain, Larsson and Ziegel 2024; Koning 2023.
