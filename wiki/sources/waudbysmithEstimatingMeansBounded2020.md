# Estimating means of bounded random variables by betting

arXiv 2010.09686v7 (25 August 2022), 70 pages: main text Sections 1–7 (pp. 3–25), proofs in Appendix A, betting strategies in Appendix B, simulations in C–D, additional theory in E, extended history in F. The paper was later published in JRSSB 86(1), 2024, as a discussion paper; the arXiv v7 text layer contains neither the discussion contributions nor a rejoinder, so nothing about the discussion is recorded here (see Versions).

## Summary
The paper treats two classical problems: (Q1) a confidence interval for the mean μ of [0,1]-valued observations with conditional mean μ given the past, and (Q2) a confidence interval for the mean of N fixed numbers in [0,1] sampled without replacement, in both cases sequentially (time-uniform confidence sequences) and at a fixed sample size [waudbysmithEstimatingMeansBounded2020, Sec. 1]. The device is a four-step procedure (Theorem 1): for each candidate mean m build a nonnegative process M^m_t that is an e-process for the composite null P^m = {all distributions on [0,1]^∞ with conditional mean m}, reject H^m_0 when M^m_t ≥ 1/α by Ville's inequality, and let C_t be the set of unrejected m [Sec. 2.2]. The Chernoff method is the special case M^m_t = ∏ exp(λ(X_i − m) − λ²/8) with Markov in place of Ville, so the construction "generalizes and improves" it [Sec. 2.3]. Two families of processes are developed. The warm-up replaces Robbins' mixture over λ by a predictable plug-in sequence λ_t, giving closed-form Hoeffding and empirical-Bernstein confidence sequences that match the conjugate-mixture sequences of Howard et al. but are over 500 times faster to compute [Prop. 1, Thm 2, Fig. 2]. The main contribution is the capital process K_t(m) = ∏(1 + λ_i(m)(X_i − m)), which is an exact test martingale for every P ∈ P^m, and in fact "E(X_t | F_{t−1}) = μ" is equivalent to "K_t(μ) is a test martingale for every predictable λ" (Prop. 2); every test martingale for any set S of distributions is of the form ∏(1 + λ_i Z_i) with conditionally mean-zero Z_i ≥ −1 (Prop. 3, the universal representation). The hedged capital process max{θK^+_t(m), (1−θ)K^−_t(m)} with m-independent, truncated bets yields a confidence sequence that is provably an interval at every time (Thm 3), and the same recipe with a time-varying conditional mean m^WoR_t handles sampling without replacement (Prop. 4, Thm 4). Appendix B derives betting strategies (predictable plug-in, GRAPA, aGRAPA, LBOW, ONS-m, diversified/grid/hedged-grid Kelly, ConBo, SRP); Appendix E shows that the capital process dominates the Hoeffding process term by term for n ≥ 40 log(2/α) (Prop. 6), that a discrete-mixture betting sequence attains the √(log log t / t) LIL rate (Prop. 7) and the hedged CI the 1/√n rate (Prop. 8), and that the hindsight-optimal capital is Owen's empirical likelihood ratio times t^t (Sec. E.6). Simulations show the betting sets dominating Hoeffding, Maurer–Pontil and Audibert et al. empirical Bernstein, Bentkus, Anderson, the conjugate-mixture sequences of Howard et al. and the adaptive Bentkus sequence of Kuchibhotla and Zheng in all four distributional regimes considered [Figs 12–15].

## Main results

### Framework (Section 2)
- **Setting**: Q^m = all distributions on [0,1] with mean m (convex, no common dominating measure); P^μ = all distributions on [0,1]^∞ with E(X_t | F_{t−1}) = μ for every t, covering i.i.d., independent and dependent data. A (1−α)-CS satisfies sup_{P∈P^μ} P(∃t ≥ 1 : μ ∉ C_t) ≤ α (eq. 4), equivalently coverage at arbitrary stopping times [waudbysmithEstimatingMeansBounded2020, Secs 2, 2.1, citing Howard et al. Lemma 3].
- **Theorem 1 (4-step procedure)**: (a) composite null H^m_0 : P ∈ P^m for each m; (b) a nonnegative process M^m_t such that, for each P ∈ P^μ, M^μ_t is upper-bounded by a test (super)martingale for P, possibly different per P (i.e. an e-process); (c) φ^m_t = 1(M^m_t ≥ 1/α); (d) C_t = {m : φ^m_t = 0}. Then (C_t) is a (1−α)-CS, by Ville's inequality. The processes for different m must be "tied together" for power and tractability [Sec. 2.2].
- **Connection to Chernoff (Sec. 2.3)**: Hoeffding's sup_{P∈P^μ} E exp(λ(X−μ) − λ²/8) ≤ 1 (eq. 6) makes eq. 7 a test supermartingale; Markov plus λ = √(8 log(1/α)/n) gives Hoeffding's CI (eq. 2) exactly, and the 4-step version is its time-uniform generalization. Hoeffding's bound is "one powerful, but rather limited way to construct an e-value" with expectation ≤ 1; the paper's processes have expectation exactly 1 and better λ.

### Predictable plug-ins (Section 3)
- **Lemma 1 (App. A)**: if E[exp(λ(X_t − μ) − v_tψ(λ)) | F_{t−1}] ≤ 1 for λ ∈ Λ, then ∏ exp(λ_i(X_i − μ) − v_iψ(λ_i)) is a test supermartingale for any predictable Λ-valued (λ_t).
- **Proposition 1 (PrPl-H)**: for any real predictable (λ_t), Σλ_iX_i/Σλ_i ± (log(2/α) + Σλ_i²/8)/Σλ_i is a (1−α)-CS, as is its running intersection; recommended λ_t = √(8 log(2/α)/(t log(t+1))) ∧ 1 (eq. 12).
- **Theorem 2 (PrPl-EB)**: with ψ_E(λ) = (−log(1−λ) − λ)/4 and v_i = 4(X_i − μ̂_{i−1})², for any (0,1)-valued predictable (λ_t), Σλ_iX_i/Σλ_i ± (log(2/α) + Σv_iψ_E(λ_i))/Σλ_i is a (1−α)-CS. Recommended λ_t = √(2 log(2/α)/(σ̂²_{t−1} t log(1+t))) ∧ c with regularised σ̂²_t = (1/4 + Σ(X_i − μ̂_i)²)/(t+1), μ̂_t = (1/2 + ΣX_i)/(t+1), c = 1/2 or 3/4 (eq. 15). Proof uses Fan, Grama and Liu's inequality exp(ξλ − 4ξ²ψ_E(λ)) ≤ 1 + ξλ plus the Howard et al. swap of (X_i − m)² for (X_i − μ̂_{i−1})² (App. A.2). Matches the conjugate-mixture EB sequence of Howard et al. but is closed-form and "over 500 times faster" [Fig. 2].
- **Remark 1 (PrPl-EB-CI)**: fixed-n version with λ_t = √(2 log(2/α)/(nσ̂²_{t−1})) ∧ c (eq. 16), taking ∩_{i≤n}C_i; derandomisation by averaging over B permutations gives an e-value and a Markov-based CI (not closed-form, running intersection no longer valid; "neither helps nor hurts"). Width: √n·W_n → σ√(2 log(2/α)) a.s. (eq. 17, Prop. 9), the Bernstein/Bennett rate without knowing σ², versus σ√(2 log(4/α)) for Maurer–Pontil.
- **Sec. 3.3, Table 1**: rates of the half-width W_t for λ_t ∝ 1/t (1/log t), √(log t / t), 1/√t (log t/√t), 1/√(t log t) (√(log t / t), recommended), 1/√(t log t log log t) (√(log t / t) up to log log log); the empirical-Bernstein λ* = √(2 log(2/α)/(σ²t*)) comes from minimising (log(2/α) + σ²t*λ²/2)/(t*λ) (eqs 20–22).

### The capital process (Section 4)
- **Definition (eq. 23)**: K_t(m) = ∏_{i≤t}(1 + λ_i(m)(X_i − m)), K_0 = 1, (λ_t(m)) predictable with values in (−1/(1−m), 1/m), so each factor is ≥ 0.
- **Proposition 2**: for [0,1]-valued X and constant μ the following are equivalent: (a) E(X_t | F_{t−1}) = μ for all t; (b) some λ ≠ 0 makes K_t(μ) a strictly positive test martingale; (c) every fixed λ ∈ (−1/(1−μ), 1/μ) does; (d) every predictable (−1/(1−μ), 1/μ)-valued (λ_t) does. The intervals may be any subinterval containing a nonzero value. Every test martingale for P^μ is of the form K_t(μ) for some predictable (λ_t) (proof: M_t/M_{t−1} = 1 + f_t(X_t) with f_t conditionally mean zero under every mean-μ law forces f_t linear). "Test martingales are not simply technical tools ... they are fundamentally at the very heart of the problem definition itself" [App. A.3].
- **Proposition 3 (universal representation)**: for any set S of (possibly unbounded) distributions, (M_t) is a test martingale for S iff M_t = ∏(1 + λ_iZ_i) with Z_i ≥ −1, E_S[Z_i | F_{i−1}] = 0 for every S ∈ S, λ_i predictable and λ_iZ_i ≥ −1; for supermartingales replace "= 0" by "≤ 0" [App. A.4]. Example: ∏(1 + λ_i(1{X_i ≤ q} − p)) is a test martingale for "p-quantile equals q" [Sec. 7].
- **Sec. 4.1 (betting)**: bet b_t = s_t|λ^m_t| with sign s_t = ±1 for "μ > m" or "μ < m"; λ = 0 is no bet, λ ∈ {−1/(1−m), 1/m} risks everything; under H^m_0 no strategy makes money in expectation; capital ≥ 1/α rejects. Test supermartingales are games "stacked against" the gambler and give conservative tests; every nonnegative supermartingale is some gambler's wealth, so the gambling analogy "can be entirely dropped".
- **Sec. 4.2 (likelihood ratios)**: P^m is not dominated, yet by Ramdas, Ruf, Larsson and Koolen (2020, Prop. 4) every composite test martingale equals a likelihood ratio of some Q′ against each Q ∈ S; test martingales are "nonparametric composite generalizations of likelihood ratios".
- **Sec. 4.3 (constrained adversary)**: the adversary may pick Q_t ∈ Q^μ at each round after seeing the statistician's disclosed bets; all results continue to hold because such P lies in the fork-convex hull of P^μ (Ramdas, Ruf, Larsson and Koolen 2021), though no fork-convexity result is used.
- **Theorem 3 (Hedged capital CS)**: K^±_t(m) = max{θK^+_t(m), (1−θ)K^−_t(m)} with K^+ using λ^+_t(m) = |λ̃^+_t| ∧ c/m, K^− using λ^−_t(m) = |λ̃^−_t| ∧ c/(1−m), λ̃^± real predictable and not depending on m, c ∈ [0,1). Then B^±_t = {m : K^±_t(m) < 1/α} is a (1−α)-CS, so is its running intersection, and B^±_t is an interval for every t (K^+ is nonincreasing and K^− nondecreasing in m, hence K^± quasiconvex, Lemma 2). Replacing the max by M^±_t = θK^+_t + (1−θ)K^−_t gives a tighter CS, empirically indistinguishable (Fig. 5) but not provably an interval (Remark 2). Recommended λ̃^±_t = √(2 log(2/α)/(σ̂²_{t−1} t log(t+1))) (eq. 26), c = 1/2 or 3/4; point estimate argmin_m K^±_t(m).
- **Remark 3 (Hedged-CI)**: fixed n with λ̃^±_t = √(2 log(2/α)/(nσ̂²_{t−1})) (eq. 27) and ∩_{i≤n}B^±_i; not symmetric and adapts to asymmetry (Figs 4, 6). Rates: O(√(log log t / t)) and O(1/√n) achievable (App. E.2) and a.s. domination of Hoeffding for large n (App. E.1).

### Sampling without replacement (Section 5)
- **Model (eq. 28)**: fixed x_1..x_N ∈ [0,1], X_t uniform on the unobserved ones; P^m = uniform permutation measures of lists with mean m. Prior supermartingales of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2020 (eqs 29–30) and the Kaplan martingale ∫_0^1 ∏(1 + γ(X_i(1−(i−1)/N)/(μ − ΣX_j/N) − 1)) dγ credited by Stark 2020 (eq. 31), a degree-t polynomial that is expensive for large t.
- **Prop. 4 and eq. 34**: with μ^WoR_t = (Nμ − Σ_{i<t}X_i)/(N − t + 1) and m^WoR_t analogously, K^WoR_t(m) = ∏(1 + λ_i(m)(X_i − m^WoR_i)) with λ_t ∈ (−1/(1−m^WoR_t), 1/m^WoR_t); "E(X_t | F_{t−1}) = μ^WoR_t for all t" iff K^WoR_t(μ) is a test martingale for every such predictable λ [App. A.8].
- **Theorem 4 (Hedged-WoR)**: the hedged WoR capital process with λ^±_t(m) = |λ̇^±_t| ∧ c/m^WoR_t, c/(1−m^WoR_t) gives a (1−α)-CS that is an interval for each t; same recommended λ̇ as eq. 26, c = 1/2; **Remark 4** gives the fixed-n Hedged-WoR-CI. Any strategy of Appendix B transfers by replacing μ with μ^WoR_t [Figs 7–8, 14–15].
- **Sec. 5.4 (composite null testing)**: for H_0 : μ ∈ S ⊂ [0,1], p_t = sup_{m∈S} 1/K_t(m) and p̃_t = inf_{s≤t}p_s are anytime-valid p-values, and e_t = inf_{m∈S}K_t(m) is an e-process; p_t is the smallest α at which the CS misses S.

### Betting strategies (Appendix B)
- **B.1**: with λ ∈ [0,1), Fan's inequality log(1 + λy) ≥ λy − 4ψ_E(λ)y² gives K^+_t(μ) ≳ M^PrPl-EB_t(μ), so the empirical-Bernstein plug-in is a sensible bet and the capital process is at least as powerful; bets in [0,1/m] ⊃ [0,1) help further (Fig. 19).
- **B.2 GRAPA** ("growth rate adaptive to the particular alternative"): solve (1/(t−1))Σ_{i<t}(X_i − m)/(1 + λ(X_i − m)) = 0 for λ^GRAPA_t(m), truncated to (−c/(1−m), c/m) (eq. 42); the empirical plug-in for the stationarity condition of argmax_λ E[log(1 + λ(X_t − m)) | F_{t−1}] (eqs 43–44), following Kumon, Takemura and Takeuchi's "sequential optimization strategy"; described as "a sequentially adaptive version of the worst-case GROW criterion" of Grünwald et al.; excellent but slow (root-finding per t and m; 197 s in Table 2 versus 0.25 s for Hedged).
- **B.3 aGRAPA**: Taylor-approximate to λ^aGRAPA_t(m) = (−c/(1−m)) ∨ (μ̂_{t−1} − m)/(σ̂²_{t−1} + (μ̂_{t−1} − m)²) ∧ c/m; sublevel sets need not be intervals (App. E.4 worst case with X_1 = X_2 = 0 and prior variance 1/20 at level 0.85).
- **B.4 LBOW** (lower bound on wealth): maximise the concave lower bound of Lemma 3, log(1+λy) ≥ λy + y²(log(1−mλ) + mλ)/m² for λ ∈ [0,1/m), giving λ^L_t(m) = (−c/(1−m)) ∨ (μ̂_{t−1} − m)/(ω_t|μ̂_{t−1} − m| + σ̂²_{t−1} + (μ̂_{t−1} − m)²) ∧ c/m with ω_t = m or 1−m by the sign (eq. 47).
- **B.5 ONS-m**: Cutkosky–Orabona online Newton step with update λ_{t+1} = λ_t − (2/(2 − log 3)) z_t/A_t, z_t = y_t/(1 − y_tλ_t), A_t = 1 + Σz_i², truncated (Algorithm 1); competitive but slower (12 s).
- **B.6 dKelly / gKelly / hgKelly**: averaging D capital processes is a test martingale with log-capital above the average log-capital (Jensen); grid Kelly averages constant bets on a G-point grid of [−1/(1−m), 1/m]; **Prop. 5**: the hedged grid Kelly θ/G Σ_g∏(1 + λ^{g+}(X_i − m)) + (1−θ)/G Σ_g∏(1 + λ^{g−}(X_i − m)) is a test martingale whose level sets are intervals (K^hgKelly is convex in m, App. A.7). The grid should grow slowly with t to mimic the optimal λ.
- **B.7 ConBo** (Corollaries 1–2): bet against the current lower and upper confidence boundaries l_{t−1}, u_{t−1} with any strategy, hedge, and update; CS is an interval; fastest in Table 2 (0.08 s with LBOW); WoR version by replacing m with m^WoR_t.
- **B.8 SRP** (sequentially rebalanced portfolio): K^SRP_t(m) = Σ_d(1 + λ^d_t(m)(X_t − m))θ^d_tK^SRP_{t−1}(m) with predictable convex weights; the most general strategy, containing all others.

### Additional theory (Appendix E)
- **Prop. 6**: for x, m ∈ [0,1], λ ∈ R, exp(λ(x−m) − λ²/8) ≤ 1 + γ^m(λ)(x−m) with γ^m(λ) = exp(−mλ − λ²/8)(e^λ − 1); γ ∈ [−1,1] whenever |λ| ≤ 0.45, so a capital-process CI dominates Hoeffding's a.s. when n ≥ 40 log(2/α).
- **Prop. 7**: the discrete-mixture capital process K^L_t = ½K^{L+}_t + ½K^{L−}_t with epoch-wise bets γ_k and zeta weights 1/(k^sζ(s)) has width O(√(log log t / t)), by comparison with a stitched Hoeffding boundary (Howard et al. Thm 1) that it eventually dominates.
- **Prop. 8**: with constant λ_n ≍ 1/√n the hedged CI has width O(1/√n), via Lemma 3 and a closed-form superset ΣX_i/n ± (log(2/α) + 4nψ_E(λ_n))/(nλ_n); the recommended predictable, truncated, intersected version is not covered by the proof but is tighter in simulation (Fig. 19).
- **Prop. 9**: √n W_n → σ√(2 log(2/α)) a.s. for PrPl-EB-CI under i.i.d. sampling (Lemmas 4–8).
- **E.5**: non-i.i.d. check, 250 or 2500 Beta(10,10) draws then Bernoulli(1/2): betting CSs stay tight and valid.
- **E.6**: the hindsight-optimal capital K^HS_t(m) satisfies EL_t(m) = (1/t)^t K^HS_t(m), Owen's empirical likelihood ratio, and is proportional to Mykland's dual likelihood ratio.
- **D.5, Figs 17–18**: plotting the CS across all α, and 1/K_t(m) against m as an unnormalised "confidence distribution" whose mode is a point estimate, superlevel sets are the CSs and heights (capped at 1) are anytime-valid p-values.

### History (Section 6, Appendix F, Figure 9)
- Four threads: probability (Ville 1939, Doob, Martin-Löf, Shafer and Vovk), statistical inference (Wald 1945, Robbins–Darling–Siegmund–Lai, Grünwald et al., Howard et al., Ramdas et al.), information theory (Kelly 1956, Breiman, Krichevsky–Trofimov, Cover), online learning (Cesa-Bianchi and Lugosi, Orabona and coauthors, Rakhlin and Sridharan); the threads have "rarely cited each other". Wald's eq. (10:10) is identified as the first predictable plug-in; Ville, Wald, Kelly and Robbins are named as the indispensable ancestors.

## Setting and assumptions
- Null for each m: the nonparametric composite class P^m of all laws on [0,1]^∞ with conditional mean m; no i.i.d. assumption, no dominating measure, no variance knowledge [waudbysmithEstimatingMeansBounded2020, Sec. 2]. Extends to a constrained adversary choosing Q_t ∈ Q^μ after seeing the bets (Sec. 4.3).
- Without replacement: a fixed finite population in [0,1], randomness only from the uniformly random order (Sec. 5).
- Bets must be predictable and keep 1 + λ_t(X_t − m) ≥ 0; the hedged construction additionally needs the untruncated bet not to depend on m to guarantee intervals (Thm 3, App. E.4).
- Optional stopping allowed throughout: all CSs are time-uniform via Ville's inequality; fixed-n CIs are the intersection ∩_{i≤n} of the sequence (Remarks 1, 3, 4). Derandomised permutation averages are only fixed-n e-values (Remark 1).
- Guiding principles (maximising expected log-capital, variance plug-ins) affect tightness only; validity holds for any predictable bets (Sec. 3.3, App. B).

- [mixture-and-plug-in-methods](../concepts/mixture-and-plug-in-methods.md): plug-in versus mixture for nonparametric supermartingales, "betting can be viewed as building up a data-dependent mixture distribution on the fly" (Sec. 2.1); dKelly/gKelly/SRP as mixtures of strategies; Krichevsky–Trofimov and Cover as mixture ancestors.
- [test-martingale](../concepts/test-martingale.md): Prop. 2 (mean-μ ⇔ K_t(μ) is a test martingale), Prop. 3 (every test (super)martingale for any S is a product of 1 + λ_iZ_i), the martingale-versus-supermartingale looseness argument (Secs 3.3, 4.1).
- [e-process](../concepts/e-process.md): step (b) of Thm 1 is exactly the e-process condition; inf_{m∈S}K_t(m) is an e-process for μ ∈ S (Sec. 5.4); fork-convex hull remark (Sec. 4.3).
- [testing-by-betting](../concepts/testing-by-betting.md): the parallel-games reading, bets as fractions of capital with sign, the constrained adversary, "the gambling analogy can be entirely dropped" (Secs 1, 4.1, 4.3).
- [gro-criterion](../concepts/gro-criterion.md): GRAPA as empirical maximisation of E[log(1 + λ(X − m)) | F_{t−1}], "a sequentially adaptive version of the worst-case GROW criterion"; Kelly's log-optimality as the motivation (App. B.2, Sec. 6).
- [villes-inequality](../concepts/villes-inequality.md): the only probabilistic tool used (Thm 1 proof); "nearly an equality" for martingales (App. E.2); Ville's theorem on null sets (Sec. 6).
- [optional-stopping](../concepts/optional-stopping.md): all sequences are valid at arbitrary stopping times; the fixed-n intersection ∩_{i≤n}B_i is validated by the same property (Remarks 1, 3).
- [e-posterior](../concepts/e-posterior.md): App. D.5's plots of 1/K_t(m) against m as an unnormalised confidence distribution.
- [martingale-history](../concepts/martingale-history.md): the four-thread history of betting (Sec. 6, App. F, Fig. 9), Wald's eq. (10:10) as the first plug-in, Doob stripping martingales of their betting roots.

## Open questions / gaps
- Whether aGRAPA/LBOW/GRAPA bets (which depend on m) can be restricted, or α restricted, so that their confidence sets are always intervals; the link to empirical likelihood suggests it may be possible [waudbysmithEstimatingMeansBounded2020, App. E.4].
- The recommended hedged CI (predictable, m-truncated, intersected) is not covered by the O(1/√n) proof of Prop. 8 [App. E.2.2].
- The derandomised permutation-averaged e-value is not closed-form and "neither helps nor hurts" [Remark 1].
- Regret-to-concentration reductions (Rakhlin–Sridharan, Jun–Orabona) are loose in constants; "exploring deeper connections may yield other confidence sequences or betting strategies" [Sec. 7].
- Quantile CSs via ∏(1 + λ_i(1{X_i ≤ q} − p)) are left to future work against the Howard–Ramdas benchmark [Sec. 7].
- The JRSSB discussion and rejoinder are not in the raw file; a separate source or an updated text layer would be needed to cover them.
