# Sanov property, generalized I-projection and a conditional limit theorem

## Summary
For i.i.d. X_1, X_2, … with common distribution P_X on an arbitrary measurable space, a set Π of probability measures has the Sanov property if the empirical distribution P̂_n satisfies lim (1/n) log Pr{P̂_n ∈ Π} = −D(Π ‖ P_X), where D(Π ‖ Q) = inf_{P ∈ Π} D(P ‖ Q) (1.1)–(1.2) [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984]. The paper obtains, by one elementary information-theoretic identity, both the known large-deviation statement for convex Π and a new description of the joint conditional law of X_1, …, X_n given P̂_n ∈ Π. The limiting object is the generalized I-projection P* of P_X on Π: the variation limit of every sequence in Π whose divergence from P_X tends to the infimum, which exists for every convex Π with finite D(Π ‖ P_X) but need not belong to Π. The main theorem gives an upper bound on Pr{P̂_n ∈ Π} valid for every n ("a general Chernoff bound") and shows that, given the event, the observations are asymptotically quasi-independent with limiting distribution P*, a notion introduced here. When the event is that a sample mean of a statistic ψ with values in a locally convex topological vector space lies in a convex set C, P* is a member of the exponential family generated by P_X and ψ, and the rate has a dual (Legendre-type) expression. The paper frames this as a justification of the maximum entropy principle in physics.

## Main results
- **I-projection and generalized I-projection (Section 1).** For convex Π with D(Π ‖ Q) < ∞: P* is the I-projection if P* ∈ Π and D(P* ‖ Q) = D(Π ‖ Q); it exists if Π is variation-closed. In general there is a P*, not necessarily in Π, to which every sequence P_n ∈ Π with D(P_n ‖ Q) → D(Π ‖ Q) converges in variation (Csiszár 1975, Theorem 2.1 and Remark); this is the generalized I-projection [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984].
- **Pythagorean inequality (1.5).** D(P ‖ Q) ≥ D(P ‖ P*) + D(Π ‖ Q) for every P ∈ Π. Attributed here to Csiszár 1975, Theorem 2.2, when P* is the I-projection, and to Topsøe 1979, Theorem 8, when P* is the generalized I-projection ("called by him the relative center of attraction"); moreover (1.5) uniquely determines P*. Consequently every minimising sequence converges to P* in information, D(P_n ‖ P*) → 0, which is stronger than convergence in variation [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984].
- **(1.6) and the two examples.** Always D(P* ‖ Q) ≤ D(Π ‖ Q), by lower semicontinuity, and strict inequality is possible (Example 3.2: Q on the half-line with density proportional to (s + 1)^{−3} e^{−s}-type tail, Π_a the laws with mean a; the OCR of the density is unreliable). Example 3.1 (after Csiszár 1962): it is not possible in general to define a "closure" of Π such that the generalized I-projection on Π is the I-projection on the closure [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984].
- **Definition 2.1 (asymptotic quasi-independence).** Variables with joint laws P^{(n)} are asymptotically quasi-independent with limiting distribution Q if (1/n) D(P^{(n)} ‖ Q^n) → 0 (2.8). Then any event of exponentially small Q^n-probability has P^{(n)}-probability tending to 0 (2.9). If the marginals agree, each marginal converges to Q in information, by D(P_n ‖ Q) ≤ (1/n) D(P^{(n)} ‖ Q^n) (2.10), which follows from the identity D(P^{(n)} ‖ Q_1 × … × Q_n) = D(P^{(n)} ‖ P_1 × … × P_n) + Σ D(P_i ‖ Q_i) (2.11) [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984].
- **Convergence in information.** Stronger than variation: |P − Q| ≤ √(2 D(P ‖ Q)) (2.12), and D(P_n ‖ Q) → 0 implies ∫ f dP_n → ∫ f dQ whenever ∫ exp(tf) dQ < ∞ for small |t| (2.13; Csiszár 1975, Lemma 3.1) [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984].
- **Definitions 2.2–2.3.** The τ_0-topology on the set Λ of all probability measures, slightly weaker than the τ-topology of Groeneboom, Oosterhoff and Ruymgaart 1979 (2.14)–(2.15). Π is completely convex if it is closed under arbitrary mixtures (Markov-kernel averages); almost completely convex if it is convex and contains an increasing sequence of completely convex subsets whose union contains Π ∩ Λ_f, with Λ_f the finitely atomic measures [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984].
- **Theorem 1 (main).** Let Π be almost completely convex, and if D(Π ‖ P_X) < ∞ let P* be the generalized I-projection of P_X on Π. Then (2.16) (1/n) log Pr{P̂_n ∈ Π} ≤ −D(Π ‖ P_X) for every n, and for each Π′ ⊂ Π with Pr{P̂_n ∈ Π′} > 0, (2.17) (1/n) log Pr{P̂_n ∈ Π′} ≤ −D(Π ‖ P_X) − (1/n) D(P_{X^n | P̂_n ∈ Π′} ‖ P*^n). If moreover D(int_{τ_0} Π′ ‖ P_X) = D(Π ‖ P_X) < ∞ (2.18), then Π and Π′ have the Sanov property and X_1, …, X_n are asymptotically quasi-independent under the condition P̂_n ∈ Π′ with limiting distribution P*. Corollary (2.19): D(P_{X | P̂_n ∈ Π′} ‖ P*) ≤ −(1/n) log Pr{P̂_n ∈ Π′} − D(Π ‖ P_X), tying the speed of the conditional limit to the speed in the Sanov property [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984].
- **Theorem 2 (finitely many inequality constraints).** For Π = {P : ∫ f_i dP ≥ 0, i = 1, …, k} (2.20): D(Π ‖ Q) < ∞ iff some P ∈ Π has P ≪ Q. The generalized I-projection has Q-density exp{D + Σ ϑ*_i f_i} on {s : (f_1(s), …, f_k(s)) ∈ M} and 0 elsewhere, for a linear subspace M of R^k and ϑ* ∈ R^k_+ (2.21). It belongs to the exponential family dP_ϑ/dQ = exp Σ ϑ_i f_i / ∫ exp(Σ ϑ_i f_i) dQ (2.22) iff some P ∈ Π is mutually absolutely continuous with Q; then D(Π ‖ Q) = max_{ϑ ∈ Θ ∩ R^k_+} [−log ∫ exp(Σ ϑ_i f_i) dQ] (2.23), attained iff P_ϑ = P*. Corollary: the same with equality constraints, R^k_+ replaced by R^k [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984].
- **Theorem 3 (mean in a convex set).** V a locally convex topological vector space, Q a convex-tight probability measure on it (automatic on separable Fréchet spaces), C ⊂ V convex whose interior meets the convex hull of the support of Q. With Π(C) = {P : E(P) ∈ C} (2.27): D(Π(C) ‖ Q) = D(Π_0(int C) ‖ Q) = D < ∞ (2.28), and the generalized I-projection P* belongs to the exponential family dP_ϑ/dQ = exp ϑ(·) / ∫ exp ϑ(·) dQ, ϑ ∈ V′ (2.29). Further D = max_{ϑ ∈ Θ} [inf_{v ∈ C} ϑ(v) − log ∫ exp ϑ(·) dQ] (2.30), attained iff P_ϑ = P*. Remark 2.2 compares with Theorem 2 for half-space constraints: Theorem 2 is slightly stronger there [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984].
- **Theorem 4 (sample means).** ψ a measurable map into V, Q the image of P_X under ψ, convex-tight; C convex whose interior meets the support of Q. Then lim (1/n) log Pr{(1/n) Σ ψ(X_i) ∈ C} = −D with D from (2.30) (2.34), and given (1/n) Σ ψ(X_i) ∈ C the X_i are asymptotically quasi-independent with limiting distribution dP*/dP_X = c exp ϑ*(ψ(·)) (2.36), ϑ* attaining the maximum in (2.30). The bound (2.19) holds in this case as (2.37) [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984].
- **Discussion of prior work (end of Section 2).** The every-n bound (2.16) "may be viewed as a general Chernoff bound"; for the discrete case it is in Csiszár and Körner 1981, and a special case is Bártfai's 1977 multidimensional Bernstein–Chernoff inequality. Exponential-family representation of generalized I-projections under moment constraints: Jupp and Mardia 1983, generalising Topsøe 1979. Earlier conditional limit theorems: Vasicek 1980, Van Campenhout and Cover 1981, Bártfai, Zabell 1980, Bahadur–Zabell 1979, Groeneboom–Oosterhoff–Ruymgaart 1979 [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984].
- **Sections 3–4 (tools).** Lemma 3.1–3.3 on generalized I-projections under restriction and mappings; Lemma 3.4, a generalisation of Csiszár 1975, Theorem 3.1, to convex cones of constraint functions; Lemma 4.1 (lower bound under τ_0-interior condition); Lemma 4.2 (the key step for completely convex sets); Lemma 4.3: sets defined by families of moment constraints, restricted to an increasing sequence of subsets of the space, are almost completely convex [csiszarSanovPropertyGeneralized1984]. These were skimmed, not checked line by line.

## Setting and assumptions
- Data: i.i.d. X_i on an arbitrary measurable space; non-measurable events are handled with outer and inner probabilities (2.4).
- The set Π is convex (almost completely convex for Theorem 1). Complements of convex sets, such as the complement of a KL ball, are outside the scope of every theorem here.
- The reference measure is in the second argument: D(P ‖ Q) is minimised over P ∈ Π.
- Fixed n; no stopping, no testing, no e-values. The every-n bound (2.16) is a large-deviation inequality, not an anytime-valid one.
- Theorems 3–4 need convex-tightness and a nonempty-interior condition on C; no moment generating function assumption appears in their statements (the exponential family is taken over those ϑ for which the integral is finite).
