# Exact anytime-valid confidence intervals for contingency tables and beyond

Short communication (Statistics & Probability Letters, received June 2022, accepted March 2023). Companion to the k-sample paper [turnerGenericEvariablesExact2024](turnerGenericEvariablesExact2024.md), whose Theorem 1 it generalises. Implemented in the R package `safestats` (Ly, Turner & ter Schure 2022).

## Summary
Two i.i.d. data streams Y_{i,a} ~ P_{theta_a} and Y_{i,b} ~ P_{theta_b} arrive in blocks of n_a and n_b observations; earlier work gave GRO e-variables for the equality null theta_a = theta_b [turnerGenericEvariablesExact2024]. This note extends the construction to an arbitrary null set Theta_0 subset of Theta^2 by passing to a "modified problem" with a single outcome whose group label G is drawn with probability n_a/n, computing the reverse information projection p° of the point alternative onto the convex hull of the modified null, and re-expanding the resulting single-outcome likelihood ratio into a block likelihood ratio (Theorem 1) [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023]. For 2x2 contingency tables (both streams Bernoulli) and any closed convex Theta_0 in [0,1]^2, the projection collapses to a single KL-closest null point (theta_a°, theta_b°), the e-variable becomes a plain likelihood ratio p_{theta*}/p_{theta°}, and it is GRO (Theorem 2) [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023]. Composite alternatives are handled by plugging in a Bayes posterior-mean estimate of (theta_a*, theta_b*) from previous blocks (eq. 10), which keeps the product an e-variable for any estimator. Inverting the family of tests {Theta_0(delta)} over an effect size delta and applying Ville's inequality gives an exact, nonasymptotic (1 - alpha) anytime-valid confidence sequence (eq. 12); risk difference and relative risk have convex Theta_0(delta) and work directly, while the non-convex log-odds-ratio null is handled by a union of two one-sided sequences built from the convex half-spaces Theta_0(<= delta) and Theta_0(>= delta) [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023]. Simulations (Figures 2-3, alpha = 0.05, n_a = n_b = 1, Beta(0.18, 0.18) priors) show quick shrinkage and a modest gain from the running intersection; the authors state that exact confidence sequences for risk difference and relative risk did not previously exist.

## Main results
- **Definition (eq. 1-2)**: a block e-variable is S = s'(Y_a^{n_a}, Y_b^{n_b}) >= 0 with sup over Theta_0 of E[S] <= 1; the equality-null e-variable of [turnerGenericEvariablesExact2024] is the product of p_{theta_g*}(Y_{g,i}) / ((n_a/n) p_{theta_a*}(Y_{g,i}) + (n_b/n) p_{theta_b*}(Y_{g,i})) over all observations in both groups, and for convex {P_theta} it is GRO, i.e. maximises E_{theta*}[log S] (eq. 3) [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- **Modified problem and RIPr (eq. 4-6)**: define p'_{theta-vec}(Y|a) = p_{theta_a}(y), p'_{theta-vec}(Y|b) = p_{theta_b}(y), Q'(G = a) = n_a/n, and let p°(.|G) minimise the expected log loss inf over finitely supported priors W on Theta_0 of E_{G ~ Q'} E_{Y ~ P_{theta_G*}}[-log p'_W(Y|G)], equivalently the KL projection D(Q'(G,Y) || P°(G,Y)) = inf_W D(Q' || P'_W). Existence follows from Li (1999) under a nondegeneracy condition; Theorem 1 of Grünwald et al. (2023) [grunwaldSafeTesting2024] gives E_{G,Y}[p'_{theta*}(Y|G)/p°(Y|G)] <= 1 for all theta-vec in Theta_0 (eq. 6) [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- **Theorem 1 (general null)**: S[n_a, n_b, theta_a*, theta_b*; Theta_0] := p_{theta_a*}(y_a^{n_a}) p_{theta_b*}(y_b^{n_b}) / (p°(y_a^{n_a}|a) p°(y_b^{n_b}|b)) (eq. 7) is an e-variable for the original block problem. If the modified null H'_0 is convex, Y is finite and H'_0 is weakly compact, then (a) p° = p'_{theta-vec} for a single theta-vec in Theta_0, and (b) S is the (theta_a*, theta_b*)-GRO e-variable [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- **Theorem 2 (2x2 table, convex Theta_0)**: for Y = {0,1}, Theta_0 a closed convex subset of [0,1]^2 meeting the open square, and a point alternative in the open square, min over Theta_0 of kl(theta_a, theta_b) := D(P_{theta*}(block) || P_{theta}(block)) (eq. 8) is uniquely attained at (theta_a°, theta_b°); it equals theta* if theta* in Theta_0, otherwise it lies on the boundary of Theta_0 but not of [0,1]^2. The e-variable (7) is the likelihood ratio p_{theta_a*}(y_a^{n_a}) p_{theta_b*}(y_b^{n_b}) / (p_{theta_a°}(y_a^{n_a}) p_{theta_b°}(y_b^{n_b})) (eq. 9) and is GRO relative to Theta_0 [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- **Composite alternative, sequential form (eq. 10)**: S^{(m)}[n_a, n_b, W_1; Theta_0] = product over blocks j = 1..m and observations of p_{theta-breve_g | Y^{(j-1)}}(Y) / p_{theta-breve_g° | Y^{(j-1)}}(Y), where theta-breve_g is any estimator from the previous j - 1 blocks and theta-breve° is the KL minimiser over Theta_0 for the current estimate; an e-variable (and hence a test martingale across blocks) for any estimator. The implementation uses the Bayes posterior mean under a prior W_1 [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- **Linear boundary (eq. 11)**: for Theta_0(s, c) = {theta_b = s + c theta_a} (and the half-planes Theta_0(<= s, c), Theta_0(>= s, c)) the KL minimiser solves n_a(-theta_a*/theta_a° + (1 - theta_a*)/(1 - theta_a°)) + n_b c (-theta_b*/theta_b° + (1 - theta_b*)/(1 - theta_b°)) = 0; for s = 0, c = 1 this reduces to the pooled estimate theta_a° = theta_b° = (n_a theta_a* + n_b theta_b*)/n of [turnerGenericEvariablesExact2024]. If theta* lies above (below) the line, the minimiser over the half-plane below (above) it lies on the line [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- **Log-odds-ratio boundary**: Theta_0(<= delta) = {log[theta_b(1 - theta_a)/((1 - theta_b)theta_a)] <= delta} is convex for delta > 0 and Theta_0(>= delta) for delta < 0; the KL minimiser over the curve Theta_0(delta), found numerically, gives a GRO e-variable for the corresponding half-region by Theorem 2 [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- **Anytime-valid confidence sequence (Section 3, eq. 12)**: for an effect size delta(theta_a, theta_b) with convex level sets Theta_0(gamma), CS_{alpha,(m)} := {delta : S^{(m)}[n_a, n_b, W_1; Theta_0(delta)] <= 1/alpha} satisfies P_{theta_a, theta_b}(exists m : delta(theta_a, theta_b) not in CS_{alpha,(m)}) <= alpha for all (theta_a, theta_b) in [0,1]^2, by Ville's inequality applied to the test martingale for the true delta; exact and nonasymptotic. The smallest enclosing interval CI_{alpha,(m)} and the running intersection CS'_{alpha,(m)} = intersection of CS_{alpha,(1..m)} are again AV confidence sequences; the running intersection is the set of deltas not yet rejected under "stop at first crossing of 1/alpha" [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- **Prior choice**: independent Beta priors on theta_a and theta_b; validity holds for any prior (citing Howard et al. 2021 [howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021]); default Beta(0.18, 0.18) for n_a = n_b = 1, the value found in [turnerGenericEvariablesExact2024] to asymptotically approach REGROW [grunwaldSafeTesting2024] among priors of that form [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- **Log odds ratio CS**: Theta_0(delta) is not convex, so eq. 12 is not directly valid. Instead CS+_{alpha,(m)} = {delta >= 0 : S^{(m)}[.; Theta_0(<= delta)] <= 1/alpha} (lower bound) and CS-_{alpha,(m)} = {delta <= 0 : S^{(m)}[.; Theta_0(>= delta)] <= 1/alpha} (upper bound) are AV confidence sequences for the submodels delta >= 0 and delta <= 0 respectively; their union is an AV confidence sequence for every (theta_a, theta_b), since for a true delta > 0 the miscoverage event coincides with that of CS+ [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- **Simulations (Section 3.1, Figures 2-3)**: risk difference uses Theta_0(s = delta, c = 1); the CI is the "beam" between lines theta_b = theta_a + delta_l and theta_b = theta_a + delta_r at which the e-variable equals 1/alpha; relative risk uses Theta_0(s = 0, c = delta). Figure 2: running intersections at several m for various effect sizes, alpha = 0.05, n_a = n_b = 1, asterisks at the MLE. Figure 3: odds-ratio sequences from 500 blocks with theta_a = 0.2, log OR = 2.5 (panels a-b) and theta_a = 0.8, log OR = -2.5 (c-d); CS- is empty in a-b and CS+ in c-d. Figure B.1 (supplement) shows the running intersection improves the sequence "albeit slightly". Code: R package `safestats` [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- **Conclusion (Section 4)**: for the log odds ratio Wald's (1945) sequential probability ratio could also be reinterpreted as a product of e-variables, but it lacks the GRO property (citing Adams 2020); the construction works for any effect size with convex rejection sets Theta_0(<= delta), Theta_0(>= delta). Future work: Theorem 2 for two-sample settings beyond the 2x2 table [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].

## Setting and assumptions
- Two independent i.i.d. streams from a parametric family {P_theta : theta in Theta} with densities; data arrive in blocks of n_a and n_b observations fixed before the block starts (allowed to depend on past blocks in general, held fixed in the note). The product of block e-variables is a test martingale; the level-alpha test rejects when the product over completed blocks exceeds 1/alpha, under any stopping rule tau [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- Nondegeneracy: some theta in Theta has D(P_theta || P_theta') < infinity for all theta' (holds for exponential families); needed for existence of the RIPr (Li 1999), which may be a sub-distribution.
- Theorem 1 GRO part: modified null H'_0 convex, Y finite, H'_0 weakly compact. Theorem 2: Bernoulli streams, Theta_0 closed convex in [0,1]^2 with a point in the open square, alternative in the open square. Without convexity p° must be found numerically from eq. 4 and the GRO claim is lost.
- Confidence sequences: coverage is simultaneous over all m (time-uniform), for every (theta_a, theta_b) in [0,1]^2, irrespective of prior W_1 and stopping rule; sets need not be intervals and are convexified at a possible loss of information.
- Odds-ratio sequences require the one-sided split because Theta_0(delta) is a curve, not a convex set; only the half-regions are convex.

## Open questions / gaps
- Theorem 2 and the confidence sequences are proved only for Bernoulli streams; extension to other two-sample exponential-family settings is stated as the main open direction [turnerExactAnytimevalidConfidence2023].
- No width or shrinkage-rate guarantees are given for the sequences; the comparison with Wald's SPR is qualitative (GRO vs non-GRO), and the gain from running intersection is only described as slight (Figure B.1).
- The odds-ratio sequence uses a one-sided split with a union; whether a two-sided GRO construction exists for the non-convex Theta_0(delta) is not addressed.
- Supplementary appendices (proofs, Figure B.1) are not in raw/; verification of the proof details would need the supplement.
