# Bregman Deviations of Generic Exponential Families

PMLR vol. 195 (COLT 2023), 56 pages: main text Sections 1–6 (pp. 1–14), references, Appendix A (proofs of Theorems 3 and 7, Corollary 8), B (Lemma 1), C (ten worked families, Table 2), D (numerical comparison, tuning of c), E (GLR test, Theorem 5, Lemma 12, variance-change experiment), F (linear contextual bandits). Equation numbers exist only in the appendices, (1)–(15); main-text results are cited by theorem number and section.

## Summary
The paper revisits the method of mixtures (Laplace method) of Robbins and of Peña, Lai and Shao for a parametric exponential family p_θ(x) = h(x) exp(⟨θ, F(x)⟩ − L(θ)) of arbitrary dimension d, and measures the deviation between the parameter and an estimate in the family's own geometry, the Bregman divergence B_L(θ′, θ) = L(θ′) − L(θ) − ⟨θ′ − θ, ∇L(θ)⟩ of the log-partition function, which equals KL(p_θ, p_θ′) [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, Sec. 2]. For each hypothesised parameter θ_0 the exponential martingales M^λ_n = exp(⟨λ, Σ_t (F(X_t) − ∇L(θ_0))⟩ − n B_{L,θ_0}(λ)) are mixed over λ with the conjugate-prior density proportional to exp(−c B_L(θ′, θ_0)) on the parameter set; Bregman–Legendre duality collapses the mixture to the closed form M_n = exp((n + c) B_L(θ_0, θ_{n,c}(θ_0)) − γ_{n,c}(θ_0)), where θ_{n,c}(θ_0) is the estimate whose mean parameter is the average of the n observed features and c copies of ∇L(θ_0) (a maximum a posteriori estimate, footnote 1), and γ_{n,c}(θ_0), the "Bregman information gain", is the log-ratio of the prior and posterior normalising integrals (Definition 2). Ville's inequality then gives the main result, Theorem 3: P_θ(∃n : (n + c) B_L(θ, θ_{n,c}(θ)) > log(1/δ) + γ_{n,c}(θ)) ≤ δ, so the sets Θ_{n,c}(δ) form a confidence sequence, valid at stopping times. By Laplace's approximation γ_{n,c} = (d/2) log(1 + n/c) + O(1), the usual log n price of mixtures instead of the log log n of stitching, traded for sharpness at small n. The sets are computed in closed or numerically tractable form for Gaussian (mean, variance, or both), Bernoulli, exponential, Gamma and Weibull with fixed shape, Pareto, Chi-square and Poisson; the authors believe the two-parameter Gaussian, Chi-square, Weibull, Pareto and Poisson sequences are the first explicit time-uniform ones. Variants cover a global Legendre regulariser L_0 with dependent, non-identically distributed observations (Theorem 7, Corollary 8, applied to heteroscedastic linear bandits in Appendix F) and a doubly time-uniform bound over all windows (s, t] (Theorem 5), which yields a regularised GLR change-detection test with false-alarm probability at most δ, demonstrated on a Gaussian variance change. The paper speaks of nonnegative martingales and confidence sets throughout; it never uses the words e-value or e-process.

## Main results
- **Exponential family and Bregman divergence (Section 2).** L(θ) = log ∫ h(x) exp(⟨θ, F(x)⟩) dx; Θ_D = {θ ∈ R^d : L(θ) < ∞}; Θ_I = {θ ∈ Θ_D : det ∇²L(θ) > 0}; the parameter set is an open Θ ⊂ Θ_I. E_θ[F(X)] = ∇L(θ) (expectation or dual parameter), and KL(p_θ, p_θ′) = ⟨θ − θ′, E_θ F(X)⟩ − L(θ) + L(θ′) = B_L(θ′, θ): the first argument of B_L is the second argument of KL [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, Sec. 2].
- **Lemma 1 (properties of Bregman divergences; classical, proof in Appendix B).** With B_{L,θ}(λ) := B_L(θ + λ, θ): for θ, θ + λ ∈ Θ_D, log E_θ exp(⟨λ, F(X) − E_θ F(X)⟩) = B_{L,θ}(λ), so the Bregman divergence is the centred cumulant generating function of F(X). If ∇L is one-to-one, then for θ, θ′ ∈ Θ_D, B_L(θ′, θ) = B*_{L,θ′}(∇L(θ) − ∇L(θ′)) = B_{L*}(∇L(θ), ∇L(θ′)), with * the Legendre–Fenchel dual; more generally for α ∈ [0, 1], B*_{L,θ′}(α(∇L(θ) − ∇L(θ′))) = B_L(θ′, θ_α) with θ_α = (∇L)^{−1}(α∇L(θ) + (1 − α)∇L(θ′)). The α-version is what turns a shrunk mean deviation into a divergence to a shrunk estimate [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, Sec. 2, App. B].
- **Definition 2 (Bregman information gain).** For X_1, …, X_n i.i.d. from p_θ with θ ∈ Θ ⊂ Θ_I, a reference θ_0 ∈ Θ and a constant c > 0: θ_{n,c}(θ_0) := (∇L)^{−1}((Σ_{t ≤ n} F(X_t) + c∇L(θ_0))/(n + c)) and γ_{n,c}(θ_0) := log[∫_Θ exp(−c B_L(θ′, θ_0)) dθ′ / ∫_Θ exp(−(n + c) B_L(θ′, θ_{n,c}(θ_0))) dθ′]. Both depend on θ_0 because the Bregman geometry has non-constant curvature; for N(μ, σ²) with σ known γ_{n,c} = ½ log((n + c)/c), free of μ_0 and equal to the classical Gaussian information gain (mutual information) [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, Sec. 3.1].
- **Theorem 3 (main result: Laplace method for generic exponential families).** For δ ∈ (0, 1], with Θ_{n,c}(δ) := {θ_0 ∈ Θ : (n + c) B_L(θ_0, θ_{n,c}(θ_0)) ≤ log(1/δ) + γ_{n,c}(θ_0)}: P_θ(∃n ∈ N : θ ∉ Θ_{n,c}(δ)) ≤ δ "whenever the Bregman information gain is well-defined". Equivalently P_θ(θ ∉ Θ_{τ,c}(δ)) ≤ δ for every random stopping time τ (Howard et al. 2020, Lemma 3). The set is implicit: θ_0 enters both arguments of the divergence and the gain. In KL terms the statistic is (n + c) KL(p_{θ_{n,c}(θ_0)}, p_{θ_0}) [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, Sec. 3.1].
- **Proof of Theorem 3 (Section 3.3, Appendix A.1).** Step 1: with μ_n = n^{−1} Σ F(X_t) and μ = ∇L(θ), M^λ_n = exp(⟨λ, n(μ_n − μ)⟩ − n B_{L,θ}(λ)) is a unit-mean martingale by Lemma 1; mix it as M_n = ∫_{Λ_θ} M^λ_n q(θ + λ | α, β) dλ over Λ_θ = {λ : θ + λ ∈ Θ}, with q(θ′ | α, β) = H(α, β) exp(⟨θ′, α⟩ − βL(θ′)) the conjugate family. Step 2: α = c∇L(θ), β = c gives q ∝ exp(−c B_L(·, θ)) and M_n = G(θ, c) ∫ exp(⟨λ, n(μ_n − μ)⟩ − (n + c) B_{L,θ}(λ)) dλ, where G(θ, c) = (∫_Θ exp(−c B_L(θ′, θ)) dθ′)^{−1}; with x = (n/(n + c))(μ_n − μ), the maximiser λ*_x of ⟨λ, x⟩ − B_{L,θ}(λ) satisfies ∇L(θ + λ*_x) = (n/(n + c))μ_n + (c/(n + c))∇L(θ), i.e. θ + λ*_x = θ_{n,c}(θ). Step 3: completing the "square" around λ*_x gives M_n = exp((n + c) B*_{L,θ}(x)) G(θ, c)/G(θ_{n,c}(θ), n + c), and Lemma 1 with α = n/(n + c) gives B*_{L,θ}(x) = B_L(θ, θ_{n,c}(θ)); log of the G-ratio is −γ_{n,c}(θ). Markov gives the fixed-n bound, Ville's inequality (Doob's maximal inequality for nonnegative supermartingales) the time-uniform one [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, App. A.1].
- **Asymptotics and the constant c (Section 3.1).** θ_{n,c}(θ_0) → θ and B_L(θ′, θ) = ½(θ′ − θ)ᵀ∇²L(θ)(θ′ − θ) + o(‖θ′ − θ‖²), so Laplace's method for integrals gives γ_{n,c}(θ_0) = (d/2) log(1 + n/c) + O(1); the width is worse than the law of the iterated logarithm, "a standard feature of the method of mixtures compared to stitching". c acts as a number of virtual prior samples. A data-size-dependent c_n is not allowed: it breaks the martingale property, and c_n ∝ n would give γ = O(1) and width O(1/√n), contradicting the LIL lower bound Ω(√(log log n / n)). A fixed c tuned for one horizon n_0 is allowed; numerically the width-minimising value follows c* ≈ 0.12 n_0 across Bernoulli, Gaussian, exponential and Chi-square (Figures 2d, 12, 13), and the width is insensitive to c over [0.1, 30] [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, Secs 3.1, 4, App. D.7].
- **Bandit use (Section 3.1).** For K arms from one exponential family, the per-arm sets with n replaced by the pull count N_i(n) cover θ_i for all n with probability 1 − δ, and a union bound over arms adds log K; intended for UCB and for GLR stopping rules in pure exploration [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023].
- **Explicit sets (Section 3.2, Table 1; Appendix C, Table 2).** Notation S_n = Σ X_t, Q_n(μ) = Σ (X_t − μ)², Z_n(μ, σ) = Q_n(μ)/σ², L_n = Σ log X_t, K_n = Σ log(X_t/2), S^{(k)}_n = Σ X_t^k, μ̂_n = S_n/n. Each line holds with probability ≥ 1 − δ for all n simultaneously [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023]:
  - Gaussian, μ unknown, σ known (C.1): (S_n − nμ)²/(2σ²(n + c)) ≤ log(1/δ) + ½ log((n + c)/c).
  - Gaussian, μ known, σ unknown (C.2, eq. 8; θ = −1/(2σ²), F(x) = (x − μ)², L(θ) = −½ log(−2θ)): Q_n/(2σ²) − ((n + c)/2 + 1) log((Q_n/σ² + c)/(n + c)) ≤ log(1/δ) − (n/2) log 2 − (c/2 + 1) log c + ((n + c)/2 + 1) log(n + c) + log Γ(c/2 + 2) − log Γ((n + c)/2 + 2).
  - Gaussian, μ and σ unknown (C.3; d = 2, θ = (μ/σ², −1/(2σ²)) ∈ R × R_{<0}, F(x) = (x, x²), L(θ) = −½ log(−θ_2) − θ_1²/(4θ_2), det ∇²L = −1/(4θ_2³) > 0): ½ Z_n(μ, σ) − ((n + c + 3)/2) log((n/(n + c)) Z_n(μ̂_n, σ) + (c/(n + c)) Z_n(μ, σ) + c) ≤ log(1/δ) − (n/2) log 2 − (c/2 + 2) log c + ½ log(n + c) + log Γ((c + 3)/2) − log Γ((n + c + 3)/2). The prior normaliser is ∫_Θ exp(−c B_L(θ′, θ)) dθ′ = 2√(π/c) (2/c)^{(c+1)/2+1} Γ((c + 1)/2 + 1) (−θ_2)^{3/2}, and γ_{n,c} = (3/2) log(θ_2/θ_{n,c}(θ)_2) + constants in (n, c). The joint sets for (μ, σ) are convex and bounded (Figures 1a, 7), unlike a baseline from Chi-square quantiles with a union bound over a fixed horizon, which is unbounded and projects to trivial marginal sets (D.1).
  - Bernoulli (C.4; θ = log(μ/(1 − μ)), B_L(θ′, θ) = kl(μ, μ′)): S_n log(1/μ) + (n − S_n) log(1/(1 − μ)) + log[Γ(S_n + cμ)Γ(n − S_n + c(1 − μ))/(Γ(cμ)Γ(c(1 − μ)))] ≤ log(1/δ) + log(Γ(n + c)/Γ(c)); the two normalising integrals are Beta functions divided by μ^{cμ}(1 − μ)^{c(1−μ)}-type terms, and the sets stay inside [0, 1] without clipping.
  - Exponential with mean μ (C.5; θ = −1/μ, L(θ) = log(−1/θ)): S_n/μ − (n + c + 1) log(S_n/μ + c) ≤ log(1/δ) + log(Γ(c)/Γ(n + c)) − log(n + c) − c log c.
  - Gamma with fixed shape k, scale λ (C.6, eq. 9): S_n/λ − ((n + c)k + 1) log(S_n/λ + ck) ≤ log(1/δ) + log(Γ(ck)/Γ((n + c)k)) − log((n + c)k) − ck log(ck).
  - Weibull with fixed shape k, scale λ (C.7, eq. 10; F(x) = x^k): S^{(k)}_n/λ^k − (n + c + 1) log(S^{(k)}_n/λ^k + c) ≤ log(1/δ) + log(Γ(c)/Γ(n + c)) − log(n + c) − c log c.
  - Pareto with scale 1, shape α > 0 (C.8, eq. 11; θ = −α − 1, F(x) = log x): αL_n − (n + c + 1) log(αL_n + c) ≤ log(1/δ) + log(Γ(c)/Γ(n + c)) − log(n + c) − c log c; valid also for α < 1, where the mean does not exist.
  - Chi-square with k degrees of freedom (C.9, eq. 12; θ = k/2 − 1, F(x) = log x, L(θ) = (θ + 1) log 2 + log Γ(θ + 1)): n log Γ(k/2) − (k/2)K_n − log J(c, cψ_0(k/2)) + log J(n + c, K_n + cψ_0(k/2)) ≤ log(1/δ), with J(a, b) = ∫ exp(−a log Γ(k′/2) + bk′/2) dk′ and ψ_0 the digamma function. Remark 9: for the Gamma(k/2, ½) family with real k the mixing measure is Lebesgue on (0, ∞); restricted to the Chi-square family, Θ = N and it is the counting measure, turning integrals into sums. Both give the same bound numerically, but integer rounding shrinks the Chi-square sets sharply (D.5).
  - Poisson (C.10; θ = log λ, L(θ) = e^θ): nλ − S_n log λ ≤ log(1/δ) + log I(c, cλ) − log I(n + c, S_n + cλ), with I(a, b) = ∫_R exp(−ae^θ + bθ) dθ, evaluated numerically (Remarks 10–11).
- **Theorem 7 (Laplace method for non-i.i.d. samples; Appendix A.2).** Filtration (H_t); X_t is H_t-measurable, the feature and base functions F_t, h_t are H_{t−1}-measurable, and given H_{t−1}, X_t ∼ p_{θ_0,t} from an exponential family with parameter θ_0 and log-partition L_t. For any Legendre (strictly convex, continuously differentiable) L_0 : Θ → R with exp(−L_0) integrable, put θ_{n,L_0} := (Σ_{t=0}^n ∇L_t)^{−1}(Σ_{t=1}^n F_t(X_t)) and γ_{n,L_0} := log[∫_Θ exp(−L_0(θ′)) dθ′ / ∫_Θ exp(−Σ_{t=0}^n B_{L_t}(θ′, θ_{n,L_0})) dθ′]. Then P(∃n : Σ_{t=0}^n B_{L_t}(θ_0, θ_{n,L_0}) − L_0(θ_0) > log(1/δ) + γ_{n,L_0}) ≤ δ. The mixing prior is q ∝ exp(−L_0) (eq. 2), global instead of centred at the true parameter, so estimate and gain no longer depend on θ_0 [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, App. A.2, eqs 1–7].
- **Corollary 8 (i.i.d. case of Theorem 7).** θ_{n,L_0} = (∇L + n^{−1}∇L_0)^{−1}(μ_n) and P(∃n : n B_L(θ, θ_{n,L_0}) + B_{L_0}(θ, θ_{n,L_0}) ≥ log(1/δ) + L_0(θ) + γ_{n,L_0}) ≤ δ. With L_0 = cL the formulas simplify (γ's numerator becomes ∫ exp(−cL)), and for the known-variance Gaussian the set coincides with Theorem 3's; but integrability of exp(−cL) fails for many families, e.g. the exponential one with L(θ) = −log(−θ), so the authors recommend the local regularisation of Theorem 3 (Remark 4) [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, App. A.2].
- **Appendix F (heteroscedastic linear contextual bandits).** X_t = θᵀφ(A_t) + ε_t with ε_t Gaussian of known arm-dependent variance σ²_{A_t}; F_t(x) = xφ(A_t)/σ²_{A_t}, L_t(θ) = ½‖θ‖² in the φφᵀ/σ² norm, V_n = Σ φ(A_t)φ(A_t)ᵀ/σ²_{A_t}, L_0(θ) = ½‖θ‖²_{V_0}. Theorem 7 gives θ_{n,L_0} = (V_n + V_0)^{−1} Σ X_tφ(A_t)/σ²_{A_t}, γ_{n,L_0} = log(det(V_0 + V_n)^{1/2}/det(V_0)^{1/2}) and the set ‖θ − θ_{n,L_0}‖²_{V_n+V_0} ≤ ‖θ‖²_{V_0} + 2 log(det(V_0 + V_n)^{1/2}/(δ det(V_0)^{1/2})), contained in the set of Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2011 and Kirschner–Krause 2018 because √(a + b) ≤ √a + √b [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, App. F].
- **Numerical comparison (Section 4, Appendix D).** Running intersections ∩_{n′ ≤ n} Θ_{n′,c}(δ) with δ = 0.05, c = 1, n ≤ 200, medians over 1000 replicates. Comparators: sub-Gaussian Chernoff–Laplace mixture (Peña et al. 2008), Bentkus with geometric peeling (Kuchibhotla–Zheng 2021, Thm 4), the hedged-capital betting sequence (Waudby-Smith–Ramdas 2023, Thm 3) for Bernoulli, and Kaufmann–Koolen 2021 (Corollaries 10 and 12) for Gaussian and exponential. Finding (D.6): comparable or sharper, especially at small n (Bernoulli lower bound versus hedged capital, exponential upper bound versus Kaufmann–Koolen); intervals are asymmetric where the distribution is and respect the support. Code: the concentration-lib Python package [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023].
- **GLR for change detection (Section 5, Appendix E).** G_{1:s:t} := inf_θ′ sup_{θ_1,θ_2} log[∏_{t′ ≤ s} p_{θ_1}(X_t′) ∏_{s < t′ ≤ t} p_{θ_2}(X_t′) / ∏_{t′ ≤ t} p_θ′(X_t′)] = inf_θ′ {s B_L(θ′, θ_{1:s}) + (t − s) B_L(θ′, θ_{s+1:t})} (eq. 15), with θ_{1:s}, θ_{s+1:t} the maximum-likelihood estimates on the two segments and the infimum attained at θ_{1:t}. Under no change, P_θ(τ(α) < ∞) is bounded by a union of a forward deviation event (Theorem 3) and a scan event over all windows (Theorem 5) [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023].
- **Theorem 5 (doubly time-uniform concentration).** For δ ∈ (0, 1] and g : N → R_{>0} with Σ_{t ≥ 1} 1/g(t) ≤ 1, e.g. g(t) = κ(1 + t) log²(1 + t) with κ = 2.10975: P_θ(∃t, ∃s < t : (t − s + c) B_L(θ, θ_{s+1:t,c}(θ)) ≥ log(g(t)/δ) + γ_{s+1:t,c}(θ)) ≤ δ, where θ_{s+1:t,c} and γ_{s+1:t,c} are Definition 2 applied to X_{s+1}, …, X_t. Proof: for each start s the window mixture M_{s+1,t} is a nonnegative supermartingale in t; with M̄_t = Σ_{s < t} M_{s+1,t}/g(t), a telescoping argument using g non-decreasing gives E[max_{s < τ} M_{s+1,τ}/g(τ)] ≤ Σ_t 1/g(t) for every stopping time τ (reproduced from Maillard 2019b, Thm 3.2). Generalises Maillard 2019a's sub-Gaussian mean result to generic exponential families (Remark 6). Lemma 12: for f(t) = (1 + t) log^{1+η}(1 + t), Σ_{t ≥ 1} 1/f(t) ≤ Σ_{t ≤ p} 1/f(t) + 1/(η log^η(1 + p)), which is how κ is computed [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, Sec. 5, App. E.1–E.2].
- **Regularised GLR test.** Θ_{s,c}(δ) = {θ : (s + c) B_L(θ, θ_{1:s,c}(θ)) ≤ log(1/δ) + γ_{1:s,c}(θ)}, Θ_{s+1:t,c}(δ) = {θ : (t − s + c) B_L(θ, θ_{s+1:t,c}(θ)) ≤ log(g(t)/δ) + γ_{s+1:t,c}(θ)}, and τ^δ_c := min{t : ∃s < t, Θ_{s,c}(δ/2) ∩ Θ_{s+1:t,c}(δ/2) = ∅}; by Theorems 3 and 5 and a union bound, P_θ(τ^δ_c < ∞) ≤ δ. For a known horizon T, κ may be replaced by the finite sum. Experiment (E.3): centred Gaussians, σ_0 = 1 changing to σ_1 ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4} at t* = 50, T = 100, δ = 5%: observed false-positive rate 0.2%, detection rates 72.4%, 99.9%, 100% for σ_1 = 2, 3, 4; this settles the variance-change question left open in Maillard 2019a. No detection-delay bound is proved [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, Sec. 5, App. E].

## Setting and assumptions
- **Family.** Densities or mass functions p_θ(x) = h(x) exp(⟨θ, F(x)⟩ − L(θ)) on a set X, feature map F : X → R^d, base function h : X → R_+, natural parameter θ; the reference measure is written dx and is left implicit (Lebesgue or counting in the examples) [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, Sec. 2].
- **Parameter set.** Θ ⊂ R^d is "some open set" with Θ ⊂ Θ_I = {θ : L(θ) < ∞, det ∇²L(θ) > 0}. The authors call this "tantamount to assuming that the family is minimal" and use it to have ∇L one-to-one. Θ need not be the full natural parameter space Θ_D, and no convexity of Θ is stated. The Chi-square example takes Θ discrete (integer k) with the counting measure, which is outside the "open set" wording but is treated explicitly in Remark 9.
- **Regularity actually used.** (i) ∇L one-to-one on Θ, so that (∇L)^{−1} and the Bregman duality of Lemma 1 are available; (ii) the estimate θ_{n,c}(θ_0) exists, i.e. the convex combination (n μ_n + c∇L(θ_0))/(n + c) lies in the image of ∇L (the worked examples exhibit the image, e.g. {(x, y) : y > x²} for the two-parameter Gaussian; the general statement does not discuss this); (iii) both integrals in γ_{n,c}(θ_0) are finite, which is the meaning of "whenever the Bregman information gain is well-defined" in Theorem 3. No steepness or Legendre-type condition on L itself is stated; "Legendre" is used only for the regulariser L_0 of Theorem 7, defined as strictly convex and continuously differentiable, with exp(−L_0) integrable over Θ.
- **Mixing (prior) measure.** For the hypothesis θ_0 the mixture is over λ ∈ Λ_{θ_0} = {λ : θ_0 + λ ∈ Θ} with density q(θ_0 + λ | c∇L(θ_0), c) ∝ exp(−c B_L(θ_0 + λ, θ_0)) with respect to Lebesgue measure on Θ (counting measure for a discrete Θ): the conjugate prior of the family with c pseudo-observations at mean ∇L(θ_0). The prior is recentred at each candidate θ_0 ("local regularisation"), so there is one martingale per candidate and the confidence set is the set of candidates whose own martingale stays below 1/δ. c > 0 must be a constant fixed in advance. Theorem 7 uses the single global prior ∝ exp(−L_0) instead.
- **Data model.** Theorems 3 and 5: X_1, X_2, … i.i.d. from p_θ, well-specified. Theorem 7: adapted, conditionally exponential-family observations with predictable F_t, h_t and a common parameter. The bandit paragraph applies the Theorem 3 construction to adaptively sampled arms.
- **Optional stopping.** Allowed: all bounds are time-uniform over n ∈ N, equivalently valid at arbitrary stopping times; Theorem 5 is uniform over both window endpoints at the cost of log g(t).
- **Not covered.** Misspecified or unknown log-partition function (named as future work in Section 6); composite nulls or nuisance parameters (the sets are joint in the full d-dimensional θ); rates better than (d/2) log n.

## Relation to other sources
- [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021](kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021.md): the closest prior work according to this paper. It is described here as extending the method of mixtures to exponential families by discrete mixtures and stitching, "which only works for single parameter families", with case-specific calculations limited to Gaussian with known variance and Gamma with known shape and with constants "seemingly unrelated to the exponential family model"; this paper's conjugate prior is induced by the family and covers d > 1. Appendix D.3–D.4 restates Kaufmann–Koolen's Corollary 10 (Gaussian) and Corollary 12 (exponential) for a single arm, B_L-ball radius (2/n) log(4 + log n) + (1/n) C_g(log(1/δ)), as numerical comparators; the Bregman sets are "comparable, if slightly tighter" for n ≤ 200 [chowdhuryBregmanDeviationsGeneric2023, Sec. 3.1, 4, App. D].
- [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a](grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a.md): not cited (it is later), but the same object from the fixed-n side. Both bound the null probability that the sample-mean statistic of a d-dimensional exponential family lands outside a KL neighbourhood of the null, with KL taken from the estimate to the null. There: fixed n, the exact KL ball, a Shtarkov/NML mixture over the ball's boundary, price ((d − 1)/2) log n + O(1), not anytime-valid. Here: all n simultaneously, a conjugate prior over the whole parameter set, a ball around a shrunk estimate θ_{n,c}(θ_0) with factor n + c, price (d/2) log(1 + n/c) + O(1). The CSC paper's Section 6 asks how its bounds relate to anytime-valid ones of Kaufmann–Koolen type; Theorem 3 here is a multivariate member of that anytime-valid family.
- [howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021](howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021.md): cited, with Howard et al. 2020, for the history of time-uniform bounds and for stitching; the 2020 paper supplies the equivalence between time-uniform and stopping-time coverage (its Lemma 3) and the reference point for the c ≈ 0.12 n tuning. The wiki page's Corollary 6 gives one-parameter exponential-family confidence sequences from sub-ψ boundaries, with conjugate mixtures for specific families; Theorem 3 here is the d-parameter conjugate mixture written in Bregman form, and it keeps the √(log n) mixture rate that Howard et al. contrast with stitching's √(log log n).
- [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025](kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025.md): cites this paper (as Chowdhury et al. 2023) among generalisations of the method of mixtures. Its prior likelihood mixing (Theorem 3 there) with a fixed prior is the general form of this paper's Theorem 7 and Corollary 8; its linear-regression set ½‖θ − θ̂‖²_{V_t} ≤ log(1/δ) + γ_t + ½‖θ − θ_0‖²_{V_0} has the same shape as Appendix F here, and its Laplace expansion of the evidence matches γ ≈ (d/2) log n. This paper's Theorem 3 differs by recentring the prior at each candidate parameter.
- [waudbysmithEstimatingMeansBounded2020](waudbysmithEstimatingMeansBounded2020.md): cited (as Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2023) for the "capital process" construction for bounded variables; its hedged-capital sequence is the Bernoulli comparator in Appendix D.2.

- [shekharSequentialChangeDetection2023](shekharSequentialChangeDetection2023.md): according to that wiki page (Sec. 5.6), cites this paper for exponential-family confidence sequences to plug into its forward/backward detector. The regularised GLR test here has the same "stop when two confidence sets are disjoint" form, but pairs a forward set with all window sets Θ_{s+1:t,c} under a log g(t) union penalty and controls the false-alarm probability, where the backward-CS detector controls the average run length and proves delay bounds. Both papers cite Maillard 2019a,b as the sub-Gaussian precursor.

They give change detectors with average-run-length (and, in the non-partitioned paper, false-alarm-probability) control and delay bounds of order log(1/α)/KL; Theorem 5 is a false-alarm-probability route for parametric exponential families with unknown pre- and post-change parameters and no delay analysis.

## Open questions / gaps
- Misspecified or unknown log-partition function, to be learned alongside the parameter (the authors' stated future work, Section 6).
- A joint concentration bound on the pair (θ_{s,c}, θ_{s+1:t,c}) to replace the loose union over windows in the GLR test (observed false-alarm rate 0.2% at nominal 5%); "nontrivial", left for future work (Appendix E.3). No detection-delay guarantee is given.
- When is the Bregman information gain well-defined? The paper gives no general condition for finiteness of ∫_Θ exp(−c B_L(θ′, θ_0)) dθ′ or for θ_{n,c}(θ_0) to exist in Θ; these are checked family by family.
- The O(1) term in γ_{n,c} = (d/2) log(1 + n/c) + O(1) is not made explicit, and the Laplace argument is given as an approximation ("≈"), not as a theorem; the comparison with the fixed-n ((d − 1)/2) log n of [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a](grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a.md) is therefore only at the level of leading terms.
- Geometry of the sets for d > 1 beyond the Gaussian example: convexity and boundedness are observed in figures for the two-parameter Gaussian, not proved in general; the set is a level set in θ_0 of an implicit function, not a Bregman ball around a fixed centre.
- The c* ≈ 0.12 n_0 tuning is empirical.
- Internal inconsistencies in the text, none affecting the statements used above: the proof of Theorem 5 writes (t − s − c) where the statement and the test definition have (t − s + c); κ appears as 2.10975 (statement) and 2.10974 (Appendix E); the roles of the thresholds α_1, α_2 are swapped between Section 5 and Appendix E; in Appendix E the sets Θ_{s,c}, Θ_{s+1:t,c} are first written with "≥ α" before the final definition with "≤".
