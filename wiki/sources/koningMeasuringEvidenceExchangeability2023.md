# Measuring evidence against exchangeability and group invariance with e-values

arXiv 2310.01153v5 (February 2026), 45 pages including a 16-page supplement (Sections 8–13). Earlier versions carried the title "Post-hoc and anytime valid permutation and group invariance testing"; other wiki sources cite it as "Koning 2023" (e.g. the numeraire paper for the symmetry numeraire).

## Summary
The paper replaces the binary permutation test by e-values for the null that a random variable Y is invariant under a compact group G acting on the sample space, i.e. GY has the law of Y for every G in G, which covers exchangeability (permutations), sign-symmetry (sign flips), sphericity (orthonormal matrices) and conformal prediction [koningMeasuringEvidenceExchangeability2023, Secs 1, 3]. The whole theory rests on one decomposition: an invariant law is a mixture of uniform (Haar-image) laws on the orbits, so an e-value is valid iff E_G[ε(Gy)] ≤ 1 for every y, i.e. valid under Unif(O) on every orbit, and only the realised orbit O_Y matters (Theorem 1, Corollary 1). Exact e-values are exactly the orbit-normalised statistics ε_T(y) = T(y)/E_G[T(Gy)] for a nonnegative statistic T (Theorem 3), computable by a Monte Carlo average over M random group elements plus the identity while staying exact in expectation (Theorem 4). Optimality is local-to-global: an e-value that is valid and K_O-optimal on every orbit is globally optimal for any monotone aggregation of orbit objectives (Theorem 5), which yields expected-utility-optimal e-values of the form (U′)^{-1}(λ_O · dQ̄/dQ_a) where Q̄ = E_G[GQ] is the G-average of the alternative (Theorem 6); the Neyman–Pearson utility recovers the Lehmann–Stein most powerful invariance test (Corollary 2), log utility gives ε^log = dQ/dQ̄ so that Q̄ is the reverse information projection of Q onto the invariant laws (Corollary 3), and power utilities give generalised-mean-optimal e-values (Corollary 4). Sequentially, an e-process is anytime valid iff it is dominated by a nonnegative Unif(O)-martingale on every orbit (Theorems 7–8), which is constructed by inducing Doob martingales from an optimal e-value orbit by orbit and taking the infimum, the obstacle being that the orbit is typically not measurable along the filtration (Sec. 5, Example 8). Test martingales in the natural filtration multiply conditional e-values whose validity reduces to invariance under the stabiliser subgroup of the past data (Proposition 3, Lemma 4), which degenerates for exchangeability but not for within-batch exchangeability (Examples 12–13). The supplement works out the Gaussian location-shift alternative under orthogonal subgroups (t-test, softmax for permutations, 2e^{μy}/(e^{μy}+e^{−μy}) for symmetry), representative-inversion kernels generalising ranks and signs, and impoverished filtrations, and applies the method to the hot hand data of Gilovich, Vallone and Tversky 1985.

## Main results
- **Sec. 1.2**: the reciprocal p = 1/ε of an e-value is a post-hoc p-value with E[sup_α I{p ≤ α}/α] = E[1/p] ≤ 1, so "reject at level p" is valid for data-dependent α [koningMeasuringEvidenceExchangeability2023, Sec. 1.2].
- **Lemma 1** (Eaton 1989): five equivalent definitions of G-invariance of Y, including Y =_d G[Y] with G ~ Unif(G) independent of the orbit representative [Y], and Unif(O_Y) being a version of the conditional law given the orbit [Sec. 2.4].
- **Def. 2**: Y "looks G-invariant through S" if S(GY) | O_Y =_d S(Y) | O_Y; dropping the conditioning is insufficient for validity (Remark 1, counterexample in Sec. 10.2 with Y = (Y_1, Y_2, …, Y_2), rejection probability 1/2 at any α) [Sec. 2.6].
- **Sec. 2.7**: representative inversion kernel γ : Y → G with γ(y)[y] = y (unique for free actions, uniform over the coset G_y S_[y] otherwise, Kallenberg 2017 Thm 7.14); ranks, signs and y/‖y‖_2 are inversion kernels for permutations, sign flips and rotations [Remark 2, Sec. 11].
- **Theorem 1 / Corollary 1**: ε is valid (exact) for G-invariance iff E^{Unif(O)}[ε] ≤ 1 (= 1) for every orbit O, equivalently iff it holds for Unif(O_Y) almost surely [Sec. 3.2]. Remark 4: for non-compact groups ergodic laws replace Unif(O) and Corollary 1 fails because the ergodic component is not identified by the orbit.
- **Theorem 2**: the classical test ε_α = (1/α) I{T(y) > q^G_α[T(Gy)]} + (c([y])/α) I{T = q_α} is exact conditional on the orbit whenever Y looks G-invariant through T, a weaker condition than invariance; the statistic may depend on the orbit [Sec. 3.3]. Example 3: the t-test as the spherical invariance test with T = ι′y/‖y‖_2; Example 4: conformal prediction as the permutation test on (Y^n, y*).
- **Theorem 3**: ε is exact for G-invariance iff ε = ε_T = T/E_G[T(G·)] for some nonnegative T with 0 < E_G T(Gy) < ∞; any non-exact e-value can be plugged in as T to make it exact [Sec. 3.4]. Proposition 1: ε_T is exact under the weaker condition E[T(Y) | O_Y] = E[T(GY) | O_Y]. Example 6: exact conformal e-values.
- **Theorem 4**: the Monte Carlo e-value ε^M_T = T(y)/((M+1)^{-1} Σ_{i=0}^M T(G^{(i)}y)) with G^{(0)} = I and M i.i.d. Haar draws is exact in expectation over the draws for every M ≥ 1, by exchangeability of (T(G^{(0)}Y), …, T(G^{(M)}Y)) under the null [Sec. 3.5]. Remark 6: a compact subgroup may replace G.
- **Lemma 2** (from Koning 2024c, "Continuous testing"): for a simple null P, a non-decreasing utility U and λ ≥ 0, ε_λ ∈ argmax_x (dQ_a/dP) U(x) − λx with ε_λ = ∞ on the singular part and E_P[ε_λ] = 1 is U-optimal; for differentiable U, ε_λ = (U′)^{-1}(λ dP/dQ_a); the Neyman–Pearson utility x ∧ 1/α gives the NP lemma (eq. 4.1) [Sec. 4.1].
- **Theorem 5** (local ⇒ global optimality): if ε*|_O is valid for Unif(O) and K_O-optimal among Unif(O)-valid e-values for every orbit, then ε* is valid for G-invariance and K-optimal for K = Ψ(K_O(·|_O))_O with Ψ non-decreasing in each argument; hence optimal uniformly over aggregations Ψ (Remark 7) and over any partition-with-known-conditionals null, not only groups (Remark 8) [Sec. 4.2].
- **Lemma 3**: y ↦ dQ_a(· | O_y)/dUnif(O_y)(y) is a version of dQ_a/dQ̄, where Q̄ = E_G[GQ] and Q_a is the part of Q absolutely continuous w.r.t. Q̄ [Sec. 4.3].
- **Theorem 6**: ε^U exact on every orbit with ε^U(y) ∈ argmax_x (dQ_a/dQ̄)(y) U(x) − λ_O x and ε^U = ∞ on Q_s is exact for G-invariance and U-optimal against Q [Sec. 4.3].
- **Corollary 2** (Neyman–Pearson): the orbit-wise threshold test on dQ_a/dQ̄ is NP-optimal, recovering Lehmann and Stein 1949 without their implicit invariant reference measure [Sec. 4.3].
- **Corollary 3** (log-optimal): ε^log(y) = (dQ_a/dQ̄)(y) / E_G[(dQ_a/dQ̄)(Gy)], equal to dQ/dQ̄ when Q ≪ Q̄ (always for finite groups); Q̄ is thereby the RIPr of Q onto the G-invariant laws [Sec. 4.3].
- **Corollary 4** (power utility U_h(x) = (x^h − 1)/h, h < 1): ε^{(h)} ∝ (dQ_a/dQ̄)^{1/(1−h)} normalised by its orbit average; h → 1 is all-or-nothing, h → −∞ the constant e-value [Sec. 4.3]. Remark 9: T ∝ dQ_a/dQ̄ or T ∝ (dQ_a/dQ̄)^{1/(1−h)} in ε_T.
- **Proposition 2**: Q̄ may be replaced by any σ-finite G-invariant reference measure H ≫ Q, Q̄ (e.g. Lebesgue), since dQ̄/dH is constant on orbits [Sec. 4.3].
- **Corollary 5**: specifying an alternative Q^O on each orbit (replacing dQ_a/dQ̄ by dQ^O_a/dUnif(O)) gives an e-value that is exact and U-optimal against Q^μ = ∫ Q^O dμ(O) for every marginal μ over orbits; in practice only the realised orbit's alternative is needed, chosen from O_Y but not from Y [Sec. 4.4]. Example 7: hot hand, where orbits are (#hit, #miss).
- **Sec. 4.5**: alternatives on the group itself, ε*_G(γ(Y)), valid iff E_G[ε(G)] ≤ 1; rank and sign tests are the case G = permutations or sign flips with the rank/sign inversion kernel.
- **Theorem 7 / Corollary 6**: (ε_n) is anytime valid for G-invariance iff ε_τ is valid for Unif(O) for every orbit and stopping time; the conditional version on O_Y is correct but unusable because O_Y is typically not I_n-measurable [Sec. 5.1].
- **Theorem 8**: (ε_n) is anytime valid iff for every orbit O it is Unif(O)-a.s. dominated by a nonnegative Unif(O)-martingale starting at 1 (via Lemma 6 of Ramdas et al. 2022a); set ε^O_n = ∞ off O to "drop" infeasible orbits (Remark 10) [Sec. 5.2].
- **Sec. 5.3**: Doob-martingale construction: start from an orbit-wise optimal e-value ε (Corollary 5), induce ε^O_n = E^{Unif(O)}[ε|_O | I_n] per orbit, take the infimum; if O_Y is I_τ-measurable then ε^{O_Y}_τ(Y) = ε(Y), so the e-process builds towards the terminal e-value. Example 8: two-letter example with ε_1(A) = ε_1(B) = 2/3, ε_1(C) = 4/3, explicitly not a supermartingale for the invariant null.
- **Remark 11**: non-compact groups via ergodic decomposition; de Finetti makes the ergodic laws i.i.d., the case of Ramdas et al. 2022b, and tracking one martingale per i.i.d. law is impractical beyond binary data [Sec. 5.3].
- **Sec. 6**: test martingales for invariance of (X^n) under a nested sequence of compact subgroups (G_n) of a latent group acting on nested subspaces X^n with compatible projections; three readings: easier than e-processes, anytime valid for the fork-convex hull, adaptively anytime valid (E_P[ε_τ | I_σ] ≤ ε_σ for σ < τ), hence less powerful when that extra guarantee is not needed. Filtration I_n = σ(X^n, O_{X^{n+1}}) [Secs 6.1–6.2].
- **Proposition 3 / Lemma 4**: a conditional e-value is valid given O_{X^n} and X^{n−1} iff E_{K_n}[ε_n(K_n x^n)] ≤ 1 for every x^n, where K_n(x^{n−1}) = {G ∈ G_n : Gx^{n−1} = x^{n−1}} is the compact stabiliser subgroup (Lemma 5, Proposition 5), because X^n | (O, X^{n−1}) =_d K_n x^n; so Sections 3–4 apply with the data-dependent group K_n, e.g. ε_n = T_n(X^n)/E_{K_n} T_n(K_n X^n) or the utility-optimal e-value from dQ_n/dQ̄_n with Q̄_n = E_{K_n}[K_n Q_n] [Sec. 6.3]. Remark 12: the test martingale tests the larger (K_n)-invariance null, which is where it loses power.
- **Examples 11–13**: sequential sphericity reduces to sign flips of the latest coordinate; for exchangeability with distinct values K_n = {I}, so no powerful test martingale exists in the data filtration (recovering Vovk 2021 and Ramdas et al. 2022b); within-batch exchangeability has K_n = {I_0} × … × {I_{n−1}} × P_n, uniform on the latest batch, with adaptive batch sizes allowed [Sec. 6.3].
- **Sec. 7.1**: case-control simulation with Poisson(25) batches, treated N(a,1) vs control N(b,1), test martingale from the log-optimal permutation e-value against a plug-in Gaussian alternative exp{(â−b̂)/σ̂² · 1′X_t}/E_G exp{…GX_t} with 100 Monte Carlo permutations; 95% of 1000 runs exceed 20 by batch 23 when a − b = 0.2, and 95% stay below 20 under the null (Fig. 1).
- **Sec. 7.2**: sign-symmetry against N(1,1): the log-optimal e-process grows far faster than the de la Peña 1999 exponential e-process (Fig. 2), matching its inadmissibility shown by Ramdas et al. 2022a.
- **Sec. 7.3, Tables 1–2**: hot hand, 26 shooters of Gilovich et al. 1985, orbit-conditional alternative p_hot = (p_neutral)^β after k consecutive hits; worked example 111010 with β = 0.9, k = 2 gives e ≈ 0.0673/(1/15) = 1.0095; product e-values over shooters 0.007/0.180 (1-hit trigger, β = 0.85/0.90), 3.108/4.460 (2 hits), 7.489/5.525 (3 hits), i.e. post-hoc p-values 0.322–0.134 for 2- and 3-hit triggers.
- **Sec. 8.2, eq. 8.2, Remarks 13–14**: for Y ~ N_d(μι, I) against invariance under a compact G ⊆ O(d), the NP-optimal e-value is (1/α) I{ι′y > q^G_α[ι′Gy]}, free of μ; for G = O(d) it is the t-test (Koning and Hemerik 2023 Thm 6), NP-optimal against any density increasing in the correlation ι′y/‖y‖_2, extending Lehmann and Stein; for sign flips it is Efron 1969.
- **Sec. 8.3, eq. 8.3**: the log-optimal e-value is exp{μy′ι}/E_G exp{μy′Gι}; it is not uniformly log-optimal in μ, so there is no unique "e-value version" of the t-test (Example 5), though Sec. 8.4 shows it is log-optimal against any mixture of the orbit-conditional (von Mises–Fisher-type) alternatives.
- **Sec. 8.5**: alternative on SO(2) via the projected normal pushforward, density (8.4), giving ε^log(γ(y)) increasing in ι′y/‖y‖_2.
- **Sec. 8.6, eq. 8.5**: for permutations and ι = e_1, ε^log = exp{μy_1}/(d^{-1} Σ_i exp{μy_i}), the softmax with inverse temperature μ, a likelihood ratio for exchangeability against N((μ,0,…,0), I). Remark 15: the soft-rank e-value of Wang–Ramdas 2022 / Ignatiadis et al. 2023 (T = (e^{κy_1} − e^{κ min_j y_j})/κ) exceeds the softmax e-value iff the softmax e-value exceeds 1, so it is a more volatile version.
- **Sec. 8.7, eq. 8.7, Remarks 16–17**: for G = {−1,1}^d and ι = d^{-1/2}1, ε^log = ∏ exp{d^{-1/2}μy_i}/E exp{d^{-1/2}μ g_i y_i}; for d = 1 this is 2e^{μy}/(e^{μy} + e^{−μy}); de la Peña's exp{Z − Z²/2} normalised by Theorem 3 becomes exactly 2e^Z/(e^Z + e^{−Z}), and the Vovk–Wang 2024 symmetry e-value is shown to be the optimal one against a Gaussian shift.
- **Proposition 4** (Sec. 9): for a subgroup F ⊆ G and an F-equivariant statistic H, an e-value valid for F-invariance of H(X) is valid for G-invariance of X; sequential version with compatible (H_n), (F_n); Example 14 reduces exchangeability to within-batch exchangeability by censoring into batches.
- **Sec. 9.1**: reduction to a single orbit via the inversion kernel γ : X → G, under which admissible e-processes are martingales (Ramdas et al. 2022a) and may be induced backwards as Doob martingales E_G[ε(G) | I_n] (Koning and van Meer 2025) or built forwards from conditional e-values of γ_n(X^n) given γ_{n−1}(X^{n−1}); Example 15: ranks are the inversion kernel for exchangeability (4213 × 1347 = 7314), the last rank is Unif{1,…,n} independent of past ranks and determines Rank_n given Rank_{n−1}, which underlies conformal last-rank constructions; Example 16: sequential sphericity through X^n/‖X^n‖_2 on a semicircle, with the t-test recovered via ι′_n H_n.

## Setting and assumptions
- Spaces are second-countable locally compact Hausdorff with Borel σ-algebras; G is a compact group acting continuously on the sample space Y, with Haar probability Unif(G); orbit selectors are measurable (possible for compact G) [koningMeasuringEvidenceExchangeability2023, Secs 1.8, 2.1–2.2].
- Null: H = {P : GP = P for all G ∈ G}, the invariant laws themselves, not an invariant family of laws; this is disjoint from the free-action setting of Pérez-Ortiz et al. 2024 except for the trivial group (Sec. 1.7). Exactness is "uniform": E_P[ε] = 1 for every P ∈ H (Remark 3).
- Alternatives: a single Q on Y (Sec. 4.3), one Q^O per orbit (Sec. 4.4), or a law on the group pulled back through an inversion kernel (Sec. 4.5); Lebesgue decomposition Q = Q_a + Q_s with respect to Q̄, with ε = ∞ on the singular part.
- Validity: E_P[ε] ≤ 1 for all P ∈ H, i.e. post-hoc validity of p = 1/ε at data-dependent levels; e-processes are anytime valid for stopping times of an arbitrary filtration (I_n) in Section 5; test martingales in Section 6 require the predictable group sequence and the coarsened filtration σ(X^n, O_{X^{n+1}}), with the stronger adaptive anytime validity for all σ < τ.
- Optional stopping is fully allowed for e-processes (Theorems 7–8) and for test martingales within their filtration; impoverished filtrations (Sec. 9) trade stopping times for power.
- Non-compact groups are treated only in Remarks 4 and 11 via ergodic decompositions.

## Open questions / gaps
- Sequential Monte Carlo e-values with a stopping-time number of draws M are expected to need substantial modification of Fischer and Ramdas 2025, and continuous evidence needs many more draws than a binary decision (Remark 5).
- Constructing e-processes for non-compact groups requires tracking one martingale per ergodic law, impractical beyond binary exchangeability (Remark 11).
- The Doob-martingale e-process of Sec. 5.3 requires computing conditional expectations of the terminal e-value on each feasible orbit; no general algorithm is given beyond Example 8.
- Theorem 8 assumes a greatest measurable lower bound of the orbit-wise martingale family exists; existence is not guaranteed without a dominating measure (Sec. 5.2).
- Optimality of e-processes (as opposed to e-values) for invariance nulls is not characterised; the test martingale is shown to lose power only qualitatively (Remark 12).
- Open-access status: preprint only; the version of record was not located.
