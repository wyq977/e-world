# Testing hypotheses generated by constraints

## Summary
The paper characterises the full set E of [e-variables](../concepts/e-variable.md) for any null P that is "generated by constraints": P consists of all probability measures µ on an arbitrary measurable space X that integrate a given family Φ of measurable functions and satisfy ∫ f dµ ≤ 0 for every f ∈ Φ (Definition 2.1). Φ may be uncountable, discontinuous, and X need not carry any topology or reference measure; equality constraints are encoded by including f and −f. The abstract main result, Theorem 2.2, says that a function has nonpositive expectation under all of P iff it lies in the weak closure of C = cone(Φ) − L^Φ_p, the cone of functions dominated quasi-surely by a conic combination of constraint functions; hence E is exactly {1 + f : f in the weak closure of C}, proved by the bipolar theorem for the dual pair (L^Φ, M^Φ) [larssonTestingHypothesesGenerated2025, Thm 2.2]. Sections 3–5 make the closure explicit: for finitely many constraints the cone is already weakly closed, so every e-variable is dominated by 1 + Σ π_i g_i with π ∈ R^d_+ (Theorem 3.1); for one-sided sub-ψ laws every e-variable is dominated by a mixture ∫ e^{λx − ψ(λ)} π(dλ) with π a probability measure on [0, λ_max] (Theorem 4.3, via the Krein–Šmulian theorem); for laws invariant under a compact group every e-variable is dominated by 1 + f − f_π with f_π the Haar orbit average and f_π ≤ 0, and these are exact (Theorem 5.5). Section 6 shows minimal complete classes of admissible e-variables need not exist in general (Example 6.4) but do for these three families (Theorem 6.5); Section 7 proves existence of optimal e-variables for any objective inf_Q ∫ U(h) dQ − χ(Q) with U nondecreasing, upper semicontinuous and linearly bounded, which covers [GRO, GROW and REGROW](../concepts/gro-criterion.md), power utilities and variational preferences (Theorems 7.3, 7.4), with a uniqueness criterion (Theorem 7.6). Sections 8–10 treat unions of constraint-generated nulls (CVaR), the relaxed integrability condition, and the open sequential problem. Throughout, the paper greatly generalises Clerico 2024, which needed X ⊂ R^n closed, continuous and finitely many constraints, finite e-variables and 0 in the relative interior of the convex hull of the constraints [larssonTestingHypothesesGenerated2025, Sec. 1].

## Main results
- Definition 2.1 / eq. (2.1): P = {µ ∈ M_1 : ∫|f| dµ < ∞ and ∫ f dµ ≤ 0 for all f ∈ Φ}. Spaces L^Φ (functions integrable under every µ ∈ P; contains all bounded functions, all constraint functions and all finite e-variables) and M^Φ (signed measures integrating L^Φ; contains P and all Dirac masses) form a dual pair with ⟨f, µ⟩ = ∫ f dµ. L^Φ_p is the P-quasi-surely nonnegative cone, C = cone(Φ) − L^Φ_p (eq. 2.2).
- Theorem 2.2: f ∈ L^Φ has ∫ f dµ ≤ 0 for all µ ∈ P iff f ∈ closure(C) in σ(L^Φ, M^Φ); E = {[0, ∞]-valued h : h = 1 + f P-q.s., f ∈ closure(C)}. Proof: polar C° = R_+ P, then the bipolar theorem (Thm A.2). Remark 2.3: holds even if P = ∅ (then closure(C) = L). Remark 2.4: a reference measure µ̄ is imposed by adding the indicators 1_A of µ̄-null sets to Φ. Lemma 2.5: L^Φ_p is weakly closed.
- Theorem 3.1 (finite Φ = {g_1, …, g_d}, arbitrary measurable g_i): C is already weakly closed, so f has nonpositive expectation on P iff f ≤ Σ π_i g_i P-q.s. for some π ∈ R^d_+, and E = {h : h ≤ 1 + Σ π_i g_i P-q.s.}. Proof uses nets, a "non-redundant support" set K (eq. 3.1) and Lemma 3.2 (every conic combination has a P-version with π ∈ K) to show ‖π_α‖ cannot diverge.
- Corollary 3.3: every admissible e-variable is P-q.s. equal to 1 + Σ π_i g_i with π ∈ Π^Φ = {π ∈ R^d_+ : 1 + Σ π_i g_i ≥ 0 P-q.s.}; conversely every such function is admissible under the constraint qualification (3.3): g ≤ g′ P-q.s. for g, g′ ∈ cone(Φ) forces g = g′. Remark 3.4: π may be restricted to K_0 = K ∩ Π^Φ, which is compact (Lemma 7.2). Corollary 3.5: every admissible level-α test is φ = 1 ∧ (α + Σ π_i g_i), π ∈ Π^Φ.
- Example 3.6 (mean zero, sd ≤ σ, Φ = {x, −x, x² − σ²}): admissible e-variables 1 + αx + β(x²/σ² − 1) with σ²α² + (2β − 1)² ≤ 1, an ellipse; (3.3) holds. Example 3.7 (α-quantile ≤ q, constraint α − 1_{(−∞,q]}): admissible e-variables 1 + π(α − 1_{(−∞,q]}(x)), π ∈ [0, (1 − α)^{−1}]. Example 3.8 (X = [0, 1], mean ≤ m): 1 + π(x − m), π ∈ [0, 1/m]; mean = m gives π ∈ [−1/(1 − m), 1/m], the betting e-variables of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2024, Orabona and Jun 2023, Clerico 2025 and [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025](larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025.md). Example 3.9: mean zero on R (or nonpositive mean) admits only trivial e-variables h ≤ 1.
- Theorem 3.10: Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.3 persist for P_µ̄ = {µ ∈ P : µ ≪ µ̄} with the augmented constraint set Φ_µ̄ = Φ ∪ {1_A : µ̄(A) = 0}, with the same proof.
- Section 4 (one-sided sub-ψ): ψ closed convex, nonnegative, ψ(0) = 0, dom(ψ) = [0, λ_max) or [0, λ_max]; µ is sub-ψ if ∫ e^{λx} µ(dx) ≤ e^{ψ(λ)} for λ ∈ dom(ψ) (Definition 4.1). Lemma 4.2: sub-ψ laws live on dom(ψ*). Constraint functions g_λ = e^{λx − ψ(λ)} − 1, λ ∈ Λ = [0, λ_max], with g_{λ_max} as a limit if needed (redundant for P, needed for the representation).
- Theorem 4.3: (i) f ∈ L^Φ has nonpositive expectation on P iff f(x) ≤ ∫_Λ g_λ(x) π(dλ) for some finite measure π ≥ 0 on Λ; (ii) E = {h : h(x) ≤ ∫_Λ e^{λx − ψ(λ)} π(dλ) on dom(ψ*), π ∈ M_1(Λ)}. Corollary 4.4: admissible level-α tests are 1 ∧ ∫ αe^{λx − ψ(λ)} π(dλ). Proof: Lemma 4.5 (two-point sub-ψ laws charging any x_0), Lemma 4.6 (no nonempty P-negligible set), Lemma 4.7 (L^Φ ⊂ functions with sup |f| e^{−ψ*} < ∞); embed L^Φ in the dual of the weighted Banach space E = {µ : ∫ e^{ψ*} d|µ| < ∞} and apply Krein–Šmulian (Thm A.3) to G − E′_+, using compactness of G_r = T({π : π(Λ) ≤ r}).
- Section 5 (compact group Σ acting measurably, left Haar probability π): orbit average f_π(x) = ∫ f(σx) π(dσ), symmetrisation µ_π; Lemma 5.2 (µ_π is Σ-invariant; adjoint identity ∫ f_π dµ = ∫ f dµ_π); Lemma 5.4 (µ ∈ P iff ∫ f dµ = ∫ f_π dµ for all f bounded below). Remark 5.3: σ*f_π = f_π only for right Haar measure (unimodular groups).
- Theorem 5.5: E = {h : h ≤ 1 + f − f_π pointwise, f [−1, ∞]-valued, f_π ≤ 0}, and every 1 + f − f_π is an exact e-variable (equality under all µ ∈ P); proved directly, without Theorem 2.2, via the symmetrised Dirac mass (δ_{x_0})_π ∈ P.
- Theorem 5.6: for a separating set F of bounded functions and a generating set Σ_0 of Σ, P is generated by Φ = {σ*f − f : σ ∈ Σ_0, f ∈ F ∪ (−F)}; Hahn–Banach plus a telescoping sum.
- Example 5.7 (exchangeability, Σ(n)): e-variables 1 + f(x) − (1/n!) Σ_σ f(σx), all finite; equivalently g(x) / ((1/n!) Σ_σ g(σx)) for any g ≥ 0 (0/0 = 1), which is a conditional likelihood ratio given the order statistics against the uniform law on the orbit and log-optimal for its implied alternative; Σ(n) is generated by one transposition and one cyclic shift.
- Definition 6.1 / Lemma 6.2: complete class (every e-variable dominated by a member) and minimal complete class; a minimal complete class exists iff every e-variable is dominated by an admissible one, and then it is the set of admissible e-variables, unique up to versions. Theorem 6.3: any P with a reference measure admits a minimal complete class (transfinite induction over countable ordinals, following [ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022](ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022.md)). Example 6.4: X = [0, 1], P = {Uniform} ∪ {(δ_0 + δ_x)/2}: the only admissible e-variable is 1, yet h = 2·1_{x} is not dominated by it, so no minimal complete class exists.
- Theorem 6.5: (i) finite Φ: {1 + Σ π_i g_i ≥ 0} is a complete class, minimal under (3.3); (ii) sub-ψ: E′ = {∫ h_λ π(dλ), π ∈ M_1(Λ)} is complete, and minimal if ψ is the CGF on R_+ of a zero-mean law µ̄ whose support has a cluster point in the interior of dom(ψ*) (case dom(ψ*) = (−∞, x̄]) or after restricting to Λ_0 = [0, λ_max) (case dom(ψ*) open on the right); proof via ∫ h_λ dµ̄ = 1 and the identity theorem for real-analytic functions; (iii) group invariance: {1 + f − f_π, f_π ≤ 0} is a minimal complete class (exact e-variables are admissible).
- Section 7 objective: U(h) = inf_{Q ∈ Q} ∫ U(h) dQ − χ(Q), Q ≪ P (Q-negligible ⊃ P-negligible), U nondecreasing, upper semicontinuous, U(x) ≤ a + bx, χ: Q → R arbitrary. Example 7.1: (i) log utility, point Q, χ = 0: e-power, GRO e-variable, [numeraire](../concepts/numeraire.md) of [grunwaldSafeTesting2024](grunwaldSafeTesting2024.md) and [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025](larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025.md); (ii) any proper concave U, e.g. power utilities; (iii) composite Q with reference measure: GROW; (iv) bounded χ(Q) = sup_h ∫ log h dQ: REGROW (worst-case regret), boundedness not needed; (v) Maccheroni–Marinacci–Rustichini variational preferences. Since U is monotone, it suffices to optimise over a complete class, which is compactly parameterised.
- Lemma 7.2: K_0 = K ∩ Π^Φ is compact. Theorem 7.3 (finite Φ, ∫ Σ|g_i| dQ < ∞ for all Q ∈ Q): an optimiser h* = 1 + Σ π*_i g_i with π* ∈ K_0 exists (eq. 7.1; u is usc by Fatou). Theorem 7.4 (sub-ψ, ∫ e^{ψ*} dQ < ∞ for all Q ∈ Q): an optimiser ∫ e^{λx − ψ(λ)} π*(dλ), π* ∈ M_1(Λ), exists (Prokhorov on the compact Λ).
- Example 7.5: ψ the CGF of a nondegenerate mean-zero µ_0 on [0, ∞), Q* = µ_{λ*} in the exponential family; h*(x) = e^{λ* x − ψ(λ*)} is an e-variable and the likelihood ratio dQ*/dµ_0 against an element of P, hence the numeraire (Safe Testing Cor. 2; numeraire paper Thm 4.1), so π* = δ_{λ*}. The same h* is GROW for the composite alternative Q = {Q : ∫ x^- dQ < ∞, ∫ x dQ ≥ ψ′(λ*)} by a saddle-point argument ∫ log h* dQ ≥ ∫ log h* dQ* ≥ ∫ log h dQ*.
- Theorem 7.6 (uniqueness): if U is concave, sup U(h) finite, and inf_Q ∫ [U((h_1 + h_2)/2) − U(h_1)/2 − U(h_2)/2] dQ = 0 forces h_1 = h_2 Q-q.s., the optimiser is unique up to Q-negligible sets; in particular when U is strictly concave and Q lies in the convex hull of finitely many mutually absolutely continuous laws.
- Lemma 8.1: for a union P = ∪_θ P_θ, E = ∩_θ E_θ; no analogue for intersections (Example 3.9 shows E_f for f(x) = x carries no information). Example 8.2 (CVaR_α(µ) ≤ c, Rockafellar–Uryasev representation (8.1)): P = ∪_{θ ≤ c} P_θ with single constraint θ − c + (x − θ)^+/(1 − α); the complete class is h(x) = 1 + inf_{θ < c} w_θ ((x − θ)^+/((1 − α)(c − θ)) − 1) for x ≠ c, h(c) = 1, w_θ ∈ [0, 1] (eq. 8.2). CVaR is testable without any moment bound: for c = 0, h = 0 on x < 0, 1 at 0, (1 − α)^{−1} on x > 0 is a nontrivial e-variable; α = 0 recovers the trivial mean case. Extends Theorem 5 of Wang, Wang and Ziegel's e-backtesting by removing monotonicity.
- Section 9 (relaxed integrability, eq. 9.1: only ∫ f^+ dµ < ∞ and ∫ f dµ ∈ [−∞, 0]): Example 9.1 on X = N shows the relaxed e-variable set can be strictly smaller and that adding a negative constraint function can change P and E; Theorem 9.2: for finite Φ the relaxed and original e-variable sets coincide, via Lemma 9.3 (any µ and finitely many integrable f_i admit a finitely supported ν with the same integrals). Infinite Φ is open.
- Section 10: product-form e-variables Π h_i(x_i) and their convex combinations do not exhaust E for i.i.d. n-samples (for P = all laws on R, h(x_1, x_2) = 2·1_{x_1 < x_2} + 1_{x_1 = x_2} is nontrivial). Sequential setting: for a finite Φ and P̄ = {P on X^N : E_P[g_i(X_t) | F_{t−1}] ≤ 0 ∀ i, t}, every process Y_t = Π_{s ≤ t}(1 + Σ π_{i,s} g_i(X_s)) with π predictable in Π^Φ is a P̄-supermartingale, hence an [e-process](../concepts/e-process.md) (eq. 10.1); open whether every admissible e-process has this form (Clerico 2025 gives a positive answer for the bounded-conditional-mean case), and what the e-processes for the smaller i.i.d. class P^∞ are.
- Appendix A: nets (Thm A.1), dual pairs, polar C° = {µ : ⟨f, µ⟩ ≤ 0 ∀ f ∈ C} and the bipolar theorem C°° = weak closure of a convex cone (Thm A.2, Schaefer–Wolff), Krein–Šmulian (Thm A.3, Conway).

## Setting and assumptions
- Single observation X on an arbitrary measurable space X; the observation may itself be a finite or infinite sample. Null P generated by a nonempty constraint set Φ ⊂ L as in (2.1), with integrability of every constraint function built into membership (Section 9 discusses relaxing this). No topology, no reference measure, no convexity beyond what the constraints imply, no bound on the cardinality of Φ [larssonTestingHypothesesGenerated2025, Secs 1–2].
- E-variables are [0, ∞]-valued; the value +∞ is allowed (needed, e.g., for 1 + ∞·1_{(c,∞)} in Example 8.2). Equalities among e-variables are P-quasi-sure (outside P-negligible sets); pointwise statements appear when no nonempty negligible set exists (sub-ψ, Lemma 4.6) or when exactness is proved pointwise (group invariance).
- Admissibility: h is admissible if h′ ≥ h P-q.s. for an e-variable h′ forces h′ = h P-q.s. (Section 3, Section 6).
- Optimality (Section 7): composite alternative Q with Q ≪ P in the negligible-set sense, utility nondecreasing, usc, linearly bounded (so U(x) = log x and all proper concave U qualify), arbitrary penalty χ; integrability conditions ∫ Σ|g_i| dQ < ∞ (finite case) or ∫ e^{ψ*} dQ < ∞ (sub-ψ case).
- Non-sequential: no filtration or stopping times, except for the product construction (10.1) in the open-problems section. Products Π h(X_i) of a single-observation e-variable are e-variables whenever each conditional law given the past lies in P [larssonTestingHypothesesGenerated2025, Sec. 1].

## Open questions / gaps
- Characterise the e-variables for the i.i.d. n-sample null P^{⊗n}; products and their convex combinations are not enough [larssonTestingHypothesesGenerated2025, Sec. 10].
- Sequential: are all admissible e-processes for the conditionally constrained class P̄ of the form (10.1) under the constraint qualification (3.3)? What are the admissible e-processes for the i.i.d. class P^∞ ⊂ P̄? Infinite constraint sets likewise [larssonTestingHypothesesGenerated2025, Sec. 10].
- Minimal complete classes are conjectured to exist for the three families without the extra assumptions of Theorem 6.5 (e.g. ψ not a CGF) [larssonTestingHypothesesGenerated2025, Sec. 6].
- The relaxed e-variable set Ẽ(Φ) is uncharacterised for infinite Φ [larssonTestingHypothesesGenerated2025, Sec. 9].
- No analogue of Lemma 8.1 for intersections of nulls, so the e-variables of a multi-constraint null cannot be assembled from single-constraint ones [larssonTestingHypothesesGenerated2025, Sec. 8].
- Theorems 7.3–7.4 give existence, not computation; uniqueness beyond the finite-mixture alternative of Theorem 7.6 is open.
- Missing from the bib: Clerico 2024 and 2025; Fan, Jiao and Wang 2025; Orabona and Jun 2023; Agrawal et al. 2020/2021; Saha and Ramdas 2024; Koning 2025 (Continuous testing).
