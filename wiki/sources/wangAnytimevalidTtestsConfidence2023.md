# Anytime-valid t-tests and confidence sequences for Gaussian means with unknown variance

arXiv 2310.03722v5 (November 2024), to appear in Sequential Analysis. Seven sections plus three appendices: A reviews two competing t-confidence sequences from the literature (Jourdan, Degenne and Kaufmann 2023; Howard and Ramdas 2022), B gives universal-inference Z-tests, C holds the proofs including the reverse-information-projection computation (Proposition C.3).

## Summary
The paper builds test processes and confidence sequences (CSs) for the mean μ of a Gaussian with unknown variance, where the null N_{μ=0} = {N(0, σ²) : σ > 0} is composite and scale-invariant [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Secs 1, 2.5]. Two constructions are compared throughout. Universal inference (Section 3) takes the plug-in likelihood-ratio martingale of Corollary 3.1 with any predictable estimators (μ̃, σ̃) in the numerator and the null-maximised Gaussian likelihood in the denominator; the result R_n (Theorem 3.2) is an e-process in the canonical data filtration, has closed form, and inverts to the CS μ̂_n ± √(μ̂_n² − X̄²_n + W_n) with radius of order σ α^{−1/n} n^{−1/2}; a one-sided version R_n^− handles N_{μ≤0} (Theorem 3.4). Scale invariance (Section 4) works in the scale-invariant filtration F*_n = σ(X_i/|X_1|) (Definition 4.2, Proposition 4.3): the right-Haar (dτ/τ) mixture of Gaussian likelihoods is the density of the maximal-invariant reduction (Lemma 4.4), so the scale-invariant likelihood ratio h_n(θ; θ_0) between standardised means is a nonnegative martingale on F* (Lemma 4.5, Corollary 4.6). Lai 1976 mixed h_{θ,n} with a flat improper prior over θ, which gives the parameter-free process H_n = √(2π/n)(1 + T²_{n−1}/(n−1))^{n/2}, an extended (nonintegrable) nonnegative supermartingale started at H_1 = ∞ whose extended Ville inequality yields Lai's CS with its starting time m and numerical root-finding (Theorems 4.1, 4.7). Replacing the flat prior with N(0, c^{−2}) gives the classical test martingale G_n^{(c)} (Theorem 4.9), a closed-form CS without root-finding, and a semi-one-sided e-process G_n^{(c−)} (Theorem 4.11). Every two-sided process has e-power ½ log(1 + μ²/σ²) (Propositions 3.3, 4.8, 4.10), which Section 6.3 shows is the ceiling: N(0, σ² + μ²) is the reverse information projection of N(μ, σ²) onto N_{μ=0} and the corresponding likelihood ratio is the numeraire (Proposition 6.5, C.3). Section 6.1 proves a minimax lower bound of order α^{−1/n} on t-interval width (Theorem 6.1, Corollary 6.2), attained by universal inference, nearly attained by the Gaussian mixture (α^{−1/(n−1)}), and missed by the classical fixed-n t-interval, which also scales as α^{−1/(n−1)} and is vacuous at n = 1 where a universal-inference e-value already gives a finite interval (Section 6.2). Simulations and an anorexia data set compare the methods, and Remark V (Proposition 4.13) shows that stopping the F*-martingale at a data-filtration stopping time keeps the tail bound P(M_T ≥ 1/α) ≤ α but not the e-value property, so an adjuster is needed for downstream e-value use [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023].

## Main results
- **Definitions (Sec. 2.3)**: NSM, NM, extended NSM (ENSM: drops E(M_0) < ∞), e-value, e-process on a filtration {G_n} (M_τ an e-value for every {G_n}-stopping time), with the Ramdas et al. 2020 characterisation by P-dependent dominating test supermartingales; Ville's inequality (Lemma 2.1) and the extended Ville inequality P(∃n: M_n ≥ ε) ≤ ε^{−1} E(1_{M_0<ε} M_0) + P(M_0 ≥ ε) (Lemma 2.2); σ-finite mixtures of ENSMs are ENSMs [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023].
- **E-power (Def. 2.3)**: liminf (1/n) log M_n a.s. under Q, following Vovk and Wang; related to expected rejection time via Wald's identity, log(1/α) ≈ E_Q(log M_1) E_Q(τ) (eq. 4) [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 2.4].
- **Lemmas 2.4–2.6**: likelihood ratios with predictable numerator kernels (Lemma 2.5) and for non-i.i.d. joint laws (Lemma 2.6, from Lai 1976 Sec. 2) are nonnegative martingales [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 2.6, App. C.1].
- **Corollary 3.1**: the universal-inference Z-test martingale ℓ_n^{μ,σ} = Π p_{μ̃_{i−1}, σ̃²_{i−1}}(X_i)/p_{μ,σ²}(X_i) is a martingale for N(μ, σ²) on {F_n} (eq. 8) [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023].
- **Theorem 3.2 (UI t-test e-process)**: R_n = (X̄²_n)^{n/2} e^{n/2} Π_{i≤n} σ̃_{i−1}^{−1} exp(−½((X_i − μ̃_{i−1})/σ̃_{i−1})²) is an e-process for N_{μ=0} on {F_n} (eq. 9, inf over σ of (8) with μ = 0, the null MLE s² = Σ X_i²/n); CS: CI_n = μ̂_n ± √(μ̂_n² − X̄²_n + W_n) with W_n = α^{−2/n} e^{−1} exp(n^{−1} Σ[log σ̃²_{i−1} + ((X_i − μ̃_{i−1})/σ̃_{i−1})²]) (eq. 10) [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, App. C.2].
- **Power-preserving translation (eq. 11)**: for the null N_{μ=μ_0} one must not replace every X_i by X_i − μ_0 (valid but power-losing); only the X̄²_n term becomes n^{−1} Σ(X_i − μ_0)², i.e. R_n^{μ_0} = (n^{−1}Σ(X_i − μ_0)²)^{n/2} e^{n/2} Π σ̃_{i−1}^{−1} exp(−½((X_i − μ̃_{i−1})/σ̃_{i−1})²); this is how the CS of Theorem 3.2 is obtained [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 3].
- **Proposition 3.3**: under any P with mean μ and variance σ² (Gaussianity not needed), if μ̃_n → μ in L³ at rate n^{−γ}, σ̃_n^{−2} → σ^{−2} in L² at rate n^{−γ} and a.s., with bounded sixth inverse moments, then (1/n) log R_n → ½ log(1 + μ²/σ²) a.s.; satisfied by sample mean and variance and by normal-inverse-gamma posterior estimates [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, App. C.2]. The radius of the CS scales as σ α^{−1/n} n^{−1/2} (eq. 13).
- **Theorem 3.4, Proposition 3.5 (one-sided)**: R_n^− = (X̄²_n − (μ̂_n ∧ 0)²)^{n/2} e^{n/2} Π(…) is an e-process for N_{μ≤0} on {F_n}, with e-power ½ log(1 + (μ ∨ 0)²/σ²) (eqs 14–15) [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023].
- **Theorem 4.1 (Lai 1976, Thm 2)**: for starting time m ≥ 2 and a > 0, b = (1 + a²/(m−1))^m/m, ξ_n = √(s_n²[(bn)^{1/n} − 1]), the intervals μ̂_n ± ξ_n satisfy P(∃n ≥ m: μ ∉ CI_n) ≤ 2(1 − F_{m−1}(a) + a f_{m−1}(a)); so a ≍ α^{−1/(m−1)}, growth rate α^{−m/(n(m−1))} (eq. 22), shrinkage σ√(log(bn)/n) (eq. 23) [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.1].
- **Definition 4.2, Proposition 4.3**: scale-invariant filtration F*_n = σ(f(X_1..X_n): f scale invariant) = σ(X_1/|X_1|, …, X_n/|X_1|) when X_1 ≠ 0; the UI processes R_n, R_n^− are not F*-adapted (μ̃_0, σ̃_0 are constants) [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.2].
- **Lemma 4.4 (Lai 1976 Sec. 5)**: for a location-scale family with density σ^{−1} g(σ^{−1}(x − μ)), the push-forward of P^{⊗n} under φ_n(x) = (x_1/|x_1|, …, x_n/|x_1|) has density ∫_{τ>0} Π_i (1/τ) g(x_i/τ − μ/σ) dτ/τ (eq. 28), depending on (μ, σ) only through μ/σ; this is the Jeffreys/right-Haar 1/σ mixture (eq. 29) and a special case of the Pérez-Ortiz et al. maximal invariant [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.3].
- **Lemma 4.5, Corollary 4.6**: h_n(θ; θ_0) = ∫ Π dP_{τθ,τ²}(X_i) dτ/τ / ∫ Π dP_{τθ_0,τ²}(X_i) dτ/τ is an NM with mean 1 for {μ_0/σ_0 = θ_0} on {F*_n}; Gaussian case h_{θ,n} = e^{−nθ²/2} Γ(n/2)^{−1} ∫_{y>0} y^{n/2−1} exp(−y + θ S_n √(2y/V_n)) dy (eq. 31), an NM for N_{μ=0} on F* [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.3–4.4, Lemma C.1 (homogeneity of degree −n)].
- **Theorem 4.7 (Lai's extended martingale)**: flat mixture ∫ h_{θ,n} dθ gives H_1 = ∞, H_n = √(2π/n) (nV_n/(nV_n − S_n²))^{n/2} = √(2π/n)(1 + T²_{n−1}/(n−1))^{n/2} (eqs 32–33), an ENSM for N_{μ=0} on F*; Lai's CS follows from the extended Ville inequality; Proposition 4.8: (1/n) log H_n → ½ log(1 + μ²/σ²) a.s., H_n → 0 a.s. under the null [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.4, App. C.3 eqs 154–159].
- **Theorem 4.9 (Gaussian-mixed scale-invariant t-test martingale)**: for c > 0, G_n^{(c)} = √(c²/(n + c²)) ((n + c²)V_n/((n + c²)V_n − S_n²))^{n/2} (eq. 35) is an NM for N_{μ=0} on F* with G_1^{(c)} = 1; CS μ̂_n ± radius_n with radius_n² = (n + c²)(1 − (α²c²/(n + c²))^{1/n})(X̄²_n − μ̂_n²) / ((α²c²/(n + c²))^{1/n}(n + c²) − c²)_+ (eqs 36–37); near-optimal fixed-n choice c²/(n + c²) = α^{2/(n−1)} 2^{−n/(n−1)} gives radius √((α^{−2/(n−1)} 2^{n/(n−1)} − 2)(X̄²_n − μ̂_n²)) (eqs 38–39), growth α^{−1/(n−1)}; for fixed c the CS is trivial until some n_0 (n ≥ 3 for c = 0.01, α = 0.05), and shrinks like √(log(n/α²c²)/n) (eq. 40); Proposition 4.10: same ½ log(1 + μ²/σ²) e-power for every c [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.5]. Lindon et al. 2022 (Prop. 3.7) obtained the same martingale as a covariate-free F-test.
- **Theorem 4.11 (semi-one-sided)**: half-Gaussian prior on θ > 0 gives G_n^{(c−)} = 2G_n^{(c)} − 2G_n^{(c)}|_{S_n ← S_n ∧ 0}, an e-process (not a martingale, an approximation step is used) for the point null N_{μ=0} on F*, e-power ½ log(1 + (μ ∨ 0)²/σ²); by Pérez-Ortiz et al. Corollary 8 it is a fixed-n e-value for N_{μ≤0}, but whether it is an e-process for N_{μ≤0} is left open [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.5].
- **Remark I**: scale-invariant processes depend on data only through (S_n, V_n), so they are permutation-symmetric and immune to "optional permutation" hacking, unlike universal inference [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.6.1].
- **Theorem 4.12 (Remark II)**: the CS (37) is an asymptotic (1 − α)-CS (Waudby-Smith et al. 2024 sense) for μ over all finite-variance distributions; a non-asymptotic nonparametric CS is impossible even for bounded laws with unknown bounds (Bahadur–Savage) [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.6.2, App. C.3].
- **Remark III (Pérez-Ortiz et al.)**: their Corollary 8 optimises worst-case e-power over separated null and alternative sets with Dirac minimisers on the boundary ("ratio of mixtures"); their Corollary 9 covers only the marginal (prior-mixed) null and gives no per-distribution power statement; here the "mixture of ratios" ∫ h_n(θ; 0) ϖ(dθ) with a proper ϖ is an e-process for the whole point null with e-power ½ log(1 + μ²/σ²) at every alternative [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.6.3].
- **Remark IV (JZS Bayes factor)**: Rouder et al.'s B_n^{JZS} with Jeffreys prior on σ and Cauchy on μ/σ equals 1/M_n^ϖ with ϖ = Cauchy, so 1/B_n^{JZS} is an NM for P_{μ=0} on F* and B_n^{JZS} is an anytime-valid p-value; the Cauchy arises as a χ²_1 hyperprior on c² of the Gaussian mixture and has no closed form [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.6.4, eqs 46–49].
- **Proposition 4.13 (Remark V)**: for an NSM on a coarser filtration {G_n} and any A-measurable random time T, P(M_T ≥ 1/α) ≤ α (via sup_n M_n); so stopping G^{(c)} at a data-filtration stopping time is safe for rejection and coverage, but M_T need not be an e-value; use an adjuster such as f(x) = (x ∨ 1 − 1 − log(x ∨ 1))/log²(x ∨ 1) before e-BH, multiplication or averaging [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 4.6.5].
- **Table 1 (Sec. 5.1)**: growth rates α^{−1/n} (UI), α^{−m/(n(m−1))} (Lai), α^{−1/(n−1)} CI and (α − α_*)^{−1/n} CS (Thm 4.9), α^{−(1+η)/(n−1)} (Jourdan et al. plug-in, Thm A.3), √log(1/(α − α_*)) (Howard–Ramdas median CS, Prop. A.4, degenerate once log(10/α) > n/10); shrinkage n^{−1/2} polylog n for the first three vs n^{−1/2}√loglog n for the stitched two, which however carry large constants (Figs 3–4). Tables 2–3 align UI and mixture Z-tests (e-power μ²/2σ²) with t-tests (½ log(1 + μ²/σ²)) [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023].
- **Simulations (Secs 5.2–5.4)**: Gaussian mixture is less sensitive to the prior than UI, whose MAP plug-ins from a normal-inverse-gamma prior need a burn-in (40 observations) to be robust; anorexia data (Hand et al. 1993): Treatment I rejected at τ_rej = 15–17 of 17 by all methods, Treatment II (classical p = 0.035) rejected by none [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Figs 1–2].
- **Theorem 6.1, Corollary 6.2**: for α < 1/3 the minimax 2α-quantile excess of lower t-confidence bounds, and the minimax α-quantile width of two-sided t-CIs, normalised by σ, are at least (6α − 9α²)^{−2/n} − 1 = O(α^{−1/n}); two-point argument with N(μ, σ²) vs N(0, μ² + σ²) at total variation ≤ 1 − 3α [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 6.1, App. C.4].
- **Section 6.2**: the classical t-CI width scales as α^{−1/(n−1)} (t-tail O(x^{−(n−1)})), so for fixed n and α → 0 the UI CS is eventually tighter (Fig. 5, n = 3); at n = 1 the e-value |X_1 − μ| exp((1 − X_1²)/2) (eq. 59) gives the CI X_1 ± α^{−1} exp((X_1² − 1)/2) (eq. 62) where the t-test is undefined; at n = 2 a UI e-value gives O(α^{−1/2}) vs the Cauchy O(α^{−1}); Remark 6.3 gives the one-observation RIPr likelihood ratio (eq. 64); Remark 6.4 relates to Stein/Portnoy one-observation CIs and halves the width by randomised Markov (Ramdas–Manole) [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023].
- **Proposition 6.5 / C.3 (log-optimality)**: min_{P ∈ N_{μ=0}} D(N(μ,σ²) ‖ P) = D(N(μ,σ²) ‖ N(0, σ² + μ²)) = ½ log(1 + μ²/σ²); M* = dN(μ,σ²)/dN(0, σ²+μ²)(X_1) is an e-value for N_{μ=0} (eq. 224: (y/z) exp((z² − y²)/2z²) ≤ 1), hence the numeraire, N(0, σ²+μ²) the reverse information projection, and every t-test e-value has e-power ≤ ½ log(1 + μ²/σ²) with equality only for M* [wangAnytimevalidTtestsConfidence2023, Sec. 6.3, App. C.4, Lemma C.2 from Larsson et al.].

A.4) comes from the sign-test supermartingale B_n^λ (Prop. A.5) and its beta-binomial mixture (Prop. ## Setting and assumptions
- Data: i.i.d. real observations; validity statements are for the Gaussian classes N_{μ=0}, N_{μ≤0}, N_{μ/σ=θ_0}; e-power statements of Propositions 3.3, 4.8, 4.10 hold under any law with finite variance; Theorem 4.12 gives asymptotic coverage over L².
- Nulls are composite in σ (point null μ = 0 means a one-parameter family), and Section 2.5 notes that processes for N_{μ/σ=θ_0} are the strongest objects, yielding tests and CSs for both μ and μ/σ.
- Optional stopping: UI processes are e-processes in the canonical filtration {F_n}, so any stopping time is allowed. Scale-invariant processes are martingales or e-processes on {F*_n} only; stopping at data-filtration times keeps the tail/coverage bound (Prop. 4.13) but not the e-value property. The filtration issue disappears once tests are converted to CSs.
- Lai's construction needs a starting time m ≥ 2 because H_1 = ∞; the Gaussian mixture CS is trivial for n below some n_0(c, α) instead.
- Power-one (Darling–Robbins) formulation: only type-I error is controlled, power quantified by e-power rather than rejection probability.

## Open questions / gaps
- Whether the scale-invariant likelihood ratio h_{θ,n} (and hence G^{(c−)}) is an e-process, not just a fixed-n e-value, for the one-sided null N_{μ≤0} (after Theorem 4.11).
- Why Lai 1976 chose an improper flat mixture over a proper Gaussian one is unexplained; the Gaussian mixture is tighter in all experiments.
- The Gaussian-mixture CS growth rate α^{−1/(n−1)} does not quite match the α^{−1/n} lower bound; whether an F*-adapted process can attain α^{−1/n} is not addressed.
- Extensions to Hotelling's T² with confidence-sphere sequences and to sequential F-tests for linear models beyond the Gaussian-mixture case (Lindon et al. 2022) are left to future work (Sec. 7).
- The stitched CSs' iterated-logarithm shrinkage never shows in practice below n = 10⁴; stitching the three main CSs is suggested but not done.
