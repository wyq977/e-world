# A complete characterization of testable hypotheses

## Summary
Given two nonempty sets P, Q of countably additive probability measures on an arbitrary measurable space, the paper asks when a nontrivial (strictly unbiased) test exists, i.e. a measurable ϕ: Ω → [0, 1] with sup_{µ∈P} E_µ[ϕ] < inf_{ν∈Q} E_ν[ϕ], equivalently minimax risk R(P, Q) = inf_ϕ (sup_P E_µ[ϕ] + sup_Q E_ν[1 − ϕ]) < 1 [larssonCompleteCharacterizationTestable2026, Sec. 1]. The classical Kraft–Le Cam theorem answers this only under a common dominating measure: then R(P, Q) = 1 − d_TV(co(P), co(Q)) (Theorem 1.1), but the hypothesis fails for standard nonparametric nulls (bounded support with fixed mean, symmetric laws, sub-Gaussian laws, TV or Wasserstein balls), and the conclusion itself fails for Diracs on [0, 1] against the uniform law (Example 1.3); the ordinary weak closure is the wrong fix too (Example 1.4). The main theorem states that, with no assumptions at all, R(P, Q) = 1 − d_TV(co*(P), co*(Q)) where co* is the closure of the convex hull in the weak-∗ topology σ(ba, L) of the space ba of bounded finitely additive measures, and the TV infimum is attained by a pair in the closures (Theorem 1.5); the proof is a direct application of Fan's minimax theorem, compactness of ba_1 coming from Banach–Alaoglu [larssonCompleteCharacterizationTestable2026, Sec. 2]. Finitely additive limits cannot be avoided: Example 3.5 (under the consistent set-theoretic assumption that no diffuse probability lives on the power set of R) shows a point alternative where the distance to the countably additive part of co*(P) is 1 but the distance to co*(P) is 1/2. Section 3 ties the result to the [numeraire](../concepts/numeraire.md) paper: for a singleton alternative, co*(P) ∩ M_1 = P_eff ∩ M_1, P_eff is the solid hull of the countably additive parts of co*(P) (Theorems 3.3, 3.4), and a bounded uniformly powered [e-variable](../concepts/e-variable.md) exists iff d_TV(co*(P), co*(Q)) > 0 (Corollary 3.7). Appendices explain why Le Cam's own generalised tests (functionals on measures rather than functions of the sample) are statistically opaque, flag incorrect uses of minimax theorems in the literature, and give a convex-programming representation of the minimax risk over finite partitions.

## Main results
- Theorem 1.1 (Kraft 1955, credited to Le Cam): with a common σ-finite dominating measure, a test with power exceeding level by more than ε exists iff d_TV(co(P), co(Q)) > ε; R(P, Q) = 1 − d_TV(co(P), co(Q)), the risk is attained by a test but the TV infimum need not be attained by any µ ∈ co(P), ν ∈ co(Q) (Example 1.2: Q = {δ_0}, P = {(1/2 − 1/n)δ_0 + (1/2 + 1/n)δ_n}, distance 1/2 not attained) [larssonCompleteCharacterizationTestable2026].
- Example 1.3 (van der Vaart 2002): P = {δ_x : x ∈ [0, 1]}, Q = {Uni[0, 1]}; d_TV(Uni, co(P)) = 1 yet no nontrivial test exists, R = 1. Example 1.4: P = {δ_x : x < 0.5}, Q = {δ_y : y > 0.5}; a perfect test exists although the weak closures of both sets contain δ_{0.5}. So the TV closure is too small and the weak closure too large.
- Theorem 1.5 (main): for any P, Q ⊂ M_1 and ε ≥ 0, a test with inf_Q E_ν[ϕ] > sup_P E_µ[ϕ] + ε exists iff d_TV(co*(P), co*(Q)) > ε; in fact R(P, Q) = 1 − d_TV(co*(P), co*(Q)) (eq. 1.4), and the TV infimum is attained by some µ ∈ co*(P), ν ∈ co*(Q). The authors suspect the minimax risk itself need not be attained by any test outside special cases.
- Proposition 1.6: under a common reference measure, d_TV(co(P), co(Q)) = d_TV(co*(P), co*(Q)) and a minimax optimal test always exists (Hahn–Banach in L¹(γ) plus weak-∗ compactness of tests in L^∞(γ)), so Theorem 1.5 implies Theorem 1.1, even though co(P) ≠ co*(P) in general (e.g. P = {δ_n : n ∈ N} has a purely finitely additive limit δ_∞ in co*(P)).
- Theorem 1.8: if Ω is a metric space with Borel σ-algebra and P, Q are convex and weakly compact in the usual sense, then R(P, Q) = inf over continuous tests = 1 − d_TV(P, Q) = 1 − d_TV(co*(P), co*(Q)), with the TV infimum attained in P × Q. Incomparable to Theorem 1.1 (no reference measure needed); Remark 1.9 shows P ⊊ co*(P) still (symmetric laws on [−1, 1], Uni[−1/n, 1/n] has a weak-∗ subnet limit outside M_1), and Lemma 2.3 (TV distance computed over continuous tests via Urysohn and regularity) is the only place the metric is used.
- Corollary 1.10 (weak duality certificate): R(ϕ) ≥ 1 − d_TV(µ, ν) for any test and any µ ∈ co*(P), ν ∈ co*(Q); exhibiting equality certifies ϕ as minimax optimal and (µ, ν) as the TV-closest pair. Example 1.11: P = {mean ≤ m_1}, Q = {mean ≥ m_2} on [0, 1]; the test ϕ*(x) = x has risk m_1 + 1 − m_2, matched by Bernoulli(m_1), Bernoulli(m_2), so it is minimax optimal although Theorem 1.8 does not guarantee existence.
- Theorem 1.12 (Le Cam 2012, p. 476): with generalised tests (elements of the TV-dual M′ taking values in [0, 1] on M_1), the equivalence holds with the plain TV closure of the convex hulls. Appendix B shows such a test may depend on the whole generating law (e.g. ϕ*(µ) = mass of the ν-absolutely-continuous part of µ in Example 1.3), i.e. it needs a "tag" beyond the sample; whenever no nontrivial test exists but a generalised one does, extra information is always needed. Appendix B constructs the extended sample space explicitly as a disjoint union of copies of Ω indexed by a maximal family of mutually singular probabilities, identifying (M, TV) with L¹ of a non-σ-finite but strictly localizable measure, and Remark B.1 realises any generalised test as a weak-∗ limit of ordinary tests.
- Section 2 (machinery): ba is the dual of (L, ‖·‖_∞); weak-∗ convergence means E_{µ_α}[f] → E_µ[f] for all bounded measurable f; ba_1 is weak-∗ compact (Banach–Alaoglu); d_TV(µ, ν) = sup_ϕ (E_µ[ϕ] − E_ν[ϕ]) extends verbatim to ba_1 (eq. 2.1). Proof of Theorem 1.5: Fan's minimax theorem (Theorem 2.2) with X = co*(P) × co*(Q), Y = Φ, F = E_ν[ϕ] − E_µ[ϕ]. Weak-∗ closures of convex hulls are the largest extensions of P, Q preserving the risk of every test; the need for ba (rather than M_1 with σ(M_1, L)) comes from compactness.
- Corollary 3.1 (from the numeraire paper): ν ∈ P_eff iff E_ν[ϕ] ≤ sup_P E_µ[ϕ] for every test. Corollary 3.2 (from Theorem 1.5): ν ∈ co*(P) iff the same. Theorem 3.3: co*(P) ∩ M_1 = P_eff ∩ M_1. Neither set contains the other: 0 ∈ P_eff \ co*(P), and co*(P) may contain finitely additive elements.
- Theorem 3.4 (proved in Appendix D): every µ ∈ P_eff is setwise dominated by a maximal element of P_eff, each maximal element is the countably additive (Yosida–Hewitt) part of some element of co*(P), and P_eff is the solid hull of the countably additive parts of co*(P). Lemma D.4–D.5: the finitely additive effective null P_eff^ba = ba_+ ∩ co*(P − ba_+) equals the solid hull of co*(P), whose maximal elements are exactly co*(P).
- Example 3.5: Ω = [−1, 1] with F generated by all subsets of [−1, 0] and Borel subsets of [0, 1]; P = {(δ_x + δ_{−x})/2 : x ∈ (0, 1)}, Q = {Uni[0, 1]}. Assuming (3.1) (no diffuse probability measure on Pow(R), implied by the continuum hypothesis and consistent with ZFC, Remark 3.6), d_TV(co*(P) ∩ M_1, Q) = 1 but d_TV(co*(P), Q) = 1/2; the minimising element restricted to [−1, 0] is purely finitely additive. Hence even for a point alternative the quantitative theorem cannot be stated with P_eff or with co*(P) ∩ M_1. Open: whether such examples exist without assumption (3.1).
- Corollary 3.7: a bounded e-variable for P that is uniformly powered against Q (inf_Q E_ν[Z] > 1) exists iff d_TV(co*(P), co*(Q)) > 0; by Theorem 3.14 of the e-value book "powered" may be replaced by "e-powered" (inf_Q E_ν[log Z] > 0). Extends results of the book and of Zhang, Ramdas and Wang 2024.
- Appendix A: topologies on the set of tests Φ cannot make it compact with continuous risk in the nondominated case (pointwise/product topology kills continuity; sup-norm and σ(Φ, M) kill compactness); Gül and Zoubir 2017 and Levy 2008 are named as works that invoke minimax theorems incorrectly.
- Appendix C: Proposition C.1, for P = all Diracs on any measurable space, co*(P) = ba_1; Remark C.2, Lebesgue-density laws on [0, 1] have co*(P) ≠ ba_1 and Uni[0, 1/n] does not converge weak-∗ (witness f(ω) = sin log(1/ω)); Proposition C.3, under (3.1) every finite measure on Pow(R) is purely atomic.
- Appendix E (practical): E.1, contaminated null (1 − ε)µ_0 + εη with η ranging over the convex hull of Diracs and atomless µ_0, ν_0: the classical formula gives d_TV(P, Q) = ½‖(1 − ε)µ_0 − ν_0‖ + ε/2 but d_TV(co*(P), Q) ≤ ½(1 − ε)‖µ_0 − ν_0‖, strictly smaller, gap up to ε, so the classical theory underestimates the minimax risk; no issue if everything is dominated. E.2, uniform null on the circle against quantised, shifted alternatives {(q_θ)_* ν_0}: all elements of co(Q) are singular to µ_0 (risk 0 classically), but co*(Q) contains the mixtures over θ, which have Lebesgue densities; for 1/m-periodic ν_0 the risk is 1. E.3, Corollary E.1: 1 − R(P, Q) = sup over finite measurable partitions π of min_{p∈C_π, q∈D_π} ½‖p − q‖_1 (Euclidean closures of the pushed-forward convex hulls on the simplex), so d_TV(co*(P), co*(Q)) = sup_π d_TV(C_π, D_π), an increasing net under refinement, and risk below 1 − ε is witnessed by a test constant on some finite partition.

## Setting and assumptions
- Arbitrary measurable space (Ω, F); P, Q arbitrary nonempty sets of countably additive probability measures; tests are [0, 1]-valued measurable functions of a single observation (products of n observations are a special case) [larssonCompleteCharacterizationTestable2026, Sec. 1].
- Main theorem: no assumptions whatsoever. Theorem 1.1 needs a common σ-finite dominating measure; Theorem 1.8 needs a metric space and convex, weakly compact hypotheses; Example 3.5 needs set-theoretic assumption (3.1).
- Worst-case (minimax) level and power; the risk is unchanged by convexification. Fixed-sample, non-sequential; no filtrations or optional stopping.
- The authors stress that they do not advocate finite additivity as a modelling axiom (de Finetti, Dubins–Savage): the question and the answer are about countably additive models, but least favourable pairs witnessing the minimax risk can have purely finitely additive components [larssonCompleteCharacterizationTestable2026, Sec. 1].

## Concepts touched
- [e-variable](../concepts/e-variable.md) — e-variables as the polar of P and P_eff as the bipolar; existence of bounded uniformly powered e-variables for composite alternatives (Corollary 3.7); tests and bounded e-variables are interconvertible (ϕ/sup E_µ[ϕ] and Z/B).
- [e-power](../concepts/e-power.md) — uniformly e-powered bounded e-variables exist iff d_TV(co*(P), co*(Q)) > 0 (Corollary 3.7 with the book's Theorem 3.14).
- [numeraire](../concepts/numeraire.md) — the effective null P_eff versus co*(P): Corollary 3.1, Theorems 3.3, 3.4, Example 3.5; why the numeraire theory never needed finitely additive measures.
- [reverse-information-projection](../concepts/reverse-information-projection.md) — P_eff is the domain of the RIPr; Theorem 3.4 characterises its maximal elements as countably additive parts of weak-∗ limits of convex combinations of the null.
- [universal-inference](../concepts/universal-inference.md) — no direct contribution; the paper's examples of nondominated nulls are the regime where split-LRT and numeraire differ (passing relevance only).
- testable-hypotheses (new) — the characterisation of nontrivial tests by TV separation of weak-∗ convex closures in ba: Theorems 1.1, 1.5, 1.8, 1.12, Proposition 1.6, Corollary 1.10, Examples 1.2–1.4, 1.11, 3.5, Appendix E.
- effective-null (new, already proposed from the numeraire paper) — P_eff ∩ M_1 = co*(P) ∩ M_1, solid-hull representation, and the finitely additive effective null P_eff^ba = solid co*(P) (Theorem 3.3, Theorem 3.4, Lemmas D.4–D.6).

## Open questions / gaps
- Whether a minimax optimal test always exists in the nondominated case: the authors suspect not, but give no counterexample [larssonCompleteCharacterizationTestable2026, after Theorem 1.5].
- Whether Example 3.5 (finitely additive witnesses needed) can be built without the set-theoretic assumption (3.1) [larssonCompleteCharacterizationTestable2026, Sec. 3].
- Whether the conclusions of Gül and Zoubir 2017 and Levy 2008, obtained by misapplied minimax theorems, are false or merely unproved, and whether the techniques here repair them [larssonCompleteCharacterizationTestable2026, App. A].
- The result is fixed-sample and about tests; the e-value link (Corollary 3.7) is qualitative. A quantitative e-value counterpart (e.g. a minimax e-power formula for composite alternatives via co*) is not given; the numeraire paper's duality handles only point alternatives.
- The convex-program representation (Corollary E.1) is a supremum over all finite partitions; no algorithm or rate is offered.
- Missing from the bib: Kraft 1955; Le Cam 2012; van der Vaart 2002; Huber and Strassen 1973; Zhang, Ramdas and Wang 2024 (already in gaps.md); Klein and Köstenberger 2026; Liebrich, Maggis and Svindland 2022.
