# Multiple testing in multi-stream sequential change detection

arXiv 2501.04130v4 (3 February 2025), 38 pages including five appendices. Code at github.com/sanjitdp/multistream-change-detectors [dandapanthulaMultipleTestingMultistream2025, Sec. 9].

## Summary
The paper studies K data streams monitored simultaneously, each with its own unknown changepoint ξ^(k) ∈ (1, ∞], and asks which multiple-testing error metrics can be controlled by a change *monitoring* algorithm φ (a {0,1}^K-valued adapted process of current declarations) when it is stopped at an arbitrary stopping time τ [dandapanthulaMultipleTestingMultistream2025]. The first result is an impossibility: if the average run length to the first declaration under the global null is finite, then the worst case over configurations ξ and stopping times τ of FDR, FWER and GER is exactly 1 and of PFER is at least η (Theorem 4.1, Theorem 5.1, Proposition 5.2, Proposition 7.1), because the natural stopping time "first time η changes are declared" is almost surely finite and every declaration at it is false. The proposed remedy is the error over patience, EOP = sup_ξ sup_τ (Type I error at τ)/E[τ], which forces small error for impatient stopping and relaxes it for patient stopping, and which under the global null at τ_1* reduces to ARL ≥ 1/α (Definitions 4.1, 5.1, 5.2, 7.3). Four procedures feed per-stream [e-detectors](../concepts/e-detector.md) M^(k)_t (nonnegative processes with E[M_τ] ≤ E[τ] for every stopping time and pre-change law) into fixed-time multiple-testing rules: e-d-BH applies [e-BH](../concepts/e-bh.md) to the K current e-detector values, e-d-Bonferroni thresholds each at K/β, e-d-Holm applies a step-down Holm rule, and e-d-GNT sums the e-detectors for a global-null test (Algorithms 1–4). Theorems 4.2, 5.3, Corollary 5.3.2 and Theorem 7.2 prove EOP_FDR ≤ α, EOP_PFER ≤ β, EOP_FWER ≤ β and EOP_GER ≤ α with proofs of a few lines that use only the e-detector property at the stopping time τ ∧ (ξ^(k) − 1) and the inequality 1{x ≥ 1} ≤ x; the bounds are in fact uniform in a post-hoc α, and require the e-detectors to be valid for the global cross-stream filtration, which Section 8 shows holds under arbitrary "past-only" dependence but fails when one stream sees another's future. If the ARL is forfeited, the same algorithms give universal fixed-time control (FDR at level tα_t|H_0(t)|/K, so α_t = α/t gives FDR ≤ α for all t; Theorems 4.3, 5.4, 6.1, 7.3), and with [e-processes](../concepts/e-process.md) in place of e-detectors they control the probability of false alarm and the worst-case error uniformly over stopping times (Theorems 4.4, 5.5, 7.4). Simulations on Gaussian mean change (K = 50 and 1000), nonparametric symmetry change, conformal change from exchangeability and kernelised independence change show FDR, FWER and PFER far below the naive per-stream rule at small extra delay (Section 9, Appendix C).

## Main results
- **Setting (Sec. 2.1, Defs 2.2–2.9)**: K streams, each with pre-change class P_0^(k) and post-change class P_1^(k) over whole sequences (within-stream dependence allowed; cross-stream joint law arbitrary); configuration ξ ∈ N^K; global null ξ_G = (∞, …, ∞); a monitoring algorithm φ may retract declarations; a detection algorithm is a pair (φ, τ) with τ a stopping time for the global filtration F. Metrics at time t: FDP/FDR, FWER, PFER (Defs 2.7–2.9); ARL_η(φ) = E_∞[τ_η*] with τ_η* the first time ≥ η streams are declared, and its false-detection analogue ARL_η(φ, ξ) (Def. 2.5); PFA(φ) = P_∞(τ_1* < ∞) (Def. 2.6) [dandapanthulaMultipleTestingMultistream2025].
- **Defs 2.10–2.12 and the SR/CUSUM constructions (Sec. 2.2)**: j-delay e-process Λ^(j) (equal to 1 before j, an e-process for (F_t)_{t ≥ j} afterwards; the e_j-process of [shinEdetectorsNonparametricFramework2023](shinEdetectorsNonparametricFramework2023.md)); e-detector M with M_0 = 0 and E_P[M_τ] ≤ E_P[τ]; M^SR_t = Σ_{j ≤ t} Λ^(j)_t and M^CU_t = max_{j ≤ t} Λ^(j)_t are e-detectors (Wald's equation), reducing to the Shiryaev–Roberts recursion M_{t+1} = (p_1/p_0)(X_{t+1})(M_t + 1) and the CUSUM recursion with max{M_t, 1} for likelihood ratios; with weights γ_j summing to 1, E^SR_t = Σ_j γ_j Λ^(j)_t is an e-process. The threshold 1/α is always valid; a simulated c_α < 1/α may be used, and all theorems then hold with 1/c_α in place of α (Appendix A).
- **Theorem 4.1 (FDR versus ARL)**: if ARL_1(φ) < ∞ then sup_ξ sup_τ FDR(φ, ξ, τ) = FDR(φ, ξ_G, τ_1*) = 1. Proof: Markov gives P(τ_1* ≤ (1 + α)ARL_1) ≥ α/(1 + α), and on {τ_1* < ∞} the FDP at τ_1* is 1 under the global null. Same conclusion for FWER (Prop. 5.2), PFER ≥ η when ARL_η < ∞ (Thm 5.1), and GER (Prop. 7.1). Appendix D: ARL_K < ∞ forces sup_t CCD(φ, ξ, t) = 1 (Prop. D.1, Cor. D.1.1).
- **Definition 4.1 (EOP_FDR)**: EOP_FDR(φ) = sup_{ξ ∈ N^K} sup_{τ ∈ T} FDR(φ, ξ, τ)/E[τ]; analogously EOP_PFER (Def. 5.1), EOP_FWER (Def. 5.2), EOP_GER (Def. 7.3). EOP ≤ α implies ARL_1 ≥ 1/α by taking ξ = ξ_G and τ = τ_1*, and is strictly stronger (all configurations, all stopping times) yet strictly weaker than level-α Type I error control, which is impossible (Sec. 4.2).
- **Algorithm 1 (e-d-BH)**: at each t, sort M^(k)_t decreasingly, k_t* = max{k : M^[k]_t ≥ K/(k α_t)}, declare the top k_t* streams; this is e-BH of [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021](wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021.md) applied to the vector of current e-detector values.
- **Theorem 4.2 (e-d-BH controls EOP_FDR)**: for e-detectors w.r.t. the global F and constant α, sup_ξ sup_τ FDR(φ, ξ, τ) / ((Σ_k E[τ ∧ (ξ^(k) − 1)]) ∨ 1) ≤ α/K, hence EOP_FDR ≤ α. Proof: FDR ≤ (α/K) Σ_k E[M^(k)_τ 1{ξ^(k) > τ}] ≤ (α/K) Σ_k E[M^(k)_{τ ∧ (ξ^(k) − 1)}] ≤ (α/K) Σ_k E[τ ∧ (ξ^(k) − 1)] ≤ α E[τ]. The proof also gives the post-hoc form sup_ξ sup_τ E[sup_{α∈(0,1)} FDP_α(τ)/α] / ((Σ_k E[τ ∧ (ξ^(k) − 1)]) ∨ 1) ≤ 1/K, where FDP_α is the FDP of e-d-BH run at level α.
- **Corollary 4.2.1**: ARL_1(φ) ≥ 1/α under the global null and ARL_1(φ, ξ) ≥ 1/α under every configuration.
- **Theorem 4.3 and Corollary 4.3.1 (universal FDR control)**: for any sequence (α_t), sup_ξ sup_t FDR(φ, ξ, t)/(t α_t |H_0(t)|) ≤ 1/K, so α_t = α/t gives FDR(φ, ξ, t) ≤ α|H_0(t)|/K ≤ α for all fixed t; Theorem 4.1 then forces ARL_1 = ∞. Needs only E[M^(k)_t] ≤ t at fixed t, so no condition on the cross-stream filtration (Sec. 8.1).
- **Theorem 4.4 (e-d-BH with e-processes)**: if each M^(k) is an e-process for F and α_t = α, then PFA(φ) ≤ α, ARL_1 = ∞ and sup_ξ sup_τ FDR(φ, ξ, τ) ≤ α. The PFA bound comes from [Ville's inequality](../concepts/villes-inequality.md) applied to sup_t M^(k)_t and BH on the p-values 1/sup_t M^(k)_t; the FDR bound repeats the proof of Theorem 4.2 with E[τ] replaced by 1.
- **Algorithm 2 (e-d-Bonferroni)** declares stream k at t iff M^(k)_t ≥ K/β_t (the e-Bonferroni rule of [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021](vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021.md)). **Theorem 5.3**: EOP_PFER ≤ β, with the same normalised and post-hoc forms as Theorem 4.2; **Corollary 5.3.1**: ARL_η ≥ η/β under the global null; **Corollary 5.3.2**: EOP_FWER ≤ β for β ∈ (0, 1) since PFER ≥ FWER; **Theorem 5.4 / Corollary 5.4.1**: PFER(φ, ξ, t) ≤ t β_t |H_0(t)|/K, so β_t = β/t gives PFER ≤ β|H_0(t)|/K for all t; **Theorem 5.5**: with e-processes, PFA ≤ β (union bound plus Ville), ARL_1 = ∞ and worst-case PFER ≤ β.
- **Algorithm 3 (e-d-Holm)**: k_t* = max{k : M^[i]_t/(K − i + 1) ≥ 1/α_t for all i ≤ k}, declare the top k_t*. **Theorem 6.1 / Corollary 6.1.1**: FWER(φ, ξ, t) ≤ t α_t, so α_t = α/t gives FWER ≤ α for all t. The proof shows that any false declaration at t forces some null stream to have M^(k)_t/|H_0(t)| ≥ 1/α_t, then applies Markov with E[M^(k)_t] ≤ t. e-d-Holm is not claimed to control EOP_FWER; e-d-Bonferroni is the tool for that.
- **Algorithm 4 (e-d-GNT)** declares a global change at t iff Σ_k M^(k)_t ≥ K/α_t, i.e. the arithmetic [e-merging](../concepts/e-merging.md) of the e-detectors crosses 1/α_t; at least as powerful as e-d-Bonferroni by nonnegativity. **Theorem 7.2**: EOP_GER ≤ α with the same normalised and post-hoc forms; **Corollary 7.2.1**: ARL_1 ≥ 1/α under every configuration; **Theorem 7.3 / Corollary 7.3.1**: GER(φ*, ξ, t) ≤ t α_t, α_t = α/t gives GER ≤ α|H_0(t)|/K; **Theorem 7.4**: with e-processes, PFA ≤ α (Ville applied to the average, itself an e-process), ARL_1 = ∞ and worst-case GER ≤ α.
- **Section 8 (dependence)**: EOP theorems need each M^(k) to be an e-detector for the global filtration F, which holds if streams are independent or, more generally, if no stream's data up to t carries information on another stream's future; universal fixed-time theorems need nothing. Counterexample 8.2: X^(2)_t = X^(1)_{t+1} with i.i.d. N(0,1) breaks the stopping-time condition. Positive examples 8.3: for a bivariate Gaussian with known diagonal and unknown off-diagonal covariance, M^(k)_t = Σ_{j ≤ t} exp{λ Σ_{s=j}^t X^(k)_s − λ²σ_k²(t − j + 1)/2} is an SR e-detector for F; for a second stream that is N(0,1) or Cauchy(0,1) depending on the sign of the first, the symmetry e-process Λ_{t+1} = Λ_t(1 + h(X_t)) with odd |h| ≤ 1 (from [ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022](ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022.md)) gives SR e-detectors valid for F.
- **Section 9 (simulations)**: Gaussian mean change N(−δ,1) → N(δ,1) with SR e-detector M_{t+1} = exp{−((X − δ)² − (X + δ)²)/2}(M_t + 1); under the global null with K = 1000 and α = 0.001 the naive rule "declare when M^(k)_t ≥ 1/α" has FDR rising towards 1 and PFER to several hundred by t = 200, while e-d-BH keeps FDR ≤ 0.12, e-d-Holm keeps FWER ≤ 0.10 and e-d-Bonferroni (β = 10) keeps PFER ≤ 5 (Figures 2–4). With ξ = 10 in all streams and δ ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2}, all three procedures reach K detections within tens of steps, ordered e-d-Bonferroni ≤ e-d-BH ≤ naive in speed (Figure 5); with 100 of 1000 streams changing at t = 400 and δ = 0.125 the naive rule overshoots to about 140 declarations while e-d-BH and e-d-Bonferroni stay near 100 (Figure 6). Symmetry change (K = 50, N(0,1) → N(1,1) at t = 400, sign e-process of Ramdas et al. 2020) and conformal change from exchangeability (nonconformity score x_n − mean, randomised conformal p-values, [calibrator](../concepts/calibrator.md) f(p) = 1/(2√p), recursion M_{t+1} = f(p_t)(M_t + 1)) show the same pattern; the conformal e-detector peaks and then decays because long post-change stretches look exchangeable again (Figures 7–11). Appendix C: HSIC-based sequential kernelised independence e-detectors (Podkopaev et al. 2023), the hardest case, same qualitative result (Figures 13–14).
- **Appendix B (piggybacking)**: in e-d-BH, existing detections lower the threshold K/(k*α) for the remaining streams, so a second batch of changes (streams 11–20 at t = 500) is fully detected after 247 steps versus 340 for the first batch (streams 1–10 at t = 100), K = 50, α = 0.0002 (Figure 12).
- **Appendix E**: background on e-variables, e-BH with FDR ≤ αk_0/K under arbitrary dependence, e-Bonferroni, and averaging e-values for global null testing.

## Setting and assumptions
- Null for stream k: ξ^(k) = ∞, with pre-change law in a composite, possibly nonparametric, non-i.i.d. class P_0^(k); the post-change class is only used to design the e-processes. Configurations are arbitrary and unknown; results hold uniformly over ξ ∈ N^K [dandapanthulaMultipleTestingMultistream2025, Sec. 2.1].
- Stopping times are for the global filtration F, so they may depend on all streams, on the e-detector values, or on external circumstances; EOP guarantees are uniform over them (anytime-valid), hence [optional stopping](../concepts/optional-stopping.md) and optional continuation of the monitoring are free.
- EOP control requires each M^(k) to be an e-detector for F (independent streams suffice; past-only dependence suffices; "seeing into the future" of another stream breaks it). Universal fixed-time control (Theorems 4.3, 5.4, 6.1, 7.3) requires only E[M^(k)_t] ≤ t under the null at fixed t and holds under any cross-stream dependence.
- Finite ARL and nontrivial worst-case Type I error are mutually exclusive; EOP trades them off, and the PFA/worst-case route (e-processes) gives up finite ARL and hence bounded worst-case delay.
- No power, delay or computational theory: the paper is deliberately agnostic to the per-stream e-detectors, in the way BH is agnostic to how its p-values arise (Sec. 1); delay is studied only empirically.

## Concepts touched
- [e-detector](../concepts/e-detector.md): multi-stream use; the ARL-versus-Type-I impossibility (Thm 4.1, 5.1, Props 5.2, 7.1); EOP metrics (Defs 4.1, 5.1, 5.2, 7.3); e-d-BH, e-d-Bonferroni, e-d-Holm, e-d-GNT with EOP, universal and PFA guarantees (Thms 4.2–4.4, 5.3–5.5, 6.1, 7.2–7.4); the global-filtration condition and dependence examples (Sec. 8); adaptive thresholds c_α (App. A); piggybacking (App. B).
- [e-bh](../concepts/e-bh.md): e-BH applied to e-detector values controls FDR/E[τ] rather than FDR; the same proof gives universal fixed-time FDR ≤ tα_t|H_0(t)|/K and, with e-processes, worst-case FDR ≤ α plus PFA ≤ α (Thms 4.2–4.4); e-d-Holm as an e-value Holm step-down with FWER ≤ tα_t (Thm 6.1).
- [e-process](../concepts/e-process.md): j-delay e-processes and SR e-processes with summable weights (Sec. 2.2); e-processes as the inputs that yield PFA and worst-case control (Thms 4.4, 5.5, 7.4).
- [e-merging](../concepts/e-merging.md): e-d-GNT sums e-detectors across streams, the arithmetic-mean merger at the e-detector level (Alg. 4, Thm 7.2, App. E).
- [post-hoc-validity](../concepts/post-hoc-validity.md): the EOP bounds hold in the form sup_ξ sup_τ E[sup_α FDP_α(τ)/α] / ((Σ_k E[τ ∧ (ξ^(k) − 1)]) ∨ 1) ≤ 1/K, i.e. uniformly over a data-dependent α (and β for e-d-Bonferroni) (remarks after Thms 4.2, 5.3, 7.2).
- [villes-inequality](../concepts/villes-inequality.md): PFA bounds for the e-process variants (Thms 4.4, 5.5, 7.4) and the uniform-in-time bound on each e-process (Sec. 2.2).
- [calibrator](../concepts/calibrator.md): p-to-e calibrator f(p) = κp^{κ−1}, κ = 1/2, turning conformal p-values into e-processes and the conformal SR recursion (Sec. 9.3).
- [optional-stopping](../concepts/optional-stopping.md): EOP is a supremum over all global stopping times, including data- and externally-driven ones; the proofs use the e-detector property at τ ∧ (ξ^(k) − 1).

## Open questions / gaps
- No detection-delay theory for any of the four procedures; delay is examined only empirically, and the authors note that delay statements require a concrete per-stream problem (Sec. 1) [dandapanthulaMultipleTestingMultistream2025].
- Whether e-d-Holm controls EOP_FWER is left open; only universal fixed-time FWER control is proved (Sec. 6).
- The ARL lower bound 1/α with an adaptive threshold c_α < 1/α (Appendix A) is stated as an empirical finding, not a theorem.
- Piggybacking (Appendix B) is observed, not analysed; whether it is desirable or should be corrected is not discussed.
- Minor inconsistencies in v4: the second claim of Corollary 5.3.1 reads ARL_1(φ, ξ) ≥ 1/α although the procedure's parameter is β; Theorem 7.4 is titled "controls the worst-case PFER" but is about GER; A later version may fix these.
- Multi-stream e-detectors from Shin et al.'s suggested min/average rules, computational schemes for large K, and changes with unspecified pre- and post-change classes in many streams (the non-partitioned setting of Saha and Ramdas) are not treated.
- Cited but not in the bib: Vovk (2021) on conformal testing of randomness, Podkopaev et al. (2023) on sequential kernelised independence testing, Chen, Zhang and Poor (2020) on FDR-oriented parallel change detection, Mei (2010) and Chan (2017) on multi-stream delay optimality.
