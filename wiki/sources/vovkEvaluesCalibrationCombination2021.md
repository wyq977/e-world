# E-values: calibration, combination, and applications

Annals of Statistics 49(3), 2021. First arXiv version December 2019. The text layer read here is arXiv v4 (September 2020), which announces itself as the version to appear in the Annals; section and equation numbers below follow v4 (main Sections 1-8, Appendices A-I).

## Summary
The paper proposes e-values as the natural competitor of p-values for multiple testing of a single hypothesis (merging) and for testing multiple hypotheses. An e-variable is an extended random variable E: Omega -> [0, infinity] with E_Q[E] <= 1 under the null Q; an e-value is a realised value of an e-variable, read as the payoff of a one-dollar bet against Q, so that Q(E >= c) <= 1/c by Markov [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021, Section 2]. Section 2 characterises p-to-e calibrators (decreasing f with integral at most 1 over [0,1]; admissible iff upper semicontinuous, f(0) = infinity and integral exactly 1) and shows that e-to-p calibration has essentially one admissible solution, e -> min(1, 1/e). Sections 3-4 give the central combination results: under arbitrary dependence the symmetric e-merging functions form the complete class {lambda + (1 - lambda) M_K}, with the arithmetic mean M_K essentially dominating every symmetric merger (Theorem 3.2, Proposition 3.1); for independent or merely "sequential" e-values the product is admissible and weakly dominates every ie-merging function, and the U-statistics of the e-values and their convex mixtures are all admissible (Propositions 4.1-4.2). Section 5 turns averaging and multiplication into O(K^2) and O(K) family-wise valid adjustment procedures for K hypotheses (Algorithms 1-2, Theorem 5.1), which are the closure principle applied to e-merging. Section 6 re-derives Rueger's and the M-family of p-merging functions as "precise but inadmissible" e-mergers via the rough correspondence 1/e ~ p, and Section 7 reports Gaussian experiments in which the product e-value beats the Vovk-Sellke bound of Fisher's method and Algorithm 1 almost attains the Vovk-Sellke bound of closed Simes/Bonferroni while producing bona fide e-values [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].

## Main results

### Definitions and calibration (Section 2)
- **e-variable / e-value / p-variable**: E: Omega -> [0, infinity] with E_Q[E] <= 1 (set E_Q); E = infinity for a pre-chosen E entitles rejection of Q. P-variable: Q(P <= eps) <= eps for all eps in (0,1) (set P_Q). The emphasis is that e-values are defined through expectations, p-values through probabilities [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021, Section 2].
- **Calibrator (p-to-e)**: a decreasing f: [0,1] -> [0, infinity] with f(P) in E_Q for every p-variable P on every probability space. **Proposition 2.1**: f is a calibrator iff integral_0^1 f <= 1; admissible (not strictly dominated) iff f is upper semicontinuous, f(0) = infinity and integral_0^1 f = 1. The domination structure of calibrators is "very rich" [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Calibrator families**: f_kappa(p) = kappa p^(kappa - 1), kappa in (0,1) (eq. 1), the class of Vovk 1993 rediscovered by Sellke, Bayarri and Berger 2001; the mixture F(p) = integral_0^1 f_kappa dkappa = (1 - p + p ln p) / (p (-ln p)^2) (eq. 2, credited to Ramdas), with F(p) ~ p^(-1) (-ln p)^(-2) as p -> 0, closer to the unattainable 1/p than any f_kappa; the Shafer et al. 2011 family H_kappa(p) = kappa (1 + kappa)^kappa p^(-1) (-ln p)^(-1-kappa) on p <= exp(-1 - kappa) (eq. B.1); and the incomplete-gamma generalisation F_kappa(p) = kappa gamma(1 + kappa, -ln p) / (p (-ln p)^(1 + kappa)) (Appendix B). The **VS bound** VS(p) = max_kappa f_kappa(p) = -exp(-1)/(p ln p) for p <= exp(-1) is explicitly not a valid e-value, only an overoptimistic upper bound on the class (1) [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021, Section 2, Appendix B].
- **Proposition 2.2 (e-to-p calibration)**: f(t) = min(1, 1/t) is an e-to-p calibrator (Markov), dominates every other e-to-p calibrator, and is therefore the only admissible one. **Remark 2.3**: the rough relation 1/e ~ p is exact in the e-to-p direction and approximate in the p-to-e direction; f(p) <= 1/p for every calibrator, with equality only for f = 1_[0,p]/p [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].

### Merging arbitrarily dependent e-values (Section 3, Appendix E)
- **e-merging function**: an increasing Borel F: [0, infinity]^K -> [0, infinity] with F(E_1, ..., E_K) in E_Q whenever E_1, ..., E_K in E_Q, for every probability space, no dependence assumption (eq. 3). Domination F >= G; admissibility = maximality. **Proposition E.5**: every e-merging function is dominated by an admissible one [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Proposition 3.1**: the arithmetic mean M_K essentially dominates every symmetric e-merging function F, i.e. F(e) > 1 implies M_K(e) >= F(e); hence any symmetric positively homogeneous F is dominated outright by M_K. Proof by randomly permuting the inputs and thinning with an event of probability 1/a [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Theorem 3.2 (complete class)**: every symmetric e-merging function is dominated by lambda + (1 - lambda) M_K for some lambda in [0,1]; F is admissible iff F = lambda + (1 - lambda) M_K with lambda = F(0). The family (6) is the minimal complete class of symmetric e-merging functions, and all its members with lambda != 1 carry the same statistical information. Proof via Propositions E.1-E.6 (upper semicontinuous and increasing versions, Rieder's measurable-selection theorem) [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021, Appendix E].
- **Appendix D**: the universal quantifier over probability spaces can be replaced by a single atomless space or any "rich" statistical model (Propositions D.3, D.5, D.6); without richness the maximum can be a merger (Remark D.4) [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Appendix C**: infinite e-values can be disposed of when discussing merging (Proposition C.1) [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].

### Merging independent and sequential e-values (Section 4)
- **ie-merging function**: as above but only required for independent E_1, ..., E_K. **Proposition 4.1**: any increasing Borel F with E[F(E)] = 1 for all E with E[E] = 1 (independent components, respectively) is an admissible (i)e-merging function; this gives admissibility of M_K, of the product e_1 ... e_K (eq. 7, Fisher's method's analogue, the capital after K successive full-stake bets) and of all U-statistics U_n (eq. 8) and their convex mixtures [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Proposition 4.2**: the product weakly dominates every ie-merging function: F(e) <= e_1 ... e_K whenever all e_k >= 1 (proof by two-point e-variables taking e_k with probability 1/e_k). **Remark 4.3**: the convex mixtures of U-statistics are not a complete class; (1/2)(e_1/(1+e_1) + e_2/(1+e_2))(1 + e_1 e_2) is admissible and outside it [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Sequential e-values and test supermartingales** (Section 4, "Testing with martingales"): E_1, ..., E_K are sequential if E[E_k | E_1, ..., E_(k-1)] <= 1 a.s.; equivalently the partial products form a test supermartingale (test martingale if equality). Convex mixtures of U-statistics, in particular the product, remain valid for sequential e-values ("se-merging"); sequential in any order suffices [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Appendix F (maximin view)**: for fixed e, sup over symmetric mergers of F(e) subject to validity equals M_K(e) or 1 under arbitrary dependence and prod_k (e_k or 1) under independence; the minimax counterpart (Neyman-Pearson argument) coincides, giving alternative proofs of Propositions 3.1 and 4.2 but not of the full complete-class Theorem 3.2 [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].

### Testing multiple hypotheses (Section 5, Appendix A.3)
- **Family-wise validity (FWV)**: adjusted E*_1, ..., E*_K are FWV for H_1, ..., H_K if there is a conditional e-variable (E_Q)_{Q in P(Omega)}, E_Q[E_Q] <= 1, with E_Q >= E*_k for all Q in H_k (eq. 9); equivalently E_Q[max_{k: Q in H_k} E*_k] <= 1 for all Q. Via e-to-p calibration this is the e-analogue of FWER control (eq. A.2-A.3), and calibrators in either direction preserve FWV (Remark A.1) [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Algorithm 1 (averaging)**: E*_k = min over I containing k of (1/|I|) sum_{i in I} E_i (eq. 13), computed in O(K^2) from the order statistics as min_{i < k} (e_pi(k) + S_i)/(i+1). Witness E_Q = mean of {E_k : Q in H_k} (eq. 11). Valid under arbitrary dependence; "free combinations" assumed, so logical relations among the H_k (Shaffer 1986) could be exploited further (Remark 5.2) [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Algorithm 2 (product, sequential)**: E*_k = min over I containing k of prod_{i in I} E_i (eq. 14) = e_k times the product of all e_i < 1; O(K), needs no sorting; requires the base e-variables of the true nulls to be sequential under every Q [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Theorem 5.1**: Algorithms 1 and 2 are family-wise valid. Both are the closure principle (Marcus, Peritz and Gabriel 1976; Goeman and Solari 2011) applied to the e-merging functions M_K and product; the closure of a symmetric merger f gives E*_k = min_{I ni k} f(E_i, i in I) (Appendix A.3). Allowing non-singleton J in the closure gives e-value FDP control, pursued in Vovk and Wang 2019b/2020a ("true and false discoveries with e-values") [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021]. The paper does not contain an e-BH procedure.

### p-merging and cross-merging (Section 6, Appendix G)
- **Section 6**: Rueger's family (K/k) p_(k) and the M-family a_{r,K} M_{r,K} (Vovk and Wang 2020, Biometrika) are the two natural p-merging families under arbitrary dependence; both start at Bonferroni and end at the maximum. **Proposition 6.1**: Bonferroni is admissible; the maximum is not. Their e-analogues (k/K) e_[k] and (K^(1/r - 1) and 1) M_{r,K} are "precise" (no constant c > 1 keeps them valid) but inadmissible unless r = 1, since all are dominated by M_K (eq. 18-19); the e-counterpart of Simes, max_k (k/K) e_[k], is valid without independence but pointless. Remark 6.2 explains the coefficient r/(r+1) K^(1 + 1/r) for p-averaging as the imprecision of 1/p ~ e [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Proposition G.1**: K/(e_1 + ... + e_K) and 1 (average, then invert) dominates every symmetric e-to-p merging function; it coincides with Wilson's harmonic-mean p-value applied to 1/e_k, which is invalid as a p-merger. **Proposition G.2**: calibrate then average, (1/K) sum g(p_k) for an admissible calibrator g, is an admissible p-to-e merging function; any p-to-e merger is dominated by a convex combination sum lambda_k f_k(p_k) (Vovk, Wang and Wang 2020, Thm 4.1). Order matters: e-to-p = merge then calibrate; p-to-e = calibrate then merge. For sequential e-values, Ville's theorem gives the se-to-p merger min_k 1/(e_1 ... e_k) [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021, Appendix G].

### Bayes factors and history (Appendices A.1-A.2, A.4, B)
- A Bayes factor is an e-variable when the null is simple; for a composite null with prior mu_0 it satisfies the e-constraint only on average over mu_0, so a Bayes factor need not be an e-value for the composite null (eq. A.1). Jeffreys' scale (1961) is adopted for e-values: sqrt(10) ~ 3.16 "substantial", 10 "strong", 31.6 "very strong", 100 "decisive" (Section 7). In Efron's two-groups model the local fdr equals pi_0/(pi_0 + pi_1 e) with e the likelihood ratio, and fdr <= 0.2 means e >= 4 pi_0/pi_1, which is >= 36 for the common case pi_0 >= 0.9 (the text prints "pi_0 >= 0.1", evidently a typo) [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021, Appendix A].
- Algorithmic randomness: Martin-Loef 1966 (p-values) and Levin 1976 (e-values) as the ancestors; calibration of p-values into Bayes factors raised by Berger and Delampady 1987, class (1) in Vovk 1993, full characterisation in Shafer et al. 2011 [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021, Appendices A.2, B].

### Experiments (Section 7, Appendix H)
- N(mu, 1) data, H0: mu = 0, H1: mu = delta = -0.1, 10,000 i.i.d. observations, medians over 100 seeds. Product of likelihood-ratio e-values (eq. 21) lies below 1/p for Fisher's method for about the first 2000 observations and then catches up; it is above the VS bound of Fisher throughout, hence above any calibrated Fisher e-value. The "universal" mixture test martingale S_K = (K+1)^(-1/2) exp((sum x_k)^2 / (2(K+1))) (eq. 23, delta ~ N(0,1)) catches up with Fisher by the end and overtakes it afterwards. Averaging, Simes and Bonferroni have almost no power here (Figure 2) [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- Multiple testing, 20 hypotheses (10 false with mu = -4), base e-values the mixture likelihood ratio (1/2) exp(x delta - delta^2/2) + 1/2 (eq. 24): Algorithm 1 (average) produces better e-values than calibrating closed Simes or Holm/Bonferroni, and with 200 hypotheses (Figure 4) it is barely distinguishable from the VS bound of those closures, the "very good result" of the paper; closed Fisher (via Dobriban's FACT, Appendix I) does not scale; Algorithm 2 (product) is poor for many hypotheses. True nulls receive e-values below 1 under averaging, which the authors read as the price of validity [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- Appendix H: when only the first half of 10,000 observations is from the alternative, the product with the correct mixture likelihood ratio ends near Fisher and above Fisher-VS, while the product with the wrong (pure-alternative) likelihood ratio loses all accumulated evidence and ends near 1; the universal martingale is robust [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].

## Setting and assumptions
- Simple null Q on an arbitrary probability space for Sections 2-4; composite nulls H subset of P(Omega) from Section 5 on, with E an e-variable for H iff E_Q[E] <= 1 for every Q in H. No alternative is needed for validity; alternatives enter only in the experiments (known delta, or a mixture over delta) [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- Merging under arbitrary dependence requires validity on every probability space (equivalently one atomless space, Appendix D); ie-merging assumes independence; se-merging assumes the weaker sequential (supermartingale) condition. Algorithm 1 needs nothing about dependence; Algorithm 2 needs the true-null e-variables to be sequential in some order under every Q [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- Multiple testing is in the "free combinations" setting (all intersections (10) may be non-empty); fixed K, no optional stopping or continuation analysis beyond the remark that the product line in Figure 1 is a test (super)martingale trajectory [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- Experiments assume the alternative is known exactly; the authors stress that getting mu wrong hurts e-value methods, which "require more detailed knowledge" than p-value methods (Section 7, Appendix H) [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].

## Open questions / gaps
- Complete class of ie-merging functions: the convex mixtures of U-statistics are admissible but not complete (Remark 4.3); the full domination structure under independence is left to Vovk and Wang 2020b ("A class of ie-merging functions") and is open here.
- Non-symmetric e-merging under arbitrary dependence: Theorem 3.2 assumes symmetry; the paper does not characterise asymmetric admissible mergers (later settled as weighted means by Wang 2025, per ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025).
- FDP/FDR control by closing e-merging over non-singleton index sets is flagged as "an interesting program of research" (Appendix A.3), with first steps in Vovk and Wang 2019b/2020a; e-BH itself is not in this paper.
- Exploiting logical relations between hypotheses (Shaffer-type improvements of Algorithms 1-2 when some intersections (10) are empty) is noted but not developed (Remark 5.2).
- How to choose e-variables without exact knowledge of the alternative: the experiments show total loss of evidence under a misspecified likelihood ratio (Appendix H) and recommend mixtures, but give no theory.
- Whether the maximin/minimax approach of Appendix F can be pushed to recover Theorem 3.2 is left open.
- Works cited here but not in the bib (record in wiki/gaps.md): Shafer, Shen, Vereshchagin and Vovk 2011; Vovk 1993; Sellke, Bayarri and Berger 2001; Vovk and Wang 2020 (Biometrika p-averaging); Vovk, Wang and Wang 2022 (admissible p-merging); Vovk and Wang 2019b/2020a (true and false discoveries with e-values); Vovk and Wang 2020b (ie-merging class); Duan, Ramdas, Balakrishnan and Wasserman 2019 (interactive martingale tests); Dobriban 2020 (FACT); Levin 1976; Berger and Delampady 1987; Goeman and Solari 2011; Wilson 2019 and Goeman, Rosenblatt and Nichols 2019 (harmonic mean p-value).
