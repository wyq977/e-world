# Anytime-valid testing with e-values and confirmatory adaptive designs

University of Bremen. A short bridge-building note: the authors say explicitly that they "do not claim to derive substantially new methodology" but to expose the unexplored connections between the e-value literature and the thirty-year-old literature on confirmatory adaptive designs for clinical trials [brannathAnytimevalidTestingEvalues2026a, Sec. 1]. The one genuinely new construction is in Appendix A.

## Summary
Confirmatory adaptive designs (Bauer 1989, Bauer and Köhne 1994, Proschan and Hunsberger 1995, Müller and Schäfer 2001/2004) let a clinical trial change its second-stage design at an interim analysis without a prespecified adaptation rule, controlling the type I error through a prespecified conditional error function A or a combination test on stage-wise p-values. The paper shows that these tools are formally equivalent to anytime-valid tests built from sequential e-values: a two-stage design with conditional error function A and decision ψ is the pair of sequential e-values E_1 = A/α, E_2 = ψ/A, and conversely A = αE_1, ψ = αE_1E_2 [brannathAnytimevalidTestingEvalues2026a, eq. 5]. For multi-stage designs a sequence of conditional error functions (A_t) satisfying E_P[A_1] ≤ α and E_P[A_t | A_1, …, A_{t−1}] ≤ A_{t−1} is a nonnegative supermartingale, and every anytime-valid e-value test is such a design with A_t = min(αM_t, 1) [eq. 6, Sec. 2.2]. Recursive combination tests (Brannath, Posch and Bauer 2002) are the same thing expressed through p-values satisfying the sequential "p-clud" condition, and partial conditional error functions for adaptive graphical closed tests are weighted-average merged e-values. The methodological difference is one of emphasis: adaptive designs are built to exhaust the level, with E_1E_2 ∈ {0, 1/α}, while e-value tests deliberately leave E_1E_2 unrestricted so as to permit optional continuation, data-dependent α and post-hoc losses, and composite nulls without a least favourable configuration. Appendix A shows that the price of this flexibility is strict conservatism (Remark 5) and that any e-value test with known continuous least-favourable conditional null distributions can be uniformly improved by a recursive combination test that exhausts the level stage by stage.

## Main results
- **Sequential e-values and the test supermartingale (Sec. 1, eqs 1–2).** Statistics E_1, E_2, … with E_P[E_t | E_1, …, E_{t−1}] ≤ 1 for all P ∈ H_0 ("sequential e-values" in Vovk's sense); M_t = ∏ E_i is a test supermartingale, rejection when M_t ≥ 1/α is level α by Ville's inequality, and every stopped M_τ is an e-value by optional stopping. Example 1: i.i.d. likelihood ratios give the SPRT without a lower boundary; the null density may be the maximum over a composite null and the alternative a maximum over a training sample (universal inference). Remark 1: tests that need one supermartingale M^P per null P and a process dominated by all of them (e-processes) are set aside because "in most testing problems that are relevant for clinical trials" a single sequential e-value per stage suffices.
- **Conditional error function principle (eqs 3–4).** Prespecify A ∈ [0, 1] on the interim data with E_P[A] ≤ α; choose any decision ψ ∈ {0, 1} with E_P[ψ | A] ≤ A for all P ∈ H_0; then E_P[ψ] ≤ E_P[A] ≤ α. ψ need be specified only at the observed interim point, which is what permits unforeseen design changes; A = 1 means stop and reject, A = 0 stop and accept. Remark 2: one may use a different A^P, ψ^P per null P and reject if ψ^P = 1 for all P (the analogue of Remark 1). Example 2: Fisher's product test, A = 1 for P_1 ≤ α_1 and A = min(1, c/P_1) otherwise. Example 3: the conditional rejection probability principle A^P = E_P[φ | D] for any prespecified fixed-sample or group-sequential level-α test φ, identified with Koning and van Meer's induced tests.
- **Two-stage equivalence (Sec. 2.1, eq. 5).** E_1 = A/α and E_2 = ψ/A are sequential e-values with E_1E_2 ∈ {0, 1/α}, so the e-value test rejects iff the adaptive design does. Conversely A = αE_1 and ψ = αE_1E_2 satisfy (3)–(4); A > 1 is avoided by bounding E_1 by 1/α, and bounding E_2 by 1/(αE_1) makes ψ ∈ [0, 1] a randomised test (Koning's continuous testing), non-randomised iff E_2 ∈ {0, 1/(αE_1)}. Three reasons for not restricting E_1E_2 to {0, 1/α}: (1) optional continuation by multiplying further e-values later; (2) data-dependent α and losses, justified by E_P[sup_α L_α] = E_P[E_1E_2] ≤ 1 with L_α = (1/α)1{E_1E_2 ≥ 1/α}; (3) composite nulls without a least favourable configuration, where the level cannot be exhausted by either approach.
- **Multi-stage equivalence (Sec. 2.2, eq. 6).** A sequence of conditional error functions A_t ∈ [0, 1] on the data D_t with E_P[A_1] ≤ α and E_P[A_t | A_1, …, A_{t−1}] ≤ A_{t−1} is a nonnegative supermartingale with E[A_1] ≤ α, so rejecting when A_t = 1 is level α by Ville's inequality or optional stopping; the design of stage t + 1 and A_{t+1} must be fixed at stage t. The sequential e-values are E_1 = A_1/α, E_t = A_t/A_{t−1}, so M_t = A_t/α. Conversely, for any sequential e-values, A_t := min(αM_t, 1) satisfies (6) since E_P[A_t | past] ≤ min(αE_P[M_t | past], 1) ≤ min(αM_{t−1}, 1) = A_{t−1}, and A_t = 1 iff αM_t ≥ 1. Remark 4: per-null A^P_t with rejection iff all equal 1.
- **Combination tests (Sec. 3.1, eqs 7–9).** First-stage conservative p-value P_1, early boundaries α_1 < α < α_0, combination function C(u, v) non-decreasing in u, increasing in v, continuous, critical value c with α_1 + ∫_0^1 ∫_{α_1}^{α_0} 1{C(u, v) ≤ c} du dv = α (eq. 8); second-stage p-value with the p-clud property P[P_2 ≤ u | P_1] ≤ u (eq. 9). Rejecting when C(P_1, P_2) ≤ c is level α and exhausts it if some P_0 makes both p-values exactly uniform. Conditional error function A(P_1) = sup{v : C(P_1, v) ≤ c} on (α_1, α_0], 1 below α_1, 0 above α_0, with E_P[A(P_1)] ≤ α_1 + ∫ A(u) du = α. The overall p-value Q(P_1, P_2) is valid, exactly uniform when the stage-wise p-values are, equals P_1 on early stopping, and Q ≤ α iff the test rejects; Q reveals the stopping stage since Q ∈ [0, α_1] ∪ (α_0, 1] at stage 1 and (α_1, α_0] otherwise.
- **Recursive combination tests (Sec. 3.2, eq. 10).** The second argument of C_1 is a placeholder for all future evidence, filled by the p-value Q_2 of another combination test run at level A_1(P_1), and so on; at each stage one chooses, from all internal and external information, whether to finish with the next stage or continue with at least two more, together with the next design, test and combination function. Type I error is controlled under the sequential p-clud condition P[P_t ≤ u | P_1, …, P_{t−1}] ≤ u (eq. 10); the resulting (A_t) satisfy (6), with equality throughout when the P_t are i.i.d. uniform. Conversely a sequence (A_t) with A_t = A_t(P_t) a function of a p-clud p-value is a recursive combination test with C_t(P_t, v) = v/A_t and c_t = 1; A_t can be so written whenever it has a known least favourable conditional survival function S_t, via P_t = S_t(A_t).
- **Multiple testing (Sec. 4, eq. 11).** For the consonant weighted Bonferroni closed test of Hommel, Bretz and Maurer 2007 behind the graphical approach of Bretz et al. 2009, the adaptive extension of Klinglmüller, Posch and Koenig 2014 applies the conditional rejection probability principle to each marginal test φ^I_i = 1{P_i ≤ w^I_i α}, giving partial conditional error functions A^I_i = E_P[φ^I_i | D] (Posch and Futschik 2008) with Σ_{i ∈ I} E_P[A^I_i] ≤ α; A^I = min(Σ_i A^I_i, 1) is a conditional error function for H^I_0 that permits dropping hypotheses and reweighting; A^I = 1 at the interim with every A^I_i < 1 is a costless uniform improvement of the single-stage weighted Bonferroni closed test. Any marginal conditional error functions with E_P[A^I_i] ≤ αw^I_i (eq. 11) will do. Equivalently E^I_i = A^I_i/(αw^I_i) are e-values and E^I = Σ_i w^I_i E^I_i their weighted average, the standard merge of Vovk and Wang; E^I ≥ 1/α iff A^I = 1, so the e-closed procedure rejects the same hypotheses. Hartog and Lei's shortcuts are the case E^I_i = E_i. Wang's theorem that weighted averaging is the only admissible dependence-free merge therefore also governs the combination of conditional error functions.
- **Discussion (Sec. 5).** For clinical trials from the e-value side: post-hoc choice of loss and level (Grünwald 2024, Koning 2023), impossible with conditional error functions since α must be prespecified, motivated by two treatments with different safety profiles deserving different bars; log-optimality as a principled choice among the many sequential tests that differ only by stage weighting, with the caveat that the criterion presumes a potentially infinite number of stages and "other criteria may be more appropriate" when stages are few; the e-closure principle of Xu et al. for any expectation-based error metric chosen post hoc, including weighted and population-wise error rates; universal inference for irregular models where no p-value is computable and no adaptive design exists; betting interpretation, continuous evidence, reporting an e-value and multiplying across future experiments for meta-analysis. From the adaptive-design side: a conditional error function is a level for future tests, directly usable in conditional power and sample-size calculations, obtainable by the conditional rejection principle, and exhausts the level when (3)–(4) hold with equality; combination tests work with standard p-values, supply an overall p-value, and exhaust the level by design when the p-values are exact, which e-value tests do not.
- **Appendix A (exhausting e-value tests).** 1/E is a strictly conservative p-value, with strict inequality on an interval when E is continuous; with a known least favourable P_0 and S_t(u) := P_0(1/E_t ≤ u | E_1, …, E_{t−1}), the p-values P_t = S_t(1/E_t) satisfy the sequential p-clud property (10) and P_0[P_t < 1/E_t | past] > 0 when S_t is continuous. The two-stage e-value test equals the combination test with α_{1,1} = S_1(α), C_1(P_1, P_2) = P_2/E_1, c_1 = α and A_1 = min(αE_1, 1); when P_0[αE_1 ≥ 1] > 0, E_0[min(αE_1, 1)] = S_1(α) + E_0[min(αE_1, 1)1{P_1 > S_1(α)}] < α (eq. 12), because α ≥ E_0[αE_1] = E_0[min(αE_1, 1)] + E_0[(αE_1 − 1)1{αE_1 > 1}] with the last term positive. So the equivalent combination test is conservative even with exact uniform p-values and can be uniformly improved by raising α_{1,1} and/or c_1; at stage t ≥ 2 the e-value test is the combination test with α_{t,1} = S_t(αM_{t−1}), C_t(P_t, u) = S_t^{−1}(P_t)·u, c_t = αM_{t−1}, improved likewise, and the final stage uses P_t = S_t(1/E_t). Remark 5: a two-e-value test with a positive chance to reject at stage 1 is inevitably conservative whenever P_0[E_1 ≥ 1/α_1] > 0 for some α_1 > α, continuous or not, so "flexibility with respect to significance levels necessarily comes for the price of a strict conservatism".
- **Appendix A.1 (the exhausting critical values).** Under positive conditional Lebesgue densities, a known least favourable P_0 attaining the maxima in S_t, and w.l.o.g. E_0[E_t | past] = 1 (otherwise divide by the conditional expectation to get a uniformly larger e-value): raise c_1 to c_1^max = max{v : S_1(α) + E_0[min(vE_1, 1)1{P_1 > S_1(α)}] ≤ α}, which exhausts the level if the expression at v = 1 reaches α (and then simply reporting the combination test's p-value Q_1 achieves it); otherwise take c_1 = 1 and raise α_{1,1} to the α^min_{1,1} solving α_{1,1} + E_0[min(E_1, 1)1{P_1 > α_{1,1}}] = α (eq. 13). Any c_1 between c_1^min = sup{u : E_0[min(αE, 1)] ≤ u} and c_1^max, with α_{1,1} solved accordingly, also works. Fischer and Ramdas's uniform improvement of Wald's SPRT (IEEE TIT 2026) is the choice α_{1,1} = S_1(α̃), c_1 = α̃ for the α̃ > α solving E[min(α̃E_1, 1)] = α.

## Setting and assumptions
- Null: arbitrary composite H_0; the main text uses one sequential e-value per stage valid for all P ∈ H_0 (equivalently one conditional error function), with the per-null e-process version relegated to Remarks 1–4.
- Data: a possibly infinite stream processed in stages; each E_t or A_t may depend on all data and external information up to stage t − 1 and on the design chosen then. The number of stages may be data-driven and unbounded.
- Optional stopping and continuation are allowed throughout; the conditional error function formulation requires the next stage's design and A_{t+1} to be fixed at stage t.
- Exhaustion results (Sec. 3, App. A) need a least favourable null P_0 under which the stage-wise p-values are exactly uniform, and App. A.1 further needs continuous conditional null distributions of the e-values.
- No optimality criterion is used beyond the discussion of log-optimality; no data examples.

## Open questions / gaps
- Whether exhausting the level is worth losing optional continuation and post-hoc α is left to the user; the paper says the two emphases are complementary and "the first cannot be achieved without violating the second".
- The e-process version of the equivalence (Remarks 1–4) is only sketched.
- Optimality criteria for designs with few stages, where log-optimality's infinite-stage premise fails, are not proposed.
- Brannath, Posch and Bauer 2002 on recursive combination tests and Fischer and Ramdas 2026 on the SPRT overshoot are the primary documents for Sec. 3.2 and App. A.1 and are not sources.
