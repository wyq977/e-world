# Derandomized knockoffs: leveraging e-values for false discovery rate control

## Summary
The model-X knockoff filter selects variables by comparing feature-importance statistics W_j with those of synthetic knockoff copies, and because the knockoffs are random, repeated runs on the same data give different selected sets; the earlier fix of Ren, Wei and Candès 2021 (threshold the selection frequency over M runs) bounds the expected number of false discoveries but not the FDR [renDerandomizedKnockoffsLeveraging2022]. The paper's first result is that the knockoff filter is literally an e-BH procedure: with e_j = p·1{W_j ≥ T}/(1 + Σ_k 1{W_k ≤ −T}) and T the knockoff threshold, e-BH at level α returns exactly the knockoff set (Theorem 1). These e_j are not e-values individually but satisfy the relaxed condition Σ_{j∈H_0} E[e_j] ≤ p (eq. 9), which is all that the e-BH FDR proof uses (Theorem 2, from Wang and Ramdas) and which the authors call "relaxed e-values" (the compound e-values of later work). Because the relaxed condition is linear, averaging the e-values over M independent knockoff draws keeps it, and e-BH on the averaged e-values e_j^avg controls FDR at α_ebh for every M and every inner level α_kn, with no assumptions beyond those of knockoffs themselves (Theorem 3, Algorithm 1). As M → ∞ the averaged e-values converge to E[e_j^(1) | X, Y] and the selected set becomes a deterministic function of the data, with a Hoeffding bound on how many runs are needed (Section 3.3). Simulations (n = 1000, p = 800 or 600) and an HIV drug-resistance study (n = 1840, p = 219, M = 100) show comparable power to a single knockoff run, lower realized FDR, and conditional selection variability close to zero; the recommended defaults are α_kn = α_ebh/2 and offset c = 1 [renDerandomizedKnockoffsLeveraging2022].

## Main results
- Setup (Section 1.1–1.2): H_j : Y ⊥ X_j | X_{−j} for j ∈ [p] in the model-X framework (P_X known, Y | X arbitrary). Knockoffs X̃ satisfy the swap-exchangeability (4); W = W([X, X̃], Y) flips sign under swapping; T = inf{t > 0 : (1 + Σ_j 1{W_j ≤ −t})/Σ_j 1{W_j ≥ t} ≤ α} and S_kn = {j : W_j ≥ T} (5); Barber and Candès 2015 and Candès et al. 2018 give FDR ≤ α.
- Knockoff e-values (eq. 7): e_j := p·1{W_j ≥ T}/(1 + Σ_{k∈[p]} 1{W_k ≤ −T}).
- Theorem 1 (knockoffs = e-BH): e-BH (6) at level α applied to e_1, …, e_p returns exactly S_kn. Proof: for j ∈ S_kn, e_j ≥ p/(α|S_kn|) by definition of T, so k̂ ≥ |S_kn|; for j ∉ S_kn, e_j = 0.
- Relaxed e-values (Section 2.2, eqs 8–9): the knockoff bound E[Σ_{j∈H_0} 1{W_j ≥ T}/(1 + Σ_{j∈H_0} 1{W_j ≤ −T})] ≤ 1 gives Σ_{j∈H_0} E[e_j] ≤ p, strictly weaker than E[e_j] ≤ 1 per null. Theorem 2 (Wang and Ramdas 2022): e-BH on any e_1, …, e_p satisfying (9) has FDR ≤ α (they are weighted e-values e_j = E[e_j]·ē_j with weights summing to at most p over the nulls). From then on "e-value" means (9).
- Remark 1 (sharpness): e-BH's proof uses 1{e_j ≥ p/(α|S_ebh|)} ≤ e_j α|S_ebh|/p, tight when e_j ∈ {0, p/(α|S_ebh|)}; knockoff e-values are 0 or p/(1 + Σ 1{W_j ≤ −T}) ≥ p/(α|S_kn|), and by (12) the ratio is < p/(α|S_kn|) just below T, so when Σ_j 1{W_j ≤ −T} is reasonably large the nonzero e-values are close to p/(α|S_ebh|) and the inequality is close to tight.
- Algorithm 1 (derandomized knockoffs): draw X̃^(1), …, X̃^(M) i.i.d. given (X, Y); for each m compute W^(m), the threshold T^(m) at inner level α_kn (eq. 15), and e_j^(m) = p·1{W_j^(m) ≥ T^(m)}/(1 + Σ_k 1{W_k^(m) ≤ −T^(m)}) (14); average e_j^avg = M^{−1} Σ_m e_j^(m); output e-BH at level α_ebh: k̂ = max{k : e_(k)^avg ≥ p/(α_ebh k)}, S_kn–derand = {j : e_j^avg ≥ p/(α_ebh k̂)}.
- Theorem 3 (FDR control): for any α_kn, α_ebh ∈ (0,1) and any M ≥ 1, FDR(S_kn–derand) ≤ α_ebh. Proof: (8) with the m-th copy and level α_kn gives Σ_{j∈H_0} E[e_j^(m)] ≤ p for each m; averaging preserves it; apply Theorem 2.
- Remark 2: with α_kn = α_ebh = α and M = 1 the procedure is the original knockoff filter at level α, and for M = 1 α_kn = α_ebh is the optimal inner level (α_kn < α_ebh can only shrink the support of the e-values; α_kn > α_ebh adds e-values that fall below p/(α_ebh K) for every K > |S(α_ebh)|).
- Remark 3: different knockoff generators and different statistics W across runs (e.g. two labs) still give FDR control, since each run only needs its own validity. Remark 4: any stopping time T^(m) with respect to the masked filtration of W^(m) (magnitudes |W_j|, signs of those with |W_j| < t, and the count of positive signs among |W_j| ≥ t) yields valid relaxed e-values, e.g. offsets c ≥ 0 in (c + Σ 1{W_k ≤ −t})/Σ 1{W_k ≥ t} ≤ α_kn. Remark 5: take M as large as computation allows.
- Uniform improvement (eq. 15, inspired by Luo, Fithian and Lei 2022): stop at T^(m) = inf{t : ratio ≤ α_kn or Σ_j 1{W_j^(m) ≥ t} < 1/α_kn}; this differs from (13) only in the hopeless case where (13) stops at ∞ and all e-values vanish, so it is uniformly more powerful and still valid by Remark 4; the gain is largest when α_kn or the non-null fraction is small.
- Section 3.1.1 (vs Ren, Wei and Candès 2021): the old procedure thresholds the unweighted selection frequency Π_j = M^{−1} Σ_m 1{j ∈ S^(m)} at a constant η; the new one is a weighted frequency with run weight ∝ 1/(1 + Σ_k 1{W_k^(m) ≤ −T^(m)}) and a data-dependent e-BH cutoff.
- Section 3.1.2 (vs Dai, Lin, Xing and Liu 2022, 2023): their inclusion rate over data splits is (2) with weight_j^(m) = p/(α_kn Σ_k 1{W_k^(m) ≥ t}) (16), always ≤ the knockoff weight p/(1 + Σ_k 1{W_k^(m) ≤ −T^(m)}) by definition of T, so the e-value method is strictly more powerful; their guarantee is asymptotic and needs a consistent-ranking condition, whereas the relaxed-e-value argument also gives their weights finite-sample FDR control.
- Section 3.2 (choice of α_kn): a heuristic with s strong non-nulls selected in every run gives e_j^avg ≈ p(1 − α_kn)/(α_kn s) for non-nulls and ≈ 0 for nulls, so e-BH at α_ebh has power only if α_kn ⪅ α_ebh/(1 + α_ebh); α_kn = α_ebh fails, α_kn = α_ebh/2 with c = 1 works well empirically (Figures 1, C.1–C.3); optimal tuning is open.
- Section 3.3 (derandomization limit): conditional on (X, Y) the e_j^(m) are i.i.d. over m, so e_j^avg → e_j^∞ := E[e_j^(1) | X, Y] a.s. (17); with Δ = min_k |e_(k)^∞ − p/(αk)|, Hoeffding gives S_kn–derand = S_kn–∞ with probability ≥ 1 − 2p exp(−2Δ²M/p²).
- Section 4 (simulations): Gaussian linear (p = 800, 80 non-nulls, A ∈ {4,…,8}) and logistic (p = 600, 50 non-nulls, A ∈ {10,…,35}) models, n = 1000, AR(0.5) covariance, LCD statistic, M = 50, α_ebh = 0.1, α_kn = 0.05; 100 datasets × 20 repetitions. Derandomized knockoffs match single-run power for moderate or strong signals, lose some power for weak ones, have consistently lower realized FDR, and sharply lower conditional selection variability (Figures 2–4); nulls that single-run knockoffs select often are rarely selected after derandomization.
- Section 5 and Appendix D (HIV-1 lopinavir resistance, Rhee et al. 2006): n = 1840, p = 219, second-order Gaussian knockoffs, M = 100, 50 repetitions; selection frequencies concentrate at 0 and 1 for the derandomized method; 63.5 vs 77.0 discoveries on average, 38 of 63 vs 39 of 74 verified by the Stanford HIVDB annotations, i.e. the same number of confirmed hits with fewer total discoveries (Tables D.1–D.2).
- Appendix A (extensions, each with the same three-line proof): Theorem A.1 fixed-X knockoffs with random orthogonal U^(m); Theorem A.2 robust knockoffs with estimated conditionals Q_j: the fraction of rejections among nulls with max_m KL̂_j^(m) ≤ ε is at most e^ε α, hence FDR ≤ min_ε {e^ε α + P(max_j KL̂_j^max ≥ ε)}; Theorem A.3 multi-environment knockoffs (consistent and partially consistent hypotheses of Li et al. 2021); Theorem A.4 side information, weighted version e_j^(m) = p u_j 1{W_j^(m) ≥ T^(m)}/(u_j + Σ_k u_k 1{W_k^(m) ≤ −T^(m)}) (A.6) and adaptive version via the ordered screening of Ren and Candès 2020 (A.8).
- Appendix C: heatmaps over (c, α_kn) at α_ebh ∈ {0.1, 0.2}; a high-dimensional setting n = 800, p = 1000; the Candès et al. 2018 settings (n = 3000, p = 1000 or 6000); ROC comparison with the PFER-type scheme of Ren, Wei and Candès 2021 (comparable power at equal realized FDR, Figure C.7); robustness to a sample-covariance estimate of Σ from n_0 ∈ {600,…,1000} unlabeled samples; a two-environment study where derandomized MEKF has higher power and lower FDR than MEKF; side information u_j = exp(−j) raising power substantially (Figure C.10).

## Setting and assumptions
- Null H_j : Y ⊥ X_j | X_{−j}; model-X assumption that P_X is known exactly (Appendix A.2 relaxes it to approximate knowledge with an e^ε inflation); no model for Y | X; n i.i.d. samples; fixed p [renDerandomizedKnockoffsLeveraging2022].
- Knockoff validity per run: swap exchangeability (4), antisymmetric statistics W, and a stopping time with respect to the masked filtration (Remark 4); the M copies are drawn i.i.d. conditional on the data, but validity only needs each run separately (Remark 3).
- The e_j are relaxed/compound e-values, Σ_{j∈H_0} E[e_j] ≤ p, not individual e-values; FDR control is through the e-BH theorem under arbitrary dependence, with no PRDS or independence needed among the e_j or across runs.
- Fixed-sample, non-sequential; no optional stopping (the "stopping time" T is over the threshold t, not over data).
- Guarantee is on FDR ≤ α_ebh; realized FDR is far below α_ebh in every experiment, and no sharper bound is proved.

## Relation to other sources
- [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021](wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021.md): supplies e-BH (6) and Theorem 2 here, i.e. FDR control under the weighted/compound condition Σ_{j∈H_0} E[e_j] ≤ p (its Section 5.2 weighted e-BH and the unnamed compound constraint in its Theorem 3); this paper is the first to recognise an existing FDR procedure, the knockoff filter, as e-BH on such values (Theorem 1), and its Remark 1 reuses the e-BH proof inequality to argue near-sharpness [renDerandomizedKnockoffsLeveraging2022].
- [ignatiadisAsymptoticCompoundEvalues2024](ignatiadisAsymptoticCompoundEvalues2024.md): the "relaxed e-values" of (9) are that paper's compound e-values (its Definition 2.1 and Appendix A terminology list name Ren and Barber as a source of the term); its universality Theorem 4.4 generalises Theorem 1 here from knockoffs to every FDR-α procedure via E_k = K·1{k ∈ D}/(α(R_D ∨ 1)), and its Appendix C.1 recovers Algorithm 1 as the special case of averaging implied compound e-values across L = M runs. Knockoff e-values are the one case where the implied e-values have an interpretable denominator (the negative count) rather than α|D|.
- [ignatiadisEvaluesUnnormalizedWeights2022](ignatiadisEvaluesUnnormalizedWeights2022.md): the Biometrika paper that first used the name compound e-values and cites Ren and Barber 2022 as a parallel use of the relaxed condition; both exploit linearity of the compound constraint, there to build data-driven weights, here to average runs.
- [xuBringingClosureFalse2025](xuBringingClosureFalse2025.md): its Theorem 38 closes the knockoff filter via an e-collection built from the same sign-flip property, yielding simultaneity over subsets of S_kn but no power gain; this paper's e-values are the compound-e-value side of that dictionary (its Propositions 24–26), on which closed e-BH reduces to e-BH, so derandomized knockoffs is not improved by closure either.
- [ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025](ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025.md): Chapter 9's compound e-values and universality are the textbook home of Theorems 1–3; the knockoff e-value is the standard worked example of a compound e-value that is not an e-value.
- [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021](vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021.md): cited for the fact that the average of e-values is an e-value (its Proposition 3.1), the one property Section 3 needs; the paper also cites Vovk 2020 (data splitting with e-values) as an earlier use of e-values for derandomization.
- [wassermanUniversalInference2020](wassermanUniversalInference2020.md): its Section 4 derandomizes the split LRT by averaging over splits with the identical linearity argument; this paper applies the same device to a compound rather than individual e-value and to the randomness of knockoff sampling rather than of data splitting.
- [waudbysmithEstimatingMeansBounded2020](waudbysmithEstimatingMeansBounded2020.md): its Remark 1 averages over random permutations for a fixed-n e-value, another instance of derandomization by averaging, there reported as neither helping nor hurting; here derandomization measurably lowers realized FDR and variability at some cost in weak-signal power.
- [ramdasGametheoreticStatisticsSafe2023](ramdasGametheoreticStatisticsSafe2023.md): survey background on averaging and e-BH; not cited.

## Open questions / gaps
- Sharper FDR bound: realized FDR is well below α_ebh in every experiment; the authors ask under what mild conditions a tighter bound holds, as for stability selection (Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2010; Shah and Samworth 2013) or the PFER-type derandomization of Ren, Wei and Candès 2021 (Section 6).
- Tuning: α_kn = α_ebh/2 and c = 1 are empirical defaults; whether they are optimal or can be chosen adaptively is open (Section 3.2).
- Mixing knockoff generators and statistics across runs for robustness (Remark 3) is proposed but not studied.
- Weighted versus adaptive use of side information (Theorem A.4): the trade-off between explicit weights and learning the ordering is left open.
- Combination with group knockoffs (Dai and Barber 2016) and missing-value knockoffs (Koyuncu and Yener 2022) is suggested, not done.
- Number of runs: the Hoeffding bound in Section 3.3 is in terms of the unobservable gap Δ, so no practical rule for M beyond "as large as affordable" (Remark 5).
- Power loss in the weak-signal regime relative to single-run knockoffs (Figures 2, C.4, C.6) is observed but not explained or bounded.
