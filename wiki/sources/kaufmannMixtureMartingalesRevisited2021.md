# Mixture Martingales Revisited with Applications to Sequential Tests and Confidence Intervals

## Summary
The paper proves deviation inequalities that hold uniformly in time and under any adaptive sampling rule for a K-armed bandit whose arms belong to one one-parameter exponential family [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021]. The quantity controlled is the self-normalised sum Σ_{a∈S} N_a(t) d(μ̂_a(t), μ_a) over a subset S of arms (eq. 1), where N_a(t) is the random number of draws of arm a, μ̂_a(t) its empirical mean and d the KL divergence of the family in mean parameters; over all arms this sum is the log generalised likelihood ratio of μ̂(t) against μ. The headline bound (eq. 2) says that, with probability at least 1 − δ, for all t simultaneously Σ_{a∈S} [N_a(t) d(μ̂_a(t), μ_a) − O(ln ln N_a(t))] < |S|·C(ln(1/δ)/|S|) with an explicit calibration function C(x) = x + o(x): the iterated-logarithm price is paid per arm, but the confidence price is about ln(1/δ) in total, where a union bound over per-arm inequalities would give |S| ln(1/δ). The method ("g-VCC", for Ville–Cramér–Chernoff) is to exhibit, for each arm and each λ in an interval, a test martingale that dominates exp(λX_a(t) − g(λ)), multiply the per-arm martingales, apply Ville's inequality to the product and optimise λ as in the Cramér–Chernoff method. The dominating martingales are mixtures of the exponential martingales exp(ηS_a(t) − φ_{μ_a}(η)N_a(t)) under hierarchical priors on η: a continuous mixture of discrete priors on a geometric grid for general families (Theorem 7), and a discrete mixture of continuous priors that reproduce exp(λ t d(x, μ)) exactly for Gaussian and Gamma arms (Theorems 9, 10), the latter giving calibration functions close to the "ideal" one suggested by the χ² limit of Wilks. The inequalities are then used three ways: thresholds that make the generalised-likelihood-ratio (GLR) stopping rule δ-correct for any partition-identification problem and, with the Tracking sampling rule, asymptotically optimal in sample complexity (Propositions 15, 16, Theorem 17); smaller thresholds in which the number of arms K is replaced by the "rank" of the problem (Proposition 21); and time-uniform confidence regions whose projection gives confidence intervals for linear functions and minima of the means that beat the per-arm union bound by up to a factor √K (Propositions 23–26).

## Main results
- **Setting (Section 2.1).** Canonical one-parameter exponential family with densities exp(xθ − b(θ)) with respect to a reference measure, parameterised by the mean μ = ḃ(θ) ∈ I; d(μ, μ′) := KL(ν_μ, ν_μ′). At round t an arm A_t is chosen, measurable with respect to σ(F_{t−1}, U_t) with U_t independent uniform randomisation, and X_t ~ ν_{μ_{A_t}} is observed; N_a(t) and S_a(t) are the count and sum for arm a, μ̂_a(t) = S_a(t)/N_a(t). Target processes have the form X_a(t) = N_a(t) d(μ̂_a(t), μ_a) − c ln(d + ln N_a(t)) (eq. 4) [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Definition 1 (g-VCC).** For g : Λ → R on an interval Λ, the process X(t) = {X_a(t)}_a is g-VCC if (1) for every arm a and λ ∈ Λ there is a test martingale M_a^λ (nonnegative, initial value 1, terminology of Shafer et al. 2011) with M_a^λ(t) ≥ exp(λX_a(t) − g(λ)) for all t (condition ∗), and (2) for every S and λ the product Π_{a∈S} M_a^λ(t) is a martingale [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Definition 3.** C_g(x) := min_{λ∈Λ} (g(λ) + x)/λ and the convex conjugate g*(x) := max_{λ∈Λ} (λx − g(λ)).
- **Lemma 4 (g-VCC deviation inequality).** If X is g-VCC then for every S, P(∃t : Σ_{a∈S} X_a(t) ≥ |S| C_g(x/|S|)) ≤ e^{−x} for all x > 0, and P(∃t : Σ_{a∈S} X_a(t) > u) ≤ exp(−|S| g*(u/|S|)) for all u > 0. Proof: exponentiate, bound by the product martingale, apply Ville's inequality (eqs 5, 6), optimise λ. **Proposition 5:** if g* is increasing then C_g = (g*)^{−1} on g*(R+) [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Remark 2 (ideal g).** By Wilks' phenomenon 2X_a(t) is asymptotically χ² distributed when c = 0, and E[e^{λY}] = (1 − λ)^{−1/2} for 2Y ~ χ², which suggests that g should be at least the "ideal function" g_χ²(λ) = −½ ln(1 − λ) (sign as in Section 3.3; Remark 2 prints it without the minus); its calibration function is about x + ln x [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021, Section 3.3, Figure 2].
- **Mixture martingales (Section 2.3).** With φ_μ(η) the cumulant generating function of ν_μ, Z_a^η(t) := exp(ηS_a(t) − φ_{μ_a}(η)N_a(t)) is a test martingale under any sampling rule (eq. 7); so is the mixture Z_a^π(t) := ∫ Z_a^η(t) dπ(η) for any prior π (eq. 8, Tonelli), and so is the product Π_{a∈S} Z_a^{π_a}(t) over arms with a family of priors, because only the sampled arm's factor moves at each round. Hence it suffices to find priors π_{a,λ} with Z_a^{π_{a,λ}} satisfying (∗). A Gaussian prior (Darling–Robbins, Robbins) gives thresholds of order √(t ln t); priors with an asymptote at η = 0 are needed for the √(2t ln ln t) rate [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021, Section 2.3.1].
- **Theorem 7 (any one-parameter exponential family).** Let d⁺(u, v) = d(u, v)1(u ≤ v), d⁻(u, v) = d(u, v)1(u ≥ v), Y_a(t) = [N_a(t) d(μ̂_a(t), μ_a) − 3 ln(1 + ln N_a(t))]⁺ and Y_a^±(t) the same with d^±; let each X_a be one of Y_a, Y_a⁺, Y_a⁻. With h(u) = u − ln u on u ≥ 1, h̃_z(x) = e^{1/h^{−1}(x)} h^{−1}(x) if x ≥ h(1/ln z) and z(x − ln ln z) otherwise (eq. 9), and C_exp(x) = 2 h̃_{3/2}((h^{−1}(1 + x) + ln(2ζ(2)))/2) (eq. 10): for every S and x > 0, P(∃t : Σ_{a∈S} X_a(t) ≥ |S| C_exp(x/|S|)) ≤ e^{−x}. If every X_a is one-sided, ln(2ζ(2)) may be replaced by ln ζ(2) (calibration C̃_exp). C_exp(x) ~ x as x → ∞, and for x ≥ 5 approximately x + 4 ln(1 + x + √(2x)) [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Proposition 8.** h is increasing on [1, ∞), h^{−1}(x) = −W_{−1}(−e^{−x}) with W_{−1} the negative branch of the Lambert function, and h^{−1}(x) ≤ x + ln(x + √(2(x − 1))) for x ≥ 1.
- **Proof chain for Theorem 7 (Section 3.4, Appendix B).** Lemma 27 (peeling): for N_a(t) in the slice [(1 + ξ)^{i−1}, (1 + ξ)^i] there are η_i^± with KL(θ + η_i^±, θ) = x/(1 + ξ)^i such that {N_a(t) d^±(μ̂_a(t), μ_a) ≥ x} ⊆ {η_i^± S_a(t) − N_a(t) φ_{μ_a}(η_i^±) ≥ x/(1 + ξ)}. Lemma 13: for ξ ∈ (0, 1/2) and x > 0 the discrete prior π(x) = Σ_i γ_i (δ_{η_i^+} + δ_{η_i^−}) with γ_i = 1/(2 i² ζ(2)) and levels x_i = x + ln(1/γ_i) gives {X_a(t) − (1 + ξ) ln C(ξ) ≥ x} ⊆ {Z_a^{π(x)}(t) ≥ e^{x/(1+ξ)}} for all t, with C(ξ) = 2ζ(2)/(ln(1 + ξ))² (one-sided: γ_i = 1/(i² ζ(2)) and C(ξ) = ζ(2)/(ln(1 + ξ))², eq. 15). Lemma 11: X is g_ξ-VCC for ξ ∈ [0, 1/2] with g_ξ(λ) = λ(1 + ξ) ln C(ξ) − ln(1 − λ(1 + ξ)) on λ ∈ [0, 1/(1 + ξ)); the martingale is M_a^λ(t) = (1 − λ(1 + ξ))(1 + ∫_1^∞ W_a^{z,λ}(t) dz) with W_a^{z,λ}(t) := Z_a^{π(ln z/λ)}(t) e^{−ln z/(λ(1+ξ))} ≥ 1{e^{λ(X_a(t) − (1+ξ) ln C(ξ))} ≥ z} (eq. 16), i.e. an exponential mixture over thresholds that turns "indicator-dominating" martingales into an "exponential-dominating" one. Lemma 12 (with Lemmas 28, 30) optimises ξ and λ in closed form: the infimum over ξ ∈ [0, z], λ ∈ [0, 1/(1 + ξ)] of (x − ln(1 − λ(1 + ξ)))/λ + (1 + ξ) ln C(ξ) equals 2 h̃_{1+z}((h^{−1}(1 + x) + ln(2ζ(2)))/2) [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Remark after Lemma 11 (capital calibrators).** Building M_t ≥ e^{λX_t} from martingales guaranteeing Z_t ≥ e^x 1{X_t ≥ x} is the problem characterised by Dawid et al. 2011 (admissible capital calibrators); a polynomial instead of exponential mixture over thresholds would give M_t ≥ e^{X_t − O(ln X_t)}, not pursued because the extra ln X_t does not combine well across arms [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021, Section 3.4].
- **Corollary 31 (one arm, Section 3.3.2, eq. 14).** P(∃t : t d(μ̂_t, μ) ≥ 3 ln(1 + ln t) + 2 h̃_{3/2}((x + ln(2ζ(2)))/2)) ≤ e^{−x}, directly from Lemma 13 without the Cramér–Chernoff step; stated as the first bound uniform over t ∈ N with a ln ln t threshold for general exponential families. The multi-arm threshold has h^{−1}(1 + x) > x where this has x: that gap is the overhead of the Cramér–Chernoff route [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021, Appendix B.3].
- **Theorem 9 (Gaussian, known variance).** X_a(t) = N_a(t) d(μ̂_a(t), μ_a) − 2 ln(4 + ln N_a(t)) is g_G-VCC with g_G(λ) = 2λ − 2λ ln(4λ) + ln ζ(2λ) − ½ ln(1 − λ) on λ ∈ (1/2, 1]; hence P(∃t : Σ_{a∈S} [N_a(t) d(μ̂_a(t), μ_a) − 2 ln(4 + ln N_a(t))] ≥ |S| C_G(x/|S|)) ≤ e^{−x} with C_G := C_{g_G} [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Theorem 10 (Gamma, known shape α).** Same statement with d(μ, μ′) = α(μ/μ′ − 1 − ln(μ/μ′)) and g_Γ(λ) = 2λ − 2λ ln(4λ) + ln ζ(2λ) − ln(1 − λ), calibration C_Γ := C_{g_Γ} [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Assumption 32 and Theorem 33 (Appendix C, engine behind Theorems 9–10).** Assume for every λ ∈ (0, 1), μ ∈ I functions p_t^{λ,μ} with ∫ p_t^{λ,μ}(η) e^{ηtx − φ_μ(η)t} dη = e^{λ t d(x, μ)} for all x (eq. 27) and p_{n1}^{λ,μ}(η) ≥ √(n1/n2) p_{n2}^{λ,μ}(η) for n1 ≤ n2 (eq. 28). With C_0(t, λ) := sup_μ ∫ p_t^{λ,μ}, grid T_i = (1 + ξ)^{i−1}, weights γ_i = 1/(i^{λc} ζ(λc)) and g_0(λ, ξ, c) = ln Σ_i γ_i C_0(T_i, λ), the process N_a(t) d(μ̂_a(t), μ_a) − c ln(ln(1 + ξ) + ln N_a(t)) is g_{ξ,c}-VCC on λ ∈ (1/c, 1] with g_{ξ,c}(λ) = g_0 + ½ ln(1 + ξ) + λc ln(1/ln(1 + ξ)) + ln ζ(λc); the martingale is the normalised Σ_i γ_i ∫ p_{T_i}^{λ,μ_a}(η) Z_a^η(t) dη. Finding p_t is a bilateral inverse Laplace transform problem. Gaussian: p_t is (1 − λ)^{−1/2} times the N(0, λ/(t(1 − λ))) density (variance as printed in Appendix C.1), C_0 = (1 − λ)^{−1/2}, (28) with equality. Gamma: p_t^λ of eq. 29 (Proposition 34), C_0(t, λ) decreasing in t (Proposition 35) from 1/(1 − λ) to (1 − λ)^{−1/2}, so −½ ln(1 − λ) ≤ g_0 ≤ −ln(1 − λ). In both cases c = 2 and the optimal grid is ln(1 + ξ) = 4λ [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Shape of the priors (Section 3.4.1).** Theorem 7 uses a continuous mixture of discrete priors, Theorems 9–10 a discrete average of continuous priors; both implement the Laplace technique (get close to the data-dependent maximiser η̂) and peeling (adapt to the random N_a(t)). The combined density has an asymptote at η = 0, proportional nearby to 1/(η (ln η)²); the log factor makes the prior proper and is the source of the ln ln N_a(t) terms. Alternative noted: mixing the increments Z_a^η(t) − Z_a^η(0) under the improper prior 1/η (Koolen and van Erven 2015), which can go negative but no lower than −ln N_a(t) for bounded outcomes [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Comparison with prior work (Sections 1.1, 3.3).** Stylised form (eq. 23): threshold Σ_{a∈S} c ln(d + ln N_a(t)) + |S| C(x/|S|), with (c, d) = (2, 4) for Gaussian/Gamma and (3, 1) in general. Because of the positive parts, Theorem 7 also holds uniformly over sub-subsets S′ ⊆ S with the threshold of S. The only earlier multi-arm bound, Magureanu et al. 2014 Theorem 2 (eq. 11), is for a finite horizon n, uses peeling and stochastic dominance, and has a larger threshold than the finite-horizon corollaries (12), (13) of the new theorems (Figure 3). C_G and C_Γ are close to the ideal x + ln x; C_exp is off by an additive term of order 10 (Figure 2) [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Definition 14, Proposition 15 (GLR stopping rule).** For a partition O = ∪_{i≤M} O_i of the mean space into open sets and Alt(μ) the union of the pieces not containing μ, Λ̂_t := inf_{λ∈Alt(μ̂(t))} Σ_a N_a(t) d(μ̂_a(t), λ_a) (eq. 17) and τ_δ := inf{t : Λ̂_t > ĉ_t(δ)} (eq. 18); {Λ̂_t > ĉ_t(δ)} = {C_t(δ) ⊆ O_{î(t)}} for the region C_t(δ) = {λ : Σ_a N_a(t) d(μ̂_a(t), λ_a) ≤ ĉ_t(δ)} (eq. 19). With ĉ_t(δ) = 3 Σ_a ln(1 + ln N_a(t)) + K C_exp(ln(1/δ)/K) (eq. 20), for every sampling rule P_μ(∀t : μ ∈ C_t(δ)) ≥ 1 − δ and P_μ(τ_δ < ∞, î(τ_δ) ≠ i*(μ)) ≤ δ [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Proposition 16 (lower bound).** With T*(μ)^{−1} := sup_{w∈Σ_K} inf_{λ∈Alt(μ)} Σ_a w_a d(μ_a, λ_a), every δ-correct strategy has E_μ[τ_δ] ≥ T*(μ) ln(1/(3δ)); by the change-of-measure argument of Garivier and Kaufmann 2016.
- **Theorem 17 (asymptotic optimality).** If the optimal weights w*(μ) (eq. 21) are unique for every μ ∈ O and continuous on each O_i, and the deterministic thresholds are increasing in t with ĉ_t(δ) ≤ ln(Dt/δ) for t ≥ C, then the Tracking rule (forced exploration of arms with N_a(t) ≤ √t − K/2, otherwise track t·w*(μ̂(t)), eq. 22) with the GLR stopping rule satisfies limsup_{δ→0} E_μ[τ_δ]/ln(1/δ) = T*(μ). Proof in Appendix D (Lemmas 36, 37, Berge's maximum theorem). When uniqueness fails (e.g. Minimum Threshold), Degenne and Koolen 2019 treat w* as set-valued [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Lemma 18 (weighted union bound).** If (23) holds and π̃ is a probability distribution over subsets, then P(∃t, ∃S : Σ_{a∈S} N_a(t) d(μ̂_a(t), μ_a) > Σ_{a∈S} c ln(d + ln N_a(t)) + |S| C((x − ln π̃(S))/|S|)) ≤ e^{−x}.
- **Best arm identification (Section 5.1).** The GLR statistic involves only pairs of arms (eq. 24), so Lemma 18 over the K − 1 pairs {1, a} gives δ-correctness with ĉ_t(δ) = 6 ln(ln(t/2) + 1) + 2 C_exp(ln((K − 1)/δ)/2) (eq. 25), smaller for large t than the ln(2t(K − 1)/δ) of Garivier and Kaufmann 2016 and still within Theorem 17. **Remark 19:** this threshold gives a δ-correct stopping rule but the corresponding region (19) is not a 1 − δ confidence sequence; improved stopping rules and improved confidence regions are not equivalent [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Definition 20, Proposition 21 (rank).** A problem has rank R if every O \ O_i is a finite union of sets each defined through only R arms. Then the GLR rule is δ-correct with ĉ_t(δ) = 3R ln(1 + ln(t/R)) + R C_exp(ln((M − 1)/δ)/R), using concavity of s ↦ ln(1 + ln s). Best arm identification has rank 2; Largest Profit Identification (K pairs of arms, largest difference of means) has rank 4; best action in a depth-two maxmin game tree has rank L + 1 with L the second player's number of moves, against K·L leaves [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021, Sections 5.2.1–5.2.2].
- **Confidence regions (Section 6, eq. 26).** For any weight vector π̃ over subsets, C_t^{π̃}(δ) := {λ : ∀S, Σ_{a∈S} N_a(t) d(μ̂_a(t), λ_a) ≤ c Σ_{a∈S} ln(d + ln N_a(t)) + |S| C_exp(ln(1/(π̃(S)δ))/|S|)} satisfies P(∀t : μ ∈ C_t^{π̃}(δ)) ≥ 1 − δ for every sampling rule; f(μ) is then covered by the projection {f(λ) : λ ∈ C_t}.
- **Propositions 23, 24 (linear functions, Gaussian arms, c = 2, d = 4, g = g_G).** Box (π̃ uniform on singletons): vᵀμ̂(t) ± Σ_a √(2(C_g(ln(K/δ)) + c ln(d + ln N_a(t))) v_a²/N_a(t)). Ellipse (π̃ a point mass on the full set): vᵀμ̂(t) ± √(2(K C_g(ln(1/δ)/K) + Σ_a c ln(d + ln N_a(t))) Σ_a v_a²/N_a(t)). With N_a(t) = t/K and C_g(x) ≈ x the box scales with ‖v‖₁ and the ellipse with ‖v‖₂, a gain of up to √K at the price of a factor K on the ln ln t term; for two arms and the difference μ_1 − μ_2 the gain is √2 in width, hence a factor 2 in sample complexity in typical adaptive stopping problems (Section 1.2.3, Figure 1) [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021].
- **Propositions 25, 26 (minimum of the means).** Lower bound: no gain from combining arms; with θ_a(t) ≤ μ̂_a(t) solving N_a(t) d⁻(μ̂_a(t), θ_a(t)) = 3 ln(1 + ln N_a(t)) + C_exp(ln(K/δ)), P(∀t : min_a μ_a ≥ min_a θ_a(t)) ≥ 1 − δ. Upper bound: θ(t) solving max_S [Σ_{a∈S} [N_a(t) d⁺(μ̂_a(t), θ) − 3 ln(1 + ln N_a(t))]⁺ − |S| C_exp(ln(1/(δ π̃(S)))/|S|)] = 0 satisfies P(∀t : min_a μ_a ≤ θ(t)) ≥ 1 − δ. For cardinality-based weights π̃(S) = π(|S|)/binom(K, |S|) the objective is evaluated in O(K ln K) by sorting; in a Bernoulli experiment with M arms at the minimum and δ = 10^{−10}, π uniform over sizes gives consistently smaller upper bounds than the box, more so as M grows (Figure 4) [kaufmannMixtureMartingalesRevisited2021, Section 6.2.3].

## Setting and assumptions
- Data model: K independent arms from the same one-parameter canonical exponential family (Bernoulli, Poisson, Gaussian with known variance, Gamma with known shape are the named examples), parameterised by means; the true mean vector μ is the only unknown. The refined Theorems 9–10 are for Gaussian and Gamma only, because the required exact "prior" (Assumption 32) could be computed only there.
- Adaptive sampling: any sampling rule, possibly randomised, adapted to the past. All inequalities are of the form P(∃t ∈ N : …) ≤ δ, so they hold at arbitrary stopping times; this is what the sequential-test analysis needs.
- The inequalities are parametric: they use the KL geometry of the family rather than sub-Gaussian relaxations.
- The correction ln(1 + ln N_a(t)) is vacuous at N_a(t) = 0; algorithms are assumed to pull each arm once, or the term can be replaced by 3 ln(1 + ln(1 + N_a(t))) (Remark 6).
- Sequential identification: hypotheses H_i : μ ∈ O_i for open sets O_1, …, O_M partitioning the parameter set O; a strategy (sampling rule, stopping rule, recommendation) is δ-correct if P_μ(μ ∈ O_î) ≥ 1 − δ for all μ ∈ O. Optimality (Theorem 17) is asymptotic in δ → 0 and needs unique, continuous oracle weights.
- No e-value vocabulary: the objects are called test martingales and mixture martingales; there is no growth-rate or power criterion, the yardstick is the size of the threshold and the sample complexity.

## Open questions / gaps
- Closing the gap between C_exp and the ideal calibration (an additive term of order 10, Figure 2); for Gamma arms g_0 may be closer to −½ ln(1 − λ) than the bound −ln(1 − λ) used (Appendix C.2).
- Assumption 32 beyond Gaussian and Gamma: the exact prior is an inverse Laplace transform the authors could not compute for other families; the Appendix C construction also does not adapt to one-sided deviations (Section 3.3).
- Improper-prior mixtures of martingale increments (Koolen and van Erven 2015) as a route to the ln ln term (Section 3.4.1).
- Polynomial mixing over thresholds to get M_t ≥ e^{X_t − O(ln X_t)} (after Lemma 11), not pursued.
- Which weight vector π̃ over subsets gives the best confidence region is left open in general (Section 6); only linear functions and minima are worked out.
- Uniqueness and continuity of the oracle weights w*(μ) are assumed in Theorem 17 and only numerically accessible in the game-tree example (Section 5.2.2).
- Apparent misprints in the version of record, checked against the PDF and reproduced here in corrected or flagged form: Remark 2 prints g_χ²(λ) = ½ ln(1 − λ) where Section 3.3 has −½ ln(1 − λ); the proof of Proposition 21 drops the factor R in front of C_exp; the display defining C_t^{π̃,±}(δ) in Section 6.2 has the argument ln(π̃(S)/δ)/δ where (26) and Proposition 26 have ln(1/(δ π̃(S)))/|S|; the proof of Proposition 24 writes the threshold with ln(1 + ln N_a(t)) and C_exp although the statement uses c, d and C_g; Appendix B.1 writes the one-sided constant as "ln ζ(2)/(ln(1 + ξ))²" where Section 3.4 has ζ(2)/(ln(1 + ξ))²; the Figure 3 legend refers to (13) and (14) where the text compares (11) and (12); Appendix C.1 calls the Gaussian prior variance σ_t² = λ/(t(1 − λ)), reusing the symbol σ² of the arm variance.
