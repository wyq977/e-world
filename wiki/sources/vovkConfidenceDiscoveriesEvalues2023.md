# Confidence and discoveries with e-values

Statistical Science 38(2):329-354, 2023, with supplement. The text layer read here is arXiv v5 (November 2022), which announces itself as revised beyond the journal version; section, equation, algorithm and figure numbers below follow v5 (main Sections 1-10, Appendices A-E).

## Summary
The paper develops the e-value counterparts of three classical objects: critical regions, confidence regions and confidence bounds on the number of true discoveries. An e-test is a family of e-variables (E_θ)_{θ∈Θ}; the e-confidence region at level α > 1 is Γ_{e,α}(ω) = {θ : E_θ(ω) < α}, read through a Fisher-type disjunction "Γ covers the true θ unless ω is α-strange", and it is simultaneously valid for every derived parameter g(θ) because strangeness does not depend on g (Section 4, eqs 15, 17). Necessity and possibility measures repackage these regions as set functions (Section 5). Specialising to Θ = P(Ω) and K base e-variables for hypotheses H_1, …, H_K, the discovery e-vector D^R(j) = min over index sets I with |R \ I| = j of F(E_i : i ∈ I) gives, for any rejection set R chosen by the researcher, the statement "R contains more than j true discoveries unless ω is D^R(j)-strange", uniformly in R and j and under arbitrary dependence, with F a symmetric e-merging function, in practice the arithmetic mean (Section 6, eq. 29). Restricting to the nested rejection sets R_r of the r largest e-values gives the discovery e-matrix D_{r,j}, which for the arithmetic mean is monotone along rows, columns and diagonals (Proposition 6.4) and is computable in O(K) per row, O(K^2) overall, by a convex-hull argument (Section 9, Algorithm 4). This is the e-translation of the Genovese-Wasserman / Goeman-Solari closed-testing (GWGS) procedure (Appendix C), and the paper argues it is admissible for arithmetic averaging. Simulations with Gaussian likelihood-ratio e-values and the BRCA dataset with permutation ("Monte Carlo") e-values show the e-matrix giving more true discoveries than hommel's closed Simes/Hommel p-matrices even after crude e-to-p calibration, and far more under arbitrary dependence [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023].

## Main results

### Testing and comparability (Sections 2-3)
- **Three approaches** (Figure 1): basic testing (critical region of size α) embeds into p-testing via P = α on A, 1 off A (eq. 1) and into e-testing via E = 1/α on A, 0 off A (eq. 2); a basic p-variable can be improved by nesting further critical regions, whereas the basic e-variable (2) cannot be enlarged on any set of positive probability because the single budget of 1 is spent [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 2].
- **Jeffreys's scale** for reading e-values: < 1 supports the null; (1, √10) bare mention; (√10, 10) substantial; (10, 10^{3/2}) strong; (10^{3/2}, 100) very strong; > 100 decisive. Used as the colour code of every discovery-matrix plot [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 2].
- **Toy example** N(0,1) vs alternatives: the unique |x|-monotone p-variable 2Φ(−|x|) against a continuum of e-variables |x|^d / (π^{−1/2} 2^{d/2} Γ((d+1)/2)) (eq. 3, "signed χ_{d+1}" alternatives) and the symmetrised likelihood ratios (exp(δx − δ²/2) + exp(−δx − δ²/2))/2 (eqs 4-5); decisive evidence (e ≥ 100, δ = 3) needs |x| ≳ 3.27, highly significant (p ≤ 0.01) needs |x| ≳ 2.58 [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 2, Figure 2].
- **Comparing p and e**: the calibrator family f_κ(p) = κ p^{κ−1} (eq. 6) and its envelope VS(p) = −e^{−1}/(p ln p) (eq. 7), which is not itself an e-variable; the only reasonable e-to-p calibrator min(1, 1/e) (eq. 8). Round-trip efficiency is poor: 1/VS(0.005) ≈ 0.072 (eq. 9); VS(0.05) ≈ 2.456. Informal correspondences: Jeffreys p = 5% ~ e = √10, p = 1% ~ e = 10 (eq. 10); Good's rule e ∈ (1/(30p), 3/(10p)) with geometric mean 1/(10p) giving 5% ~ 2, 1% ~ 10 (eqs 11-12). Users split into frequentist, Bernoullian, Bayesian and betting camps; the one objective advantage of e-values is ease of merging [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 3].

### Confidence regions and necessity measures (Sections 4-5)
- **Definitions**: basic confidence estimator Γ(ω) = {θ : ω ∉ A_θ} (eq. 13); p-confidence region Γ_{p,α}(ω) = {θ : P_θ(ω) > α}, α ∈ (0,1), nested in α (eq. 14); e-confidence region Γ_{e,α}(ω) = {θ : E_θ(ω) < α}, α ∈ (0, ∞) (eq. 15). ω is (e, α)-strange for θ if E_θ(ω) ≥ α. Levels 1, 10^{1/2}, 10, 10^{3/2}, 100 are called weak, substantial, strong, very strong, extremely strong e-confidence regions [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 4].
- **Simultaneity**: for any family of maps g: Θ → Θ_g, Γ^g_{e,α}(ω) = {g(θ) : E_θ(ω) < α} (eq. 17) contains g(θ) for all g simultaneously unless ω is α-strange for the true θ; the same holds for p-regions provided one p-test serves all g [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 4].
- **Toy intervals** for N(θ, 1) with the symmetrised e-test (18)-(19), δ = 3: Γ_{e,100} ≈ x ± 3.27, Γ_{e,10^{3/2}} ≈ x ± 2.88, Γ_{e,10} ≈ x ± 2.50, Γ_{e,√10} ≈ x ± 2.11, against Γ_{p,0.01} ≈ x ± 2.58 (eqs 20-21); e-intervals depend on the chosen alternative δ, a cost tied to the absence of UMP tests [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 4].
- **Necessity and possibility measures**: □_p(B | ω) = sup_{θ∉B} P_θ(ω) (eq. 22), □_e(B | ω) = inf_{θ∉B} E_θ(ω) (eq. 23), with derived-parameter versions □^g = □(g^{−1}(B) | ω) (eqs 24-25) and possibility measures ◇(B | ω) = □(B^c | ω); interpretation "θ ∈ B unless ω is □(B | ω)-strange". Related to Dubois-Prade possibility theory and Dempster-Shafer belief, but adds no information beyond the confidence regions (Remark 5.1) [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 5].

### Controlling the number of true discoveries (Section 6)
- **Setting**: Θ = P(Ω); I_θ = {k : θ ∈ H_k}; for a rejection set R the number of true discoveries is g_R(θ) = |R \ I_θ| (eq. 26). E_θ := F(E_k : k ∈ I_θ) for a symmetric e-merging function F (eq. 28) is an e-test [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 6].
- **Discovery e-vector** (eq. 29): ◇_e^{g_R}(j | ω) ≥ D^R_{e,F}(j) := min_{I : |R\I| = j} F(E_i : i ∈ I), with equality under Holm's free-combinations condition (eq. 30), which is not needed for validity. Algorithm 1 computes it in polynomial time; only the j smallest elements of R and the i smallest elements of R^c need to be tried [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 6].
- **Arithmetic-mean vector** AV^R(j) = min_{I : |R\I| = j} (1/|I|) Σ_{i∈I} E_i; the regularised vector D̄^R(j) = min_{j' ≤ j} D^R(j') satisfies, for every e-merging F, (1) decreasing in j, (2) increasing in R, (3) D̄^{R'}(j + |R' \ R|) ≤ D̄^R(j) (**Proposition 6.3**). Then □_e^{g_R}({j+1, j+2, …} | ω) ≥ D̄^R(j): "R contains more than j true discoveries unless ω is D̄^R(j)-strange" [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 6].
- **Discovery e-matrix**: for sorted e_1 ≤ … ≤ e_K, R_r = {K−r+1, …, K} (eq. 33) is the optimal rejection set of size r (a complete class in Wald's sense); D_{r,j} := D^{R_r}(j), computed by Algorithm 2. For the arithmetic mean AM_{r,j} = min_{I : |R_r \ I| = j} mean(e_i : i ∈ I) = min over |R_r \ I| ≤ j, and **Proposition 6.4** gives unregularised monotonicity in j, in r and along diagonals r − j = const, using only the property e ≥ max(e) ⇒ F(e, e) ≥ F(e) (eq. 34). Interpretation: more than j true discoveries among the r largest e-values unless ω is D_{r,j}-strange [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 6].
- **Remark 6.5**: under independence the U-statistic Σ_{i<j} E_i E_j / C(|I_θ|, 2) as F gives much better results (pursued in Vovk and Wang 2020b); the plain product is poor because small base e-values dominate [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 6].

### Experiments (Sections 7-8, Appendix D)
- **Simulation** (Section 7): N(μ,1) observations, nulls N(0,1), alternatives N(δ,1), δ = −3, K/2 each; base e-values exp(δx − δ²/2) (eq. 36). For K = 20 row 10 of AM gives decisive evidence of ≥ 3 true discoveries and strong evidence of ≥ 6 (Figure 3). For K = 200 (Figure 4, Table 1) row 50 gives decisive ≥ 11, very strong ≥ 27, strong ≥ 40, substantial ≥ 46 true discoveries; row 31 is the lowest with at most 10% of entries below 10 (FDP-style use); row 51 the lowest with at most 10 entries below 10 (k-FWER-style use) [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023].
- **Comparison with hommel** (GWGS discovery p-matrix with base p-values Φ(x), closed Hommel under arbitrary dependence or closed Simes under independence): the AM matrix is better than both under Jeffreys's correspondence; after the uncontroversial e-to-p map 1/e it still looks slightly better than the arbitrary-dependence p-matrix, and the VS-transformed p-matrices look much worse than AM even assuming independence (Figure 4, lower panels). BH/BY reject 87/61 (independence) and 55/28 (arbitrary dependence) at FDR 5%/1% (Table 2), roughly matching AM's strong/substantial bounds 40/46 in row 50 [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 7].
- **BRCA data** (Section 8): 3170 genes × 15 samples (7 BRCA1, 8 BRCA2) from Hedenfalk et al. 2001 / Storey and Tibshirani 2003. Null per gene: all orderings of the row equally likely. Monte Carlo e-value e_k = T_k / ((1/(B+1))(Σ_b T_k^{0b} + T_k)) (eq. 38), B = 10000 permutations, nonconformity T_k = |t_k|^d with Welch t-statistic (eq. 39), d = 10 motivated by the Bayesian two-sample t-test (eq. 40). Valid conditionally on the multiset of scores, no independence or gene-exchangeability assumption. Result: strong evidence for at least Hedenfalk's 9-11 differentially expressed genes, substantial evidence for many more (Figure 5; Table 3: for d = 10, 7 entries of the last row above 10 and 56 above √10). The simplified e_k = T_k / mean_b T_k^{0b} (eq. 43) is only approximately valid, visibly invalid at B = 100 (Figure 6). Storey-Tibshirani's p-value formula (41) can be exactly zero and needs the +1 correction (42). hommel assuming independence is poor (Figure 7) and under arbitrary dependence extremely poor (Figure 11); BH rejects 88 at 5% assuming independence, BY rejects 1 (Table 4) [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023].
- **Generalized Bayes factor** (Appendix D): E_η(x) = exp(ηδx − η²δ²/2) (eq. 50) is the likelihood ratio for the false alternative N(ηδ, 1); with 10,000 nulls, 1000 false, weak signal δ = −2, η = 2 greatly improves the AM matrix over the true likelihood ratio (Figure 12), so the likelihood ratio, optimal for a single e-variable, "may be far from being optimal" in multiple testing (Remark D.1) [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023].

### Computation (Section 9)
- Generic Algorithm 2 costs O(K^3) per row, O(K^4) total. Algorithm 3 (prefix sums s_k and suffix sums σ_{r,j}) gives O(K^2) per row, O(K^3) total. **Algorithm 4** computes one row in O(K): AM_{r,j} is the slope of the line through Q_j = −(r − j, σ_j) touching the convex polyline (k, s_k) from below, and the touching vertex moves monotonically left as j increases (Figure 8); overall O(K^2), optimal. Threshold-only variants can use Tian et al. 2021 [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 9].

### Other mergers and closed testing (Appendices B-C)
- Bonferroni e-merger (1/n) max e_i (eq. 44) and Simes e-merger max_i i e_{[i]}/n (eq. 45) are lower bounds of the mean; Simes satisfies (34) so needs no regularisation (**Proposition B.1**); the regularised Bonferroni matrix has constant columns (Algorithm 5, O(K^2)). Ratios: 1 ≤ F/S ≤ Σ_{k≤n} 1/k ≤ ln n + 1, 1 ≤ F/B ≤ n, 1 ≤ S/B ≤ n (Remark B.2). e-Bonferroni is much worse than AM for FDP control although fine for family-wise validity; Simes e-matrix is intermediate and still beats the VS-transformed GWGS p-matrix (Figure 10). Under 1/e ~ p the mean corresponds to the harmonic-mean p-value, which is invalid unless scaled by about 2.5 ln K [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Appendix B].
- **Appendix C**: with a symmetric p-merging function F, D̄^R_{p,F}(j) = max_{I : |R\I| ≤ j} F(p_i : i ∈ I) (eq. 47) satisfies f_α(R) > j ⇔ D̄^R_{p,F}(j) ≤ α (eq. 48), where f_α(R) = |R| − t_α(R) is Goeman and Solari's closed-testing lower bound on true discoveries (eq. 49); the discovery p-vector is the distribution function whose quantile function is Goeman-Solari's confidence distribution. Goeman, Hemerik and Solari 2021 show only GWGS procedures are admissible for p-based validity; 1/D̄^R_{e,F} is such a p-based procedure and is surely inadmissible because e-to-p calibration is crude, but the authors state Algorithm 1 itself is admissible when F is arithmetic averaging (no proof given) [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Appendix C].

## Setting and assumptions
- Measurable sample space (Ω, A), statistical model (Q_θ)_{θ∈Θ} with Θ an arbitrary set (no measurability in θ); from Section 6 on Θ = P(Ω) with Q_θ = θ, so hypotheses are arbitrary sets of probability measures and the null for H_k is composite [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Sections 2, 6].
- Base e-variables E_1, …, E_K are given; E_k must be an e-variable for every θ ∈ H_k; no assumption on their joint dependence anywhere in the main text (independence only in Remark 6.5 and in hommel's Simes option). The free-combinations condition (30) is not required [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 6].
- Rejection set R may be chosen by the researcher after seeing the e-values; the guarantee is uniform in R and j through one fixed e-test (28), i.e. simultaneous in the Goeman-Solari sense [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 6].
- Fixed sample size; no optional stopping or e-processes (mentioned in Section 1 as not the focus). Alternatives enter only through the choice of base e-variables (likelihood ratios, |x|^d, permutation e-values) [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023].
- The p-versus-e comparisons rest on Jeffreys's and Good's informal correspondences and on the VS bound as an optimistic upper envelope, which the authors flag as subjective [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023, Section 3].

## Open questions / gaps
- Admissibility of Algorithm 1 with arithmetic averaging is asserted in Appendix C ("it can be shown") without proof; the e-analogue of Goeman-Hemerik-Solari's "only closed testing is admissible" theorem is not stated.
- Weighted and non-symmetric versions (different weights per hypothesis, cf. Genovese, Roeder and Wasserman 2006) are flagged in Section 10 as the obvious extension; derived parameters beyond counts (weighted counts, group counts, Remark 6.1) are not developed.
- Why the likelihood ratio is suboptimal in multiple testing and how to choose the learning rate η of the generalized Bayes factor is left as an open theoretical question (Remark D.1).
- Choice of d in |t|^d / |x|^d e-values is admitted to matter (Table 3) with only a heuristic (d of the order of the degrees of freedom).
- The exact relation between discovery-matrix bounds and FDR procedures (BH, BY, e-BH) is described only informally (Tables 2, 4, 5).
