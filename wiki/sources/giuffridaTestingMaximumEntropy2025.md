# Testing maximum entropy models with e-values

## Summary
The paper constructs growth-rate-optimal (GRO) e-variables for testing between two maximum entropy models (MEMs) that share a uniform carrier but differ in their sufficient statistics, in both the microcanonical formulation (hard constraints c(x) = c, uniform law on the constraint set) and the canonical formulation (soft constraints E[c(x)] = c, an exponential family with discrete outcomes) [giuffridaTestingMaximumEntropy2025]. The motivation is the usual one for e-values: type-I control under optional continuation across studies plus post-hoc choice of α, which the paper takes as given from the literature rather than develops (Section II). The microcanonical GRO problem is solved exactly: the RIPr prior on the null is the marginal law of the null sufficient statistic c_0(x) under the alternative's universal distribution, giving S^GRO_mic = Ω_0(c_0)/Ω_1(c_1) · W_1(c_1)/W*_0(c_0) with E_0[S] = 1 for every null element (eqs 29, 32, 33). Because a microcanonical model is the canonical model conditioned on its sufficient statistic, every microcanonical e-variable is a canonical e-variable (eq. 40); the paper therefore proposes S^GRO_mic as a "microcanonical approximation" to the usually uncomputable canonical GRO e-variable, with a non-e-variable "pseudo approximation" S_pseudo giving an upper bound on the canonical GRO e-power, so that the width r of the sandwich (eq. 47–48) certifies closeness. A concentration result (Theorem 1, error O(log m / m)) gives a heuristic reason why r → 0, and Section II.A ties the choice of numerator to MDL: the worst-case regret of an e-variable with numerator P̄_1 is at most the redundancy of P̄_1 (eq. 22), so Bayes mixtures and NML yield regret (d_1/2) log m + O(1). Applied to 2 × 2 and 2 × k binary contingency tables (Erdős–Rényi vs stochastic block model, bipartite ER vs partial configuration model, stationarity of binary time series), simulations show the e-power gap vanishing with group size m even when the number of groups k grows, and a regret dichotomy at the Beta prior parameter γ = 1 under which Jeffreys' prior (γ = 1/2) is suboptimal [giuffridaTestingMaximumEntropy2025].

## Main results
- Section II, eqs 6–10: GRO e-variable for prior w_1 on the alternative is arg max over e-variables of E_{P_1^{w_1}}[log E]; it is the Bayes factor P_1^{w_1}/P_0^{w*_0} with w*_0 the KL minimiser (RIPr), the only e-variable among Bayes factors with that numerator, citing Theorem 1 of grunwaldSafeTesting2024 (Figure 1) [giuffridaTestingMaximumEntropy2025].
- Eqs 11–15: general numerator P̄_1; regret REG_1(θ_1; S) = E_{θ_1}[log S^GRO(θ_1) − log S] against the oracle GRO e-variable for the point alternative θ_1 (what Safe Testing calls REGROW), and worst-case regret over Θ_1; the minimax-regret e-variable is "generally infeasible".
- Section II.A, eq. 19 (standard MDL result): for INECCSI parameter subsets (Definition 1) and regular priors, minimax redundancy is (d_j/2) log m ± C and Bayes marginals attain it within a constant; the NML distribution (eq. 20) attains the same for the MEMs considered.
- Eq. 22 (proof S1): REG_1(Θ′_1; P̄_1) ≤ RED_1(Θ′_1; P̄_1) for any INECCSI Θ′_1, i.e. worst-case regret of the GRO e-variable with numerator P̄_1 is bounded by the worst-case redundancy of P̄_1; hence Bayes-mixture and NML numerators give regret (d_1/2) log m + O(1). The paper reads this as an answer, for two models, to Open Problem 9 (p. 413) of Grünwald's 2007 MDL book: restricting to code-length differences that are e-values gives MDL a type-I error guarantee [giuffridaTestingMaximumEntropy2025].
- Section III.A, eqs 27–33 (proofs S2): microcanonical test, null statistic c_0, alternative statistic c_1, Ω_j(c) the number of configurations with c_j(x) = c. The optimal null prior is W*_0(c_0) = Σ_{x: c_0(x)=c_0} P̄_1(x) (eq. 29, Gibbs' inequality); under Condition A (c_0 = f(c_1), eq. 30) this is the push-forward of W_1 (eq. 31). S^GRO_mic(x) = Ω_0(c_0(x))/Ω_1(c_1(x)) · W_1(c_1(x))/W*_0(c_0(x)) (eq. 32) and E_0[S^GRO_mic] = 1 for every microcanonical null element (eq. 33, S15). The microcanonical NML equals the Bayes marginal with uniform prior on the sufficient statistic (cites Giuffrida et al. 2025, Phys. Rev. Res.).
- Example A: 2 × 2 table with independent discrete uniform priors on (n^a_1, n^b_1); Condition A holds with n_1 = n^a_1 + n^b_1, and W*_0 is the triangular convolution of two discrete uniforms (eq. 35, Figure 2 with n_a = 8, n_b = 10).
- Section III.B, eqs 36–40 (proofs S3): canonical test. Two facts: (i) any canonical universal distribution P̄_can,1 equals a microcanonical Bayes marginal with W_can,1 the law of c_1 induced by P̄_can,1 (eqs 38–39); the converse fails since canonical marginals are strictly positive while microcanonical priors can vanish (S3.B); (ii) a microcanonical e-variable is a canonical e-variable by the tower rule, P_mic(x; c) = P_can(x; θ | c) (eq. 40, S16–S20), and E_can[S^GRO_mic] = 1.
- Eqs 41–49: microcanonical approximation S^GRO_mic = P̄_can,1/P^{W*_0}_mic,0 (valid canonical e-variable, e-power ≤ canonical GRO, eq. 43); pseudo approximation S_pseudo = P̄_can,1/P^{w_pseudo,0}_can,0 with w_pseudo,0 the high-resolution smoothing of W*_0 to a density on M_0 (pseudocode S5), not an e-variable unless w_pseudo,0 = w*_0, with e-power ≥ canonical GRO (eqs 45–46). Sandwich eq. 47; width r = E_{P̄_can,1}[log W*_0(c_0) − log W_pseudo,0(c_0)] ≥ 0 (eq. 49); worst-case version r′ = max_{μ_1} r′(μ_1) (eqs 50–51).
- Example B: canonical 2 × 2 with uniform priors on (p_a, p_b); Bayes marginal eq. 53, induced W_can,1 is uniform so S^GRO_mic is Example A's; r → 0 as n = 2m grows (Figure 3).
- Theorem 1 (Section III.C, proof S4): for a canonical MEM with finite C ⊂ R^d, regular prior w on the mean-value space M, i.i.d. extension with sufficient statistic s^(m) = Σ_j c(x_j): for any INECCSI M′ ⊂ M, |P^{w(m)}_can(s^(m)/m ∈ M′) − Q_w(μ ∈ M′)| = O(log m / m). Proof via Csiszár's multivariate Chernoff/Sanov bound (Csiszár 1984; Grünwald, Hao, Balsubramani 2024) with Fisher-information eigenvalue bounds on INECCSI sets, sets B^±_m at distance √(a log m / m), a = 1 [giuffridaTestingMaximumEntropy2025].
- Example C and the discussion after it: with n_a = n_b = m and s_0 = (n^a_1 + n^b_1)/2, Theorem 1 suggests the discrete law V*_0 of s_0/m converges to the continuous convolution w²_pseudo,0 of the prior components, so w¹_pseudo,0 (high-resolution limit) and w²_pseudo,0 coincide and r → 0. Explicitly labelled heuristic: convergence of set probabilities is "too weak to formally imply r → 0", which involves expectations of log densities. Requires s_0 linear (or, more weakly, a function: Condition A) in s_1 and a factorised regular prior; the w¹ route also works for NML and any universal P̄_1.
- Section IV.A, eqs 60–79: 2 × 2 microcanonical test (W*_0 = W^a_1 * W^b_1, eq. 68); canonical test with NML numerator (exact NML of a Bernoulli model with upper incomplete gamma function, eq. 76; induced W_can,1 eqs 77–79, convolution numerical) and with independent Beta(α_i, β_i) priors (Bayes marginal eq. 82, beta-binomial induced priors eqs 84–85, Figure S1).
- Evaluation (Section IV.A, Figures S2–S3): the canonical GRO e-variable computed numerically for small n; the e-power of S^GRO_mic converges to it from below and S_pseudo from above, the microcanonical one faster; r → 0 for γ ∈ {0.5, 1, 3} and NML, n_a = n_b and n_a = 2 n_b. NML ≈ Jeffreys (γ = 1/2) on INECCSI sets (eq. 86, o(1)) but not at the boundary, where Jeffreys diverges; the simulations use exact NML.
- Eq. 87 (proof S6, using Theorem 3 parts 3–4 and Proposition 3 of haoEValuesExponentialFamilies2025 for misspecified redundancy): if r′(μ_1) → 0, both S^GRO_can and S^GRO_mic have REG_1(μ_1; ·) = ((d_1 − d_0)/2) log m + O(1) uniformly on INECCSI sets, provided w_pseudo,0 is regular (continuous density); for 2 × 2, (1/2) log m + O(1) [giuffridaTestingMaximumEntropy2025].
- Regret dichotomy (Figure 5, m ∈ {600, …, 1800}, (p_a, p_b) ∈ [0.02, 0.98]² step 0.02, fitted slope a of a log m + b): γ < 1 (e.g. Jeffreys γ = 0.5): the convolution of the Beta priors is non-differentiable at p_0 = 1/2, convergence of V*_0 to a density may fail, slope a > 1/2 even on small INECCSI sets, S_pseudo becomes incomparable (Figure S4); γ = 1: moderate, with a yellow counter-diagonal of excess regret at these m; γ > 1 (1.5): slope 1/2 as predicted. Conclusion: Jeffreys' prior, minimax for MDL redundancy (Clarke–Barron 1994), is not worst-case-regret optimal for these e-values.
- Section IV.B, eqs 88–98: 2 × k tables. Microcanonical: W*_0 = W^1_1 * … * W^k_1 (eq. 94); for k ≫ 1 and regular independent priors a CLT gives a discrete Gaussian W*_0 with μ_k = Σ E[n^i_1], σ²_k = Σ Var(n^i_1) (eq. 95, Figure 6), so the alternative prior matters only through its mean and variance. Uniform priors: bounded stars-and-bars formula (eqs 97–98). Canonical: NML (eqs 102–104) and Beta (eqs 105–107) numerators, convolution numerical or Gaussian (Figure S5, k = 10).
- Section IV.B evaluation (Figure S6, all n_i = m, γ = 1): r → 0 quickly for fixed k as m grows; r grows slowly for fixed n = mk as k grows; r → 0 when k and m grow together under power laws; the only non-convergent case is m = O(1/k) decreasing. Verdict: S^GRO_mic is near-optimal "as long as m, i.e. the data size of each group, is big enough".
- Section IV.C: dictionary to network models. Null = Erdős–Rényi (statistic: number of links); 2 × k alternative = stochastic block model with k blocks (links per block), so SBM vs ER is the 2 × k test; partial configuration model for bipartite k × m networks (row sums) vs bipartite ER is 2 × k with group size m; the same for binary multivariate time series (units active/inactive per time step), where PCM vs ER tests non-stationarity vs stationarity. Highlighted regime: growing numbers of features where canonical/microcanonical ensemble equivalence breaks (Squartini et al. 2015; Zhang–Garlaschelli 2022).

## Setting and assumptions
- Data: discrete outcome space X; sufficient statistics c_j(x) with realisable value sets C_j ⊂ R^d (finite or countable); Ω_j(c) configuration counts. Microcanonical model: uniform on {x : c_j(x) = c}, parameter space C_j itself. Canonical model: P_can(x; θ) = e^{−θ·c(x)}/Z(θ), an exponential family with uniform carrier; for finite C the canonical space is R^d and the mean space is the interior of conv(C) (Barndorff-Nielsen 1978, Thm 9.2, under steepness) [giuffridaTestingMaximumEntropy2025].
- Null and alternative: both microcanonical (Section III.A) or both canonical (III.B), same carrier, different sufficient statistics c_0 ≠ c_1; typically Condition A (c_0 a function of c_1), in all examples a linear function (total count from per-group counts). This is the opposite of the exponential-family setting of grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024 and haoEValuesExponentialFamilies2025, where P and Q share the sufficient statistic and differ in carrier.
- Alternative handled through a universal distribution P̄_1: Bayes marginal with a regular prior (continuous, strictly positive on a convex parameter space) or NML; no simple-alternative analysis beyond the regret definition.
- Priors in the examples are independent across groups (product form), which makes W*_0 a convolution; Theorem 1 needs regular priors and INECCSI subsets; the regret rate eq. 87 additionally needs w_pseudo,0 to have a continuous density and r′ → 0.
- Type-I guarantees are exact and nonasymptotic (E_0[S^GRO_mic] = 1 under every microcanonical and canonical null element). Optimality claims for the canonical test are asymptotic and partly heuristic (Theorem 1 only bounds set probabilities); e-power comparisons are by simulation.
- Optional continuation (multiplying e-values over independent batches with data-dependent choices of what to test next) is the stated motivation; it holds because each S is an e-variable. Optional stopping within a sample is not addressed: no filtration, e-process or test-martingale statement appears. Note (not stated in the paper): since S^GRO_mic = P̄_can,1(x)/[P̄_can,1(c_0(x))/Ω_0(c_0(x))] = P̄_1(x | c_0(x))/P_can,0(x | c_0(x)), the microcanonical GRO e-variable is exactly the conditional e-variable S_COND of haoEValuesExponentialFamilies2025 (eq. 1.6 there) with Z = c_0 and a Bayes-mixture or NML numerator, which that paper shows is in general not an e-process in the data filtration.

## Open questions / gaps
- The microcanonical approximation is justified only heuristically: Theorem 1 controls probabilities of sets, not the expected log-density difference r (Section III.C). A formal proof that r → 0, and conditions on how c_0 depends on c_1 beyond linearity, are open.
- The exact canonical GRO e-variable (RIPr prior w*_0 on the mean-value space) has no general analytic or numerical solution; it was computed numerically only for small 2 × 2 samples (Figure S2).
- Minimax-regret e-variables are "generally infeasible"; the paper settles for the (d_1/2) log m + O(1) rate from universal numerators (Section II.A).
- Which prior is worst-case-regret optimal for these tests? Jeffreys (γ = 1/2) is MDL-minimax but regret-suboptimal; γ > 1 attains the (1/2) log m rate; no optimum identified (Section IV.A, Figure 5). Compare the γ ≈ 0.18 REGROW prior of turnerGenericEvariablesExact2024 for the sequential block e-variable, a different criterion and e-variable.
- Regime of growing k: r does not vanish when the group size decays as O(1/k); no theory for which (k, m) scalings preserve near-optimality (Figure S6).
- Optional stopping and e-process-ness of S^GRO_mic are not discussed; by the correspondence with the conditional e-variable of haoEValuesExponentialFamilies2025 one expects failure in the data filtration.
- Extending the e-value/MDL link beyond two models (Section V, Conclusion).
- Only binary (2 × k) tables are worked out; general l × k tables, weighted networks, and MEMs beyond count constraints (degree sequences, reciprocity) are described as covered by the general formulas but not instantiated.
