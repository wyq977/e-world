# Safe testing

JRSSB discussion paper, read before the Royal Statistical Society on 24 January 2024 (Robin Evans in the chair). Received Dec 2021, accepted Mar 2023, advance access 7 Mar 2024. First arXiv version June 2019 (where the objects were still called "S-values"). The published text includes 20 discussion contributions and the authors' reply (pp. 1129-1171).

## Summary
An e-variable is a nonnegative statistic E with E_P[E] <= 1 for every P in the null H0; the threshold test "reject iff E >= 1/alpha" has type-I error <= alpha by Markov, and products of conditional e-variables from successive studies remain e-variables even when the decision to run study m+1 depends on the e-values of studies 1..m ("optional continuation", OC) [grunwaldSafeTesting2024]. The paper's claimed novelty is a single theorem in four increasingly general forms (Theorem 1): for any alternative Q with finite KL distance to the convex hull of the null, the e-variable maximising E_Q[log E] over all e-variables exists, is essentially unique, and equals q/p*_0 where P*_0 is the reverse information projection (RIPr) of Q on the Bayes marginals {P_W0 : W0 a prior on Theta_0}; its optimal growth rate equals the KL divergence D(Q || P*_0) [grunwaldSafeTesting2024]. This "GRO" (growth-rate optimal) criterion is extended to composite alternatives without a prior via a maximin criterion GROW (worst-case growth) and a regret-type criterion REGROW (growth relative to the oracle GRO rate), and to coarsened data and convex prior sets so as to cover nuisance parameters with a group structure. Worked examples: 1-parameter exponential families with a minimum effect size (GROW e-variable is a point-vs-point likelihood ratio), the 2x2 contingency table (RIPr is a degenerate prior on the pooled success probability; REGROW needs numerics), and the one-sample t-test (the right-Haar/Jeffreys-scale Bayes factor is an e-variable and is GROW for any prior on the effect size with 2+epsilon moments). Sections 5-6 analyse the difference between optional continuation (study level) and optional stopping (data level), the "sequentially decomposable" property that permits both, and sample-size competitiveness: fixed-n GROW tests need about 2.2x the Neyman-Pearson sample size at alpha=0.05, but under optional stopping the expected sample size to reach 80% power is below the NP fixed sample size. The closing sections argue that e-values unify Fisherian (evidence), Neyman-Pearson (error control) and Jeffreysian (Bayes factor) testing.

## Main results

### Definitions (Sections 1-4)
- **e-variable / e-value** (eq. 1): nonnegative E with E_P[E] <= 1 for all P in H0; "sharp" if expectation equals 1. For simple H0 = {P0}, the e-variables are exactly the likelihood ratios q/p0 with q a sub-probability density (eq. 9) [grunwaldSafeTesting2024].
- **Threshold test / safety under OC**: reject iff E >= 1/alpha. A process (E_(m)) is "safe under OC w.r.t. type-I error" if P(exists m: E_(m) >= 1/alpha) <= alpha for all P in H0 (eq. 7) [grunwaldSafeTesting2024].
- **Conditional e-variable / conditional e-variable collection** (Definition 1): E_(m) is F_(m)-measurable with E_P[E_(m) | F_(m-1)] <= 1 a.s. for all P in H0. The filtration may be coarser or finer than the data (side information, covariates; Appendix B.1).
- **Test (super)martingale, e-process** (Prop. 2): the running product of conditional e-variables is a nonnegative supermartingale with initial value <= 1 under every P in H0; any such process is an e-process (stopped value at any stopping time is an e-variable). The authors note (post-2019) that for some composite H0 useful e-processes exist that are not test martingales (ramdasTestingExchangeability2021) and cannot be built by their product construction.
- **GRO e-variable** (Section 2.1, eq. 12): for simple alternative P_theta1, the e-variable maximising E_theta1[log E] over all e-variables for Theta_0; GRO(theta1) := sup E_theta1[log E] = D(P_theta1 || P*_0). **W1-GRO**: same with Q the Bayes marginal P_W1 under a prior W1 on Theta_1 (Section 2.2).
- **RIPr (reverse information projection)**: P*_0 attaining inf_{W0} D(Q || P_W0) over all Bayes marginals of the null; may be a sub-probability measure in principle (Harremoes, personal comm.), is a proper P_{W*0} in all the paper's examples.
- **GROW e-variable** (Section 3, eq. 16): maximises the worst-case growth inf_{theta in Theta_1} E_theta[log E].
- **REGROW e-variable relative to offset f** (Section 4, eq. 19-21): maximises inf_{theta in Theta_1} (E_theta[log E] - f(theta)); with f(theta) = GRO(theta) it is a minimax pseudo-regret e-variable (regret = loss in expected log capital from not knowing theta1).
- **e-specification, plug-in vs sequential application, seqdec** (Section 5): an e-specification is a family s[1], s[2], ... of functions giving an e-variable for each sample size n. It is "sequentially decomposable" (seqdec) w.r.t. a filtration if s[n](Y^n) = prod_{i<=n} s_i(Y^i) with the s_i conditional e-variables. Plug-in application uses s[N(m)] on study m's batch; sequential application continues the single-stream martingale across studies.
- **Coherence (local vs global GRO)** (Section 5): applying a seqdec specification sequentially to two studies gives the same e-value as treating them as one concatenated study; plug-in application is coherent only when additionally s_i = s' for all i (e.g. Example 4).

### Theorems and propositions
- **Proposition 1**: p[e] := 1/E is a conservative p-value; P(E >= 1/alpha) <= alpha. Reciprocals of standard p-values are not e-variables; typically p << 1/E on the same data [grunwaldSafeTesting2024].
- **Proposition 2 + Corollary 1 ("Ville-Robbins")**: running products of conditional e-variables are test supermartingales, hence e-processes; Ville's inequality gives P(exists m: E_(m) >= 1/alpha) <= alpha, i.e. type-I safety under OC regardless of the continuation rule, which may be externally imposed or unknowable [grunwaldSafeTesting2024]. Example 1: for simple H0, multiplying Bayes-factor e-values with the posterior reused as the next prior reproduces Bayesian updating (eq. 8); any other data-dependent prior ("local perspective") is also allowed, as long as batches are disjoint.
- **Theorem 1, simplest version** (Section 2; proof Appendix A.1 via Li 1999 / Grünwald & Mehta 2020): if Q has full support and inf_{W0} D(Q || P_W0) < infinity, then E* = q/p*_0 is an e-variable and essentially uniquely sup_{E} E_Q[log E] = E_Q[log E*] = inf_{W0} D(Q || P_W0) = D(Q || P*_0) (eq. 11); if the infimum is attained by W*0 then P*_0 = P_{W*0}. Special case (discrete outcomes, simple H1, convex H0) already in Zhang, Glancy & Knill (2011).
- **Corollary 2**: E* is the only e-variable of likelihood-ratio form q/p_W0; hence (a) W*0 is the essentially unique KL minimiser, and (b) finding any e-variable q/p_W0 identifies W0 = W*0. Consequence (Section 8): projecting with any other divergence D' cannot yield an e-variable unless it coincides with the KL projection, so non-GRO-optimal e-variables are never of the projection form.
- **Theorem 1, first generalisation** (Section 3): under finite pairwise KL and full support on Theta_1, if the joint minimum inf_{W1, W0} D(P_W1 || P_W0) is attained at W*1, then E* = p_{W*1}/p*_0 is GROW and its worst-case growth equals D(P_{W*1} || P*_0): the GROW e-variable is the Bayes factor between the two components of the joint information projection (Csiszár-Tusnády; Figure 1).
- **Proposition 3** (Section 3.1; proof Appendix B.2): with monotone likelihood ratio in a 1-d statistic T and Theta_0 = {theta <= delta-}, Theta_1 = {theta >= delta+}, the GROW e-variable is p_{delta+}/p_{delta-}: both optimal priors are point masses. Example 4 applies this to 1-parameter exponential families in mean-value parameterisation.
- **Theorem 1, further generalisation** (Section 4): adds a bounded offset f on Theta_1; the REGROW e-variable E_f = p_{W*1}/p*_0 attains sup_E inf_theta (E_theta[log E] - f(theta)) = D(P_{W*1} || P*_0) - f(W*1) (eq. 20). For nested models with simple null, eq. 21 collapses to the redundancy-capacity theorem (eq. 22); W*1 converges to Jeffreys' prior (Clarke & Barron 1994) and the regret is (d/2) log n + O(1) (Example 5). For the 2x2 table with Theta_1 = (0,1)^2, numerics confirm (1/2) log n + O(1) (Example 6, citing turnerGenericEvariablesExact2024).
- **Nuisance-parameter recipe** (Section 4.2, eq. 23-24): for each fixed delta >= delta-, take the REGROW e-variable E*_delta relative to f = GRO over the nuisance gamma; then apply GROW on the meta-level over {E*_delta : delta >= delta-}. In the 2x2 Example 7 (effect = risk difference, delta- = 0, n_a = n_b) the result again puts a point mass on delta+; W*0 and W*1 have finite support (Carathéodory; Appendix A.3).
- **Theorem 1, full generality** (Section 4.3, eq. 25-27; proof Appendix A.2 via Grünwald & Dawid 2004 saddle-point theorem and Topsøe 1979): allows a coarsening V = g(Y) and a convex set W_1 of priors on Theta_1; E_f = p'_{W*1}(V)/p*'_0(V) attains sup_E inf_{W in W_1} (E_{P_W}[log E] - f(W)). With constant f this is the "W_1-GROW" e-variable.
- **t-test result (eq. 28-29) and Theorem 2** (special case of Pérez-Ortiz et al. 2022 Thm 4.2 / Cor. 8.3): with V_i = Y_i/|Y_1|, the scale-free likelihood ratio p'_{W[delta]}(V)/p'_0(V) equals the Bayes factor with the improper right-Haar prior 1/sigma on both hypotheses and is a sharp e-variable for H0 = {N(0, sigma^2)}; if W[delta] has a finite (2+epsilon)-th moment, it is GROW relative to all product priors with marginal W[delta] on delta (eq. 30). Unknown whether this holds for Jeffreys' moment-less Cauchy prior. With point priors on delta+/delta- the REGROW-then-GROW safe t-test is p'_{delta+}(V)/p'_{delta-}(V), a ratio of noncentral t densities (Appendix B.3). Generalises to amenable group invariances (regression with g-priors, Weibull vs log-normal) as an "e-variant of Hunt-Stein"; in group-invariant settings GROW and REGROW coincide (Pérez-Ortiz et al. 2022, Prop. 4.4).
- **Section 5 (OC vs OS)**: OC concerns study-level martingales with free choice of the next conditional e-variable from the set E_(m); OS concerns data-level martingales with only stop/continue. Seqdec specifications give three properties: (i) study-level plug-in martingales are also martingales on the concatenated data stream, so OS is safe within studies; (ii) plug-in extends to non-constant stopping times N(m); (iii) the sequential application defines a single long-stream martingale. For the t-test, stopping times must be measurable w.r.t. the coarsened filtration sigma(V); otherwise "fake" conditional e-variables with E_P0[E_(m) | F_(m-1)] > 1 can be created and OC safety is lost (Pérez-Ortiz et al. 2022, App. B). Example 9: for the 2x2 table with paired design, the REGROW prior depends on n (not seqdec), but a Beta(0.18, 0.18) prior updated sequentially on batches of size 2 is numerically almost REGROW for all but the smallest n (eq. 31) [grunwaldSafeTesting2024].
- **Section 6 (competitiveness; Appendix B.4)**: (a) evidential planning: n_GROW = ceil(L / D(P_delta || P_0)) for target growth L, = ceil(2L/delta^2) for Gaussian location. (b) fixed-n power 1-beta against mean >= delta: n_NP = (z_alpha + z_beta)^2/delta^2 (= 6.18/delta^2 at alpha=0.05, beta=0.2); n_grow-fixed = c_alpha n_NP with c_alpha = 2(-log alpha)/z_alpha^2, c_0.05 ~ 2.2, c_0.01 ~ 1.7, c_alpha -> 1 as alpha -> 0 (eq. 32); the W1-GRO Bayesian e-variable needs a further logarithmic factor. (c) under OS with the aggressive rule "stop when E_n >= 1/alpha or n = n_max", Wald/Breiman give E_delta[tau_1] ~ (-log alpha)/D(P_delta || P_0) = 2(-log alpha)/delta^2, already below n_NP at 80% power; with n_max(0.8) = C/delta^2, Brownian-motion limits for delta -> 0 give C_{0.2,0} = 8.5936 (worst case) and C'_{0.2,0} = 4.971 (expected), vs 6.18 for NP (Figure 4). Experiments with the R package `safestats` (z-, logrank, t-, 2x2 tests) confirm: less data on average, more in the worst case. The authors call the resulting incentive problem a "Tragedy of the Commons" for adoption [grunwaldSafeTesting2024].
- **Section 7 (history, p-to-e)**: e-variables trace to Ville (1939) test martingales, Levin (1976) "tests of randomness", Zhang et al. (2011) "prediction-based ratio"; the authors' own earlier name was "S-value". Sequential (Wald) tests use two-sided e-variables and fixed stopping thresholds (1-beta)/alpha, so do not permit OS in the paper's sense. Calibrator f(p) = 1/sqrt(p) - 1 maps p = 0.05 to e = 3.47 and requires p <= 1/441 = 0.0023 for e >= 20; with it, power-80% sample size is ~3.0 n_NP vs ~2.2 n_NP for the GROW likelihood ratio.
- **Section 8 (critique of GRO)**: GRO's justification rests on independence across studies and bounded variance of log E (otherwise long "draw-downs"); Breiman's optimal-stopping-time argument needs i.i.d. data and seqdec. REGROW yields anytime-valid CIs of width O(log n / n), whereas switching (van Erven et al. 2012) or stitching (howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021) achieve O(log log n / n); whether such constructions are variants of GRO is open.
- **Section 9**: e-values satisfy NP's type-I requirement (extended over an unplanned sequence of studies, where power is "upgraded to power one" and GRO replaces it), Fisher's alternative-free evidence reading (with a monetary interpretation and OC validity), and are Bayes factors with matched priors; differences from Bayes: guarantees hold under every P in H0 rather than under the prior, and partial or prequential likelihoods are admissible.

## Setting and assumptions
- Null and alternative are arbitrary sets {P_theta : theta in Theta_0}, {P_theta : theta in Theta_1} of distributions or processes for a sample Y = Y^N; N may be a stopping time. Everything in Theorem 1 assumes densities w.r.t. a common measure mu (nonparametric settings without densities are excluded and deferred to Waudby-Smith & Ramdas, Orabona & Jun).
- Theorem 1 conditions: Q (or every P_theta, theta in Theta_1) has full support; inf_{W0} D(Q || P_W0) < infinity; for composite H1, pairwise finite KL on Theta_1 and existence of the minimising W*1 (strong in general, verified for all examples in Appendix A.3; boundary points of exponential-family parameter spaces must be excluded from Theta_1 but may be in Theta_0). Priors are always proper; the right-Haar improper prior enters only through the equivalence (29).
- Study batches must be disjoint data; e-values on overlapping data may be averaged (vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021) but not multiplied.
- OC safety needs only conditional e-variables w.r.t. the study-level filtration; OS safety within a study needs a seqdec specification and stopping times adapted to the (possibly coarsened) filtration used by the e-variable.
- 2x2 table: design X^n fixed in advance, N_1 random; Theta_0 = [0,1], Theta_1 = (0,1)^2. t-test: Y_1 != 0 assumed; H0 = {N(0, sigma^2)^n : sigma > 0}.
- Section 6 computations are for the Gaussian location family with known unit variance; the authors state they generalise to 1-d exponential families.

## Discussion and authors' reply (brief)
Proposer R. Wang: e-power E_Q[log E] can be -infinity for e-variables that reject with probability 0.99 (Pareto examples) and +infinity for ones that shrink to 0, so GRO needs caveats; combining positive-e-power tests keeps positive e-power. Seconder R. Martin: asks exact vs asymptotic growth optimality (predictive recursion + UI), and proposes treating the stopping time as a nuisance parameter and optimising only over plausible stopping rules. Larsson, Ramdas & Ruf: the "numeraire" e-variable generalises GRO/RIPr with no regularity conditions, via E_Q[X/X*] <= 1. Vovk: finite-KL condition fails for infinite sequences and exchangeability nulls; conformal testing / Bayes-Kelly. Bickel: GRO e-variables are not logically coherent across nested nulls. Ly: GROW point priors keep delta+ inside the AV confidence interval forever, so test and CI cannot be both well-behaved and consistent; REGROW avoids this at the cost of informed effect-size priors (reply: exploring nonlocal priors). Hennig, Pawel & Held: "safe" is marketing language; outliers can zero out a product; interim analyses threaten blinding (reply: OC across studies, not interim analysis, is the point; use (1-gamma)E + gamma for robustness; the name was unfortunate). Greenland: chi-square example where naive e-values have no power (reply: likelihood-ratio e-variables; UMP Bayes factor at delta* = 7.378 rejects at p <= 0.007). Bon & Robert: links to objective Bayes and least favourable priors; worry about point-mass RIPr. Mulder: fractional Bayes factors appear to satisfy the e-variable constraint for the t-test numerically. Pace & Salvan: strong likelihood principle; suggest threshold (1-alpha)/alpha under likelihood-ratio symmetry (reply: partial answer in Grünwald 2023, e-posterior, eq. 31). Dey, Martin & Williams: generalised UI with empirical risk for misspecified models. Also: Dickhaus (multiple testing, Bayes-factor-to-e calibration), Xu (f-divergence and information-processing view), Cattaneo (minimum-e-value estimators under OC), Rizzelli (empirical-Bayes plug-in e-variables are not e-variables; reply: renormalise by sup_H0 E_P, turning redundancy into regret), ter Schure (cumulative power plots, no SESOI needed), Srakar, Tian-Yu-Wang, Wen & Dowe (MML priority), Chai (0.8 always means power).

## Open questions / gaps
- Computing W*0 (and W*1) in general: existence with finite support follows from Carathéodory for finite Y, but no general fast algorithm is given; accuracy of numerical approximation for infinite Y is unclear (Section 2).
- Does the RIPr ever fail to be a probability measure for a practically relevant H0? (Section 2; Harremoës' construction is artificial.)
- Is the right-Haar t-test Bayes factor GROW under Jeffreys' Cauchy prior (no second moment)? (Section 4.3.)
- When does a GRO criterion admit a seqdec (or epsilon-seqdec) specification, so that OS is possible within studies? (Section 5, eq. 31, Example 9.)
- Can switching/stitching constructions with O(log log n / n) rates be understood as optimising a variant of GRO; what "meta-GRO" criteria are reasonable; low-variance alternatives to the global GRO e-variable (Section 8).
- Conditional e-variables as building blocks vs general e-processes under OC (switching between e-variable types across studies) (Section 1.3).
- From the reply: regularity-free versions (numeraire, description gain), filtration coarsening as a design principle, Rényi projections and non-log utilities, UMP Bayes factors and nonlocal priors as GROW/REGROW compromises, empirical-e renormalisation, minimum-e-value estimators under OC, misspecification and Gibbs-posterior analogues, the (1-alpha)/alpha threshold under likelihood-ratio symmetry.
