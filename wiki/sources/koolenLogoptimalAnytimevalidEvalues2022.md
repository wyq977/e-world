# Log-optimal anytime-valid E-values

Short paper (14 pages) in the IJAR special issue in honour of Glenn Shafer (Probability and Statistics: Foundations and History). Received 20 Feb 2021, accepted 16 Sep 2021, online 24 Sep 2021. The paper gives no arXiv identifier.

## Summary
The paper asks which anytime-valid e-value (the authors' name for a safe e-process) one should use against a composite null H when the sample size is decided by an external, possibly randomised, stopping rule [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022]. It takes the GROW criterion of grunwaldSafeTesting2024 (maximise expected log evidence under the alternative, designed for fixed n) and the anytime-valid e-value concept of Ramdas, Ruf, Larsson and Koolen (2020) and combines them into Problem 3: fix an alternative (Q, σ) consisting of a distribution Q and a randomised stopping time σ, and maximise E_Q[ln E_σ] over all anytime-valid e-values E for H; the optimum is called the log-optimal anytime-valid e-value (LOAVEV) and its value the implied target, after Shafer (2021) [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022]. Three equivalence results characterise the optimum: a primal representation as a pointwise infimum over a collection of test martingales, one per null P (Lemma 4); a primal-dual equivalence showing the implied target equals the minimum KL divergence from Q^σ to Bayesian mixtures of stopped null distributions P^τ, a reverse information projection jointly over P in H and stopping times τ (Theorem 5); and a "flow" reparametrisation that turns the dual into a concave programme with only singly exponentially many variables in the horizon T (Proposition 6, Lemma 7) [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022]. The LOAVEV is a plain likelihood ratio, independent of σ and a test supermartingale under every null element, when the null is a point (Theorem 8), when the null is zero-mean Gaussians of unknown variance against a fixed Gaussian (Theorem 10, Lemma 11), and more generally whenever the single-outcome reverse information projection of an i.i.d. alternative onto the convex hull of an i.i.d. null lands inside the null (Theorem 12), which covers convex nulls, 2x2 contingency tables and one-sided exponential-family intervals; for a deterministic σ the problem collapses to the batch GROW problem at sample size σ (Corollary 9). A numerical example with five Bernoulli nulls, a two-point Bernoulli mixture alternative and a uniform stopping time (T = 3 and T = 6) shows that in general the LOAVEV is order-dependent rather than a function of sufficient statistics, that it can lose evidence deterministically and regain it later (so it is not a supermartingale), and that the dual mixture puts mass only on the extreme points of the null [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].

## Main results
- **Definition 1 (anytime-valid e-value)**: a nonnegative process E on X^{≤T} with E_{P^τ}[E] ≤ 1 for every randomised stopping time τ (equivalently, by linearity, every deterministic one); for a class H, this must hold for every P in H [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Proposition 2 (type-I error control)**: for any P in H, any α in (0,1) and any randomised stopping time τ, P(E_τ ≥ 1/α) ≤ α E_P[E_τ] ≤ α (Markov plus Definition 1); this includes greedy stopping at the first t with E(x^t) ≥ 1/α, and 1/E is an anytime-valid p-value [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Problem 3 (LOAVEV)**: given null H and alternative (Q, σ), maximise E_Q[ln E_σ] over E in E_H, the set of anytime-valid e-values for H; the maximum is the implied target. Motivated by a doubling-rate argument: over n i.i.d. replications of the whole stopped experiment, evidence grows like exp(n E_Q[ln E_σ]) [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022, Section 2.2].
- **Section 2.3, Figure 1**: game tree for binary X and T = 2; E labels the terminal nodes, an opponent controls the stop/continue nodes and any P in H controls the chance nodes; the Problem is already non-trivial here [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Lemma 4 (primal representation)**: sup_{E in E_H} E_Q[ln E_σ] = sup over collections (M^P)_{P in H} of test martingales of E_{Q^σ}[ln inf_{P in H} M^P(x^m)]. Proof: every anytime-valid e-value is dominated by a P-test martingale for each P (Ramdas et al. 2020, Cor. 24(2)), and inf_P M^P is anytime-valid. Reduces the number of variables for finite X from doubly to singly exponential in T [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Theorem 5 (primal-dual equivalence; Appendix A)**: for finite X, sup_{E in E_H} E_Q[ln E_σ] = inf_{P̄ in B} KL(Q^σ || P̄), where B is the set of Bayesian mixtures E_{(P,τ)~w}[P^τ] of stopped null distributions under a joint prior w on P in H and randomised τ. The optimiser has the form E(x^m) = Q^σ(x^m) / ∫ w(P,τ) P^τ(x^m) d(P,τ). The finite-alphabet restriction can be relaxed under regularity conditions on H (a minimax theorem suffices) [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Reduction to batch viewpoint (after Theorem 5)**: Problem 3 is the batch GROW problem of grunwaldSafeTesting2024 on outcome space X^{≤T} with alternative Q^σ and null H' = {P^τ : P in H, τ randomised}; of limited practical use because H' has about 2^{|X|^{T-1}} stopping times and no general batch solution exists [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Proposition 6 (flow representation)**: for fixed P, randomised stopping times τ are in bijection with "flows" φ: X^{<T} → [0,1] with φ(empty) ≤ 1 and φ(x^{m-1}) P(x_m | x^{m-1}) ≥ φ(x^m) 1_{m<T}, via φ(x^m) = P(X^m = x^m, τ > m); the stopped distribution is P^φ(x^m) = φ(x^{m-1}) P(x_m | x^{m-1}) − φ(x^m) 1_{m<T} (and 1 − φ(empty) at m = 0) [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Lemma 7 (dual representation; Appendix B)**: inf_{P̄ in B} KL(Q^σ || P̄) equals the minimum over φ: H × X^{<T} → [0,1] of Σ_{1≤m≤T, x^m} Q^σ(x^m) ln [Q^σ(x^m) / Σ_P (φ(P,x^{m-1}) P(x_m|x^{m-1}) − φ(P,x^m) 1_{m<T})] subject to Σ_P φ(P, empty) = 1 − Q^σ(empty) and the flow inequalities for each P. Concave in φ with |H| (|X|^T − 1)/(|X| − 1) variables [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Theorem 8 (singleton null)**: for H = {P} and any (Q, σ), the likelihood ratio E_t = Q_t/P_t is LOAVEV regardless of σ (proof via Lemma 4 and a one-sided derivative argument on mixtures) [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022, Section 4.1].
- **Corollary 9 (deterministic σ)**: if σ is deterministic, sup_{E in E_H} E_Q[ln E_σ] = inf_{w prior on H} KL(Q^σ || ∫ P^σ w(dP)): anytime-validity drops out and one is back in the batch setting of grunwaldSafeTesting2024 with n replaced by σ [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022, Section 4.2].
- **Theorem 10 (zero-mean Gaussian null, unknown variance)**: for H = {N(0, ξ²): ξ² > 0} and Q = N(μ, ρ²), the LOAVEV is the likelihood ratio of N(μ, ρ²) to N(0, ρ² + μ²) (eq. 3), i.e. E(x^n) = ((μ²+ρ²)/ρ²)^{n/2} exp(U_n/(2(μ²+ρ²)) − (U_n − 2μS_n + nμ²)/(2ρ²)); the RIPr prior is the point mass at ξ² = ρ² + μ². Implied target at fixed n: (n/2) ln((μ²+ρ²)/ρ²) [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022, Section 4.3].
- **Lemma 11**: the e-value of eq. 3 is a test supermartingale under every N(0, ξ²) in the null; the expected multiplicative increment is quasi-concave in ξ² with maximum 1 at ξ² = ρ² + μ² [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Comparison with the ML e-value (Section 4.3)**: E_ml = P_{μ,ρ²}(x^n)/max_ξ² P_{0,ξ²}(x^n) = (U_n/(nρ²))^{n/2} exp(−(U_n − 2μS_n + n(μ²−ρ²))/(2ρ²)) is anytime-valid but has a lower implied target; at μ = 1, ρ² = 1, n = 10 the targets are 3.46574 (LOAVEV) vs 3.07214 (ML) [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Theorem 12 (point reverse information projection)**: i.i.d. alternative Q with one-outcome q, i.i.d. null generated by a set P of one-outcome distributions; if inf_{p in conv(P)} KL(q || p) is attained by some p̃ in P, then E(x^m) = Π_i q(x_i)/p̃(x_i) is LOAVEV for every σ and a test supermartingale under every P in H. Key step: p̃ optimal in the convex hull iff E_{x~q}[p(x)/p̃(x)] ≤ 1 for all p in P, which equals E_{x~p}[q(x)/p̃(x)] [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022, Section 4.4].
- **Four instances of Theorem 12** (Section 4.4): (i) convex P, e.g. convex sets of Bernoullis; (ii) the Gaussian case of Section 4.3, where the null is not convex; (iii) 2x2 contingency tables with X = {0,1}², null p_{θ,θ} (independence with equal marginals), alternative p_{θ1,θ2}: the RIPr is p_{θ̄,θ̄} with θ̄ = (θ1+θ2)/2 (as in Turner 2019, Section 2.3), extending to M×K tables; (iv) one-parameter exponential family p_β(x) = e^{βx − φ(β)} r(x), null β in [a,b], alternative β1 < a: the RIPr is p_a and the LOAVEV is exp((β1 − a) Σ x_i − m(φ(β1) − φ(a))), by convexity of φ [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Section 4.5 (numerical example, CVX in MATLAB)**: H = {Ber(θ): θ in {0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6, 0.7}}, Q = ½ Ber(0.2) + ½ Ber(0.8) i.i.d. mixture, σ uniform on {0, ..., T} (conditional stop probability 1/(1+T−t)). Q^σ has full support on {0,1}^{≤T} so the objective is strictly concave and the LOAVEV unique. T = 3 (Figure 2): implied target 0.002413; E(001) = 0.807, E(010) = 0.890, E(100) = 1.029 although null, alternative and σ are all exchangeable. T = 6 (Figure 3): implied target 0.021367; E(110) = 0.814 vs E(011) = 0.923; multiplicative updates after a 0 from contexts 100 vs 010 are 1.182 vs 1.043; both depth-1 values are < 1, so the LOAVEV loses evidence deterministically and regains it later, which no supermartingale can do; the process stops evolving at some nodes before T even though σ does not stop there deterministically; the dual mixture is supported only on θ in {0.3, 0.7} [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- **Section 5 (discussion)**: composite alternatives H1 may be handled by universal-prior mixing or by a (stratified) worst-case approach as in grunwaldSafeTesting2024, after separating H1 from H; for two consecutive stopped experiments, the product E_prod = E(x^(1)) E(x^(2)) is safe even under adversarially changing P1, P2 in H (an imprecise-probability setting; Vovk and Wang 2021 on the product as e-merging function; Ramdas et al. 2021 on fork-convexity), while E_smart := inf_P M^P(x^(1)) M^P(x^(2)) ≥ E_prod is safe against a fixed P only; LOAVEVs are not sufficient-statistic based; reduced filtrations for group-invariant nuisance parameters are open; for unbounded σ, Proposition 2, Lemma 4, Theorems 8 and 12 stay valid, Theorem 5 remains plausible, Proposition 6 and Lemma 7 need redoing [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].

## Setting and assumptions
- Finite horizon T ≥ 1 throughout; outcome space X finite (X = {0,1}) or X = R in examples; distributions on X^T given by mass functions or densities; measure-theoretic issues deferred to Section 5 [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- Stopping times are randomised ("behavioural") processes τ: X^{≤T} → [0,1] with τ(x^T) = 1; they are closed under mixtures (needed for convex duality) and give a compact numerical representation (Section 2.1). The stopped distribution P^τ is eq. (1).
- Null H is an arbitrary set of distributions on X^T (composite); the alternative is a single pair (Q, σ) with Q not in H; several alternatives are merged into one by a Bayesian mixture (footnote 4). Optional stopping is exogenous and adversarial under the null; the alternative's stopping time σ is a prediction, not a constraint on how data will actually be stopped.
- Theorem 5 is proved for finite X (via a minimax theorem on finite X^{≤T}); Theorem 12 requires i.i.d. null and alternative and that the one-outcome RIPr over conv(P) be attained inside P; Theorem 10 requires μ fixed and ρ² known.
- Validity is at the level of a single stopped experiment; optional continuation across experiments is discussed only in Section 5 (product rule and E_smart).
- Natural filtration F_t = σ(X^t) throughout; coarser (group-quotient) filtrations are left open.

## Open questions / gaps
- General solution of Problem 3 beyond point nulls, deterministic σ and point-RIPr cases; the authors say the problem "remains ill understood" (Section 2.2) [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022].
- Extension of Theorem 5 to infinite X and to unbounded horizon (Proposition 6 and Lemma 7 would need to be redone) (Section 5).
- Whether any remnant of sufficient statistics survives (LOAVEVs are order-dependent in Section 4.5) and whether this can be exploited computationally (Section 5).
- LOAVEV theory under reduced filtrations for group-invariant nuisance parameters (Section 5).
- When the null "collapses to a point" beyond the i.i.d. case of Theorem 12 (Section 5).
- Whether assuming equal stopping rules τ1 = τ2 across experiments buys more than E_smart (Section 5).
- Choosing the alternative (Q, σ) for composite H1 without a prior: universal mixing vs separated worst case (Section 5).
- Cited works not in the project: Ramdas, Ruf, Larsson and Koolen (2020) on admissible anytime-valid inference and nonnegative martingales; Ramdas, Ruf, Larsson and Koolen (2021) on exchangeability and fork-convexity; Shafer, Shen, Vereshchagin and Vovk (2011) on test martingales; Shafer and Vovk (2019); Turner (2019) master's thesis.
