# Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences

Annals of Statistics 49(2), 2021. The file is arXiv v9 (6 August 2022), which postdates the journal version. Companion to Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe and Sekhon (2020, Probability Surveys), "Time-uniform Chernoff bounds via nonnegative supermartingales", whose sub-psi framework and linear boundaries this paper takes as given [howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021, Sec. 2].

## Summary
A (1-α)-confidence sequence is a sequence of sets (CI_t) with P(∀t ≥ 1 : θ_t ∈ CI_t) ≥ 1-α, where the estimand θ_t may itself change with t (eq. 1); the paper constructs such sequences whose width shrinks to zero at rate √(t^{-1} log log t) or √(t^{-1} log t), with nonasymptotic coverage under nonparametric conditions and with no restriction on the stopping rule (properties P1-P4) [howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021, Sec. 1]. The building block is the sub-psi condition of Howard et al. 2020: a centred process S_t with intrinsic time V_t is l_0-sub-psi if exp{λS_t - ψ(λ)V_t} is dominated by a nonnegative supermartingale L_t(λ) with E L_0 ≤ l_0 for each λ ∈ [0, λ_max) (Definition 1), and a "sub-psi uniform boundary with crossing probability α" is any u with P(∃t : S_t ≥ u(V_t)) ≤ α for all such pairs (Definition 2). Linear boundaries u(v) = log(l_0/α)/λ + ψ(λ)v/λ (Lemma 1, i.e. Ville's inequality applied to one exponential supermartingale) grow at O(V_t), so the paper's contribution is four ways of producing curved, sublinear boundaries: closed-form stitching over geometric epochs (Theorem 1), conjugate mixtures with explicit normal, beta-binomial, gamma-Poisson and gamma-exponential forms (Lemma 2, Propositions 5-10), discrete mixtures for numerical work (Theorem 2), and inverted stitching that bounds the crossing probability of a user-chosen concave boundary (Theorem 3). Stitched and discrete-mixture boundaries attain the law-of-the-iterated-logarithm rate √(2 V_t log log V_t) with explicit constants, while every continuous mixture boundary grows as √(v log v) (Proposition 2); the authors argue the latter is usually preferable in practice because it is tighter over the two to three orders of magnitude of sample size that matter (Sec. 3.5). Sub-Gaussian mixture boundaries are unimprovable (Proposition 4). Applications: an empirical-Bernstein confidence sequence for bounded observations proved by a new self-normalisation argument (Theorem 4), sequential estimation of the average treatment effect in the Neyman-Rubin model with adaptive randomisation (Corollary 2), a matrix finite LIL and covariance-matrix confidence sequences (Corollaries 3-5), and the one-parameter exponential family, where mixture boundaries recover Robbins' mixture SPRT (Corollary 6, Sec. 6). Section 6 proves the equivalence of uniform coverage, coverage at all stopping times and coverage at all random times (Lemma 3), and the duality between confidence sequences, always-valid p-values and sequential tests of power one.

## Main results

### Framework (Section 2)
- **Definition 1 (sub-psi)**: (S_t, V_t) adapted, S_0 = V_0 = 0; for each λ ∈ [0, λ_max) a supermartingale L_t(λ) with E L_0(λ) ≤ l_0 and exp{λS_t - ψ(λ)V_t} ≤ L_t(λ) a.s. V_t is "intrinsic time" and may be adapted rather than predictable; l_0 = 1 for scalars, l_0 = d for d×d matrices [howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021].
- **Five ψ functions**, all scaled so ψ''(0+) = 1: ψ_B,g,h (two-point/Bernoulli), ψ_N = λ²/2 (Gaussian), ψ_P,c (Poisson), ψ_E,c (exponential), ψ_G,c = λ²/(2(1-cλ)) (sub-gamma, Boucheron-Lugosi-Massart; c = 0 is sub-Gaussian). Figure 2 / Proposition 11 / Table 1 give the implications among them (any sub-gamma or sub-exponential boundary serves in all five cases after rescaling v ↦ u(av)).
- **Proposition 1**: if ψ is twice differentiable with ψ(0) = ψ'(0+) = 0, any sub-gamma or sub-exponential boundary family u_c gives a sub-psi boundary v ↦ u_{k1}(k2 v); this covers every mean-zero variable with a CGF near zero.
- **Lemma 1 (linear boundary, Howard et al. 2020 Thm 1)**: u(v) = log(l_0/α)/λ + ψ(λ)v/λ is a sub-psi uniform boundary. Width of the resulting confidence sequence does not shrink (Figure 1).
- **Example 1**: confidence sequence for a Gaussian variance with unknown mean; the centred sample variance is sub-exponential with scale 2 and V_t = 2t (Appendix H), tighter than Darling-Robbins 1967a.

### Curved boundaries (Section 3)
- **Theorem 1 (stitched boundary)**: for c ≥ 0, η > 1, m > 0 and increasing h with Σ 1/h(k) ≤ 1, S_α(v) = √(k_1² v ℓ(v) + k_2² c² ℓ(v)²) + k_2 c ℓ(v), with ℓ(v) = log h(log_η(v/m)) + log(l_0/α), k_1 = (η^{1/4}+η^{-1/4})/√2, k_2 = (√η+1)/2, is a sub-gamma uniform boundary via v ↦ S_α(v ∨ m) (eq. 8); moreover crossings after intrinsic time v_0 have probability ≤ Σ_{k ≥ ⌊log_η(v_0/m)⌋} α/h(k) (eq. 9). Proof: linear boundary per epoch η^k ≤ V_t < η^{k+1}, optimised at the geometric centre, union bound (Appendix A.1).
- **Polynomial stitching (eq. 10)**: h(k) = (k+1)^s ζ(s) gives S_α(v) = k_1 √(v (s log log(ηv/m) + log(ζ(s)/(α log^s η)))) + c k_2 (...), a finite LIL bound with leading constant s k_1² → 2. With η = 2, s = 1.4, m = 1 and 1-sub-Gaussian summands: P(∃t : S_t ≥ 1.7 √(t (log log(2t) + 0.72 log(5.2/α)))) ≤ α (eq. 11), i.e. the confidence sequence X̄_t ± 1.7 √((log log(2t) + 0.72 log(10.4/α))/t) (eq. 2). Exponential h gives O(√(v log v)); h ∝ k log^s k reproduces the Darling-Robbins/Robbins-Siegmund log log log terms (eq. 35).
- **Corollary 1 (asymptotic upper LIL)**: if ψ(λ) ~ λ²/2 as λ ↓ 0, lim sup S_t / √(2 V_t log log V_t) ≤ 1 on {sup V_t = ∞}.
- **Lemma 2 (method of mixtures)**: for any probability F on [0, λ_max), M_α(v) = sup{s : m(s,v) := ∫ exp{λs - ψ(λ)v} dF(λ) < l_0/α} is a sub-psi uniform boundary; proof is Ville's inequality for the mixture supermartingale ∫ L_t(λ) dF(λ) (Appendix A.3). Mixing over λ ∈ R gives two-sided boundaries directly.
- **Conjugate mixtures (Appendix A.3)**: Proposition 5, two-sided normal mixture u(v) = √((v+ρ) log(l_0²(v+ρ)/(α²ρ))) (eq. 14; Robbins 1970 example 2); Proposition 6, one-sided normal mixture with closed-form upper bound √(2(v+ρ) log(l_0 √((v+ρ)/ρ)/(2α) + 1)), within 0.7% of exact; Propositions 7-8, two- and one-sided beta-binomial mixtures for sub-Bernoulli processes via incomplete Beta functions (the Ville 1939 / Robbins 1970 / Shafer et al. 2011 Beta mixture generalised to range parameters g, h); Proposition 9, gamma-exponential mixture (truncated gamma on c^{-1}-λ) for sub-exponential processes; Proposition 10, gamma-Poisson mixture. All carry a common precision parameter ρ (mixing variance 1/ρ).
- **Proposition 2 (rate of any mixture boundary)**: if f is continuous and positive near 0 and ψ is regular, M_α(v) = √(v [c log(c l_0² v/(2π α² f(0)²)) + o(1)]), i.e. √(v log v) growth depends only on ψ and f at the origin (Laplace's method, Fulks 1951).
- **Theorem 2 (discrete mixture)**: discretising a nonincreasing density f on (0, λ̄] on the geometric grid λ_k = λ̄/η^{k+1/2} with weights w_k = λ̄(η-1) f(λ_k √η)/η^{k+1} gives DM_α(v) = sup{s : Σ w_k exp{λ_k s - ψ(λ_k)v} < l_0/α}, a valid conservative approximation (DM_α ≥ M_α) of the continuous mixture, accurate to 0.4% for the half-normal case (Appendix A.5). Appendix A.6: stitching with h is itself a discrete mixture with f(λ) = Θ(1/(λ h(log λ^{-1}))); polynomial stitching ↔ f_s^LIL(λ) ∝ 1/(λ log^s λ^{-1}) (eq. 85), Balsubramani's density.
- **Theorem 3 (inverted stitching)**: for any nonnegative strictly concave g and v_max, u = g(1 ∨ v) on v ≤ v_max is sub-Gaussian with crossing probability ≤ l_0 inf_{η>1} Σ_k exp{-2(g(η^{k+1}) - g(η^k))(η g(η^k) - g(η^{k+1}))/(η^k (η-1)²)} (eq. 19); e.g. P(∃t : 1 ≤ V_t ≤ 10^20, S_t ≥ 1.7 √(V_t (log log(e V_t) + 3.46))) ≤ 0.025 (eq. 20).
- **Proposition 3 (tuning)**: the normalised two-sided normal-mixture boundary u(v)/√v is minimised at m with m/ρ = -W_{-1}(-α²/(e l_0²)) - 1 (Lambert W); at α = 0.05, u(m)/√m ≈ 3.0 and u(100m)/√(100m) ≈ 3.6, so mis-tuning by two orders of magnitude costs little. Linear < mixture < polynomial stitching in how slowly they degrade away from the tuned time (Sec. 3.5, Figure 4).
- **Proposition 4 (unimprovability)**: for any exact 1-sub-Gaussian mixture boundary M_α, sup over sub-Gaussian pairs of P(∃t : S_t ≥ M_α(V_t)) = α (Donsker + Robbins-Siegmund 1970 Brownian result, Appendix C.2). Hence Figure 9's discrete mixture is a lower envelope showing the stitched bound loses little.

### Applications (Section 4)
- **Theorem 4 (empirical-Bernstein confidence sequence)**: X_t ∈ [a,b], any predictable predictions X̂_t ∈ [a,b], any sub-exponential boundary u with scale b-a: P(∀t : |X̄_t - μ_t| < u(Σ_{i≤t} (X_i - X̂_i)²)/t) ≥ 1-2α, where μ_t = t^{-1} Σ E_{i-1} X_i. Radius scales with the true standard deviation, no common mean needed. Proof: exp{λ Σ Y_i - ψ_E(λ) Σ (Y_i - δ_i)²} is a supermartingale (Fan-Grama-Liu 2015 inequality), avoiding the mean/variance union bound of Maurer-Pontil and Audibert et al. (Appendix A.8). Closed form (eq. 24): X̄_t ± [1.7 √((V̂_t ∨ 1)(log log(2(V̂_t ∨ 1)) + 3.8)) + 3.4 log log(2(V̂_t ∨ 1)) + 13]/t at 95%.
- **Corollary 2 (ATE in the Neyman-Rubin model)**: fixed potential outcomes in [0,1], adaptive Bernoulli assignment with P_t ∈ [p_min, 1-p_min], IPW pseudo-outcome X_t (eq. 25); any sub-exponential boundary with scale 2/p_min gives a (1-2α) confidence sequence for ATE_t = t^{-1} Σ (Y_i(1) - Y_i(0)). Nonasymptotic justification for the Johari et al. 2017 A/B-testing platform; about twice the CLT width over t = 10²-10⁵ (Figure 5).
- **Corollary 3 (matrix finite LIL)**: for an S^d-valued martingale with γ_max(ΔY_t) ≤ b, polynomial stitching with c = b/3 and l_0 = d bounds γ_max(Y_t) by k_1 √((V_t ∨ m) ℓ_{η,s}) + b k_2 ℓ_{η,s}/3, ℓ containing log(d ζ(s)/(α log^s η)) (eq. 28). **Corollary 4**: asymptotic matrix upper LIL lim sup γ_max(Y_t)/√(2 V_t log log V_t) ≤ 1 under i.i.d. or Bernstein increments. **Corollary 5**: covariance confidence sequence ‖Σ̂_t - Σ‖_op ≲ √(b log(d log t)/t) + b log(d log t)/t for ‖x_t‖₂ ≤ √b (eqs. 31-33), uniform analogue of Tropp 2015 at the price of log log t.
- **Corollary 6 (one-parameter exponential family)**: exp{λS_t(μ) - tψ_μ(λ)} is the likelihood ratio of θ(μ)+λ against θ(μ), so sub-ψ_μ boundaries (with reversed CGF for the other tail) give CI_t = {μ : -ũ_μ(t) < S_t(μ) < u_μ(t)} with coverage 1-α_1-α_2 (eq. 34); with mixture boundaries this is dual to Robbins' mixture SPRT.

### Simulations and duality (Sections 5-6)
- **Section 5 (Figure 7)**: Hoeffding normal mixture, beta-binomial, pointwise Bernoulli, empirical-Bernstein (gamma-exponential) and "naive self-normalised" strategies on Ber(0.5), Ber(0.01) and a heavy-tailed three-point law; the naive plug-in of empirical variance into the normal mixture (as in Johari et al. 2017) inflates the false-positive rate, while empirical-Bernstein is the only one that is both valid and tight in all three cases. Code: github.com/gostevehoward/cspaper, packages `confseq` (R, Python).
- **Lemma 3**: for adapted events A_t, P(∪A_t) ≤ α ⇔ P(A_T) ≤ α for all random times T ⇔ P(A_τ) ≤ α for all stopping times τ (possibly infinite). So definition (1), the Johari et al. 2015 stopping-time definition and a random-time definition coincide, generalising Zhao et al. 2016 Prop. 1.
- **Duality (Section 6)**: confidence sequences ↔ always-valid p-values (P_0(∃t : p_t ≤ α) ≤ α) ↔ sequential tests; a confidence sequence with u(v) = o(v) yields a test of power one (Darling-Robbins 1967b, Robbins 1970). Running intersection ∩_{s≤t} CI_s is valid when θ_t ≡ θ and never larger; the paper argues for non-intersected intervals when the estimand drifts, which the ATE_t formulation makes natural.
- **Section 7.1**: Robbins-Siegmund nonanticipating (plug-in) likelihood ratios and sequential GLR are the other SPRT extensions; the paper describes itself as building "safe confidence intervals" from nonparametric test supermartingales, in the language of Shafer et al. 2011 and Grünwald et al. 2019.
- **Appendices**: D, root-finding for mixture boundaries (A(v) is an interval); E, tuning λ̄ and η for discrete mixtures; F, change of units turns one boundary into a family (m is a free scale); G, Table 2 comparing the constants A, B, C in A√(t(log log Bt + C)) across Jamieson-Nowak, Balsubramani, Zhao et al., Darling-Robbins, Kaufmann et al. and this paper (polynomial stitching: 1.7, 2.04, 3.78; inverted stitching: 1.7, e, 3.45); I, extension to (2,D)-smooth Banach spaces (Corollary 7) and continuous-time martingales (Corollary 8); J, Tables 3-4 of sufficient sub-psi conditions for scalar and matrix processes.

## Setting and assumptions
- Data: any adapted real (or Hermitian-matrix, or Banach-valued) sequence; estimand μ_t = t^{-1} Σ_{i≤t} E_{i-1} X_i, so neither independence nor a common mean is needed. Validity rests entirely on Definition 1, verified via Tables 3-4 (bounded, Bernstein, Bennett, self-normalised, symmetric, parametric CGF conditions).
- One-sided boundaries control the upper tail of S_t (lower confidence sequence); the lower tail uses -S_t, possibly with a different ψ; two-sided sequences by union bound or by two-sided mixtures.
- Arbitrary stopping rules and unbounded horizon (P2, P3): the guarantee is time-uniform, hence holds at any random time (Lemma 3). Finite-horizon tightening is available only through inverted stitching (v_max) or by tuning m/ρ.
- Everything is nonasymptotic; the only asymptotic statements are the LIL corollaries (1, 4) and the rate Proposition 2.
- The empirical-Bernstein and ATE results require bounded observations and, for ATE, treatment probabilities bounded away from 0 and 1; the matrix covariance result needs ‖x_t‖₂ ≤ √b.

## Open questions / gaps
- Optimality beyond the sub-Gaussian unimprovability of Proposition 4: expected sample size to reject under parametric alternatives, and lower bounds for the matrix LIL (Corollary 4 has no matching lower bound) [howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021, Secs. 4.3, 7.2].
- Uniform anti-concentration (a nonasymptotic "lim inf" LIL); Balsubramani 2014 Thm 3 is the only pointer (Sec. 7.2).
- Group-sequential competitiveness: whether overshoot corrections (Lai-Siegmund, Siegmund 1985) carry over to nonparametric boundaries for finite horizons and grouped observations (Sec. 7.2).
- Choice between the √(v log log v) and √(v log v) rates is argued informally (Sec. 3.5) rather than by a decision-theoretic criterion; how this relates to GRO-type optimality is left open on both sides (see Safe testing Sec. 8).
- Not in the bib: Howard et al. 2020 (Probability Surveys), Darling and Robbins 1967a/b/1968, Robbins 1970, Robbins and Siegmund 1970, Lai 1976b, Johari et al. 2015/2017, Kaufmann and Koolen 2018, Balsubramani 2014, Balsubramani and Ramdas 2016, Shafer et al. 2011, de la Peña et al. 2004.
