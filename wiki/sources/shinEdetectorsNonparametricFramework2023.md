# E-detectors: a nonparametric framework for sequential change detection

arXiv 2203.03532v4 (29 October 2023), 50 pages including appendices. Published in The New England Journal of Statistics in Data Science, 2024 (version of record not in raw/). R code at github.com/shinjaehyeok/e_detector_paper [shinEdetectorsNonparametricFramework2023, footnote 1].

## Summary
The paper introduces the e-detector, a nonnegative adapted process M with E_{P,∞}[M_τ] ≤ E_{P,∞}[τ] for every stopping time τ and every pre-change distribution P in a class 𝒫, and shows that stopping at the first time M_n ≥ 1/α guarantees an average run length (ARL) E_{P,∞}N* ≥ 1/α uniformly over 𝒫 (Theorem 2.4) [shinEdetectorsNonparametricFramework2023]. E-detectors are built by starting a fresh e-process for 𝒫 at each time j ("e_j-process") and either summing them (Shiryaev–Roberts style) or taking their maximum (CUSUM style), so any e-process from the sequential-testing literature yields a change detector with nonasymptotic ARL control, with no i.i.d., parametric or common-reference-measure assumption, and with the changepoint and post-change law allowed to depend on the past (Definitions 2.5–2.6, Remark 2.1). When the e_j-processes are products of a common predictable "baseline increment" L_n (a composite supermartingale increment), the detectors admit the classical recursions M_n = L_n(M_{n−1}+1) and M_n = L_n max{M_{n−1},1} and are computable in O(1) per step (Section 2.4). To handle unknown post-change laws, mixtures of baseline e-detectors over a grid of tuning parameters, with a slowly growing number of components, are shown to remain e-detectors (Propositions 2.3, 3.3), and for exponential baseline increments exp{λ s(X_n) − ψ(λ) v(X_n)} the worst average delay is bounded by g_α/D(Q‖𝒫) + lower order with g_α ≲ inf_η η[log(1/α) + log(1 + ⌈log_η(ψ*(Δ_U)/ψ*(Δ_L))⌉)], matching the Lorden rate log(1/α)/KL(Q‖P) in the parametric case (Theorems 4.3, Corollary 4.6, Remark 4.4). Running examples detect a performance change of the Cleveland Cavaliers in the 2014–15 season from win indicators (Bernoulli with time-varying dependent means) and from bounded Plus-Minus scores, using only a boundedness assumption and a threshold of exactly log(1/α) with α = 10⁻³ (Section 5).

## Main results
- **Definition 2.2 (e-detector)**: M nonnegative adapted with E_{P,∞}[M_τ] ≤ E_{P,∞}[τ] for all stopping times τ and all P ∈ 𝒫; only stopping times of finite expectation matter [shinEdetectorsNonparametricFramework2023].
- **Proposition 2.3**: any mixture ∫ M^a dμ(a) of e-detectors over a fixed probability measure μ is an e-detector (linearity plus Tonelli).
- **Theorem 2.4 (ARL control)**: N* := inf{n ≥ 1 : M_n ≥ 1/α} satisfies inf_{P∈𝒫} E_{P,∞}N* ≥ 1/α. One-line proof: E N* ≥ E M_{N*} ≥ (1/α) P(N* < ∞) (Appendix A.1).
- **Definition 2.5 (e_j-process)**: Λ^{(j)} with Λ^{(j)}_1 = … = Λ^{(j)}_{j−1} = 1 and sup_{P∈𝒫} E_{P,∞}[Λ^{(j)}_τ | F_{j−1}] ≤ 1 for all stopping times τ, i.e. an [e-process](../concepts/e-process.md) started at time j; j = 1 is an ordinary e-process.
- **Definition 2.6 (SR and CUSUM e-detectors)**: M^SR_n = Σ_{j≤n} Λ^{(j)}_n and M^CU_n = max_{j≤n} Λ^{(j)}_n; both satisfy (6), and M^CU ≤ M^SR pointwise (Section 2.4 derivation, valid for general e_j-processes).
- **Remark 2.7**: if the e_j-processes start with initial weights summing to at most 1, the same constructions control the probability of ever falsely alarming, sup_P P_{P,∞}(τ < ∞) ≤ α, but then the delay grows logarithmically in the changepoint ν.
- **Definitions 2.8–2.9, 2.11 (baseline increments)**: a nonnegative adapted L with sup_P E_{P,∞}[L_n | F_{n−1}] ≤ 1 for each fixed n; predictable mixtures of baseline increments are baseline increments; Λ^{(j)}_n = Π_{i=j}^n L_i is a 𝒫-supermartingale, hence an e_j-process. Likelihood ratios p_{θ1}/p_{θ0}, or plug-in ratios p_{θ̂_{n−1}}/p_{θ0} with any predictable estimator, are baseline increments with equality.
- **Recursions (13)–(14)**: baseline SR and CUSUM e-detectors obey M^SR_n = L_n[M^SR_{n−1} + 1], M^CU_n = L_n max{M^CU_{n−1}, 1}, the classical Shiryaev–Roberts and Page recursions with likelihood ratios replaced by baseline increments.
- **Remark 2.10**: baseline e-detectors are a convenience; e-processes that are not supermartingales (e.g. [universal inference](../concepts/universal-inference.md), exchangeability e-processes) also give valid e-detectors by Theorem 2.4.
- **Definition 2.12 and Section 2.5 (e-SR, e-CUSUM)**: e-SR stops at M^SR_n ≥ 1/α; e-CUSUM at M^CU_n ≥ c_α with c_α ≤ 1/α. The threshold 1/α is tight for e-SR (trivial e-processes give M^SR_n = n) but can be very conservative for e-CUSUM (M^CU_n ≡ 1 there), and the gap can be made arbitrarily large as α → 0; with non-stationary pre-change data c_α = 1/α is the only available choice and e-SR is recommended since it always stops first at a common threshold (Remark 2.13).
- **Section 2.6 (instantiations)**: eight examples where e-detectors give the first or a cleaner nonparametric change detector: parametric likelihood ratios and mixtures, RIPr-based e-values multiplied over blocks (Example 1); change from exchangeability via conformal test martingales in a coarser filtration, ARL control surviving the filtration change (Example 2); two-sample homogeneity and independence via betting test martingales (Examples 3–4); log-concavity via a universal-inference e-process where no nontrivial supermartingale exists (Example 5); symmetry (Example 6); mean change under sub-Gaussian, bounded, few-moment or heavy-tailed conditions (Example 7); Huber-robust supermartingales under ε-corruption (Example 8).
- **Proposition 2.14 (delay, known Q)**: for baseline e-detectors with L_n = f(X_n, …, X_{n−m}) and i.i.d.-after-change (strongly stationary) post-change data independent of the past, J_P(N*_SR) ≤ J_L(N*_SR) ≤ E_{0,Q}N_{1/α} + m with N_c the one-sided test's stopping time; if m = 0 and i.i.d., E_{0,Q}N_c ≤ log c / E_{0,Q} log L_1 + Var_{0,Q}(log L_1)/(E_{0,Q} log L_1)² + 1 (Lorden's excess-over-boundary inequality plus Wald's identity, Appendix A.1). J_L is Lorden's worst conditional delay, J_P Pollak's; J_P ≤ J_L.
- **Section 3 (mixtures)**: optimal baseline parameter λ_op(Q) = argmax_λ E_{0,Q} log L^{(λ)}_1, with D(Q‖𝒫) := E_{0,Q} log L^{(λ_op)}_1, often equal to inf_{P∈𝒫} KL(Q‖P); the supremum over λ is not an e-detector, but the mixture Σ_k ω_k M_n(k) is, computable in O(K) per step (Remark 3.1, eqs 24–28).
- **Proposition 3.3 (adaptive e-detectors)**: with a scheduling function K(n) (number of active components, typically O(log n)) and re-weighting factors γ_j = 1/Σ_{k≤K(j)} ω_k ≥ 1, the adaptive SR/CUSUM e-detectors (29)–(30), with recursions M_n(k) = L_n(k)[M_{n−1}(k) + γ_n], remain valid e-detectors at O(K(n)) cost per step.
- **Theorem 3.4**: delay bounds for adaptive procedures, J_L(N*_aSR) ≤ min_{j>m}[E_{0,Q}N_{1/α}(j) + j − 1] with N_c(j) the mixture test's stopping time using the first K(j) components; for constant K it reduces to E_{0,Q}N_{1/α} + m.
- **Definition 4.1, Proposition 4.2 (exponential baseline increments)**: L^λ_n = exp{λ s(X_n) − ψ(λ) v(X_n)} with ψ convex, ψ(0) = ∇ψ(0) = 0; with μ = E_{0,Q}s, σ² = E_{0,Q}v, Δ_op = μ/σ², one has D(Q‖𝒫) = σ² ψ*(Δ_op) and λ_op = ∇ψ*(Δ_op), so D > 0 iff Δ_op ≠ 0. These are the sub-ψ increments of [howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021](howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021.md).
- **Algorithm 1 / Algorithm 3 (separated case, Δ_L < Δ_op < Δ_U known)**: computeBaseline returns a geometric grid λ_U = λ_0 > … > λ_{K_α} = λ_L via ψ*(Δ_k) = D_U η^{−k}, weights ω_0 ∝ e^{−g_α}1(g_α > v_min D_U), ω_k ∝ e^{−g_α/η}, and a threshold g_α = inf{g > log(1/α) : e^{−g}1(·) + min_k k exp{−g (D_U/D_L)^{−1/k}} ≤ α} (eqs 94–96; bisection in Algorithm 4). A single baseline suffices if log(1/α) ≤ v_min ψ*(Δ_L).
- **Theorem 4.3**: the resulting mixture e-SR and e-CUSUM control ARL at 1/α and, for i.i.d. post-change data, max{J_L(N*_mSR), J_L(N*_mCU)} ≤ g_α/D(Q‖𝒫) + Var_{0,Q}(log L^{(λ_op)}_1)/D(Q‖𝒫)² + 1; the same bound holds for J_P. Proof via the deterministic sandwich N_{c_α} ≤ N_{1/α} ≤ N̄_{g_α}, where N̄_g is the sequential GLR stopping time sup_{λ∈(λ_L,λ_U)} Σ log L^{(λ)}_i ≥ g (Lemma B.1), using the tangent-line geometry of Shin, Ramdas and Rinaldo (2021).
- **Proposition B.2 / eq. (48)**: for K_max large enough (condition (99); K_max = 1000 suffices in practice), g_α < inf_{η>1} η[log(1/α) + log(1 + ⌈log_η(ψ*(Δ_U)/ψ*(Δ_L))⌉)], so the leading term is O(log(1/α)/D(Q‖𝒫)) as α → 0.
- **Remark 4.4**: for a one-parameter natural exponential family with simple P and Q the bound is O(log(1/α)/KL(Q‖P)), matching Lorden's (1971) lower-bound rate.
- **Section 4.2, Lemma 4.5, Corollary 4.6 (non-separated case, only sign of λ_op known)**: an infinite grid Δ_0 > Δ_1 > … with a boundary function g(t) = g_{rα} + sη log(1 + log_η(t/(V_0 η^{K_L}) ∨ 1)) (eq. 59), importance weight r ∈ (0,1) splitting α between the guessed interval (Δ_L, Δ_0) and its complement, s from a zeta-function equation (60)–(61), and scheduling K(n) = K_L + ⌈m log_η n⌉ (eq. 63). Algorithm 2 runs in O(m log_η n) per step; Corollary 4.6 gives a three-case delay bound: the well-specified case equals Theorem 4.3 with α ↦ rα, Δ_op ≥ Δ_0 costs a factor ψ*(Δ_op)/ψ*(Δ_0), and Δ_op ≤ Δ_L costs an additive sη log(1 + K_op − K_L) in the numerator plus a [·]^{1/m} term.
- **Section 5.1 (Bernoulli, dependent means)**: 𝒫 = {p_n := E[X_n | F_{n−1}] ≤ p_0 = 0.49}, 𝒬 = {q_n ≥ q_0 = 0.51}; L^{(λ)}_n = exp{λ(X_n − p_0) − ψ_B(λ)} is a baseline increment for all λ ≥ 0; D(Q‖𝒫) = KL(q‖p_0) (eq. 67); with Δ_L = 0.02, Δ_U = 0.41, α = 10⁻³, K_max = 1000, computeBaseline returns 69 baselines; e-SR detects on 2015-02-26 and e-CUSUM on 2015-04-05 (Figure 2).
- **Section 5.2 (bounded mean shift)**: X_n ∈ [0,1] with conditional mean ≤ m before and ≥ m + δ after; L^λ_n = 1 + λ(X_n/m − 1), λ ∈ (0,1), from the variational formula KL_inf(Q; m) = sup_λ E_{0,Q} log(1 + λ(X/m − 1)) of Honda and Takemura (eqs 69–70), identical to the betting capital increments of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas (2023); its sub-exponential lower bound exp{λ(X/m − 1) − ψ_E(λ)(X/m − 1)²} with ψ_E(λ) = −log(1−λ) − λ is an exponential baseline increment with s(x) = x/m − 1, v(x) = (x/m − 1)², giving KL_inf ≳ (E_Q X − m)²/(2 E_Q(X − m)²) for small Δ_op (eq. 74) and the bounds Δ_L = mδ/(1−m)² ≤ Δ_op ≤ m(1−m)/δ² (eq. 75). Cavaliers Plus-Minus normalised by (x + 80)/160: m = 0.494, δ = 0.0125, Δ_L = 0.024, Δ_U = 1600, 190 baselines; e-SR detects at game 378 (2015-03-10), e-CUSUM at game 444 (2016-02-06) (Figure 3).
- **Section 5.3 (simulation)**: i.i.d. Bernoulli p_0 = 0.5 → p_1 = 0.6, ARL target 500, 5000 runs, ν ∈ {0, 100, …, 500}; oracle CUSUM with simulated exact threshold has worst average delay 91.3 ± 1.7, GLR with exact threshold 123.7 ± 2.7 (the worst, at O(n) cost per step), while e-SR with the universal log(1/α) threshold is competitive throughout and beats oracle CUSUM for ν near 500, and has the lowest pre-change false alarm fraction (Figures 4–5).
- **Section 6.1 (game-theoretic reading)**: the skeptic receives one extra dollar each day and invests it in a fresh 𝒫-e-process; the SR e-detector is the total wealth (sum over start times of the minimum wealth across P ∈ 𝒫), CUSUM the max–min wealth; see [testing by betting](../concepts/testing-by-betting.md).
- **Lemma 6.1 (generalised Lorden's lemma)**: starting a level-α sequential test at every time and alarming when any rejects has ARL ≥ 1/α, with no i.i.d. assumption and with tests allowed to differ across start times (Lorden 1971 needed both); the construction is the e-detector built from the two-valued e-processes Λ_t = 1(φ_t = 1)/α. General e-detectors detect earlier because evidence from different start times adds before any single e-process reaches 1/α.
- **Section 6.3 (future directions)**: minima and averages of e-detectors across streams give multi-stream e-detectors; kernel change detection and inference on the changepoint location are open.

## Setting and assumptions
- Pre-change class 𝒫 of distributions over infinite sequences, arbitrary (nonparametric, composite, non-i.i.d.); post-change class 𝒬 likewise. The changepoint ν may be a stopping time and Q may depend on F_ν; no assumption on ν or Q is needed for ARL control (Remark 2.1) [shinEdetectorsNonparametricFramework2023].
- The filtration may be the natural one, coarsened (nuisance parameters, conformal martingales) or enlarged by external randomness; ARL control holds whatever filtration defines the e-detector, though the e-detector property itself is relative to that filtration (Example 2).
- Error metric: ARL E_{P,∞}N* ≥ 1/α, i.e. false alarm rate ≤ α; not the probability of ever alarming (Remark 2.7 gives the trade-off).
- Delay bounds additionally assume post-change observations independent of the past and strongly stationary (Prop. 2.14, Thm 3.4), or i.i.d. from Q (Thm 4.3, Cor. 4.6), and baseline increments depending on at most m + 1 recent observations; the authors expect this to be relaxable.
- Exponential baseline increments require ψ convex, finite and strictly convex on Π ∋ 0, ψ(0) = ∇ψ(0) = 0, finite moments μ, σ², and Δ_op ≠ 0 for all Q ∈ 𝒬 (Section 4).
- Optional stopping of the monitoring is built in: the stopping rule is the object of study, and the e-detector may be "continuously monitored, stopped and easily interpreted" (Section 2.2).

## Open questions / gaps
- Delay bounds assume post-change data independent of the past and (strongly) stationary or i.i.d.; the authors expect relaxation (Remark 2.1, Section 2.7) but give none [shinEdetectorsNonparametricFramework2023].
- No tight threshold c_α < 1/α for e-CUSUM is available outside stationary parametric pre-change settings; the SR–CUSUM threshold gap can be arbitrarily large (Section 2.5). Whether e-CUSUM at 1/α has any optimality is open.
- The empirical observation that e-SR delay falls below oracle CUSUM for late changepoints (Figure 4) is unexplained; the authors conjecture it is the sum-versus-max effect and leave proof to future work (Section 5.3).
- Multi-stream e-detectors (min or average across streams), kernel-based e-detectors with nonasymptotic ARL, and inference on the location of the change are all left open (Section 6.3). Later work by Saha and Ramdas on non-partitioned e-detectors and the 2025–2026 multi-stream and bounded-mean papers are in the bib but not yet ingested.
- Backward confidence sequences (Shekhar and Ramdas 2023) are mentioned only as an alternative for unspecified change classes; the relationship between the two frameworks is not developed here.
