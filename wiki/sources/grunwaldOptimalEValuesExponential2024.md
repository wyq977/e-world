# Optimal E-values for exponential families: the simple case

## Summary
The paper asks when, for a composite null P that is a regular d-dimensional exponential family and a simple alternative Q, the growth-rate-optimal (GRO) e-variable is a "simple" e-variable, i.e. a plain likelihood ratio q(U)/p(U) against one element of P [grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024]. The device is to generate from Q a second exponential family Q̄ = {q_{β;μ*} ∝ exp(β^T t(U)) q(U)} with the same sufficient statistic X = t(U) as P; Q̄ contains Q and is the natural composite alternative in many examples. Theorem 1 gives eight equivalent conditions, the most usable being that Σ_p(μ) − Σ_q(μ) is positive semidefinite for every mean μ in M_q; when they hold, q_μ(U)/p_μ(U) is the global GRO e-variable with respect to Q_μ for every μ in M_q, so the reverse information projection (RIPr) of Q onto conv(P) is the single point mass P_{μ*} with the same mean of X as Q [grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024]. The proof moves from the canonical-parameter function f(β; μ*) = log Z_q − log Z_p (the log of the null expectation of the ratio) to the mean-value function g(μ; μ*) = D(P_μ‖P_{μ*}) − D(Q_μ‖Q_{μ*}) via convex duality, and shows that signs of second derivatives (covariances), first derivatives (canonical parameters) and the KL differences themselves are all equivalent. Examples recover the Gaussian-location, Poisson and Bernoulli k-sample e-values of Hao et al. 2024, the Gaussian scale test of de Jong 2021, Gaussian location with Σ_p − Σ_q ≥ 0, and a case-by-case recipe for pairs of natural exponential families via their variance functions (negative binomial and ABM families vs Poisson, Gamma vs Gamma, Tweedie power-variance pairs). The complementary situation Σ_p − Σ_q ≤ 0, where the RIPr is an n-dependent mixture over the null, is explicitly deferred and became [haoEValuesExponentialFamilies2025](haoEValuesExponentialFamilies2025.md).

## Main results
- Setting (Section 1.1–1.2): P = {P_μ : μ ∈ M_p} regular, mean-value parameterised, with variance function Σ_p(μ); canonical parameterisations p_{β;μ*} relative to any carrier p_{μ*} (eq. 1.1), all linearly related (eq. 1.2). Q has an MGF and a density q, and E_Q[X] = μ* ∈ M_p. The generated family Q̄ (eq. 1.3) has nonempty canonical domain B_{q;μ*} containing a neighbourhood of 0 and mean space M_q containing a neighbourhood of μ*; P and Q̄ are "matching" when M_q = M_p [grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024].
- Section 1.3, eqs 1.4–1.7: GRO e-variable is q/p_{RIPr(Q)} with growth rate inf_{P ∈ conv(P)} D(Q‖P) (from grunwaldSafeTesting2024, Thm 1); a simple e-variable exists iff inf over conv(P) equals min over P, i.e. iff the RIPr is a point mass. Since P_{μ*} minimises D(Q‖P) over P (Li 1999), the only candidate simple e-variable is q(U)/p_{μ*}(U).
- Proposition 1: if a simple e-variable relative to Q exists, it is the GRO e-variable (uniqueness via grunwaldSafeTesting2024, Thm 1, Cor. 1). Consequently ∏_{i≤n} q(U_i)/p(U_i) is GRO for every fixed n, and by koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022 (Thm 12) q(U^τ)/p(U^τ) maximises E[ln S_τ] over all processes with E[S_τ] ≤ 1 for any stopping time τ [grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024].
- Section 2, eqs 2.1–2.2: f(β; μ*) := log E_{P_{β;μ*}}[q_{μ*}/p_{μ*}] = log Z_q(β; μ*) − log Z_p(β; μ*); ∇f(β) = E_{Q_β}[X] − E_{P_β}[X], so ∇f(0) = 0 and the Hessian at 0 is Σ_q(μ*) − Σ_p(μ*).
- Proposition 2: q_{μ*}/p_{μ*} is a local e-variable around μ* (null restricted to a neighbourhood of μ* in canonical space) iff Σ_p(μ*) − Σ_q(μ*) is positive semidefinite; then it is the local GRO e-variable.
- Theorem 1 (Section 3; preconditions: M_q convex, M_q ⊆ M_p, B_{p;μ} ⊆ B_{q;μ} for all μ ∈ M_q). Equivalent: (1) Σ_p(μ) − Σ_q(μ) ⪰ 0 for all μ ∈ M_q; (2) (β_p(μ; μ′) − β_q(μ; μ′))^T(μ − μ′) ≤ 0 for all μ, μ′ ∈ M_q; (3) D(Q_μ‖Q_{μ′}) ≥ D(P_μ‖P_{μ′}) for all μ, μ′ ∈ M_q; (4) log Z_p(β; μ) ≥ log Z_q(β; μ) for all μ ∈ M_q, β ∈ B_{p;μ}; (5) q_μ/p_μ is a global e-variable for all μ ∈ M_q; (6) it is the global GRO e-variable w.r.t. Q_μ for all μ; (7) it is a local e-variable for all μ; (8) it is the local GRO e-variable for all μ [grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024]. The headline implication is (7) ⇒ (5): local for every μ forces global for every μ.
- Remarks after Theorem 1: Σ_p − Σ_q ⪰ 0 iff Σ_q^{-1} − Σ_p^{-1} ⪰ 0 (Loewner order), so (1), (2), (3) are conditions on the second derivative, first derivative and value of D(P_μ‖P_{μ*}) − D(Q_μ‖Q_{μ*}) respectively (eqs 3.1–3.2). In d = 1, (1) reads σ_p²(μ) ≥ σ_q²(μ).
- Proposition 3 (d = 1 only): under σ_p² ≥ σ_q² on M_q, M_q = M_p implies B_{p;μ′} ⊆ B_{q;μ′} for all μ′, and B_{p;μ} = B_{q;μ} for all μ implies M_q ⊆ M_p; either way the preconditions of Theorem 1 hold. The authors suspect the analogue fails in higher dimensions.
- Section 4.1 (zero curvature): Gaussian-location and Poisson k-sample tests of Hao et al. 2024. Q̄ is one-dimensional with X = Σ_i Y_i; Z_p = Z_q because X has the same law under P_{μ*} and Q_{μ*}, so f ≡ 0, E_P[S] = 1 for every null element (eq. 4.1), and S = ∏_i p_{μ_i}(Y_i)/p_{μ̄}(Y_i) with μ̄ the group-mean is GRO.
- Section 4.2 (constant curvature): P = N(μ, Σ_p), Q = N(μ*, Σ_q), Q̄ = all N(μ, Σ_q), M_p = M_q = R^d. If Σ_p − Σ_q ⪰ 0 then q_μ/p_μ is GRO for every μ with growth rate D_gauss(Σ_q Σ_p^{-1}), D_gauss(B) = ½(−log det B − (d − tr B)). If Σ_p − Σ_q ⪯ 0 the simple ratio is not an e-variable; the GRO e-variable "will be reported on in future work" [grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024].
- Section 4.3.1: Bernoulli k-sample test (Hao et al. 2024) verified via condition (4): Z_p(β; μ) = (1 − μ/k + (μ/k)e^β)^k ≥ ∏_i(1 − μ′_i + μ′_i e^β) = Z_q(β; μ) by concavity of log; here E_P[S] is not constant in the null, unlike Section 4.1. Exponential, Gaussian-scale and Beta k-sample tests have no simple e-variable, and conditions (1)–(4) explain why.
- Section 4.3.2: Gaussian scale null P = {N(0, σ²)}, X = U², vs Q = N(m, s²), m ≠ 0. Q̄ consists of normals with mean cm/(c − β) and variance s²/(1 − 2βs²), c = 1/(2s²); M_q = M_p = R_+ but B_{p;σ²} ⊊ B_{q;σ²}. Condition (1) reduces to var_{Q_β}[U²] ≤ 2(E_{Q_β}[U²])² (eqs 4.7–4.8), which holds, so q/p_{m²+s²} is an e-variable; this recovers de Jong 2021 without the calculus.
- Section 4.3.3 (NEFs): with P and Q both NEFs the generated Q̄ is the NEF Q came from, so simple vs composite alternative coincide and condition (1) is a comparison of published variance functions (Morris 1982, Letac–Mora 1990). Gamma(fixed shape r) vs Gamma(fixed shape r′): σ_p² = μ²/r. Negative binomial (σ_p² = μ²/n + μ) and the whole Awad–Bar-Lev–Makov class σ_s²(μ) = μ(1 + μ/s)^r vs Poisson (σ_q² = μ): simple e-variables exist for every Poisson alternative. Tweedie–Bar-Lev–Enis power-variance pairs σ² = aμ^γ, γ ≥ 1: σ_p² ≥ σ_q² iff μ^{γ_p − γ_q} ≥ a_q/a_p, which can fail for some μ.
- Section 4.3.3, Figure 3 (Exponential null vs inverse-Gaussian alternative with λ = 2, i.e. (a_p, γ_p) = (1, 2), (a_q, γ_q) = (1/2, 3)): local e-variable iff μ ≤ 2; E_{P_{μ′}}[q_μ/p_μ] (eq. 4.9) diverges for μ′ ≥ (1/μ − λ/(2μ²))^{-1}, which is vacuous for μ ≤ λ/2 = 1 (global possible), so for μ ∈ (1, 2) one has a local e-variable that is not global, and for μ > 2 none at all; at μ = 3/2, B_{p;μ} = (−∞, 2/3) ⊋ B_{q;μ} = (−∞, 4/9) so the precondition of Theorem 1 fails and f(β) → ∞ as β ↑ 4/9 [grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024].
- Appendix A, Example 1 (Poisson regression, two groups, one covariate, n = 2): the null is the GLM with treatment coefficient 0, the generated Q̄ is a proper d-dimensional subset of the (d+1)-dimensional H_1, and with γ*_A > γ*_B > 0, X_A > X_B > 0 one gets σ_p²(μ*) < σ_q²(μ*), so condition (1) fails and the Q̄ construction cannot in general produce simple e-variables for generalised linear models.
- Section 5 (proof): key convex-duality identity −log Z_p(β; μ′) = D(P_{μ_p(β;μ′)}‖P_{μ′}) − β^T μ_p(β; μ′) ≤ D(P_μ‖P_{μ′}) − β^T μ (eq. 5.3); (1) ⇒ (2) ⇒ (3) by differentiating along segments μ(α) = (1 − α)μ′ + αμ (needs convexity of M_q, eq. 5.4); (3) ⇒ (4) by eq. 5.5–5.6; (4) ⇒ (5) by eq. 2.1; (5) ⇒ (6), (7) ⇒ (8) by Proposition 1; (8) ⇒ (1) by Proposition 2.

## Setting and assumptions
- Null: regular (hence full) d-dimensional exponential family P on U with sufficient statistic X = t(U), mean space M_p; nonconvex as a set of distributions except Bernoulli/multinomial, which is why the RIPr onto conv(P) is hard in general [grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024].
- Alternative: a single Q with a density, a moment generating function (a strong but convenient assumption) and E_Q[X] = μ* ∈ M_p. Composite alternatives are handled by checking the condition for each Q and then using the method of mixtures or prequential plug-in (ramdasGametheoreticStatisticsSafe2023); when Q̄ equals the intended H_1 one check suffices.
- Theorem 1 preconditions: M_q convex (canonical spaces are always convex, mean spaces need not be), M_q ⊆ M_p, B_{p;μ} ⊆ B_{q;μ} for all μ ∈ M_q. Proposition 3 reduces these to M_q = M_p or B_{p;μ} = B_{q;μ} in d = 1.
- Data model: a single block U; i.i.d. repetitions follow by multiplication, with GRO optimality for fixed n (Prop. 1) and for arbitrary stopping times (via koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022). Optional stopping and optional continuation are therefore both covered when a simple e-variable exists; the paper does not analyse sequential settings beyond this remark.
- Curved exponential family nulls are out of scope (Section 6).

## Open questions / gaps
- Construct GRO or near-GRO e-variables when the preconditions of Theorem 1 or the positive-semidefiniteness fail; Figure 3 shows all three outcomes (global, local-only, none) within one family pair (Section 6). The anti-simple case Σ_p − Σ_q ⪯ 0 is answered in [haoEValuesExponentialFamilies2025](haoEValuesExponentialFamilies2025.md); mixed-sign cases remain open.
- Does Proposition 3 (M_q = M_p or equal canonical spaces suffice for the preconditions) hold for d > 1? The authors suspect not (Section 3).
- Extension to curved exponential family nulls, e.g. stratified Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel-type tests (Liang 2023), where a non-canonical parameterisation β may play the role of f's argument (Section 6).
- Generalised linear models: Appendix A shows condition (1) can fail for Poisson regression even with n = 2, so whether simple e-variables exist for GLM nulls, and what the GRO e-variable is otherwise, is open.
- The MGF assumption on Q is "strong"; no results without it.
