# Étude critique de la notion de collectif

Doctoral thesis (Faculté des Sciences de Paris, defended March 1939; jury Borel, Fréchet, Garnier), written in French. Page numbers below are those of the printed thesis. Ville's own text contains no hypothesis testing, no "e-values" and no "test martingale"; every statement about the modern literature in this page is marked as the agent's reading.

## Summary
The thesis examines von Mises's definition of probability as a limiting frequency in a *collectif* (collective), in the rigorous form Wald gave it in Menger's Vienna seminar (1934–35), and reaches a different logical verdict while accepting and extending Wald's mathematics [villeEtudeCritiqueNotion1939, Introduction]. Chapter II shows that Wald's countable systems of selection rules (*sélections*) are consistent but too weak: for every countable system S and every p there is a collective in K(S; p) whose frequency converges to p from above, with deviations O(φ(μ)/μ) for an arbitrarily slowly growing φ, so no choice of selections can force the oscillations of order 1/√μ, let alone the law of the iterated logarithm, that hold almost surely (Theorems 4, 4′, V, pp. 39–53). Chapter IV replaces selections by *systèmes de jeu*, which Ville names *martingales*: nonnegative capital processes of a gambler with unit initial stake betting on a p-coin; every p-null set is contained in the set where some martingale becomes unbounded (Theorem 1, p. 74), and every martingale stays bounded almost surely (Theorem 2, p. 76), so the martingale criterion is strictly stronger than Wald's. Chapter V defines martingales for general dependent sequences through conditional expectations (Definition 1, p. 83), proves the maximal inequality Pr{sup_n s_n ≥ λ} ≤ 1/λ (Theorem 1, p. 84) from a stopped martingale, characterises all Bernoulli martingales as mixtures p^{−x} q^{−y} ∫ t^x (1−t)^y dF(t) via de Finetti's representation, and uses them to prove the convergent half of Kolmogorov's law of the iterated logarithm, Petrowsky's upper-class test for Brownian motion, and a new lower limit function for running maxima. In the agent's reading, Chapter V, Definition 1 is the test martingale and Theorem 1 is Ville's inequality, which is why this thesis is the common ancestor of [test martingales](../concepts/test-martingale.md), [e-processes](../concepts/e-process.md) and [testing by betting](../concepts/testing-by-betting.md).

## Main results
All statements are from [villeEtudeCritiqueNotion1939]; numbering restarts in each chapter, as in the thesis.

**Chapter I, frequencies of configurations (pp. 3–21).**
- Theorems 1, 1′: if two of the total frequencies [a¹…aᵐ], [a¹…aᵐ0], [a¹…aᵐ1] exist in a 0–1 sequence, so does the third and they add (relation (8)); likewise for left extension (8′).
- Theorem 2: among frequencies of configurations of length ≤ m₀ there are 2^{m₀−1} independent ones. Theorems 3, 4: a sequence with any prescribed consistent system of configuration frequencies exists and is constructed explicitly (Lemmas 1–4). Theorem 5: existence of the length-m frequencies and positivity of their ratios forces existence of all shorter ones.
- Section 6: for a regular finite Markov chain, almost sure convergence of frequencies is "an arithmetic consequence" of the Bernoulli strong law (p. 21).

**Chapter II, collectives and selections (pp. 22–53).**
- Section 1 quotes von Mises's two axioms (existence of limiting frequency; invariance under *sélection*, p. 23) and the standard objection: among all subsequence selections there is one that picks exactly the 1s, so no collective with 0 < p < 1 exists (p. 24).
- Definitions 1–3 (pp. 26–27) formalise Wald: a selection function f_n assigns 0/1 to each prefix (m₁…m_n); a selection is a sequence {f_n}; a sequence is a collective relative to a system S of selections and a system 𝓛 of sets if the limiting frequency of each L ∈ 𝓛 exists and is the same in every S{m_i}, S ∈ S. Wald's existence theorem is restated (p. 28): for countable S and Jordan-measurable 𝓛 there are infinitely many collectives.
- Theorem 1 (p. 30): on the real line with 𝓛 ⊇ the G_δ sets, a collective exists only if the distribution is purely atomic (a countable set of points carrying total probability 1); sharpens Copeland's "not absolutely continuous". Hence "the field of probabilisable events in Wald's theory is narrower than in the classical theory" (p. 32).
- Section 4 identifies 0–1 sequences with points of (0,1) and defines the *p-mesure* (the Bernoulli(p) product measure). Theorem 2 / 2′ (pp. 37–38): for any countable S, the set K(S; p) of collectives has p-measure 1. The proof (pp. 34–36) already constructs a nonnegative sequence s_n with E[s_n | X_1…X_{n−1}] = s_{n−1}, E[s_1] = 1 from the selected subsequence, stops it at the first exit above λ, and derives Pr{sup_n s_n > λ} ≤ 1/λ, hence Pr{sup_n s_n = ∞} = 0 (inequalities (16)–(18)).
- Theorem 3 (p. 39): a necessary condition for excluding a set A of sequences by a condition x ∈ K(S; p) is p-measure(A) = 0. Theorem 4 (p. 39): the converse fails: for every p there is a p-null set G that meets K(S; p) for every countable S.
- Theorem 4′ (p. 39): given countable S, p, and any continuous increasing φ with φ(μ)/μ → 0 and φ(μ) → ∞, however slowly, there is a collective in K(S; p) such that in every infinite subsequence extracted by a selection of S the frequency ν/μ satisfies −α/μ ≤ ν/μ − p ≤ β φ(μ)/μ for all μ, with α, β depending on the selection only (inequality (19), proved through (33) on p. 46). Taking φ(μ) = μ^ε with ε < 1/2 contradicts Lévy's theorem that |f_μ − p| > δ/√μ infinitely often with probability 1 (inequality (20), p. 40), which gives Theorem 4. Remark (p. 47): in the constructed sequence the frequency of 1s is always ≥ p, so the convergence is one-sided.
- Sections 5–6 (pp. 40–47) define sum, inner product, complement and "segment of length m" of selections (Definitions 4–8) and build the collective by interleaving 2ⁿ "cells" C_{a¹…aⁿ} that each keep a frequency of 1s equal to ⌈p·length⌉.
- Section 7 (Definitions 9–11) relativises von Mises's *verbindbar* (combinable) and independent collectives to a countable system; Section 8, Theorem V (p. 52): for every p and every countable system of combinability/independence conditions, there exist sequences of independent collectives in which the proportion satisfying "|ν/μ − p| > 1/√μ only finitely often" is 1, and others in which it is 0. So Borel-type almost-sure theorems (including, in the agent's reading, the law of the iterated logarithm) cannot be proved inside the de Mises–Wald theory.

**Chapter III, indifferent sequences (pp. 54–68).**
- Popper's *nachwirkungsfreie Folgen*, Reichenbach's normal sequences and Copeland's admissible numbers are shown equivalent (Definitions 1–2, Lemmas 1–2, p. 67): a sequence is indifferent iff every configuration of length m has frequency p^ν q^{m−ν} iff it is a Bernoulli sequence in Reichenbach's sense.
- Bounded selections (Definition 3, those depending on the last n terms only) form a countable system 𝒜; every indifferent sequence is a collective K(𝒜; p), and indifference is invariant under bounded selections and under arithmetically periodic selections (Definition 5), via Wald's outer product of selections (Definition 4). Ville notes this invariance had not been observed (p. 68). The defect of Chapter II applies unchanged (p. 68).

**Chapter IV, irregularity criteria based on the martingale (pp. 69–77).**
- Section 2, "Système de jeu ou martingale" (pp. 71–73): a player A with capital 1 bets, before coup n, the fractions λ_n(x¹…x^{n−1}) and μ_n(x¹…x^{n−1}) of his capital s_{n−1} on x^n = 1 and x^n = 0, with λ_n + μ_n ≤ 1, paid at odds 1/p and 1/q. The capital satisfies s_{n−1} = p s_n(…,1) + q s_n(…,0) (relation (2)); writing σ_n = p^ν q^{n−ν} s_n gives the p-free form σ_{n−1} = σ_n(…,1) + σ_n(…,0), σ_0 = 1 (relation (4)). "Nous désignerons par σ une suite {σ_n} de telles fonctions, et appellerons martingale le système de jeu correspondant" (p. 73). "A ne gagne pas indéfiniment" is the condition sup_n s_n < ∞ (inequality (5)); H(σ; p) is the set of sequences satisfying it.
- Section 3 quotes von Mises's "principe de l'impossibilité d'un système de jeu" and argues he narrowed it by allowing only the choice of coups, not the distribution of stakes (p. 73). Theorem 1 (p. 74): for every p-null set G there is a martingale σ whose complement of H(σ; p) contains G. Proof: cover G by open G_n of p-measure < 2^{−n}, set f = Σ 2ⁿ 1_{G_n} (so ∫ f dF ≤ 1) and σ_n(x¹…xⁿ) = ∫_{I_{x¹…xⁿ}} f dF; on G the capital s_m exceeds 2ⁿ for every n (relation (9)). Corollary: for every countable S and p there is σ with H(σ; p) ⊂ K(S; p); the converse fails by Theorem II.4, so the martingale criterion strictly contains Wald's (p. 76).
- Theorem 2 (p. 76): for every σ, H(σ; p) has p-measure 1 (proved in Chapter V). Hence the conditions "x ∈ H(σ; p)" satisfy property F (p. 70): each holds almost surely and every null property is excluded by one of them. Ville concludes that the martingale criterion is "relative" (it presupposes the null properties to exclude), that an arithmetic model of a sequence with all properties of a random one is insoluble, and that the martingale is useful independently of foundations (p. 77).

**Chapter V, applications of the martingale (pp. 78–113).**
- Section 1, Definition 1 (p. 83): for a sequence of random variables X_1, X_2, … with well-defined joint laws, nonnegative functions s_0 = 1, s_1(x_1), s_2(x_1, x_2), … such that 𝔐_{X_1…X_{n−1}}{s_n(X_1,…,X_{n−1}, X_n)} = s_{n−1} (conditional expectation in P. Lévy's sense, relation (14)) "définit une martingale ou un jeu équitable". Fairness is derived from the requirement that A, free to stop after any coup, has expectation 1 whatever his stopping rule E_1, E_2, … (series (13), pp. 79–83), provided the game ends with probability 1.
- Section 2, Theorem 1 (p. 84), the gambler's-ruin inequality now called Ville's inequality: if {s_n} defines a martingale and λ > 1, then Pr{sup_n s_n(X_1, X_2, …, X_n) ≥ λ} ≤ 1/λ (inequality (15)). Proof: the process σ_n frozen at the first n with σ_{n−1} > λ (relation (17)) is again a martingale, so E[σ_n] = 1 and Pr{σ_n ≥ λ} ≤ 1/λ for each n; then let n → ∞. The elementary ruin heuristic (a player with capital 1 reaches λ with probability at most 1/λ) precedes it (p. 84).
- Section 3, Bernoulli case: with x, y the counts of 1s and 0s, s(0,0) = 1 and s(x,y) = p s(x+1,y) + q s(x,y+1) (relation (14′)); σ(x,y) = p^x q^y s(x,y) solves σ(x,y) = σ(x+1,y) + σ(x,y+1), whence σ(x,y) = (−1)^x Δ^x v_y (relation (20)). The nonnegativity condition is de Finetti's complete-monotonicity condition, so by de Finetti (1937) every Bernoulli martingale is s(x,y) = p^{−x} q^{−y} ∫₀¹ t^x (1−t)^y dF(t) for a distribution function F (relation (22)), and Pr{sup_n p^{−x} q^{−y} ∫ t^x (1−t)^y dF(t) ≥ λ} ≤ 1/λ (relation (23)).
- Proposition (pp. 87–95): if φ(n) is increasing, Σ φ(n) e^{−φ(n)²/2}/n converges and √n e^{−φ(n)²/2} is eventually increasing to ∞, then the probability that x − np > φ(n)√(2npq) holds for infinitely many n is zero; example φ(n) = (1+ε)√(log log n). This is one half of the Kolmogorov theorem stated without proof by Lévy (1937, p. 266). The proof chooses F with a decreasing density f supported on (p, 1) solving a two-term system (35) so that s along the boundary tends to infinity (condition (32)).
- Second section, continuous time (pp. 95–113): martingale conditions (a)–(d) for functionals S_τ(X), with conditional probability in Doob's and Nikodym's sense. The continuous ruin formula Pr{sup_τ S_τ ≥ λ} ≤ 1/λ can fail (p. 98): a 0–1 process with one jump at a uniform time has increments that are "independent and almost surely zero" under Kolmogorov's definition, and s(t,x) = x + 1 reaches 2 with probability 1. Section 3 discusses versions of conditional probabilities and Lévy's "indépendance presque certaine". Under the Chapman–Kolmogorov (Markov) hypothesis and Slutsky's stochastic continuity, the inequality holds for the *borne supérieure stochastique* (relation (14), p. 103).
- Section 7, Gaussian increments: s(t,x) = ∫ e^{λx − λ²t/2} dF(λ) solves the martingale equation (relation (20), p. 106), and with a decreasing density this reproves Petrowsky's result that every increasing φ with ∫ φ e^{−φ²/2} dt/t < ∞ (and √t e^{−φ²/2} eventually increasing) is an upper limit function for X(t)/√(2t), "en ne faisant appel qu'à des considérations élémentaires de probabilité" (p. 107).
- Section 8, running maximum of i.i.d. uniforms: s(t,y) = ∫_y¹ ρ(u) u^{−t} du (relation (6)); if φ is increasing with tφ′ increasing and ∫ φ′ log(1/φ′) dt convergent, then Y(t) > φ(t) for all large t almost surely; writing φ = 1 − ψ/t, it suffices that ∫ ψ e^{−ψ} dt/t converge, so ψ must grow faster than log log t and may grow slower than log t. Announced as "un résultat nouveau" (p. 107).

## Setting and assumptions
- Object: infinite 0–1 sequences (the *alternative*), later sequences of real random variables and continuous-time processes. The reference law is a fixed Bernoulli(p) with 0 < p < 1 known; in modern terms (agent's reading) all martingale results are under a simple null, there is no alternative hypothesis and no decision rule.
- Measure-theoretic frame: Kolmogorov's axiomatics with Lévy's conditional expectations, Fréchet's integral and Doob's (1937) conditional probabilities for function spaces; Ville calls this the "théorie classique modernisée".
- Martingale conditions: nonnegativity, s_0 = 1, exact conditional-expectation equality (martingale, not supermartingale); stakes λ_n + μ_n ≤ 1 so capital never goes negative. In continuous time, additional stochastic continuity (Slutsky) and a Markov structure are assumed for the inequality.
- Stopping: the player may stop after any coup according to any non-randomised rule depending on the past; expectation 1 is derived for such rules under the proviso that the game stops with probability 1 (p. 82). The maximal inequality is stated for the supremum over all n, which in the agent's reading is the time-uniform form that makes it valid at arbitrary stopping times.
- Collectives: Wald's framework with a countable system S of selections is taken as given; the critique is about what such systems can and cannot exclude, not about their consistency.

## Open questions / gaps
- What is Ville's and what is later attribution (agent's reading throughout): Ville coined "martingale" for the gambling system and for the sequence σ; the use of the word for the stochastic process (s_n) is Doob's (1940, not cited in the wiki); the names "Ville's inequality", "test martingale", "e-process" are all later. Ville never considers an alternative hypothesis, a level α, or a decision; his inequality serves almost-sure limit theorems.
- Ville's Chapter IV, Theorem 1 plus Theorem 2 state that the p-null sets are exactly the sets enclosed in {sup_n s_n = ∞} for some nonnegative martingale. In the agent's reading this is the martingale characterisation of null sets later used in algorithmic randomness (Schnorr 1971) and game-theoretic probability (Shafer and Vovk 2001, 2019, the latter in gaps.md); neither is cited in the wiki.
- The thesis proves only the convergent half of Kolmogorov's LIL and of Petrowsky's test; the divergent halves are not attempted. Whether a martingale argument gives them is not raised.
- The continuous-time counterexample (p. 98) shows the maximal inequality needs path regularity; Ville's fix (stochastic supremum under stochastic continuity, Markov case) is weaker than what Doob's later theory gives. Not relevant to discrete e-processes.
- No optimality notion: Ville asks only that some martingale detect a given null set, never which one grows fastest; the GRO/log-optimality questions of [grunwaldSafeTesting2024] and [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022] have no counterpart here.
- Cited but not in the bib and central to the lineage: Wald 1937 (consistency of collectives), de Finetti 1937 (representation theorem used for (22)), Lévy 1937, Doob 1937, Kolmogorov 1933, Copeland 1928, 1937.
