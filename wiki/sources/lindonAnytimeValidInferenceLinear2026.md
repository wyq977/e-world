# Anytime-valid inference in linear models with applications to regression-adjusted causal inference

JASA 2026; the ingested file is the author manuscript of 10 September 2025 carrying the arXiv 2210.08589v5 stamp (7 July 2025). Eight main sections plus supplementary material: A (classical fixed-n F-test as a likelihood-ratio test), B (proofs for Section 4, including the Wijsman integral computation, the counterexample to maximal invariance of ξ̂_n, and a simulation study against the fixed-n test in B.7), C (proofs for Section 5 with Einmahl's strong approximation), D (proofs for Section 6), E (comparison with AIPW and empirical-Bernstein confidence sequences), F (supplementary figures).

## Summary
The paper builds anytime-valid analogues of the t-test, the F-test and the F-confidence ellipsoid for the Gaussian linear model y_i = x_i'β + z_i'δ + ε_i, with δ ∈ R^d the parameters of interest and (β, σ) nuisance [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, Sec. 4]. The composite null δ = 0 is reduced to a simple one by invariance under g: Y ↦ cY + Xα, under which the standardised coefficients ξ = δ/σ are invariant; the likelihood ratio of any sequence of invariant statistics is a test martingale (Lemma 3.1), Wijsman's representation turns it into a right-Haar Bayes factor (Lemma 3.3), and Stein's theorem (Theorem 3.4) lets one compute it on an invariantly sufficient statistic instead of the awkward maximal invariant of Pérez-Ortiz et al. [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, Sec. 3]. For the Bayesian alternative δ | σ² ~ N(0, σ²Φ^{−1}) the resulting process E_n is a ratio of two multivariate t-densities of ξ̂_n = δ̂_n/s_n (Theorem 4.1), GROW and REGROW by the Hunt–Stein result of Pérez-Ortiz et al., with growth rate ½ log(1 + ξ'Ω_Z̃ξ) (Theorem 4.2); it inverts to a shrinking confidence sequence that is an ellipsoid around δ̂_n (Corollary 4.3), extends to linear hypotheses Eδ = 0 (Corollary 4.4), and a point-alternative noncentral-t ratio handles frequentist alternatives (Section 4.1). Choosing Φ = gΩ_Z̃ and plugging in Z̃_n'Z̃_n/n gives Zellner's g-prior and a closed-form process G_n depending only on the classical F-statistic and g (Theorem 4.6), an asymptotic e-process with explicit error, with g tunable to the minimum detectable effect (Theorem 4.5) or to the width of the confidence sequence at a target n (eq. 18) [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, Sec. 4.3]. The second half drops Gaussianity and homoskedasticity: a Robbins Gaussian-mixture supermartingale for the χ² statistic (Lemma 5.1), Einmahl's multivariate strong approximation of Σ w_iε_i (Proposition 5.2) and a strongly consistent sandwich estimator (Proposition 5.3) yield a heteroskedasticity-robust asymptotic e-process with the same t-form (Theorem 5.4) and an asymptotic 't' confidence sequence that is always wider than the asymptotic Gaussian one but asymptotically equivalent (Theorem 5.5), with a built-in delayed start [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, Sec. 5]. In a randomised experiment with Bernoulli assignment the population least squares coefficient on the centred treatment indicator is the ATE regardless of linearity (Lemma 6.1), so the OLS fit of the (optionally fully interacted) regression gives anytime-valid regression-adjusted tests and sequences for average and conditional average treatment effects (Section 6); simulations with nonlinear heteroskedastic t_3 outcomes and a Netflix PlayDelay A/B test show the t-sequence protects against early false positives better than the Gaussian one and stops far sooner than the empirical-Bernstein sequence [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, Sec. 7].

## Main results
- **Section 2 (definitions)**: e-variable, conditional e-variable, test martingale (Shafer et al. 2011), e-process as a process dominated by a P-supermartingale for every P in H0 (Ramdas et al. 2023), p-process p_n = 1/E_n, confidence sequence, power one via (1/n) log E_n → c > 0 (Robbins and Siegmund 1974); asymptotic e-process (log Ē_n / log E_n → 1 a.s.) and asymptotic confidence sequence (normalised symmetric difference → 0 a.s.) in the sense of Waudby-Smith et al. 2021 [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Lemma 3.1**: for any sequence of G-invariant statistics m_i(Y_i), E_n = q(m_1, …, m_n)/p(m_1, …, m_n) is a test martingale for H0 in the filtration σ(m_i : i ≤ n), since each factor is a conditional e-variable; if the m_i are maximal invariants it collapses to q(m_n)/p(m_n) (eqs 1–2) [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, Sec. 3].
- **Definition 3.2**: GROW and REGROW e-variables over the nuisance parameter ρ (eqs 3–4), from Grünwald et al. 2021; the maximal-invariant likelihood ratio is GROW/REGROW by Pérez-Ortiz et al. 2022 [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Lemma 3.3 (Wijsman)**: q(m_n)/p(m_n) = ∫ q_ρ(Y_n) π(ρ) dρ / ∫ p_ρ(Y_n) π(ρ) dρ with π the right-Haar prior (eq. 5, Andersson 1982) [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Theorem 3.4 (Stein, via Hall, Wijsman and Ghosh 1965)**: if S_n is sufficient for θ and m^s_n(S_n) is a maximal invariant under the induced group on S_n, then m^s_n is sufficient for ξ ("invariantly sufficient") and q(m_n(Y_n))/p(m_n(Y_n)) = q(m^s_n)/p(m^s_n) (eq. 6); the usual t-statistic is the example [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Setting of Section 4**: Y_n | γ, σ² ~ N(W_nγ, σ²I), W_n = [X_n, Z_n], γ = (β, δ); δ̂_n | σ² ~ N(δ, σ²(Z̃_n'Z̃_n)^{−1}) with Z̃_n = (I − P_{X_n})Z_n; s_n² = Y_n'(I − P_{W_n})Y_n/ν_n, ν_n = n − p − d; F_n = ξ̂_n'Z̃_n'Z̃_nξ̂_n/d ~ F(d, ν_n, ξ'Z̃_n'Z̃_nξ) (eq. 8); group g: Y ↦ cY + Xα induces (β̂, δ̂, s²) ↦ (cβ̂ + α, cδ̂, c²s²) [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Theorem 4.1**: with right-Haar prior π(β, σ²) ∝ 1/σ² and alternative δ | σ² ~ N(0, σ²Φ^{−1}), E_n = √(det Φ / det(Φ + Z̃_n'Z̃_n)) · [1 + ξ̂_n'(Z̃'Z̃ − Z̃'Z̃(Φ + Z̃'Z̃)^{−1}Z̃'Z̃)ξ̂_n/ν_n]^{−(ν_n+d)/2} / [1 + ξ̂_n'Z̃'Z̃ξ̂_n/ν_n]^{−(ν_n+d)/2} (eq. 9) is a test martingale and the GROW/REGROW e-variable for δ = 0 against that alternative; ξ̂_n = δ̂_n/s_n is invariantly sufficient (not a maximal invariant on R^n, by the counterexample in B.1, but maximal invariant on the summary-statistic space), and E_n = t_{ν_n}(ξ̂_n | 0, Φ^{−1} + (Z̃'Z̃)^{−1}) / t_{ν_n}(ξ̂_n | 0, (Z̃'Z̃)^{−1}) (eq. 10) [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. B.1].
- **Theorem 4.2 (power one)**: if W_n'W_n/n → Ω_W a.s. (positive definite), (1/n) log E_n → ½ log(1 + ξ'Ω_Z̃ξ) a.s. (eq. 11), so E_n crosses 1/α with probability 1 under ξ ≠ 0 [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. B.2].
- **Corollary 4.3 (confidence sequence)**: E_n^{δ_0} = E_n(Y_n − Z_nδ_0) is a test martingale for δ = δ_0; p_n^{δ_0} = 1/E_n^{δ_0} is a sequential p-value; C_n^α = {δ : ‖δ − δ̂_n‖²_{A_n} ≤ ν_n s_n² (1 − (α² det Φ / det(Φ + Z̃'Z̃))^{1/(ν_n+d)})} with A_n = ((α² det Φ/det(Φ + Z̃'Z̃))^{1/(ν_n+d)} − 1) Z̃'Z̃ + Z̃'Z̃(Φ + Z̃'Z̃)^{−1}Z̃'Z̃ is a (1 − α) confidence sequence for δ [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Corollary 4.4 (contrasts)**: for full-row-rank E ∈ R^{k×d}, the same form with Z̃_n^† = Z̃_nE' and ξ̂_n^† = δ̂_n^†/s_n is a test martingale for Eδ = 0 (eq. 12); the denominator quadratic form equals kF_n for the classical F(k, ν_n) statistic [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. B.3].
- **Section 4.1 (simple alternatives)**: ξ̂_n ~ t^{nc}_{ν_n}(ξ, (Z̃'Z̃)^{−1}) (noncentral multivariate t), so E_n = t^{nc}_{ν_n}(ξ̂_n | ξ_1, ·)/t^{nc}_{ν_n}(ξ̂_n | ξ_0, ·) (eq. 13) is a test martingale for ξ = ξ_0 and GROW against ξ = ξ_1 [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Section 4.2 (vs Pérez-Ortiz et al.)**: their maximal invariant m_n = A_nY_n/‖A_nY_n‖ on the unit sphere of R^{n−p} has the Bhowmik–King confluent-hypergeometric density (eqs 14–15); by Theorem 3.4 its likelihood ratio equals the noncentral-t ratio of ξ̂_n (eq. 16). Differences claimed: multivariate δ, composite alternatives, shrinking confidence sequences (point-alternative inversion does not shrink), standard densities, and only OLS output (estimate and standard error) needed [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Theorem 4.5 (tuning Φ, d = 1)**: the Φ maximising E[log E_n] under the frequentist alternative ξ̂_n ~ t^{nc}_{ν_n}(ξ*, 1/‖Z̃_n‖²), with ‖Z̃_n‖² = Ω(n), satisfies Φ*_n → (ξ*)^{−2}; so set Φ = ξ_MDE^{−2} to maximise worst-case growth over |ξ| ≥ ξ_MDE [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. B.4].
- **Section 4.3.1, Theorem 4.6 (g-prior)**: with Φ = gΩ_Z̃ (g = 1 a unit-information prior) and Z̃_n'Z̃_n/n plugged in for Ω_Z̃, Zellner's g-prior δ | σ² ~ N(0, σ²(g/n · Z̃'Z̃)^{−1}) gives G_n = (g/(g+n))^{d/2} · [(1 + (g/(g+n))(d/ν_n)F_n)/(1 + (d/ν_n)F_n)]^{−(ν_n+d)/2} (eq. 17), a function of F_n and g alone; |log E_n − log G_n| = ½ Tr(Ω_Z̃^{−1}R_n) − (g/2) ξ_n'R_nξ_n + O_a.s.(R_n/n) with R_n = Ω_Z̃ − Z̃'Z̃/n, so G_n is an asymptotic e-process converging rapidly to E_n [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. B.5].
- **Eq. 18 (g-prior confidence set)**: C_n^α = {δ : F_n^δ ≤ R(g, n, α)} with R(g, n, α) = (ν_n/d)(1 − (α²g^d/(n+g)^d)^{1/(ν_n+d)}) / max(0, (α²g^d/(n+g)^d)^{1/(ν_n+d)} − g/(n+g)) and F_n^δ the F-statistic for the null δ; g can be chosen numerically to minimise R at a target n [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Proposition 4.7 (marginalisation consistency)**: ((Z̃'Z̃)^{−1})_{11} = (Z̃_1^{*'}Z̃_1^*)^{−1} when Z_2 is absorbed into X, so the g-prior on a subvector agrees whether formed directly or by marginalising, which matters for nested-model tests [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. B.6].
- **Lemma 5.1 (Gaussian χ² mixture supermartingale)**: for i.i.d. N(μ, Σ) vectors in R^k and g > 0, E_n = (g/(g+n))^{k/2} exp(½ (n/(g+n)) Q_n), Q_n = n(Ḡ_n − μ)'Σ^{−1}(Ḡ_n − μ), is a nonnegative supermartingale (eq. 20) [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. C.1].
- **Proposition 5.2 (Einmahl 1989)**: with E[ε_iw_i] = 0, Cov(ε_iw_i) = Σ and E‖ε_iw_i‖^s < ∞ for some s > 3, on a rich enough space Σ_{i≤n} ε_iw_i − Σ_{i≤n} G_i = O_a.s.(n^a) for some a < ½ with G_i i.i.d. N(0, Σ) (eq. 21) [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. C.2].
- **Proposition 5.3**: under Ω̂_{W_n} → Ω_W a.s., E[w_iε_i] = 0, E‖w_i‖⁴ < ∞, E|ε_i|‖w_i‖³ < ∞, the sandwich Σ̂_n = (n − p − d)^{−1} Σ ε̂²_{in} w_iw_i' and Σ̂_E = EΩ̂^{−1}Σ̂_nΩ̂^{−1}E' are strongly consistent [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. C.3].
- **Theorem 5.4 (heteroskedasticity-robust asymptotic e-process)**: under the moment conditions above, for full-row-rank E ∈ R^{r×k} and g > 0, E_n = (g/(g+n))^{r/2} [(1 + (g/(g+n))(r/ν_n)(Q_n/r))/(1 + (r/ν_n)(Q_n/r))]^{−(ν_n+r)/2} with Q_n = n(γ̂_n − γ)'E'Σ̂_E^{−1}E(γ̂_n − γ) (eq. 22) is an asymptotic e-process; with Σ̂_n = ns_n²Ω̂_W it reduces to Q_n/r = F_n and recovers eq. 17 [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. C.4, Lemma C.2 with a law-of-the-iterated-logarithm remainder O_a.s.(log log n)].
- **Section 5.1, Theorem 5.5 (asymptotic t vs Gaussian sequences)**: C_n^α = {γ : Q_n/r ≤ R(g, n, α)} versus the Robbins Gaussian sequence C_n^{G,α} = {Q_n/r ≤ R_G(g, n, α)} of Waudby-Smith et al. 2021 with R_G = ((g+n)/n) log((g+n)/(α²g))/r (eq. 23); R > R_G for all n and R/R_G → 1. The t-sequence is infinite until (α²g^r/(n+g)^r)^{1/(ν_n+r)} > g/(n+g): for r = k = 1, α = 0.05, g = 10⁴ this needs n ≥ 247, for g = 10² n ≥ 27, a built-in delayed start replacing the ad hoc ones of Waudby-Smith et al. 2021 Sec. 2.6 and Bibaut, Kallus and Lindon 2022 [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. C.5, Fig. 8].
- **Lemma 6.1 (population least squares in a randomised experiment)**: with T_i ~ Bernoulli(ρ), centred covariates m_i − μ_m and interaction T_i(m_i − μ_m), the population OLS coefficient on T_i − ρ is τ* = E[y_i(1) − y_i(0)], the ATE, and η* = Ω_m^{−1}(E[(m_i − μ_m)y_i(1)] − E[(m_i − μ_m)y_i(0)]) vanishes under effect homogeneity (eq. 24); Lemma 6.2: E[w_iε_i] = 0 for ε_i = y_i − w_i'γ* by definition, so Section 5 applies with no linearity assumption (SUTVA, simple random sampling, one outcome per unit) [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026, App. D].
- **Section 7 (examples)**: three processes compared, the asymptotic t E_n (eq. 25) with Q_n = (τ̂_n/rse)², the asymptotic Gaussian E_n^G = (g/(g+n))^{1/2} exp(½ (n/(g+n)) Q_n), and the linear-model G_n (eq. 26) with F_n = (τ̂_n/se)²; OLS τ̂_n equals the AIPW estimate with the same regression function under constant propensity, so E_n^G is the Waudby-Smith et al. 2021 AIPW sequence without sample splitting. Simulation y_i = 1 − 2x²_{i1} − 2 sin x_{i2} + 3|x_{i3}| + z_iδ + (1.5 + x²_{i1})ε_i, ε_i ~ t_3, g ∈ {1, 10, 1000}: the t-sequence has fewer early false positives, better calibrated for larger g (Fig. 1). Netflix PlayDelay A/B test (skew ≈ 100, kurtosis ≈ 13,000, 99.9-percentile 0.02 vs max 1): regression on a pre-treatment PlayDelay measurement; under the null the t-sequence again protects early; under the alternative the raw outcome rarely stops before 220 observations because outliers inflate the variance, so log-PlayDelay is used, and the empirical-Bernstein sequence of Howard et al. 2021 (scale 4, gamma-exponential mixture with parameter 100) stops much later (Fig. 2, App. E) [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Appendix B.7 (vs fixed-n F-test)**: d = 1, ρ = 0.5, α = 0.01, power 0.95 at ξ_MDE = 0.2 gives n* = 1785; g tuned by growth g_g = 1/(ξ²_MDE ρ(1−ρ)) = 100 or by width g_w = 151.29. At ξ = ξ_MDE the fixed-n test rejects 95.02% by n*, the anytime-valid tests 81.69%/81.68% with mean stopping times 1214/1236 and all nulls rejected eventually; at ξ = 2ξ_MDE all reject by n* with mean stopping times 350/376; under the null, rejections by n* are 1.07% (fixed-n) vs 0.04%/0.03% (Figs 3–5) [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Appendix A**: the fixed-n F-test as a likelihood ratio Λ(Y) = (1 + dF/(n − p − d))^{n/2} (eqs 28, 37), with the f-statistic a function of the maximal invariant t(Y), and the fixed-n confidence ellipsoid (Corollary A.2) [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].
- **Appendix E**: OLS equals AIPW with linear regression functions (eq. 51), whereas the sequential AIPW of Ham et al. 2022 and Howard et al. 2021 uses predictable regression fits (eq. 52), which differ from the one-shot OLS and need online learning; the OLS route needs only a point estimate and standard error [lindonAnytimeValidInferenceLinear2026].

## Setting and assumptions
- Section 4: i.i.d. Gaussian homoskedastic linear model with fixed or random covariates; null δ = δ_0 (or Eδ = e_0), composite in (β, σ); alternatives either Bayesian N(0, σ²Φ^{−1}) or point ξ = ξ_1; test martingales in the filtration generated by the invariant statistics (Lemma 3.1), so optional stopping is justified for stopping times of that invariant filtration (the paper does not discuss data-filtration stopping times). Theorem 4.2 needs W_n'W_n/n → Ω_W a.s.
- Section 4.3.1: G_n is only an asymptotic e-process (error of order R_n = Ω_Z̃ − Z̃'Z̃/n); the authors argue the linear model itself is the larger error source.
- Section 5: (w_i, ε_i) i.i.d., E[ε_iw_i] = 0, E[ε²_iw_iw_i'] = Σ, moment conditions E‖w_iε_i‖^s < ∞ (s > 3), E‖w_i‖⁴ < ∞, E|ε_i|‖w_i‖³ < ∞; validity is asymptotic in the Waudby-Smith et al. 2021 sense.
- Section 6: potential outcomes, SUTVA, Bernoulli(ρ) assignment with constant ρ, simple random sampling, one outcome per unit; outcomes may depend nonlinearly on covariates. Independent outcomes throughout; dependent (panel) data is left open.

Earlier arXiv versions (2022) had different numbering: Wang and Ramdas cite the g-prior F-test martingale as "Prop. 3.7", which is Theorem 4.6 (eq. 17) here. Whether the typeset JASA version renumbers results was not checked.

## Open questions / gaps
- Dependent outcomes (longitudinal, panel, serial correlation): the authors conjecture the heteroskedastic e-process form survives but leave it open (Section 8).
- Optional stopping at data-filtration stopping times is not discussed; Lemma 3.1 gives a martingale in the invariant filtration only (cf. Pérez-Ortiz et al. Proposition 6 and Wang–Ramdas Proposition 4.13).
- G_n (g-prior) is an asymptotic, not exact, e-process; no finite-n bound on the type-I inflation is given beyond Theorem 4.6's error expansion.
- The MDE tuning of Theorem 4.5 is for d = 1 only and "does not generalize easily" to multivariate δ.
- No growth-rate-in-α (α^{−1/n}-type) analysis of the confidence sequence width, unlike Wang and Ramdas.
- Varying propensities and nonlinear regression functions are handled by sequential AIPW, not by this method (Appendix E).
- Cited works not in the bib: Ham, Bojinov, Lindon and Tingley 2022 (design-based confidence sequences); Bibaut, Kallus and Lindon 2022; Lardy 2022 (thesis); Shafer, Shen, Vereshchagin and Vovk 2011; Hall, Wijsman and Ghosh 1965; Andersson 1982; Robbins and Siegmund 1974; Einmahl 1989; Zellner 1986; Lin 2013.
