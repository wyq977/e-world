# Admissible anytime-valid sequential inference must rely on nonnegative martingales

arXiv 2009.03167v3 (November 2022), 41 pages, unpublished preprint. First posted September 2020 and cited as "Ramdas, Ruf, Larsson and Koolen 2020" by nine other sources in this wiki; the v3 file is the one ingested.

## Summary
The paper sets up, in one measure-theoretic framework, the four instruments of anytime-valid sequential inference for a composite null Q: p-processes (Q(p_τ ≤ α) ≤ α at every stopping time), e-processes (E_Q[e_τ] ≤ 1 at every stopping time), (Q, α)-sequential tests (Q(ψ_τ = 1) ≤ α) and (φ, P, α)-confidence sequences (sup_P P(∃t : φ(P) ∉ C_t) ≤ α), with the filtration F_t = σ(U, X_1, …, X_t) that includes an independent uniform randomisation device U [ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022, Sec. 2.2, 4.1, 4.3]. It defines each instrument to be inadmissible when another valid instrument is at least as good almost surely under every Q and strictly better with positive probability under some Q (Sec. 4.2). Lemma 6 proves that six definitions of an e-process coincide, including domination by a Q-nonnegative martingale for each Q, by taking the Snell envelope of (e_t) and its Doob decomposition; this is the result other sources cite for "every e-process is dominated by a test martingale". Theorem 18 characterises admissibility under a point null: admissible e-processes are exactly nonnegative martingales with initial expectation one, admissible p-processes are closed max-martingales (conditional-supremum martingales) whose limit is uniform, and admissible sequential tests are threshold crossings of a Doob–Lévy martingale with no overshoot. Sections 7 and 8 reduce the composite case to the pointwise one via essential suprema and infima: every admissible composite e-process is an essential infimum of pointwise nonnegative martingales (Corollary 26), composite martingales suffice (Corollary 29), the gap between the two is real (Examples 31, 32), and admissibility of confidence sequences is equivalent to admissibility of the inverted family of tests (Theorem 30). The last two sections apply the theory: the sub-Gaussian supermartingale exp(Σ X_i − t/2) is an admissible e-process for the full class of conditionally 1-sub-Gaussian laws, including atomic ones, proved by a perturbation that plants an atom in a Gaussian (Theorem 35, Lemma 38); and de la Peña's exponential supermartingale for conditional symmetry is inadmissible, dominated by explicit symmetry martingales ∏ f(X_s) with f − 1 odd (Proposition 39, Example 40), which also give admissible p-processes, tests and confidence sequences for the centre of symmetry (Examples 42, 43).

## Main results

### Preliminaries (Sections 2–3)
- **Lemma 1 (equivalence lemma)** and **Lemma 2 (composite version)**: for adapted events, P(∪_t A_t) ≤ α ⇔ sup over random times T of P(A_T) ≤ α ⇔ sup over stopping times τ of P(A_τ) ≤ α, uniformly over Q; first appeared in Howard et al. 2021 [ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022, Sec. 2.1, 2.3].
- **Lemma 3 (non-equivalence lemma)**: for nonnegative processes, E[sup_t N_t] ≤ 1 ⇔ E[N_T] ≤ 1 for all random times ⇒ E[N_τ] ≤ 1 for all stopping times ⇒ E[1 ∨ sup_t √N_t] ≤ 2, and the last two implications are strict; this is why running suprema preserve p-processes but not e-processes (Remark 16).
- **Ville's inequality** in three equivalent forms for a P-NSM with initial expectation one, and a conditional version (eqs 7a–7c, 8) [Sec. 3.1].
- **Proposition 4 (composite martingales are composite likelihood ratios)**: if (M_t) is a Q-NM starting at one, then for every Q ∈ Q there is an implied alternative P locally dominated by Q with M_t = dP_t/dQ_t; generalises Shafer et al. 2011 to composite nulls [Sec. 3.2].
- **Max-martingales** (Sec. 3.3): Y_s = ∨_P[Y_t | F_s] with the conditional supremum ess inf{Z G-measurable, Z ≥ Y}; they are a.s. decreasing, obey optional stopping and a tower rule, depend on P only through its null sets, and the running infimum inf_{s≤t} 1/M_s of a nonnegative martingale is a Doob–Lévy max-martingale (eq. 9). Example 5: the max-martingale of the Bernoulli limit frequency is identically one.

### Definitions and reductions (Section 4)
- **Lemma 6 (six equivalent e-process definitions)**: E_Q[e_τ] ≤ 1 for all stopping times with e_∞ = lim sup, or lim inf; for all finite stopping times; for all bounded stopping times; (e_t) a.s. bounded by a Q-NM for each Q; or by a Q-NSM for each Q. The proof of (iv) ⇒ (v) constructs the Snell envelope L_t = ess sup_{τ≥t} E_Q[e_τ | G_t], shows it is a supermartingale with E_Q[L_0] ≤ 1, and takes its Doob decomposition L_t = M_t − A_t. Definition (i) is Grünwald et al.'s and (vi) is Howard et al.'s. If one additionally wants conditional validity E_Q[e_τ | F_s] ≤ e_{s∧τ}, the e-process must itself be a Q-NSM [Sec. 4.1].
- **Inadmissibility** (Sec. 4.2): (e_t) is inadmissible if some Q-e-process (e′_t) has Q(e′_t ≥ e_t) = 1 for all t and Q and Q(e′_t > e_t) > 0 for some t and Q; analogously p′ ≤ p, ψ′ ≥ ψ, C′ ⊆ C. Equivalent to the stopping-time formulation (Remark 7). Standing assumption: every event with positive probability under some alternative has positive probability under some null, so no free lunch on null-polar sets.
- **Proposition 8**: if Q ⊂ Q′ and Q-polar sets are Q′-polar, a Q′-e-process that is Q-admissible is Q′-admissible (admissibility is superset-proof under this condition; validity is subset-proof).
- **Proposition 9**: if Q is locally dominated, every p-process, e-process or sequential test is dominated by an admissible one; proof by transfinite induction over countable ordinals, with a decreasing [0, 2]-valued transfinite sequence of expectations that must become stationary (Appendix B). Remark 10 extends it to confidence sequences via Theorem 30.
- **Propositions 11–14 (reductions)**: p-, e-processes and CSs give nested families of tests (1_{p_t≤α}, 1_{e_t≥1/α}, 1_{φ(Q)∩C_t=∅}); an e-process gives the p-process 1 ∧ inf_{s≤t} 1/e_s, nested tests give inf{α : ψ_t(α) = 1}; a family of (P_γ, α)-tests inverts to a (φ, P, α)-CS {γ : ψ^γ_t = 0}; and a p-process calibrates to the e-process f(p_t) for any nonincreasing f with ∫_0^1 f = 1, e.g. 1/(2√p_t) [Sec. 4.4].
- **Proposition 15**: validity and admissibility of p-, e-processes and tests are closed under the convex hull of Q; not so for confidence sequences (Example 48). **Remark 16**: min(e_t, f_t) is an e-process for conv(P ∪ Q), (e_t + f_t)/2 for conv(P ∩ Q); max and 2 min for p-processes.

### Three exponential examples (Section 5)
- **Gaussian NM** G^m_t = exp(Σ(Y_s − m) − ½ Σ σ²_s) for conditionally N(m, σ²_t) data with predictable variance (eq. 11); mixing over λ gives the normal-mixture martingale behind the √((1+1/τ) log((τ+1)/α²)/τ) confidence sequence of eq. (3) (Remark 17).
- **Sub-Gaussian** N_t = exp(λ Σ Y_i − λ²σ²t/2) (eq. 16): an NSM for the point null μ_t = 0 (G↓_0) for every λ, an NSM for μ_t ≤ 0 (G↓_−) for λ ≥ 0, and an e-process but not an NSM for the weak null Σ_{i≤t} μ_i ≤ 0 (G↓_∼) [Sec. 5.2].
- **Symmetry NSM** S^m_t = exp(Σ(X_s − m) − ½ Σ(X_s − m)²) (eq. 18, de la Peña 1999, Efron 1969) for conditionally symmetric increments, a class with no common dominating measure; proof via cosh z ≤ exp(z²/2). Gives the confidence sequence C^center_t = {m : S^m_t < 1/α} [Sec. 5.3].

### Point-null admissibility (Section 6)
- **Theorem 18**: for Q = {Q}: (1) an admissible p-process is a closed Q-max-martingale with F(inf_t p_t) = inf_t p_t, F its distribution function; conversely a closed Q-MM with uniformly distributed infimum is admissible. (2) (e_t) is admissible iff it is a Q-NM with E_Q[e_0] = 1. (3) (ψ_t) is admissible iff ψ_t = 1_{sup_{s≤t} M_s ≥ 1/α} for a Q-NM with no overshoot at 1/α and M_∞ ∈ {0, 1/α}; the necessity proof uses the Doob–Lévy martingale M_t = Q(∃s : ψ_s = 1 | F_t)/α.
- **Example 20**: p_t ≡ U is admissible but is not inf_{s≤t} 1/M_s for any NM (1/U is not integrable), so max-martingales, not martingales, are the right object for p-processes in discrete time. **Corollary 21**: p_t = F(inf_{s≤t} 1/M_s) is admissible when F is atomless; **Example 22** shows this is not automatic.

### Composite admissibility (Sections 7–8)
- **Proposition 23**: (e_t) is a Q-e-process iff e_t ≤ e^Q_t Q-a.s. for some Q-e-process (e^Q_t), for each Q; likewise p_t ≥ p^Q_t and ψ_t ≤ ψ^Q_t.
- **Corollary 24 (pointwise supermartingales are sufficient for composite validity)**: for locally dominated Q, e_t = ess inf_{Q∈Q} N^Q_t with each (N^Q_t) bounded by a Q-NSM is a Q-e-process; p_t = ess sup_Q 1 ∧ inf_{s≤t} 1/N^Q_s is a Q-p-process; ψ_t = ess inf_Q sup_{s≤t} 1_{N^Q_s ≥ 1/α} is a (Q, α)-ST. Combined with Proposition 23 and Lemma 6, every e-process is dominated by an essential infimum of pointwise nonnegative martingales, which is itself an e-process; this is the "e-process = infimum of test martingales" statement cited by later work.
- **Proposition 25 / Corollary 26 (pointwise martingales are necessary)**: for locally dominated Q, a Q-admissible e-process satisfies e_t = ess inf_Q M^Q_t with each (M^Q_t) a Q-NM with E_Q[M^Q_0] = 1; an admissible p-process is nonincreasing and an ess sup of closed Q-max-martingales; an admissible test is an ess inf of no-overshoot martingale crossings.
- **Proposition 27 / Corollary 29 (sufficiency)**: if a reference family (Q_i) ⊂ Q sees every event some Q sees, then Q_i-admissibility for all i gives Q-admissibility; hence a Q-NM with E_Q[e_0] = 1 for all Q ∈ Q is Q-admissible, and a Q-NSM that is a Q*-NM for a locally dominating Q* is admissible. Example 28: exp(Σ X_s − t/2) is admissible for the Gaussian null {mean ≤ 0} although only a supermartingale for m < 0.
- **Theorem 30 (confidence sequences)**: {γ : ψ^γ_t = 0} is an admissible (φ, P, α)-CS iff each (ψ^γ_t) is an admissible (P_γ, α)-test; so admissible CSs are unions over P of {φ(P) : sup_{s≤t} M^P_s < 1/α} with (M^P_t) P-NMs with no overshoot, and CSs built from pointwise NSMs are valid.
- **Examples 31–32**: the necessary conditions of Corollary 26 are not sufficient (a p-process that is an ess sup of uniform max-martingales yet dominated by U; the minimum of two Gaussian martingales under a two-point null, dominated by the constant 1).
- **Lemma 33 / Corollary 34 (anti-concentration)**: if the multiplicative increments Y_t = M_t/M_{t−1} have infinite aggregate empirical variance and tails E_Q[Y_t | F_{t−1}, Y_t ≥ β] ≤ β(1 + ε), then Q(sup_t M_t ≥ 1/α) ≥ α/(1 + ε); for a composite NM satisfying this for every ε, sup_Q Q(sup_t M_t ≥ 1/α) = α exactly. Tightness of Ville's inequality does not imply admissibility (Example 50) [Sec. 8.2].
- **Section 8.3 (randomisation)**: if M_0 = 1 a.s. then sup_t M_t has an atom at one, so p_t = F(inf_{s≤t} 1/M_s) with unrandomised start is always inadmissible; adding εU to M_0 removes the atom. Conversely, continuous jumps make e-process-based tests inadmissible for every α because of overshoot. Example 49: admissible p-processes with an atom at 1/2 exist. Appendix A.3: coarsening the filtration (Vovk's conformal martingales for exchangeability) and enlarging it with U are both design tools.

### Applications (Sections 9–10)
- **Theorem 35**: e_t = exp(Σ_{i≤t} X_i − t/2) is an admissible e-process for the class G↓_0 of all conditionally 1-sub-Gaussian laws with zero conditional mean, and by Proposition 8 also for G↓_− and G↓_∼. **Proposition 36** handles the sub-classes with Lebesgue densities by Proposition 27 with reference Q_0 = i.i.d. N(0,1). **Example 37**: the same process is inadmissible for the intermediate class of Lebesgue-density laws plus one Rademacher law (dominated by exp(Σ X_i − t log cosh 1) on {|X_1| = 1}), so admissibility is neither subset- nor superset-proof. **Lemma 38** perturbs a standard Gaussian into a 1-sub-Gaussian variable with one atom at any x̄ inside an ε-interval; the proof of Theorem 35 chains t such perturbations with ε_i = ε^{2^{t−i}} and a backward induction (eq. 26) to force E_{Q*}[e′_t] > 1 for any strict dominator. Open: admissibility for nonatomic laws without densities (e.g. symmetrised Cantor).
- **Proposition 39 (symmetry martingales)**: (M_t) with bounded M_0 is an S-NM (S = conditionally symmetric about 0) iff its increments are Y_t = f_t(X_t) with f_t ≥ 0 predictable and f_t(x) + f_t(−x) = 2; an S-NSM has f_t(x) + f_t(−x) ≤ 2 and can be mirrored up to an NM. Elegant choices: f = 1 + (2/π) arctan h, 1 + sin h, 1 + tanh h with h odd. **Example 40**: de la Peña's g(x) = exp(x − x²/2) is dominated by f(x) = g(x) for x ≥ 0, 2 − g(−x) for x < 0, so the exponential symmetry e-process is inadmissible.
- **Proposition 41**: for f continuous, strictly monotone at zero, f − 1 odd, sup_{Q∈S} Q(sup_t ∏ f(X_s) ≥ 1/α) = α, so p_t = inf_{s≤t} 1/M_s exhausts the error budget (the symmetric analogue of Howard et al. 2021 Prop. 4); still not necessarily admissible (Example 50).
- **Example 42**: the closed S-max-martingale p_t = 1 − Σ_{s≤t} 2^{−N_s} 1_{X_s>0}, N_s = #{i ≤ s : X_i ≠ 0}, is an S-admissible p-process (also for median-zero nulls). **Example 43**: the random walk M_{t+1} = M_t + sign(X_t) absorbed at 0, started at 1, gives the admissible (S, α)-test 1_{M_t ≥ 1/α} whenever 1/α ∈ N, and via Theorem 30 an admissible confidence sequence for the centre of symmetry.

## Setting and assumptions
- Discrete time t ∈ N_0; data X_t in a general measurable space; filtration F_t = σ(U, X_1, …, X_t) with U an independent uniform randomisation device in F_0 (Appendix A.3 discusses other filtrations). Nulls Q and alternatives P are sets of laws on F_∞; nothing is assumed i.i.d. unless stated.
- Validity is at arbitrary, possibly infinite, stopping times, with p_∞ = lim inf, e_∞ = lim sup (Lemma 6 shows the choice is immaterial for e-processes). Optional stopping and continuation are built into the definitions.
- Local domination of Q by a reference measure is required where essential suprema and infima over Q must be well defined (Proposition 9, Corollaries 24 and 26) but not for the sufficiency results (Proposition 27, Corollary 29) or the symmetry class, which is not locally dominated (Sec. 10).
- Admissibility is defined without reference to an alternative; the standing assumption that alternatives do not charge null-polar sets (Sec. 4.2) makes "almost surely" unambiguous. Nested-in-α families are used for the test-to-p-process and CS-to-p-process reductions.
- Everything is finite-sample and nonasymptotic; no optimality criterion against an alternative (such as GROW) is considered, which the summary names as future work.

## Open questions / gaps
- Whether exp(Σ X_i − t/2) is admissible for the class of nonatomic 1-sub-Gaussian laws without Lebesgue densities (conjectured yes, Sec. 9).
- The gap between the necessary (Corollary 26) and sufficient (Corollary 29) conditions for composite admissibility is shown to be real (Examples 31, 32) but not closed; no clean characterisation is given.
- No optimality notion against an alternative (GROW, log-optimality) is combined with admissibility; the summary defers this. [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022](koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022.md) takes this up.
- Admissible sequential tests from e-processes exist only for α with 1/α matching the atomic jump structure (Example 43 needs 1/α ∈ N); continuous-jump e-processes give inadmissible tests for every α because of overshoot (Sec. 8.3).
- Cited works not in the bib: Ramdas, Ruf, Larsson and Koolen 2021 (exchangeability, fork-convexity); Shafer, Shen, Vereshchagin and Vovk 2011; Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe and Sekhon 2020 (Probability Surveys); Darling and Robbins 1967; Robbins 1970; Robbins and Siegmund 1974; Lai 1976; Johari, Pekelis and Walsh 2015; de la Peña 1999; Efron 1969; Larsson 2018; Vovk 2021 (testing randomness online); Kirichenko and Grünwald 2020.
