# Admissible online closed testing must employ e-values

## Summary
Hypotheses H_1, H_2, … arrive one at a time with a growing filtration (F_i), and the goal is a procedure d that returns, for any finite query set S ⊆ {1,…,t} chosen after seeing the data, a lower bound d(S) on the number of false nulls in S such that all bounds hold simultaneously with probability 1−α; equivalently an upper bound on the FDP of every candidate rejection set, valid at data-adaptive stopping times [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, eq. 2, Fig. 1]. The theoretical part proves that every coherent online true-discovery procedure is recovered or improved by an online closed procedure (Thm 2.1), that increasing families of online intersection tests are exactly the objects obtained from anytime-valid tests for each intersection hypothesis H_I (Thms 2.2–2.3), and that, by the Ramdas–Ruf–Larsson–Koolen characterisation, every anytime-valid test is recovered or improved by thresholding a test martingale (Fact 1); so admissible online closed testing must, by transitivity, be built from test martingales, i.e. products of sequential e-values. The methodological part constructs intersection tests ϕ_I = 1{sup_{t∈I} ∏_{i∈I, i≤t} E_i ≥ 1/α} from sequential e-values and gives SeqE-Guard, a shortcut computing the full closed bounds with one product per hypothesis (Algorithm 1, Thm 3.1). Plugging in the implicit binary e-values of Katsevich–Ramdas 2020 and Meah–Blanchard–Roquain 2024 uniformly improves every existing online true-discovery procedure (Prop. 3.3, Table 1, Props S.1–S.3); hedged GRO e-values 1−λ_i+λ_i E_i^GRO give a new method for exploratory multi-path querying (Prop. 3.4). Simulations show closed online-simple identifies 10–20 % more of the queried hypotheses as false than online-simple, and an IMPC analysis gives high-probability FDP bounds close to the LORD++ nominal FDR level.

## Main results
- Example 1: in the online setting, not all admissible true-discovery procedures are coherent (a two-hypothesis counterexample), unlike the offline result of Goeman, Hemerik and Solari 2021; the paper nevertheless restricts attention to coherent procedures [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Sec. 2.1].
- Theorem 2.1: for any coherent online true-discovery procedure d, ϕ_S = 1{d(S) > 0} (extended to infinite J by suprema) is an increasing family of online intersection tests and the online closed procedure d^ϕ ≥ d; the offline Goeman et al. 2021 result is the special case F_1 = F_2 = … [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Thm 2.1, Remark 1].
- Theorem 2.2: given anytime-valid tests (ψ^I_i)_{i∈I} for each H_I, ϕ_I = inf{sup_{i∈I} ψ^J_i : J ∩ {1,…,sup I} = I} is an increasing family of online intersection tests (ϕ_I = sup_i ψ^I_i for infinite I) [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, eq. 8].
- Theorem 2.3 (converse): any increasing family of online intersection tests defines anytime-valid tests ψ^I_i = ϕ_{I∩{1,…,i}}, and rebuilding ϕ from these via (8) returns the same closed procedure; hence every coherent online procedure implicitly contains a nontrivial anytime-valid test for every intersection hypothesis [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, eq. 9].
- Fact 1 (Ramdas et al. 2020, self-contained proof): M^P_i = P(∃j: ψ_j = 1 | F_i)/P(∃j: ψ_j = 1) is a test martingale for each null P, and ψ̃_i = inf_{P∈H_I} sup_{j≤i} 1{M^P_j ≥ 1/α} equals or uniformly improves ψ; together with Thms 2.1–2.3 this closes the loop of Figure 2: right path down (procedure → intersection tests → anytime-valid tests → martingales), left path up (martingales → Ville → tests → closure) never hurts and leaves an admissible procedure unchanged [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Sec. 2.3, Fig. 2].
- Section 3.1: every test supermartingale factors as a product of sequential e-values E_t = M_t/M_{t−1} and conversely; with sequential e-values E_t for H_t, W^t_I = ∏_{i∈I∩{1,…,t}} E_i is a test supermartingale for H_I and ϕ_I = 1{sup_{t∈I} W^t_I ≥ 1/α} is an increasing online intersection test by Ville's inequality; these tests are not symmetric in the e-values [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, eqs 10–12].
- Algorithm 1 / Theorem 3.1 (SeqE-Guard): along a nondecreasing query path S_t, keep A (queried, not yet excluded) and U (unqueried with E_i < 1); when t joins S_t, if ∏_{A∪U} E_i ≥ 1/α then d_t = d_{t−1}+1 and the largest e-value in A is removed; the bounds equal the full online closed procedure with tests (12), and P(d_t ≤ |S_t ∩ I^P_1| ∀t) ≥ 1−α; computation linear in max(S) for any single S [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Thm 3.1, proof in S.7].
- Corollary 3.2: running SeqE-Guard on several query paths with the same e-values keeps the simultaneous guarantee across all paths and times [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024].
- Example 2: 300 e-values of 0.9 and 700 of 1.1 give d_1000 ≥ 306 for the set with E_i > 1 at α = 0.05 (0.9^300 · 1.1^395 ≥ 20) while e-BH rejects nothing at any α; weak dense signal is detectable [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Sec. 3.1].
- Proposition 3.3: the online-simple bound of Katsevich–Ramdas 2020 is implicitly Ville applied to E^os_i = exp[θ_c(1{P_i ≤ α_i} − cα_i)]; SeqE-Guard with these e-values yields coherent, closed (drops the −cα_i summand of each excluded index) and admissible online-simple (divide by the null mean u_i < 1, e.g. u_i = 0.977 at α = α_i = 0.1, a = 1), three stacked uniform improvements [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, eqs 14–16, Algorithm 2].
- Props S.1–S.3 and Table 1: online-adaptive (Katsevich–Ramdas), u-online-simple and u-online-Freedman (Meah et al. 2024) are uniformly improved; for the union-bound methods the improvement averages test martingales over a with weights 6/(a²π²) instead of a union bound (m-online-simple, Algorithm 3); E^{Freed,a} comes from the Howard et al. improvement of Freedman's inequality [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, S.3].
- Section 3.2.2: the same plug-in improves offline pre-ordered and knockoff true-discovery bounds of Katsevich–Ramdas 2020, whose generic e-values E^KR_{(i)} are given in eq. 17 [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024].
- Proposition 3.4: for simple P_i vs Q_i and a two-groups prior τ_i on the alternative, the e-value maximising growth under (1−τ_i)P_i + τ_i Q_i is 1 − τ_i + τ_i E^GRO_i; hedging GRO e-values with a predictable λ_i = τ̂_i, default τ̂_i = (1/2 + Σ_{j<i} 1{E^GRO_j > 1})/i, fixes the collapse of raw GRO products when many nulls are true [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, eq. 18, Fig. 3, Fig. 5].
- Section 4: with n = 1000 Gaussian tests, α = 0.1, closed online-simple beats online-simple by 10–20 points of TDP; hedged GRO wins for sparse strong signal, admissible online-simple for dense weak signal; the online-simple family has only one sensible query path S_t = {i ≤ t: P_i ≤ α_i}, whereas hedged GRO supports many paths, including an FWER-controlling one [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Figs 4–6].
- S.4: any p-to-e calibrator applied to sequential p-values gives sequential e-values usable in SeqE-Guard, e.g. h_x(p) = exp(xΦ^{-1}(1−p) − x²/2) (the martingale Stouffer calibrator of Duan et al. 2020) [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, eq. S.9].
- S.5, Proposition S.1 (boosting): since SeqE-Guard discards the largest e-value whenever the bound increases, E_t can be truncated at the predictable m_t = max(max_{A_{t−1}} E_i, 1/(α ∏_{A_{t−1}∪U_{t−1}} E_i)) and then multiplied by b_t ≥ 1 with E_P[T_t(b_t E_t) | F_{t−1}] ≤ 1; e.g. Gaussian LR with δ = 3 and m_t = 20 allows b_t = 3.494; boosting is path-specific and may lower bounds on other paths [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, eqs S.10–S.12, Example S.1].
- S.6: on 172 081 IMPC p-values along the LORD++ path, admissible online-simple gives q(S_t) ≈ 0.1 for |S_t| ≥ 1000, a 90 % probability statement about FDP, simultaneous over t, versus LORD++'s fixed-time expectation bound [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Fig. S.4].

## Setting and assumptions
- Filtered space (Ω, F), F_0 ⊂ F_1 ⊂ …, model P; H_i ⊆ P may be chosen adaptively from F_{i−1}; I^P_0, I^P_1 are the true and false null indices [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Sec. 2].
- True-discovery procedure: d: 2^N_f → N∪{0} with P(d(S) ≤ |S ∩ I^P_1| ∀ finite S) ≥ 1−α; online if d(S) ∈ F_{max S}; coherent if d(S)+d(U) ≤ d(S∪U) ≤ d(S)+|U| for disjoint S, U; admissible if no procedure uniformly improves it [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, eqs 2–3].
- Online closed procedure: d^ϕ(S) = inf{|S \ I| : I ⊆ {1,…,max S}, ϕ_I = 0} with online intersection tests (ϕ_I ∈ F_{sup I}) that are increasing, ϕ_I ≤ ϕ_{I∪K} for K beyond sup I (the online closure principle of Fischer, Bofill Roig and Brannath 2024) [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, eqs 4–6].
- Anytime-valid test for H_I: (ψ^I_i)_{i∈I} adapted with P(∃i: ψ^I_i = 1) ≤ α under H_I; test (super)martingale indexed by I∪{0} with M_0 = 1 [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Sec. 2.3].
- Sequential e-values: E_t ∈ F_t with E_P[E_t | F_{t−1}] ≤ 1 for P ∈ H_t; in practice obtained by computing each e-value on fresh data so arbitrary dependence of designs on the past is allowed [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Sec. 3.1]. For the p-value methods, null p-values are valid conditional on the past, P(P_i ≤ x | F_{i−1}) ≤ x [Sec. 3.2].
- Optional stopping of the whole testing process is allowed: the guarantee is uniform in t, so stopping at the 50th discovery or at any data-dependent time is fine; offline testing is the special case F_1 = F_2 = … [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Sec. 2].

## Concepts touched
- [e-closure-principle](../concepts/e-closure-principle.md): online closed procedures with product-martingale intersection tests; necessity of anytime-valid tests per intersection hypothesis (Thms 2.1–2.3); SeqE-Guard shortcut (Thm 3.1).
- [test-martingale](../concepts/test-martingale.md): test supermartingale = product of sequential e-values (eq. 10); Fact 1 martingale construction from any anytime-valid test.
- [villes-inequality](../concepts/villes-inequality.md): converts W^t_I into intersection tests (eq. 12); sup over t makes the tests asymmetric.
- [e-process](../concepts/e-process.md): anytime-valid tests for H_I are thresholded test martingales (Fact 1); link between online multiple testing and sequential single-hypothesis testing.
- [gro-criterion](../concepts/gro-criterion.md): raw GRO products are powerless when many nulls hold; hedged GRO 1 − λ_i + λ_i E^GRO_i is growth-optimal under a two-groups mixture (Prop. 3.4, eq. 18).
- [e-merging](../concepts/e-merging.md): multiplication of sequential e-values; averaging test martingales over a parameter grid replaces union bounds (S.3.2).
- [calibrator](../concepts/calibrator.md): online-simple is a binary p-to-e calibrator in disguise (eq. 14); general calibrators and the exp(xΦ^{-1}(1−p) − x²/2) family (S.4).
- [e-bh](../concepts/e-bh.md): Example 2 contrast with e-BH; boosting for SeqE-Guard (S.5, Prop. S.1) adapted from Wang–Ramdas boosting.
- [post-hoc-validity](../concepts/post-hoc-validity.md): query sets, error metric path and stopping time are all chosen post hoc under one simultaneous guarantee (eq. 2, Cor. 3.2).
- [optional-stopping](../concepts/optional-stopping.md): the guarantee holds at every data-adaptive stopping time of the hypothesis stream, unlike fixed-time online FDR (S.6).
- online-multiple-testing (new): the filtration-based online setting (hypotheses arrive over time, decisions in F_{max S}), online closure principle, online true-discovery guarantee; also home for the queued online e-BH papers.

## Open questions / gaps
- Admissibility of the specific product-martingale intersection tests (12) is not claimed; the necessity results say admissible procedures must use test martingales, not that SeqE-Guard with given e-values is admissible [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024, Sec. 2.3].
- Whether hedging by 1 − τ + τE^GRO is growth-optimal for composite nulls is left open (stated after Prop. 3.4).
- Example 1 shows incoherent admissible online procedures exist; the characterisation of admissible but incoherent online procedures is not pursued.
- Implications of SeqE-Guard for offline pre-ordered and knockoff settings are only sketched (Sec. 3.2.2, Discussion).
- Fischer, Bofill Roig and Brannath 2024; Goeman, Hemerik and Solari 2021; Katsevich and Ramdas 2020; Meah, Blanchard and Roquain 2024; Vovk and Wang 2024 (sequential e-value discoveries); Duan et al. 2020 are cited but not sources.
