# False discovery rate control with e-values

arXiv 2009.02824v5 (December 2021), the version accepted at the Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B 84(3), 2022. Numbering below follows v5: Sections 1–9, Appendices A (z-test simulations), B (proofs), C (multiple transforms).

## Summary
The paper defines the e-BH procedure: given e-values e_1, …, e_K for K hypotheses and a level α, reject the k* hypotheses with the largest e-values, where k* = max{k : k e_[k]/K ≥ 1/α} (eq. 1, 5) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021]. Although this is literally BH applied to the p-values 1/e_k, its central result (Theorem 2) is that the FDR is at most K_0 α/K under arbitrary dependence between the e-values, with no Benjamini–Yekutieli factor l_K = Σ 1/k ≈ log K; the proof is a three-line argument from self-consistency plus linearity of expectation (Proposition 2). The full e-BH procedure adds an optional boosting step that multiplies each e-value by a factor b_k ≥ 1 computed from its null distribution (eqs 8–9), with stronger boosting under PRDS; Theorem 3 gives the exact FDR of base e-BH as (α/K) Σ_{k∈N} E[t_α 1{E'_k ≥ t_α}] and bounds it by (K_0/K) y_α, Theorem 4 gives the PRDS analogue, and Example 4 shows the bound αK_0/K is attained. Theorem 5 shows base e-BH is optimal among procedures G(φ) that apply a common increasing transform to arbitrarily dependent e-values, and Proposition 4 recovers BH and BY (with analytic proofs of their FDR bounds) as e-BH applied to calibrated p-values, every step-up procedure with y_ψ ≤ α being obtainable by calibration. Self-consistency also yields structured, post-selection, focused and grouped e-BH variants valid under any dependence (Section 5.1); simulations in an ordered multi-armed bandit (Section 7), on correlated z-tests (Appendix A) and on 496 cryptocurrencies (Section 8) compare e-BH with BH, BY and compliant BH [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].

## Main results
- **Definition, eq. (1)/(5), Proposition 1.** Base e-BH G(α) rejects the k* largest e-values with k* = max{k : k e_[k]/K ≥ 1/α}; equivalently reject H_k iff e'_k ≥ t_α where t_α := inf{t : t R(t) ≥ K/α}, R(t) = |{k : e'_k ≥ t}| ∨ 1, and t_α R(t_α) = K/α holds exactly (eq. 6) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Theorem 2.** The (boosted) e-BH procedure at level α has FDR ≤ K_0 α/K; in particular base e-BH on arbitrarily dependent raw e-values has FDR ≤ K_0 α/K [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Proposition 2 (self-consistency).** Any e-testing procedure that is self-consistent at level α (every rejected e_k ≥ K/(α R)) has FDR ≤ αK_0/K for arbitrary e-value configurations: FDP ≤ Σ_{k∈N} αE_k/K pointwise, then take expectations (eq. 12). Contrast: self-consistent p-procedures only reach α(1 + log(1/α)) under near-PRDS (Su 2018), so compliant BH must run at α' with α'(1 + log(1/α')) = α (eq. 13), e.g. α' = 0.0087 for α = 0.05 [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Section 4.2, eqs (7)–(11): boosting.** With T(x) = K/⌈K/x⌉ 1{x ≥ 1} the truncation to the grid {K/k}, choose b_k ≥ 1 with max_{x∈K/K} x P(αb_k E_k ≥ x) ≤ α under PRDS (8) or E[T(αb_k E_k)] ≤ α otherwise (9); b_k = 1 is always safe; general increasing φ_k replace b_k x (10)–(11). Boosted e-values can be ranked differently from raw ones, by design (Section 5.2 item 4) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Lemma 1.** For f(X) a data-dependent threshold with range I_f and X'_k the truncation of X_k onto I_f: E[f(X) 1{X_k ≥ f(X)}] ≤ E[X'_k] ≤ E[X_k] under any dependence (iii); ≤ sup_x x P(X'_k ≥ x) under PRDS with f decreasing (ii); conditional version under independence (i). The p-value analogue (Ramdas et al. 2019a, Lemma 1) pays l_K under arbitrary dependence; the e-version does not [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Theorem 3 (exact FDR, arbitrary dependence).** For arbitrary nonnegative E'_1..E'_K, FDR of base e-BH = (α/K) Σ_{k∈N} E[t_α 1{E'_k ≥ t_α}] ≤ (K_0/K) y_α, with y_α = (1/K_0) Σ_{k∈N} E[T(αE'_k)] (eq. 16); y_α ≤ α for raw or (9)/(11)-boosted e-values. Weighted e-BH with weights summing to K follows (Section 5.2). Conservative K-free surrogate ȳ_{α,k}(b) = E[αbE_k 1{αbE_k ≥ 1}] (eq. 17) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Example 4 (sharpness).** Null e-values K/(K_0 α) 1_A with P(A) = αK_0/K and all others 0 give FDR = αK_0/K exactly; the bound cannot be improved without extra information [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Theorem 4 (PRDS).** If raw e-values are PRDS (flipped definition, Section 3), FDR ≤ (K_0/K) z_α with z_α = (1/K_0) Σ_{k∈N} max_{x∈K/K} x P(αE'_k ≥ x) (eqs 20–21); z_α ≤ y_α by Markov [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Proposition 3.** For a continuously distributed null E_k with t ↦ t P(E_k ≥ t) decreasing on [q_{1−α}(E_k), ∞) (eq. 23), the largest PRDS boosting factor is b_k = 1/(α q_{1−α}(E_k)) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Examples 2, 3, 5, 6 (boosting magnitudes, α = 0.05).** Calibrated uniform p-values E = λP^{λ−1}, λ = 1/2: b = (2/α)^{1/2} ≈ 6.32 under arbitrary dependence, b = 2α^{−1/2} ≈ 8.94 under PRDS, FDR improves by factor λ. Gaussian likelihood ratios exp(δX − δ²/2): b ≈ 1.37 (δ = 3) and 1.11 (δ = 4) under arbitrary dependence, 7.88 and 10.31 under PRDS. "A boosting factor between 1.3 and 10 is common" (Section 5.2) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Theorem 5 (optimality).** For any increasing transform φ, if G(φ) (reject k* = max{k : kφ(e_[k])/K ≥ 1}) controls FDR at α for arbitrary e-value configurations, then G(φ) ⊆ G(α): base e-BH dominates every such procedure [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Proposition 4 (BH and BY as special cases).** For a decreasing transform ψ, the step-up p-procedure D(ψ) (reject k* = max{k : ψ(p_(k)) ≥ K/k}) has FDR ≤ (K_0/K) y_ψ with y_ψ = ψ^{−1}(1) + Σ_{j=1}^{K−1} K ψ^{−1}(K/j)/(j(j+1)) (eq. 27), and ≤ (K_0/K) z_ψ, z_ψ = max_{t∈K/K} t ψ^{−1}(t), under PRDS (eq. 28). For ψ = α/p this gives y_ψ = α l_K and z_ψ = α, an analytic proof of Theorem 1 (BH under PRDS, BY under arbitrary dependence). Every D(ψ) with y_ψ ≤ α equals e-BH on f(p_k) for the calibrator f = ψ/α; Blanchard–Roquain shape functions β(k) = ∫_0^k x dν(x) are recovered (Remark 4, eqs 30–31) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Proposition 5 (weak optimality of BH).** If D(ψ) controls FDR at α for arbitrary PRDS p-values then ψ^{−1}(1) ≤ α, and under condition (29) D(ψ) ⊆ D(α). Example 7: the calibrated procedure with ψ = (2α)^{1/2} p^{−1/2}/2 beats BY roughly when the rejection proportion exceeds 2/log K, so BY is not uniformly best under arbitrary dependence [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Proposition 6 (Appendix C).** With hypothesis-specific transforms ψ_1..ψ_K, FDR ≤ (1/K) Σ_{k∈N} y_{ψ_k} (z_{ψ_k} under PRDS); no longer a step-up rule [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Section 5.1: structured, post-selection, focused, grouped e-BH.** Structured: any S in an allowed class with every e-value in S ≥ K/(α|S|) controls FDR (greedy search acceptable). Post-selection: after any data-dependent screening to S ⊂ K, run e-BH on S at α̃ = α|S|/K. Both hold under arbitrary dependence and arbitrary selection, unlike the structured/post-selection BH of Ramdas et al. 2019a which needs BY reshaping; focused e-BH (Katsevich et al. 2021 analogue) and group-level e-BH by e-merging follow [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Section 5.2 item 7 (approximate e-values).** If null E[E_i] ≤ 1 + ε_i then FDR ≤ (α/K)(K_0 + Σ_{i∈N} ε_i) ≤ α(1 + max ε_i); for asymptotic e-values FDR ≤ α(1 + o(1))K_0/K. Errors propagate linearly; "no existing simple, analogous claim for the BH procedure" [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Fact 1.** PRDS is preserved by calibrators (p to e) and inversion (e to p); Gaussian mixture likelihood ratios of positively correlated statistics are PRDS [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Section 7 (ordered multi-armed bandits).** K arms visited in order, each pulled at most n times, stopping when the multiple-testing procedure makes a new discovery; e-values E_{k,j} = Π X^k_i are stopped e-processes and p-values are 1/max_i E_{k,i} by Ville. Stopping rules induce negative dependence, so BH has no guarantee; e-BH and BY are valid under arbitrary dependence across arms and stopping rules, cBH needs independent arms (Table 1). Defaults K = 500, n = 50, α = 0.05, log-normal rewards, non-null probability θ(K−k+1)/(K+1) with θ = 1/2: e-BH 74.4 rejections, 73.2 true, FDP 1.58%, vs BH 77.0/75.3/2.13%, BY 70.6/70.4/0.31%, cBH 71.1/70.8/0.36% (Table 2a); same ordering for K = 2000, n ∈ {10, 100}, μ ∈ {0.5, 2} [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Appendix A, Table 4 (correlated z-tests, δ = −3).** K = 1000, K_0 = 800, independent, α = 5%: BH 148.7, e-BH PRDS-boosted 147.6, e-BH AD-boosted 85.4, BY 78.8, base e-BH 70.6 rejections. K = 20,000, K_0 = 10,000, α = 10%: BY 5956 vs e-BH AD 6811. BY beats e-BH AD only for sparse signal and small α (K_0 = 19,000, α ∈ {5%, 2%}), consistent with Example 7; results unchanged under negative correlation [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- **Section 8 (cryptocurrencies).** 496 coins, monthly prices Jan 2015 – Jun 2021 (T = 78), null E[X_{k,j} | F_{j−1}] ≤ 1, wealth process W_{k,t} = Π(1 − λ + λX_{k,j}) is an e-process; e-BH at 5% with 50% rebalancing selects 42/28/13 coins for K = 496/200/100 vs BY 15/11/6; buy-and-hold with K = 496 is the one case where BY (11) beats e-BH (3) (Table 3) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].

## Setting and assumptions
- K hypotheses H_k, each a set of joint measures; N the unknown null index set, K_0 = |N|. Null e-variables satisfy E[E_k] ≤ 1, null p-variables P(P_k ≤ α) ≤ α; procedures are Borel maps from [0,∞]^K or [0,1]^K to 2^K. FDR = E[F_D/R_D] with 0/0 = 0 [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021, Section 1].
- No dependence assumption for the main theorem; PRDS (Finner et al. 2009 version, flipped for e-values: x ↦ P(E ∈ A | E_k ≥ x) decreasing for decreasing A) for the sharper bound; boosting additionally needs (partial) knowledge of null marginals, with an infimum over a set of candidate nulls when composite (Section 5.2 item 3) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- Fixed K and fixed α; no post-hoc α is discussed. Optional stopping enters only through stopped e-processes used as inputs (Sections 7–8); conditional-mean nulls E[X_{k,j} | F_{j−1}] ≤ 1 make products and empirical-Bernstein-type processes exp(λ(X−1) − ψ(λ)(X−1)²) valid e-values (Example 1) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- Section 2 lists six statistical (not philosophical) reasons to prefer e-values: high-dimensional asymptotics, irregular composite models via universal inference, sequential inference, accumulation of evidence across studies, robustness to misspecification (symmetric data: exp(λX − λ²X²/2)), robustness to dependence ((X_1 + ... + X_n)/(nμ) for nonnegative data with means ≤ μ); the price is less power under correct full specification, "different points on the validity-power curve" [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- Remark 1: if e-values are only obtainable by calibrating genuine p-values, e-BH "will typically not be more powerful than BH"; the gain is when p-values are only available by inverting e-values [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].

## Open questions / gaps
- Hybrid situations: some hypotheses with natural e-values and others with natural p-values, or both for one hypothesis from different experiments; whether to calibrate all to p or all to e, and the resulting power, is left for "thorough theoretical and empirical studies" (Section 9) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- Additional boosting under a multivariate Gaussian dependence structure (as used by knockoffs, Fithian–Lei conditional calibration) is posed as future work (Section 9) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- The FDR bound of Proposition 4 under PRDS is not sharp in general (the inequality in the proof of Theorem 4 is strict); Proposition 5 needs condition (29) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021, Section 6.5].
- Finding the largest boosting factor is only analytic under condition (23); otherwise trial-and-error or the conservative K-free surrogates (17), (24) [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021, Remark 2].
- An e-filter (multi-partition) extension of p-filter is mentioned but not derived (Section 5.1); focused e-BH details are omitted [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021].
- No post-hoc choice of α and no randomisation; both appear in later work recorded on the e-bh concept page.
