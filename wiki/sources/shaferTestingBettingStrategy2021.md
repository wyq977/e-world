# Testing by betting: a strategy for statistical and scientific communication

JRSS A discussion paper, read before the Royal Statistical Society at the Society's 2020 annual conference (online), 9 September 2020, President D. Ashby in the chair. Received 15 March 2019, accepted 11 May 2020. The arXiv version (1903.06991, first posted March 2019) carried the title "The language of betting as a strategy for statistical and scientific communication". The text layer here is the version of record plus 24 discussion contributions, a distillation of the live chat, and the author's written reply (pp. 432-478, DOIs 10.1111/rssa.12648-12672).

## Summary
A test of a probability distribution P is a bet: the tester buys a nonnegative payoff S with E_P[S] = 1 and the "betting score" S(y) is the factor by which the stake is multiplied; a large score discredits P, and Markov gives P(S >= 1/alpha) <= alpha [shaferTestingBettingStrategy2021]. For discrete P the bet S determines an "implied alternative" Q := SP and S = Q/P is a likelihood ratio, so betting scores inherit the evidential meaning of likelihood ratios; by Gibbs' inequality Q/P is the bet maximising E_Q[ln S], and Kelly's argument (the logarithm is what is additive and obeys the law of large numbers under repeated bets) is the reason to optimise expected log rather than E_Q[S] or Q(S >= 1/alpha), the latter being the Neyman-Pearson criterion appropriate only for one-off decisions (Section 2.2) [shaferTestingBettingStrategy2021]. The "implied target" S* := exp(E_Q[ln S]) = exp(E_P[S ln S]) is proposed as a replacement for power: it is determined by P and S alone, and the triple (bet, implied target, betting score) tells a coherent story where the triple (significance level, p-value, power) does not (Sections 2.3-2.4). Successive bets multiply, with no need for a strategy fixed in advance, which licenses opportunistic testing and meta-analysis across unplanned studies (Section 2.1.2). Section 3 proposes the calibrator p -> 1/sqrt(p) - 1 as an arbitrary but convenient convention for putting p-values on the betting-score scale. Section 4 formalises testing protocols (perfect-information games between Sceptic, Reality and optionally Forecaster) and introduces the (1/alpha)-warranty set {theta : S(theta) < 1/alpha} as the betting analogue of a (1-alpha)-confidence set, nested across alpha, valid under optional continuation, and extending to nonparametric settings such as bounded-mean estimation by least squares; the appendix situates the proposal historically against Ville, von Mises, Fisher and Doob [shaferTestingBettingStrategy2021].

## Main results
- **Betting score and all-or-nothing bets** (Section 2.1, eq. 1): the level-alpha test is the special bet S_E = (1/alpha) 1_E with P(E) = alpha; general bets give a graduated appraisal of evidence. A p-value is the smallest alpha at which a family of all-or-nothing tests rejects (eqs. 2-3) and "had I known y in advance I could have multiplied my money by 1/p(y)", which is why 1/p is not a betting score [shaferTestingBettingStrategy2021].
- **Opportunistic multiplication** (Section 2.1.2): with initial capital 1 and bets S_n of P-expected value equal to current capital, the capital after n bets is the product S_1(y_1)...S_n(y_n); the Bonferroni adjustment is the special case of 20 all-or-nothing bets of 1 each, and non-all-or-nothing bets can make 20 "promising" results add up.
- **Score = likelihood ratio** (Section 2.2, eq. 4): E_P[S] = 1 means Q := SP is a probability distribution and S = Q/P; conversely every likelihood ratio Q/P is a bet. A referee's identity: E_Q[S] - 1 = Var_P(S).
- **Kelly / log-optimality** (Section 2.2.1, eq. 5): Gibbs' inequality E_Q[ln(Q/P)] >= E_Q[ln(R/P)] shows Q/P maximises E_Q[ln S] over all bets; the maximum is the KL divergence (Kullback's "mean information for discrimination"). Maximising E_Q[S] would stake everything on the single outcome with the largest Q/P; the game-theoretic law of large numbers (Shafer & Vovk 2019, Ch. 2) makes the log criterion meaningful even when opportunistic bets S_1, S_2, ... define only successive conditional alternatives Q_i and no joint Q.
- **Neyman-Pearson lemma, betting form** (Section 2.2.2): the level-alpha NP bet maximises Q(S >= 1/alpha) over all bets S, not just all-or-nothing ones (proof by thresholding any S to an all-or-nothing bet of smaller P-price). By Markov, when the NP bet just barely succeeds, the bet Q/P multiplies the stake by less: alpha = P(Q/P >= Q(y_0)/P(y_0)) <= P(y_0)/Q(y_0).
- **Low scores** (Section 2.2.3): S(y) < 1/5 says P beats the implied Q, not that P is supported; a bet chosen on a whim carries no commitment to Q (Fisher: testing precedes alternatives).
- **Implied target** (Section 2.3, Table 1): S* := exp(E_Q[ln S]) >= 1, computable as exp(E_P[S ln S]) without naming Q; the all-or-nothing bet has implied target 1/alpha but an unreasonable implied Q. A study with plausible P and Q and a high implied target merits publication whatever the score, which could reduce publication bias.
- **Examples 1-3** (Section 2.4), all with P = N(0, 10^2): (1) Q = N(1, 10^2), y = 30: p = 0.00135, NP level-0.05 test rejects with 6% power, but the implied target is exp(1/200) = 1.005 and the score 1.34, "the study was a waste of time". (2) Q = N(37, 10^2), y = 16.5: p = 0.0495, NP rejects with 98% power, implied target 939, score 0.477, evidence mildly favours P. (3) Q = N(20, 10^2), y = 5: p = 0.31, implied target 7.39, score 0.368. Bet/implied target/score always cohere; p-value/level/power do not.
- **Calibrator 1/sqrt(p) - 1** (Section 3, eq. 6, Table 2): 1/p has infinite P-expectation and must be shrunk to a payoff of expected value 1; infinitely many shrinkings exist and the choice is "fundamentally arbitrary" (Shafer & Vovk 2019, Section 11.5), so a monotone, smooth, unbounded convention is proposed: p = 0.10, 0.05, 0.01, 0.005, 0.001, 10^-6 map to 2.2, 3.5, 9.0, 13.1, 30.6, 999. A shrinking rule also implies an alternative: for Fisher's Weldon dice z-test (Example 4; p = 1 in 5 million vs 0.00003 for the chi-square, scores about 2000 and 200), Q = f(p) P has the same centre as P but much heavier tails, supporting Berkson's caution about chi-square tests [shaferTestingBettingStrategy2021].
- **Protocols 1-4** (Section 4.1): testing a distribution (one bet), a stochastic process (Sceptic bets round by round with E_P[S_n | past] = K_{n-1}; any global bet can be implemented step by step and the capital is then a nonnegative martingale), a model with signals x_n, and a Forecaster who announces P_n as play proceeds (prequential in Dawid's sense, no joint distribution needed).
- **Protocol 5 and warranties** (Section 4.2): Reality announces theta, invisible to the statistician; a strategy for Sceptic yields a final capital S(theta) as a function of the data. Betting score against composite Theta_0 is inf_{theta in Theta_0} S(theta); the (1/alpha)-warranty set is W_{1/alpha} := {theta : S(theta) < 1/alpha}. Every (1-alpha)-confidence set is a (1/alpha)-warranty set from all-or-nothing bets; warranty sets are nested in alpha and come from one strategy; stopping when the statistician pleases is allowed ("the only caveat is that we cannot pretend to have stopped before we actually did"), unlike confidence intervals under continual monitoring (Cornfield's "sampling to a foregone conclusion"); SPRT confidence sequences (Lai 2009) are warranty sets. The lineage of the notion: Vovk 1993 Section 7, Schnorr 1971, Levin 1976. Choosing the strategy to get small warranty sets for all n and alpha is left open, with a pointer to Grünwald et al. 2019.
- **Protocols 6-7, nonparametric least squares** (Section 4.3): for errors e_n in [-1, 1] with mean zero the hypothesis only prices linear payoffs, so Sceptic bets a_n in [-K_{n-1}, K_{n-1}] on e_n; a Hoeffding-based strategy (Shafer & Vovk 2019, Section 3.3) guarantees K_n >= 20 whenever |mean(e_1..e_n)| > 2.72/sqrt(n), simultaneously for all n (vs 2.0/sqrt(N) from the CLT at one fixed N). Hence a 20-warranty mu in ybar_100 +/- 0.272 after 100 measurements, and a meta-analyst can multiply the functions of mu from successive studies.
- **Appendix (Section 6 in the arXiv numbering)**: testing by betting is not decision theory (play money suffices); it extends to imprecise probabilities (fewer betting offers); it differs from de Finetti (bets that are settled vs never settled; coherence is about Forecaster, discrediting is about Reality); and the betting reading of likelihood ratios and martingales is historically absent: Ville (1939, pp. 87-89) strengthened von Mises' impossibility of a gambling system, Fisher used likelihood 1/15 as "grave suspicion", Edwards and Royall advocated likelihood ratios, but no one treated the running value of a nonnegative martingale as cumulative evidence once Doob stripped martingales of their betting meaning [shaferTestingBettingStrategy2021].

## Setting and assumptions
- Simple null P for a phenomenon Y (discrete for the likelihood-ratio identity; densities in the examples), bets S >= 0 with E_P[S] = 1 declared before y is seen. No alternative is required; the implied alternative is derived from the bet.
- Sequential settings use perfect-information protocols; Sceptic's bet on round n may depend on all earlier moves and side information; capital never goes negative, so the capital process is a nonnegative martingale under the null (supermartingale when offers are one-sided or Sceptic bets suboptimally).
- Composite hypotheses are handled by a strategy indexed by theta, with the infimum of capitals over Theta_0 as the score; warranty sets assume Protocol 5 (i.i.d. observations from a parametric model) or the nonparametric Protocols 6-7.
- Optional continuation is allowed throughout; stopping may depend on the data, but reported capital must be the capital at the actual stopping time (adjusting for a reported maximum is addressed in the reply via Dawid et al. 2011).
- The calibrator argument assumes p uniform under P in the ideal case, stochastically larger in general (so f(p) has expectation <= 1).

## Discussion (brief)
Of the 24 contributions, those that matter for e-value methodology:
- **Grünwald**: proves that Shafer's composite betting score inf_theta S_theta and the Safe Testing e-variable (E_P[S] <= 1 for all theta in Theta_0) are equivalent definitions, and stresses that Bayes factors for composite nulls satisfy only E_{theta ~ W_0} E_theta[B] = 1, not the e-variable constraint, unless W_0 is a matching (RIPr) prior from the Grünwald-Dawid minimax theorem; right-Haar priors on a nuisance variance arise this way [grunwaldSafeTesting2024].
- **Vovk**: betting scores live in game-theoretic probability, e-values in measure-theoretic probability; calling betting scores "Bayes factors" confuses Bayesians; Jeffreys' rule of thumb (p = 0.05 ~ BF 10^{1/2}, p = 0.01 ~ 10) agrees closely with 1/sqrt(p) - 1 (3.47 and 9); data splitting with e-values restores inferential reproducibility (arXiv 2008.11474, 2009.08933).
- **Wang**: e-values as the abstract measure-theoretic term covering betting scores, likelihood ratios and stopped supermartingales (Table 1 analogy with p-values); the arithmetic mean is an e-merging function valid under arbitrary dependence and essentially dominates all symmetric ones; all admissible p-merging goes through calibration to e-values; e-values for FDR control [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021].
- **Ramdas**: Wald's eq. (10:10) plug-in likelihood ratio as a betting strategy with Ville's inequality; every composite nonnegative martingale is a likelihood ratio dQ/dP for every P in the null; admissible e-values must be infima of pointwise martingales (Ramdas, Ruf, Larsson & Koolen 2020); confidence sequences as measure-theoretic warranty sets [howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021]; universal inference's split likelihood ratio is an e-value [wassermanUniversalInference2020]; e-BH controls FDR under arbitrary dependence.
- **ter Schure**: applied bets should be defined through parameters of minimal interest (t-test, 2x2, logrank), deliberately forgoing profit below the smallest effect size of interest; ALL-IN meta-analysis lets each study keep its own protocol [terschureAnytimevalidLogrankTest2024].
- **Dawid** (proposer): how to choose among bets with no Q (maximum over a family needs adjustment); do warranty sets have a coverage property; tying strategies across theta (group structure in Protocol 7); selective reporting (Dawid & Dickey 1977). **Greenland**: 1/sqrt(p) - 1 is unmotivated; prefers the surprisal -log_b p. **Martin**: betting scores are below 1/p so betting procedures are less efficient; p-values as plausibilities. **Senn**: Fisher's product of p-values; conditioning on relevant subsets. **Meng**: Bayes factor as posterior expectation of a theta-indexed betting score; exponential tilting links test statistics to log-likelihood ratios. **Hennig**, **Crane**, **Pedersen**, **Coolen**, **Krakauer & Rice**, **Lai & Choi**, **Longford**, **Mateu**, **Osimani**, **Vos**, **Wijayatunga**, **Chai**, **Kumar**: communication, incentives, real stakes, decision theory.
- **Author's reply**: averaging bets is the right multiplicity adjustment (any weighted average of bets is a bet, equivalent to averaging implied alternatives; with prior weights it is the Bayes factor for a simple null); betting scores are more general than e-values because S_1(y_1)S_2(y_2) across unplanned experiments is not the value of any single bet; in Protocol 5 a (1/alpha)-warranty set is exactly a (1-alpha)-confidence set (via the game-theoretic definition of probability), while warranty extends to unplanned sequences of experiments where confidence would need a "demigod"; the calibrator inverts Jeffreys' "decisive" K = 10^-2 to p = 1/10,201; for simulation-only models use S = T/E_P[T] with the implied target from E_P[S ln S]; terminology history (Vovk & V'yugin 1993 "impossibility measure", Vovk 2019 "e-value", Vovk & Wang 2019 "e-variable"); martingale history (Ville's capital process, Wald, Doob) [shaferTestingBettingStrategy2021].

## Open questions / gaps
- How to choose Sceptic's strategy so that warranty sets are small across all n and alpha simultaneously (Section 4.2); Shafer defers to Grünwald et al. 2019.
- Whether warranty sets have a coverage property beyond Protocol 5 (Dawid); the reply gives only the tautological game-theoretic coverage for unplanned sequences of experiments.
- Principles tying bets across parameter values (group structure, exponential families, UMP analogues) (Dawid; reply says it is a matter of hunches, not principle).
- Adjusting betting scores for selective or partial reporting beyond the reported-maximum case of Dawid et al. 2011 (Dawid; reply).
- Empirical evidence that betting language is actually better understood than p-values (live chat; ter Schure promises an experiment).
- Whether the choice of calibrator can be given a non-arbitrary justification (Greenland vs Shafer); the reply accepts -ln p as valid but prefers 1/sqrt(p) - 1 for its match with Jeffreys.
- Extending Ramdas's admissibility results from measure-theoretic martingales to game-theoretic nonnegative supermartingales (reply).
