# The e-posterior

Single-author paper in the Phil. Trans. A theme issue on Bayesian inference, written for a Bayesian readership. It is the companion of [grunwaldBeyondNeymanPearsonEvalues2024](grunwaldBeyondNeymanPearsonEvalues2024.md) (cited here as Grünwald 2022): that paper keeps a Neyman–Pearson-style hard constraint on Type-I risk, this one drops the Type-I/Type-II distinction altogether and treats arbitrary decision problems minimax-style [grunwaldEposterior2023, Sec. 5]. The main text is 22 pages; a Supplementary Material (Secs A–C, Theorem 1, Props 3–4) carries the exponential-family proofs.

## Summary
Given a model {P_θ : θ ∈ Θ} and an *e-collection* S = {S_θ : θ ∈ Θ}, one [e-variable](../concepts/e-variable.md) per parameter value (E_{P_θ}[S_θ] ≤ 1, eq. 3), the e-posterior is P̄(θ | y) := 1/S_θ(y) (eq. 4). It plays the role of a Bayesian posterior in that one and the same object is combined with arbitrary, possibly post-hoc, loss functions L : Θ × A → R: the *e-posterior risk bound* of a decision rule δ is R̄(δ) := sup_θ P̄(θ | Y) · L(θ, δ(Y)) (eq. 5) and the *e-posterior minimax rule* δ_mm minimises sup_θ P̄(θ | y) L(θ, a) over a for each y (eq. 6). Proposition 1 shows that for every e-collection, every rule and every loss with sup_a L(θ, a) ≥ 0 ("no-sure-gain"), E_{P_θ}[L(θ, δ(Y))/R̄(δ)] ≤ 1 for all θ (eq. 15), and hence that any data-dependent importance weight B = b(Y, U) that is guaranteed to satisfy B · R̄(δ) ≤ ℓ yields expected loss at most ℓ (eq. 16). The guarantee holds whatever e-collection is used, so a badly chosen S makes the bounds loose rather than wrong, whereas a badly chosen prior makes Bayesian posterior-expected losses wrong [grunwaldEposterior2023, Sec. 1]. The author calls the resulting stance *quasi-conditional*: the decision maker may raise the stakes on more extreme data, as a conditioning Bayesian would, but only in games where the bookie is bound a priori, and performance is evaluated in expectation over data (Sec. 2). Section 3 compares e-collections (Savage–Dickey, likelihood-ratio, two-point-prior, Berger–Brown–Wolpert) and shows that with a well-chosen collection the bounds are within a factor 1.45 of the frequentist minimax risk for Gaussian location (Example 8), a factor 2 of the uniform-prior Bayes risk for simple testing (eq. 28), and that the BBW conditional error probabilities of 1994 and the Kiefer 1976/1977 and Vovk 1993 conditional confidence estimators are e-posteriors of a very restricted kind (Sec. 3.2). Section 4 introduces the capped e-posterior min{1, P̄} and Condition Zero, and Section 5 generalises to nuisance parameters, composite nulls and nonparametrics via eq. 38.

## Main results
- **Definitions (Sec. 1, eqs 3–6)**: e-collection {S_θ}; e-posterior P̄(θ | y) = 1/S_θ(y) with 1/0 := ∞; e-posterior risk bound R̄(y, a) = sup_θ P̄(θ | y) L(θ, a) with ∞ · 0 := 0; e-posterior minimax rule δ_mm. The reciprocal 1/S_θ was studied earlier as a "confidence distribution" by Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2022 (App. D5, first version 2020) and by Pawel, Ly and Wagenmakers 2022; the decision-theoretic analysis is new.
- **Example 1 (Savage–Dickey e-posterior, eqs 7–9)**: S^sd_θ = p_W(y)/p_θ(y) gives P̄^sd(θ | y) = w(θ | y)/w(θ), the Savage–Dickey density ratio (the "prior-to-posterior e-variable" of Neiswanger and Ramdas 2021). The Bayesian assessment is E_{θ∼W}[P̄^sd(θ | y) L(θ, δ(y))] (eq. 8) while R̄(δ) = max_θ P̄^sd(θ | y) L(θ, δ(y)) (eq. 9): the e-posterior replaces the prior expectation by a maximum and is always more pessimistic.
- **Example 2 (Gaussian location, N(θ_0, 1/λ) prior, eq. 11)**: P̄^sd(θ | x^n) = √((n+λ)/λ) · exp(−(n/2)(θ − θ̂)² + ½ · nλ/(n+λ) · (θ̂ − θ_0)²); Fig. 1 shows a maximum near 17 at n = 100, θ̂ = 1, λ = 1.
- **Example 3 / Example 8 (two-point prior e-process, eq. 12)**: S_θ = (½ p_{θ−} + ½ p_{θ+})/p_θ with ½(θ − θ±)² = C/n*, the two-sided version of Johnson's 2013 UMP Bayes factor at C = −log α; with C = 1 the minimax action is the MLE for every n and n* (SM Prop. 3) and max_θ (θ − θ̂)² P̄(θ | y) = (1/n) · g(n, n*) · 1.4467…, g(n, n*) = (n*/n) e^{n/n* − 1} (eq. 24), attained at θ = θ̂ ± 1.46/√n where P̄ ≈ 0.68 (so Condition Zero holds automatically, Example 10).
- **Example 4 (LR e-variables, eq. 13)**: for simple H_0 = {P_0} vs H_1 = {P_1} with S_1 = p_0/p_1, S_0 = p_1/p_0 and a Wald–Neyman–Pearson loss under C0 (L(0,0) = L(1,1) = 0), R̄(δ) = (p_1/p_0) L(1, δ) + (p_0/p_1) L(0, δ); this is the expression one reaches via Edwards–Lindman–Savage 1963 or Royall's 1997 universal bound.
- **Example 5 / eq. 14 (BBW e-posterior at w = ½)**: R̄(δ_mm) = p_1/(p_0 + p_1) L(1, δ_mm) + p_0/(p_0 + p_1) L(0, δ_mm), formally identical to the uniform-prior Bayes assessment and uniformly slightly better than eq. 13, but only for the one rule δ_mm = δ^bbw. The author says the p_1/p_0 versus p_1/(p_0 + p_1) discrepancy "tripped me up for about 15 years" and motivated the paper.
- **Proposition 1 (Sec. 2, eqs 15–16)**: for any model, e-collection, rule and no-sure-gain loss, E_{P_θ}[L(θ, δ(Y))/R̄(δ)] ≤ 1 with 0/0 := 0; hence for any nonnegative b(Y, U, θ, a) and any joint P'_θ with Y-marginal P_θ, E[b · L(θ, δ(Y))] ≤ sup_{y,u,θ} b(y, u, θ, δ(y)) · R̄(y, δ(y)). Also R̄(y, δ_mm(y)) ≤ R̄(y, δ(y)) for all y. Proof is three lines from E[S_θ] ≤ 1.
- **Bookie interpretation (Sec. 2)**: DM fixes δ, receives ℓ > 0, then θ, Y and external U are revealed, the bookie names B_{θ,a} = b(Y, U, θ, a) > 0 and DM pays back B_{θ,δ(Y)} L(θ, δ(Y)); DM has nonnegative expected gain iff the bookie is bound a priori to B ≤ ℓ/R̄(δ). A "simple bookie" has B = b(y, u) free of θ and a; then knowing B changes neither the Bayes nor the e-posterior minimax action.
- **Example 6 (policy makers)**: vaccine-efficacy test (null ve ≤ 30%, alternative ve ≥ 50%); unexpectedly strong evidence plus a higher reproduction number makes policy makers want the more drastic action "vaccinate every adult", modelled as a data-dependent weight B on all losses; the "final risk checker" fixes ℓ before the data and accepts any B with B · R̄(Y, δ(Y)) ≤ ℓ, however vaguely B is arrived at. Negative losses L(1,1), L(0,0) < 0 are allowed.
- **Example 7 (weighted squared error; objective Bayes fails)**: Gaussian location with a stopping time τ, L_y(θ, a) = b(y)(θ − a)²; the flat-prior posterior N(θ̂, 1/n) (which coincides with the fiducial and confidence distribution and is "matching") believes risk b(n, x^n)/n (eq. 19) while the actual risk is E[b(τ, X^τ)(θ* − θ̂)²] (eq. 21); with b := τ and τ = min{n : (θ̂ − θ*)²/n ≥ k*} (finite a.s. by the LIL) believed risk is 1 and actual risk ≥ k* for any k*. By Prop. 1 this cannot happen to an e-posterior assessment.
- **Quasi-conditional stance (Sec. 2)**: a Bayesian conditions on R̄(δ) = r and accepts any B with B · r ≤ ℓ; the e-posterior DM accepts only when the bookie is bound a priori to B ≤ ℓ/R̄(δ), a strict subset of the games a conditioning Bayesian accepts but a superset of the unconditional Neyman–Pearson games. Claimed to differ from objective Bayes with matching priors, confidence/fiducial distributions and the BBW conditionalist approach.
- **Sec. 3 (taxonomy, eqs 22–23)**: any e-variable for a simple θ is a likelihood ratio q^[n]_θ(X^n)/p_θ(X^n); an e-specification (S^[n]_θ)_n is an [e-process](../concepts/e-process.md) iff q does not depend on n, in which case E_{P_θ}[S^[τ]_θ] ≤ 1 for every stopping time τ and decision making via eq. 5 proceeds under [optional stopping](../concepts/optional-stopping.md). *Generalised Savage–Dickey e-processes* S^[n]_θ = p_{W_θ}(X^n)/p_θ(X^n) let the prior depend on θ (eq. 23); Savage–Dickey, LR and two-point collections are all of this form.
- **Example 9 (simple testing, eqs 25–29)**: with priors w_θ on {0, 1}, under C0 δ_mm picks 0 iff f(p_0/p_1) ≥ L(1,0)/L(0,1) for a strictly increasing f; pure Savage–Dickey (w_0 = w_1) gives the uniform-prior Bayes rule, LR gives the rule with exponent γ = ½ on the losses (eq. 27). Risk bounds: uniform Savage–Dickey gives 2 · E_{θ∼W_u|y}[L(θ, δ(y))] (eq. 28), within a factor 2 of Bayes-with-uniform-prior and hence of the loss-specific frequentist minimax bound; LR gives E_{θ∼W_u|y}[L(θ, δ(y))/w_u(δ(y) | y)] (eq. 29), with 1/w_u(δ_mm | y) ≤ 2 a luckiness factor under symmetric loss.
- **Sec. 3.2, eq. 30–31 (BBW e-variables)**: with V = p_1/p_0 increasing in Y, fix 0 < w < 1 and y* with ∫_{y<y*}((1−w)p_0 + w p_1) = w; then S^bbw(w)_0 = 1{y ≥ y*} ((1−w)p_0 + w p_1)/((1−w)p_0) and S^bbw(w)_1 = 1{y < y*} ((1−w)p_0 + w p_1)/(w p_1) are exact e-variables (eq. 31). Their e-posteriors are ∞ on half the sample space, so R̄ is finite only for the single rule δ^bbw(w)(y) = 1{y ≥ y*} (the only rule BBW study), and δ_mm = δ^bbw(w) does not depend on L; the BBW no-decision region [y−, y+] is where δ^bbw(w) differs from the Bayes rule under prior w. BBW e-variables are e-specifications but not e-processes, so they survive [optional continuation](../concepts/optional-continuation.md) but not optional stopping. The reply to the Safe Testing discussion reads eq. 31 at w = ½ as the (1 − α)/α device: S^bbw_0 = 1{y ≥ y*}(1 + p_1/p_0) reaches 1/α exactly when the LR e-variable p_1/p_0 reaches (1 − α)/α [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 5].
- **Sec. 3.2 (conditional frequentist reinterpretation)**: partition Y into {Y_α} with P_0(Y ≥ y+_α) = α, P_1(Y ≤ y−_α) = β(α); BBW's conditional error probabilities α = (1−w)p_0(y+_α)/((1−w)p_0 + w p_1)(y+_α) and β(α) are exactly P̄^bbw(w)(0 | y+_α) and P̄^bbw(w)(1 | y−_α). Kiefer-type conditional confidence estimators (P_θ(θ ∉ CI(Y) | Y ∈ Y_α) ≤ α) and Vovk-type ones (E_{P_θ}[1{θ ∉ CI(Y)}/α̂(Y)] ≤ 1, eq. 33) each give an e-collection S_θ = 1{θ ∉ CI(Y)}/α̂(Y), hence e-posteriors, again possibly infinite; BBW with β(α) = α is Kiefer-type (eq. 32), in general Vovk-type.
- **E-confidence sets (Fig. 1, Sec. 4)**: {θ : P̄(θ | y) ≥ α} = {θ : S_θ(y) ≤ 1/α} is a conservative two-sided (1 − α)-confidence set, citing Grünwald 2022; the e-posterior is the graphical summary of all of them at once; for sets containing θ with P̄ ≥ 1 no interpretation is offered.
- **Definition 1 and Condition Zero (Sec. 4, eq. 34)**: P̄^cap := min{1, P̄}; a rule δ satisfies C0 if sup_θ P̄ L = sup_θ P̄^cap L for every y; a loss satisfies C0 if some rule does. Rationale: S_θ(y) < 1 is less evidence against θ than the trivial S ≡ 1, so a C0 rule never lets its assessment ride on uninformative values. The old testing C0 (L(0,0) = L(1,1) = 0) is a special case.
- **Dampened e-variables (eq. 35)**: S*_θ = max{S_θ, 1}/E[max{S_θ, 1}], S°_θ = ½ max{1, S_θ}, S^[γ]_θ = (1−γ) + γ S_θ; all e-variables, all bound sup_θ P̄' ≤ 2; S^[1/2] preserves the e-process property, S* need not.
- **Example 11 / SM Prop. 4 (eq. 36, eq. 52)**: for the ½-dampened Savage–Dickey e-posterior with N(0, 1/λ) prior, the MLE is minimax for any weight b and R̄(Y, θ̂) = (2/n)(½ log((n+λ)/λ) + nλ/(n+λ) θ̂²) = (2/n) D(P^(n)_θ̂ ‖ P^(n)_W), a log n factor above eq. 24; the undampened Savage–Dickey e-posterior gives R̄(δ_mm) ≳ n^{−1/2}.
- **Proposition 2 (eq. 37)**: with R̄^cap(δ) := sup_θ P̄^cap(θ | y) L(θ, δ(y)), eqs 15–16 hold with 2 R̄^cap(δ) in place of R̄(δ), for any rule; proof via S°. Preferred over eq. 35 under optional continuation, since products of unmodified e-values keep a single factor 2.
- **Sec. 5, eq. 38 (general definition)**: for a set P and a functional φ : P → Θ ⊂ R^k, {S_θ} is an e-collection if E_P[S_θ] ≤ 1 for all P with φ(P) = θ; Props 1–2 go through unchanged. Covers nuisance parameters (via the [reverse information projection](../concepts/reverse-information-projection.md) e-variables of Safe Testing), composite nulls, Cox partial-likelihood ratios (which are e-processes, [terschureAnytimevalidLogrankTest2024](terschureAnytimevalidLogrankTest2024.md)) and the bounded-mean betting e-collection of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2022.
- **SM Theorem 1**: for a regular one-dimensional exponential family restricted to a compact Θ°, loss 2 D(θ̆ ‖ θ) and the two-point e-process with C = 1, for c ≤ n*/n ≤ 1/c, max_{θ∈Θ°} L(θ, θ̂) P̄(θ | x^n) = (1/n)(n*/n) e^{n/n*} f_n · 0.53…, f_n ∈ 1 ± √(C*/n) (eq. 41); the MLE need no longer be exactly minimax but the bound still holds for it. Key tools: KL robustness p_θ(y)/p_W(y) = exp(−n D(θ̂ ‖ θ) + D(P^(n)_θ̂ ‖ P^(n)_W)) (eq. 43) and the maximiser x* = 2.0653… of x²/(e^x + e^{−x}) (eq. 46).
- **SM Proposition 3**: in mean-value parametrisation, min_a max_θ D(a ‖ θ) f(D(θ' ‖ θ)) is attained at a = θ' for any continuous f with x f(x) → 0; applies to every dampened Savage–Dickey e-posterior (γ ∈ (0, 1]) and to the two-point Gaussian one.

## Setting and assumptions
- "Simple setting": all coordinates of θ ∈ Θ ⊆ R^k are of interest, one e-variable per θ, common dominating measure; the general eq. 38 setting replaces P_θ by {P : φ(P) = θ}. The model is assumed correct throughout; the target is bad priors, not misspecification [grunwaldEposterior2023, Sec. 1].
- Loss functions L : Θ × A → R need only the no-sure-gain condition sup_θ L(θ, a) ≥ 0 for each a; negative losses are allowed and used (Example 6). Losses may be chosen after the data; importance weights B may depend on data and external U but, in all examples, not on θ or a ("simple bookie"); the θ- and a-dependent case is left open.
- The risk cap ℓ (or the promise B · R̄ ≤ ℓ) must be fixed before the data; validity is in expectation over Y under each θ, never conditional on y.
- Optional stopping (Example 7, Examples 8, 11) requires the e-collection to be an e-process; BBW, Kiefer and Vovk-type collections and S* are e-specifications only. Optional continuation by multiplication works for any e-collection (Sec. 5).
- Gaussian examples use variance 1; the exponential-family extension (SM Thm 1) needs a regular family, a compact sub-interval, n large and n*/n bounded; the author does not know whether Examples 10–11 extend beyond exponential families.
- The BBW analysis assumes V = p_1/p_0 strictly increasing with Lebesgue densities, "merely for simplicity".

## Open questions / gaps
- The main open problem named by the author: when is the bound eq. 16 tight enough to be useful? It behaves like an ordinary risk bound when B · R̄(δ) is nearly constant in y, but the sup can be weak when R̄(δ) varies strongly with the data (Conclusion).
- Bookies whose B depends on θ and a: DM might then want to revise δ after seeing B; analysis "substantially more difficult", left for future work (Sec. 2).
- No general recommendation for e-collections in the style of reference priors; the GRO/Savage–Dickey collection is worse than the two-point one for estimation (Conclusion). The two-point construction is tuned for squared error with a fixed anticipated n*.
- Whether Examples 10–11 (Condition Zero holds automatically; MLE is minimax; log n loss for dampened Savage–Dickey) extend beyond regular one-dimensional exponential families is unknown; the proofs use steepness and the KL robustness identity.
- The (1 − α)/α device of eq. 31 is a one-shot e-variable, not an e-process; an e-process version is open [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 5].
- Which loss functions match real decision problems (squared error is convenient, not realistic) is raised but not answered.
