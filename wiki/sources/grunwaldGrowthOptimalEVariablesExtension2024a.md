# Growth-optimal e-variables and an extension to the multivariate Csiszár–Sanov–Chernoff theorem

## Summary
The paper studies the mirror image of most e-value theory: a simple null P_0 for a d-dimensional vector Y with E_{P_0}[Y] = 0 and a moment generating function, against a composite alternative H_1 specified only through a closed set of means M_1 that is separated from 0 [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a]. H_1 may be the set P_1 of all distributions with mean in M_1, the subfamily E_1 of the natural exponential family E generated by P_0 with means in M_1, or anything between. The first half re-derives, from Csiszár's Pythagorean theorem for KL and Topsøe's 1979 minimax result, that for convex M_1 the GROW e-variable is the likelihood ratio p̄_{μ*}/p_0 against the single exponential-family element whose mean μ* minimises D(P̄_μ ‖ P_0) over M_1, the same for every admissible H_1, and that this e-variable is exactly what underlies the Csiszár–Sanov–Chernoff (CSC) bound P_0(Y ∈ M_1) ≤ e^{−D}, which in one dimension is the generic Chernoff bound. The second half, which the authors call new and "work in progress", treats surrounding M_1, where the complement of M_1 is an open bounded star-shaped set around 0, the natural shape for a two-sided or multivariate test. There the absolute GROW e-variable is characterised only for d = 1 (a mixture of the two boundary points with growth equalised, proved through the variation-diminishing property of one-dimensional exponential families), while for general d the paper switches to relative GROW in the minimax individual-sequence-regret sense of Jang et al. and Orabona–Jun: the optimal e-variable is a Shtarkov (normalised maximum likelihood) distribution over a partition of M_1 divided by p_0, and it yields a new CSC-type bound P_0(Y ∈ M_1) ≤ e^{mmreg − D}. For Y a sample mean of n i.i.d. observations and a boundary with four bounded derivatives, Takeuchi–Barron and Clarke–Barron give mmreg_n = ((d − 1)/2) log n + O(1): the boundary of M_1 is a (d − 1)-dimensional curved exponential family and pays BIC-style complexity. When the complement of M_1 is a KL ball this gives the asymptotic growth rate GROW_n = nD_1 − ((d − 1)/2) log n + O(1) after all. The bounds are fixed-n and not anytime-valid; comparison with the multivariate CLT regime (KL ball of radius shrinking like 1/√n) and with Kaufmann–Koolen's mixture-martingale bounds is left open.

## Main results
- **Setting (Section 1).** Y ⊂ R^d with 0 ∈ Y, P_0 with E_{P_0}[Y] = 0 and an MGF (the "only strong assumption"), density p_0 relative to ρ (ρ = P_0 allowed); E = {P̄_μ : μ ∈ M} the natural exponential family generated by P_0 (eq. 1.14), mean-value parameterised with θ(0) = 0. Condition ALT-M1: M_1 closed, inf_{μ ∈ M_1} ‖μ‖ > 0, and every μ ∈ M_1 has a point of M ∩ M_1 on the segment from 0 to μ (bites only for non-steep families, Example 1). Condition ALT-H1: H_1 compatible with M_1 (eq. 1.1), E_1 := {P̄_μ : μ ∈ M_1 ∩ M} ⊂ H_1, and every P ∈ H_1 has a mean [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].
- **Theorem 1 (restated, Safe Testing Thm 2 special case).** If the joint KL minimum over conv(H_1) × conv(H_0) is attained at (P*_1, P*_0), then GROW = D(P*_1 ‖ P*_0) and S_grow = p*_1/p*_0 [grunwaldSafeTesting2024].
- **Proposition 1 (Pythagoras ⇒ S_grow = p*_1/p_0).** For simple H_0 = {P_0} and any H_1, not necessarily convex, with inf_{P ∈ H_1} D(P ‖ P_0) < ∞: if P*_1 satisfies the Pythagorean property D(P ‖ P_0) ≥ D(P ‖ P*_1) + D(P*_1 ‖ P_0) for all P ∈ H_1 (eqs 1.6–1.8) and E_P[log p*_1/p_0] is well-defined on H_1 (1.9), then sup_S inf_{P ∈ H_1} E_P[log S] = D(P*_1 ‖ P_0) and S_grow = p*_1(Y)/p_0(Y). Proof: every e-variable for a simple null is q/p_0 with q a sub-probability density (upper bound 1.11); the Pythagorean inequality rearranges to the lower bound 1.12–1.13. Weakens Topsøe's Theorem 9, which needs pairwise finite KL; condition (b) of Theorem 1 is not needed, so P*_1 exists even when the KL infimum is not attained [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].
- **Proposition 2 (convex M_1).** Under ALT-M1 with M_1 convex there is a unique μ* ∈ M \ {0}, on the boundary of M_1, minimising D(P̄_μ ‖ P_0) over M_1, with value θ*ᵀμ* − log Z(θ*) (2.1); for convex H_1 satisfying ALT-H1, inf_{P ∈ H_1} D(P ‖ P_0) = D(P̄_{μ*} ‖ P_0) is attained uniquely at P̄_{μ*} (2.2), so S_grow = p̄_{μ*}(Y)/p_0(Y) and GROW = D(P̄_{μ*} ‖ P_0). The GROW e-variable is the same for every H_1 with E_1 ⊂ H_1 ⊂ P_1. Key step: for P with mean μ ∈ M, D(P ‖ P_0) ≥ E_P[log p_θ/p_0] = θᵀμ − log Z(θ) = D(P̄_μ ‖ P_0) (2.3); for μ ∉ M a linearity-in-γ argument along the ray (2.4). Example 2: H_1 may contain P ≪̸ P_0 (a point mass at 0 mixed with N(0,1)) and the growth is still well-defined because E shares P_0's support; the authors suspect the numeraire ideas of Larsson, Ramdas and Ruf handle the remaining 0/0 cases [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].
- **Theorem 2 (CSC for convex M_1; not new, Csiszár 1984).** With D := inf_{μ ∈ M_1} D(P̄_μ ‖ P_0): y ∈ M_1 ⇒ S_grow(y) ≥ e^D (2.6), hence P_0(Y ∈ M_1) ≤ e^{−D} (2.7). In d = 1, for any D > 0 attained at some μ* with sign s, P_0(sup_{μ ∈ M} p̄_μ(Y)/p_0(Y) ≥ e^D, sgn(Y) = s) = P_0(p̄_{μ*}(Y)/p_0(Y) ≥ e^D) ≤ e^{−D} (2.8). The usual form P_0(Y ≥ μ*) ≤ inf_{θ > 0} E_{P_0}[e^{θᵀY}] e^{−θᵀμ*} is the same bound; a naive Markov bound on the non-e-variable sup_μ p̄_μ/p_0 is much weaker, and (2.8) shows the underlying e-variable is p̄_{μ*}/p_0. Proof via the "robustness property" of exponential families (log p̄_{μ*}(y)/p_0(y) = D at y = μ* and increasing in y) and strict monotonicity of D(P̄_μ ‖ P_0) in μ (2.10–2.11) [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].
- **Surrounding M_1 (Section 3).** M_1 is surrounding if M_1^c := conv(Y) \ M_1 is open, bounded, connected and contains 0; nice if moreover M_1^c lies in the interior of M and is 0-star-shaped (every ray from 0 crosses the boundary once). Nice implies ALT-M1. Figures 2–3 use a translated Gamma family with Y = (log X, X − c) [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].
- **Theorem 3 (absolute GROW, d = 1).** For nice M_1^c = (μ⁻_1, μ⁺_1) and H_1 = E_1: among priors on the two boundary points, the KL minimiser W* satisfies D(P̄_{W*} ‖ P_0) = E_{P̄_{μ⁻}}[log p̄_{W*}/p_0] = E_{P̄_{μ⁺}}[log p̄_{W*}/p_0] (3.1), and S_grow = S^bd_grow = p̄_{W*}(Y)/p_0(Y) (3.2): the GROW prior over all of M_1 is concentrated on the boundary. The equalising weight w° exists by continuity and monotonicity of f(μ, w) := E_{P̄_μ}[log((1 − w)p̄_{μ⁻} + w p̄_{μ⁺})/p_0] at the two endpoints (3.3); the extension from the boundary to all of M_1 needs Lemma 1: f(·, w°) is increasing above μ⁺ and decreasing below μ⁻ [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].
- **Lemma 1 and Section 5 (variation diminishing).** One-dimensional exponential families are strictly variation reducing kernels (Brown, Johnstone, MacGibbon 1981, Example 3.1, Prop. 3.1): the number of sign changes of μ ↦ E_{P̄_μ}[g(Y)] is at most that of g. With g_c = c + log(mixture/p_0), strictly convex and non-monotone, S⁻(g_c) ≤ 2 for every c, so f(·, w°) has at most one minimum and is monotone on each side; since f(0, w°) < 0 < f(μ^±, w°) the result follows. Direct differentiation gives (d/dμ) f = σ²_μ(E[Y g_0(Y)] − E[Y]E[g_0(Y)]) (5.1), which the authors could not analyse; the prior-perturbation route (5.2) fails the same way; for d > 1 no multivariate sign-change result is known ("results concerning sign changes for multidimensional families appear very weak", Brown 1986), so absolute GROW for surrounding M_1 with d > 1 is open [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].
- **Section 3.2 (relative criteria).** Given a nice partition {M_{1,r} : r ∈ R} of M_1 into closed convex pieces each touching bd(M_1^c) (3.7), each piece has a unique KL-closest boundary point, so R is identified with a subset of bd(M_1^c) and S_{r,GROW} = p̄_r/p_0. Minimax redundancy: sup_q inf_{P ∈ H_1} E_P[log q/p̄_{r(P)}] = −mmred(H_1), mmred := inf_q sup_P (D(P ‖ Q) − D(P ‖ P̄_{r(P)})) (3.8). Minimax individual-sequence regret with a hindsight estimator r̆ : Y → R: sup_q inf_y log q(y)/p̄_{r̆(y)}(y) = −mmreg(r̆) (3.9), uniquely solved by the Shtarkov distribution q_{shtarkov,r̆}(y) = p̄_{r̆(y)}(y)/∫ p̄_{r̆(y)}(y) ρ(dy), S_rel = q_shtarkov/p_0 (3.10), with constant regret log ∫ p̄_{r̆(y)}(y) ρ(dy). With r̆ = r̂ the MLE within R (3.11) this is NML relative to R, not to E. Example 3 (2-d Gaussian, half-space M_1): S_GROW = p̄_{(a,0)}/p_0, but carving the half-space into rays through a vertical line R gives a different S_rel because r̂(y) moves with y; absolute vs relative is a modelling choice for convex H_1, and the only available option for nonconvex H_1 with d > 1 [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].
- **Theorem 4 (CSC for surrounding H_1; new).** For nice M_1, any nice partition, any self-consistent r̆ (y ∈ M_{1,r̆(y)}) and any density q: E_{P_0}[1_{Y ∈ M_1} exp(D(P̄_{r̆(Y)} ‖ P_0) − reg(q, r̆, Y))] ≤ 1, where reg(q, r̆, y) := log p̄_{r̆(y)}(y)/q(y) (3.12). Hence with D := inf_{μ ∈ bd(M_1^c)} D(P̄_μ ‖ P_0), P_0(Y ∈ M_1) ≤ e^{mreg(q,r̆) − D} = e^{mmreg(r̆) − D} (q Shtarkov) ≤ e^{mmreg(r̂) − D} (3.13). Proof: the convex-piece inequality (3.14) y ∈ M_1 ⇒ p̄_{r̆(y)}(y)/p_0(y) ≥ e^{D(P̄_{r̆(y)} ‖ P_0)}, then E_{P_0}[q/p_0] = 1. The bound does not worsen when M_1 is enlarged with D fixed, so one may shrink M_1^c to the D-KL ball {μ : D(P̄_μ ‖ P_0) < D}, which is convex (Brown 1986): the "dual" of Section 2. Two ways to apply it: a polytope M'^c_1 with k corners on the KL sphere and finite R, or the continuous choice R = bd(M_1^c) with M_{1,r} the ray through r [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].
- **Section 4 (Y = n^{−1} Σ X_i).** The induced family on X has the same mean map, the likelihood ratio on n outcomes equals p̄_μ(Y)/p_0(Y), and D(P̄_μ ‖ P_0) = n D(P̄'_μ ‖ P'_0), so D_n = nD_1 grows linearly (4.2). Theorem 5 (corollary of Takeuchi–Barron 2013, Thm 2): if bd(M_1^c) = φ(U) with U ⊂ R^{d−1} open and φ having four bounded derivatives, then mmreg_n(r̂) ≤ ((d − 1)/2) log n + C. Theorem 6 (corollary of Clarke–Barron 1994, Thm 1, Conditions 1–3 verified via Brown 1986 Thm 2.2): mmred_n(E^bd_1) ≥ ((d − 1)/2) log n + C'. Since mmreg_n(r̂) ≥ mmred_n(H_1) ≥ mmred_n(E^bd_1) (4.3–4.4): mmred_n(H_1) = mmreg_n(r̂) + O(1) = ((d − 1)/2) log n + O(1) (4.5). For a KL-ball complement with fixed radius D_1 on X (4.6), the continuous partition beats any k-corner polytope eventually exponentially, because the polytope's D'_{1,k} < D_1 while its regret is only nonnegative [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].
- **Proposition 3 (asymptotic GROW).** In the KL-ball setting, for any H_1 containing E^bd_1 with all means in M_1: GROW_n = nD_1 − ((d − 1)/2) log n + O(1). Upper bound by restricting the infimum to the boundary and (4.5); lower bound by playing q_{shtarkov,r̂} and using p̄_{r̂(Y)}(Y) ≥ p̄_μ(Y) [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].
- **Section 6 (discussion).** If instead the KL ball on Y has fixed radius D (6.1), the ball on X shrinks at 1/√n, the classical scaling; the asymptotics (4.5) no longer apply, Theorem 4 still holds but is hard to evaluate; a CLT heuristic says P_0(Y ∈ M_1^c) tends to the probability that a standard normal vector lands in a Euclidean ball of radius √D, so the bound is useful only if its right-hand side stays below 1, which is unknown. Kaufmann and Koolen 2021 give anytime-valid bounds with radius growing like a log(b + c log n); whether the present analysis can reproduce them, and how non-anytime-valid e-value bounds relate to anytime-valid ones, is future work [grunwaldGrowthOptimalEVariablesExtension2024a].

## Setting and assumptions
- Null: simple, P_0 with mean 0 and a moment generating function; Y is the support of P_0 (so no 0/0 in likelihood ratios). The convex support is assumed d-dimensional without loss of generality.
- Alternative: composite, defined through a set of means M_1 (ALT-M1) and a compatible H_1 containing the exponential-family slice E_1 (ALT-H1); P_1 (all distributions with mean in M_1) is the largest case. Nothing is assumed about H_1 beyond means, except that GROW results for surrounding M_1 (Theorem 3) take H_1 = E_1 and d = 1.
- Data model: a single observation Y; Section 4 specialises to Y the sample mean of n i.i.d. X_i, for which Y is the MLE of the generated family in mean-value coordinates when the family is regular.
- No optional stopping: all e-variables and CSC bounds are for fixed n and the authors state explicitly that the bounds are "not anytime-valid themselves" (Section 6).
- Regularity for the asymptotics: nice M_1, boundary a (d − 1)-manifold parameterised with four bounded derivatives, complement inside a compact subset of the interior of M.

## Open questions / gaps
- Absolute GROW for surrounding M_1 with d > 1: conjectured to be a Bayes mixture concentrated on bd(M_1^c), unproved for lack of a multivariate variation-diminishing result (Sections 3.1, 5).
- Whether the CSC bound (3.13) stays nontrivial in the CLT scaling (6.1) with a fixed-radius KL ball on Y, and whether the Kaufmann–Koolen anytime-valid bounds can be recovered (Section 6).
- Relation between these fixed-n e-value bounds and anytime-valid ones (Section 6).
- Numeraire-style treatment of alternatives with mass outside the support of P_0 (Example 2).
- The authors' own observation that for y ∈ M_{1,r} the MLE r̂(y) within R need not equal r, which forced the general self-consistent r̆ in Theorem 4 (Section 3.2).
