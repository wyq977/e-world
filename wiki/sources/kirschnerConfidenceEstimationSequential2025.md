# Confidence estimation via sequential likelihood mixing

arXiv 2502.14689v1, 30 pages: main text Sections 1–4 (pp. 1–14), references, Appendix A (historical survey), B (Laplace's method), C (continuous exponential weights, Algorithm 1, Theorem 10), D (misspecification: sub-Gaussian, Theorem 11; convex classes, Theorem 12), E (tempered likelihood ratios, Theorem 13, Lemma 14).

## Summary
The paper gives a single construction of anytime-valid confidence sequences for a parameter θ* of a family of conditional densities p_t(y | θ) = p_θ(y | x_t), with arbitrarily dependent covariates and no i.i.d. assumption [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 2]. The object is the sequential marginal likelihood ratio S_t(θ) = ∏_{s ≤ t} ∫ p_s(y_s | ν) dμ_{s−1}(ν) / p_s(y_s | θ), where μ_{s−1} is any predictable "mixing distribution" over the parameter set; under realizability S_t(θ*) is a nonnegative martingale with unit mean, so [Ville's inequality](../concepts/villes-inequality.md) makes C_t = {θ : L_t(θ) ≤ log(1/δ) − Σ_s log ∫ p_s(y_s | ν) dμ_{s−1}(ν)} a (1 − δ)-confidence sequence (Theorem 4) [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 2.3]. Dirac mixing recovers Robbins' sequential likelihood-ratio sequence (Theorem 2), a fixed prior recovers the Robbins–Siegmund method of mixtures (Theorem 3), and Bayes-rule mixing makes the two coincide (Theorem 5), which also yields the prior-posterior ratio set of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2020 (Lemma 6) [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Secs 2.1–3.1]. Because the log-loss of a mixture is 1-exp-concave, the confidence coefficient can be bounded by the Donsker–Varadhan variational inequality (ELBO sets, Theorem 8) or by an online-learning regret bound relative to the MLE (regret-to-confidence, Theorem 9) [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Secs 3.2–3.3]. Worked cases are sequential linear and kernel regression, logistic regression, sparse regression and finite model identification; appendices extend coverage to sub-Gaussian noise, convex misspecified classes via the reverse information projection, and tempered ratios with Rényi/Hellinger-divergence sets, improving the online-to-confidence-set conversion of Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2012 [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Apps D–E].

## Main results
- **Theorem 2 (sequential likelihood ratio, Robbins 1970)**: for any F_t-adapted estimators θ̂_0, θ̂_1, …, R_t(θ) = ∏_s p_s(y_s | θ̂_{s−1}) / p_s(y_s | θ) is a nonnegative martingale at θ* (eqs 2–3), and C_t = {θ : L_t(θ) ≤ log(1/δ) − Σ_s log p_s(y_s | θ̂_{s−1})} is a (1 − δ)-confidence sequence [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 2.1].
- **Section 2.1.1 (MLE and union bound)**: plugging in the running MLE fails because max_ν R_t(ν; θ*) is not a supermartingale; for finite Θ a union bound gives the level set {θ : L_t(θ) ≤ log(1/δ) + L_t(θ̂_t) + log|Θ|} (eq. 6), minimised by the MLE; continuous Θ by covering [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025].
- **Theorem 3 (prior likelihood mixing)**: for a data-independent prior μ_0, Q_t(θ) = ∫ R_t(ν; θ) dμ_0(ν) is a martingale at θ* by Fubini, giving C_t = {θ : L_t(θ) ≤ log(1/δ) − log ∫ ∏_s p_s(y_s | ν) dμ_0(ν)}; the threshold is the log Bayesian evidence, approximated by Laplace's method as L_t(θ̂^MLE) + ½ log det I_t − (d/2) log 2π − log μ_0(θ̂^MLE) [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 2.2, App. B].
- **Theorem 4 (sequential likelihood mixing)**: for any F_t-adapted mixing distributions μ_0, μ_1, … in P(Θ), S_t(θ*) is a martingale and C_t = {θ : L_t(θ) ≤ log(1/δ) − Σ_{s ≤ t} log ∫ p_s(y_s | ν) dμ_{s−1}(ν)} is a (1 − δ)-confidence sequence; Theorem 2 is the Dirac case [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 2.3]. Any choice works: Bayes posterior, variational approximation, posterior samples μ_t = N^{−1} Σ δ_{θ̃^i_t}, or an online-estimation algorithm [Sec. 3].
- **Theorem 5 (mixing equivalence)**: with Bayes-rule mixing μ_t ∝ ∏_{s<t} p_s(y_s | θ) μ_0(θ), Σ_s log ∫ p_s dμ_{s−1} = log ∫ ∏_s p_s dμ_0, so Theorems 3 and 4 coincide; sequential mixing has no statistical advantage inside the Bayesian model, but non-Bayesian mixings may converge faster [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 3.1].
- **Lemma 6 (prior-posterior ratio set, Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2020)**: for any realizable Bayesian model C_t = {θ : −log μ_t(θ) ≤ log(1/δ) − log μ_0(θ)} is a (1 − δ)-confidence sequence equivalent to Theorems 3–4; it is not a credible region (μ_{t−1}(C_t) ≠ 1 − δ in general) and never excludes the prior's null set [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 3.1].
- **Sequential linear regression (Sec. 3.1)**: Gaussian prior N(θ_0, V_0^{−1}), likelihood N(x_tᵀθ*, σ²), posterior N(θ̂^RLS_t, V_t^{−1}) with V_t = σ^{−2} Σ x_s x_sᵀ + V_0; Lemma 6 gives ½‖θ − θ̂^RLS_t‖²_{V_t} ≤ log(1/δ) + γ_t + ½‖θ − θ_0‖²_{V_0}, with information gain γ_t = ½ log det V_t − ½ log det V_0; no norm bound S is needed, and with V_0 = λI, θ_0 = 0, intersecting with the ball B_S gives ½‖θ − θ̂‖²_{V_t} ≤ log(1/δ) + ½ log det V_t − (d/2) log λ + λS²/2, the Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2011 ellipsoid without its cross term, up to a factor 2 tighter; same statement for Gaussian-process/RKHS regression via the kernel trick, improving Abbasi-Yadkori 2012, Chowdhury and Gopalan 2017, Whitehouse et al. 2023 and matching Neiswanger and Ramdas 2021, Flynn and Reeb 2024 [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025].
- **Lemma 7, Theorem 8 (ELBO confidence set)**: Donsker–Varadhan log ∫ e^g dμ ≥ ∫ g dρ − KL(ρ‖μ) with equality at the Gibbs posterior; hence for any adapted ρ_t and prior μ_0, C_t = {θ : L_t(θ) ≤ log(1/δ) − ELBO_t(ρ_t)} with ELBO_t(ρ) = −∫ L_t dρ − KL(ρ‖μ_0) is a (1 − δ)-confidence sequence, equal to Theorem 3 at the exact posterior; variational inference becomes a coverage-preserving tool (cf. Lee et al. 2024 for logistic bandits, PAC-Bayes bounds) [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 3.2].
- **Theorem 9 (regret-to-confidence)**: if an online density-estimation algorithm has almost-sure log-regret Λ_t = Σ_s ℓ_s(μ_{s−1}) − min_θ L_t(θ) ≤ B_t for predictable B_t, then C_t = {θ : L_t(θ) ≤ log(1/δ) + L_t(θ̂^MLE_t) + B_t} is a (1 − δ)-confidence sequence; the algorithm's predictions are not needed, unlike Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2012 [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 3.3].
- **Instances of Theorem 9 (Sec. 3.3)**: logistic regression with ‖θ‖ ≤ S via Foster et al. 2018, B_t = 10 d log(e + St/(2d)), improving Lee et al. 2024a Theorem 1; k-sparse linear regression via Gerchinovitz 2011, B_t = C_0 k log t (MLE over the sparse set is computationally hard); finite Θ via exponential weights with η = 1 (= Bayes), B_t = log|Θ|, matching the union bound but never worse than Theorem 4, plus a MAP form log(1/δ) + min_ν [L_t(ν) − log μ_0(ν)] [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025].
- **Theorem 10 (continuous exponential weights, App. C)**: for η-exp-concave losses Σ_s ℓ_s(μ_{s−1}) − ∫ L_t dρ ≤ η^{−1} KL(ρ‖μ_0), and ≤ η^{−1} log(1/ρ(ν)) for finite Θ; substituted into Theorem 4 this reproduces the ELBO set of Theorem 8, but is vacuous for Dirac ρ on continuous Θ [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025].
- **Theorem 11 (sub-Gaussian misspecification, App. D.1)**: if y_t − E[y_t | F_t] is σ-sub-Gaussian and E[y_t | F_t] = f_{θ*}(x_t), the Gaussian-likelihood mixture ratio E_t(θ*) is a supermartingale, so the Gaussian construction remains valid [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025].
- **Theorem 12 (convex model classes, App. D.2)**: if M is convex and the [reverse information projection](../concepts/reverse-information-projection.md) q* of the true density p* onto M exists in M, then the sequential mixing set covers q* for all t with probability ≥ 1 − δ, by E[p_θ(y_t | x_t)/q*(y_t | x_t) | F_t] ≤ 1 (eq. 7; Li 1999, Lardy et al. 2024) [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025].
- **Theorem 13 (tempered confidence sets, App. E.1)**: with tempered log-ratio A^β_t(θ) = −β log[p_t(y_t | θ̂_{t−1})/p_t(y_t | θ)], the normalised product M_t(θ) is an exact martingale and −log ∫ e^{−A^β_t(θ)} p_t(y | θ) dy = (1 − β) D_{β,t}(θ̂_{t−1}‖θ) = β D_{1−β,t}(θ‖θ̂_{t−1}); hence C^β_t = {θ : Σ_s D_{1−β,s}(θ‖θ̂_{s−1}) − Λ_t(θ) ≤ β^{−1} log(1/δ)} and the Hellinger set C^H_t = {θ : Σ_s H²_s(θ‖θ̂_{s−1}) − Λ_t(θ) ≤ 2 log(1/δ)} are (1 − δ)-confidence sequences; the divergence is predictable, useful for controlling x_t in decision making [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025].
- **Lemma 14 (online-to-confidence conversion, App. E.2)**: Gaussian linear regression with predictions ŷ_t and squared-loss regret Λ_t(θ*) ≤ B_t gives C_t = {θ : Σ_s ½(ŷ_{s−1} − ⟨θ, x_s⟩)² ≤ (β − β²)^{−1} log(1/δ) + β(β − β²)^{−1} B_t}; at β = ½ the bound is 4 log(1/δ) + 2B_t, versus Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2012 Theorem 1's 16 log(1/δ) + ½ + 2B_t + 16 log(√8 + √(1 + 2B_t)); for k-sparse θ* the Gerchinovitz 2011 forecaster gives B_t = O(k log t) [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025].
- **Appendix A (history)**: Wald 1945 sequential likelihood ratios for testing; Darling and Robbins 1968 and Robbins 1970 as the first likelihood-ratio confidence sequences with Ville's inequality and prior mixing (Robbins and Siegmund 1970); Lai 1976 for exponential families, Lai and Robbins 1985 for bandits; Peña et al. 2009 and Howard et al. 2020 for self-normalised mixtures; Vovk–Shafer, Grünwald–de Heide–Koolen, Wang–Ramdas and the Ramdas–Wang book for e-values; Cover 1984/1991 for universal portfolios; Rakhlin and Sridharan 2017 on regret ⇔ tail bounds [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025].

## Setting and assumptions
- Data stream (x_t, y_t) with covariates x_t generated arbitrarily and adaptively (bandits, active learning); filtration F_t = {x_1, y_1, …, x_{t−1}, y_{t−1}, x_t}; P_t = P(y_t ∈ · | F_t) dominated by a base measure ξ; Θ measurable, finite or continuous [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 2].
- Assumption 1 (realizability): p_t(· | θ*) = dP_t/dξ, implying y_t | x_t is stationary given x_t; relaxable by conditioning the model on the full history, by sub-Gaussianity (Thm 11) or by convexity of M plus existence of the RIPr (Thm 12). Assumption 2 is the Bayesian model (prior μ_0 independent of data, posterior updates).
- Target: an F_t-adapted (C_t) with P(∃t ≥ 1 : θ* ∉ C_t) ≤ δ, i.e. time-uniform coverage valid at any stopping time; optional stopping and continuation are built in via Ville. No optimality theory beyond tightness remarks (Lattimore and Szepesvári 2020 lower bound for linear regression) [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 3.1].
- Mixing distributions μ_t must be predictable (chosen before y_t), priors data-independent; the paper never bets on the observation itself, only on a predictive density.

## Open questions / gaps
- The paper never uses the words e-value, e-process or test martingale for its own objects, though every result is a Ville bound on a likelihood-ratio (super)martingale; the link to GRO/log-optimality (which mixing distribution shrinks fastest) is left open, with only the remark that non-Bayesian mixings might beat the posterior [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 3.1].
- No rates are proved in general; tightness is argued per case (Lattimore–Szepesvári lower bound for linear regression, constant comparisons with Abbasi-Yadkori 2011/2012 and Lee et al. 2024a).
- Continuous exponential weights gives a vacuous bound at Dirac comparators, so a confidence set relative to the MLE on continuous Θ needs smoothness assumptions not supplied [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, App. C].
- Sparse regression via Theorem 9 requires the MLE over the sparse set; efficient sparse online prediction is called an open problem [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Sec. 3.3].
- Laplace's method is heuristic here; rigorous finite-sample versions need regularity conditions the paper does not impose [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, App. B].
- Learning structured priors from prior data, and unknown-variance or heteroscedastic Gaussian cases, are flagged as directions [kirschnerConfidenceEstimationSequential2025, Secs 3.1, 4].
