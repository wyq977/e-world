# Post-hoc α hypothesis testing and the post-hoc p-value

## Summary
The paper builds testing with a data-dependent level α̃ from the ground up, starting from a test function α ↦ φ(α) that is non-decreasing in α and summarised by its p-value p = min{α : φ(α) = 1} [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Secs 2.2–2.3]. Selecting α̃ after the data distorts the size, measured by the ratio size/α̃; the paper rejects control of the maximum size distortion as useless (it bans even conservative choices and forces p ≥ 1) and defines validity for a data-dependent level as expected size distortion at most one, E_α̃[P(p ≤ α̃ | α̃)/α̃] = E[I{p ≤ α̃}/α̃] ≤ 1 [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Def. 1, Prop. 1, Sec. 3.2]. A test function or p-value is post-hoc (α valid) if this holds for every data-dependent α̃; the central theorem is that p is a post-hoc p-value if and only if E[1/p] ≤ 1, so the post-hoc p-values are exactly the reciprocals of [e-variables](../concepts/e-variable.md) and every non-dominated post-hoc test is α ↦ I{e ≥ 1/α} [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Def. 2, Thms 1–2, Sec. 6]. The paper reads this as e-values being p-values under a stronger, data-dependent-level Type-I guarantee, "thinly veiled by the reciprocal p = 1/e", answering what e-values offer over p-values and giving the e-value a decision-theoretic foundation in binary testing [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Abstract, Sec. 1.1]. Optimality replaces power by expected utility E_Q[U(1/p)] and uses Koning's Neyman–Pearson lemma for e-values (Koning 2024, continuous testing), recovering the classical lemma with randomisation replaced by rejection at a larger post-hoc level [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Thm 3, Cors 1–3]. Section 7 abstracts p-values, e-values and tests to evidence variables on a totally ordered evidence space with validity defined through a certainty equivalent, yielding h-mean validity beyond classical e-values; Section 8 closes Markov's inequality into "Markov's equality" and derives Ville's equalities for martingales, supermartingales and e-processes; appendices contrast the framework with Grünwald's Beyond Neyman–Pearson, add double post-hoc p-values, post-hoc anytime validity, FWER and FDR, the max-distortion impossibility, and randomised post-hoc p-functions [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Secs 7–8, Apps A–E].

## Main results
- Prop. 1: the expected size distortion E_α̃[P(φ(α̃) = 1 | α̃)/α̃] equals E[φ(α̃)/α̃] = E[I{p ≤ α̃}/α̃] [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Prop. 1].
- Def. 1 and Examples 1–6: validity for one data-dependent level; α-hacking with α̃ ∈ {.01, .05} and an exact p-value has maximum distortion 100 and expected distortion 1.8; a conservative α̃ ∈ {.02, .01} has maximum distortion 50 but expected distortion .5; rejecting at level p with an exact p-value gives E[1/p] = ∞; a p-value that is 1 with probability 1/2 makes the α-hacking α̃ valid in expectation (.9) but not in maximum (100) [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Examples 1–6].
- Def. 2 and Thm 1: post-hoc α validity is sup over all data-dependent α̃ of the expected size distortion ≤ 1; it implies classical validity, since sup_α E[φ(α)/α] ≤ E[sup_α φ(α)/α] [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Def. 2, Thm 1].
- Thm 2: p is a post-hoc p-value iff E[1/p] ≤ 1; proof: the sup inside the expectation is attained at α̃ = p [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Thm 2].
- Props 2–3: products of independent post-hoc p-values and weighted harmonic means of arbitrary post-hoc p-values are post-hoc p-values [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Props 2–3].
- Remark 3: useful post-hoc p-values must exceed 1 with positive null probability; one supported on (0, 1] is a.s. 1 under the null. Remark 4: non-decreasing test functions are without loss of generality, since any post-hoc valid family is dominated by a non-decreasing one [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Remarks 3–4].
- Sec. 6: with e = 1/p, the e-value test α ↦ I{e ≥ 1/α} is not merely valid by Markov but post-hoc valid, and conversely every post-hoc valid test function has this form; "the literature on e-values has unknowingly been studying post-hoc α hypothesis testing" [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Def. 3, Sec. 6].
- Thm 3 (from Koning 2024) and Cors 1–2: for upper-semicontinuous concave non-decreasing U, an optimiser p* of E_Q[U(1/p)] over post-hoc p-values satisfies λ f_P/f_Q ∈ ∂U(1/p*) Q-a.s.; for differentiable U, 1/p* = (U′)^{−1}(λ f_P/f_Q); for U = log, p* = f_P/f_Q. Existence holds when λ with E_P[1/p_λ] = 1 exists, e.g. finite sample space, U′(x)x bounded, uniform integrability, or bounded U [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Thm 3, Cors 1–2].
- Example 7: the log-optimal post-hoc test for N(0,1) vs N(1,1) compares the likelihood ratio with 1/α = 20 at α = .05 against the Neyman–Pearson critical value about 3.14, so it has lower power at any pre-specified α [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Example 7].
- Cor. 3 (post-hoc level Neyman–Pearson): with U(x) = x ∧ 1/α*, an optimiser exists with p* = α* if f_P/f_Q < c*, p* = k ∈ [α*, ∞] on the boundary, p* = ∞ otherwise; the classical lemma's randomisation becomes rejection at a larger level [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Sec. 5.3, Cor. 3].
- Thm 4 (abstract): for a Dedekind-complete evidence space D, ε_φ = sup_d φ(d) is an evidence variable and every evidence variable is the post-hoc evidence variable of φ_ε(d) = d·I{d ≼ ε} [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Sec. 7.2, Thm 4].
- Sec. 7.3–7.4: validity is ρ(ε) ≼ "1" for an idempotent monotone certainty equivalent ρ, in particular quasi-arithmetic means ρ(ε) = L^{−1}(E[L(ε)]) (Kolmogorov 1930); the e-value is L(x) = x with ≼ as ≤, the post-hoc p-value is L(x) = 1/x with ≼ as ≥, and level-α tests are {0, 1/α}-valued binary e-values or {α, ∞}-valued binary p-values; classical p-validity is validity of the family φ(α) = I{p ≤ α}/α rather than of p itself [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, eqs 8–9, Sec. 7.4].
- Def. 4 and Props 4–7: h-validity ρ_h(e) ≤ 1 for the h-generalised mean; h⁺-valid implies h⁻-valid; h = 1 is the smallest h for which h-validity makes the induced test family classically valid; h-means and independent products preserve h-validity [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Def. 4, Props 4–7, App. F.1].
- Examples 8–10: harmonic e-values ((E[1/e])^{−1} ≤ 1, i.e. E[p] ≥ 1) are exactly post-hoc validity under the size difference size − α̃ ≤ 0 in expectation (Prop. 8); geometric e-values exp(E log e) ≤ 1 multiply under arbitrary dependence (Prop. 9); max e-values ess sup e ≤ 1 are the maximum-size-distortion notion [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Props 8–9, Examples 8–10].
- Lemmas 1–2, Prop. 10 (Markov's equality): ⌊cX ∧ 1⌋ ≤ cX ∧ 1 ≤ cX gives Markov, the randomised Markov of Ramdas and Manole 2023, and a tighter non-integrable Markov; dividing by c and taking sup over c > 0 gives sup_c I{X ≥ 1/c}/c = X, hence E[sup_c I{X ≥ 1/c}/c] = E[X] and sup over data-dependent c̃ of E[P(X ≥ 1/c̃ | c̃)/c̃] = E[X] [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Lemmas 1–2, Prop. 10].
- Prop. 11 (Ville's equalities): for a nonnegative martingale and bounded stopping time τ, sup_α̃ E[P(M_τ ≥ 1/α̃ | α̃)/α̃] = E[M_0]; for a supermartingale, sup over α̃ and τ equals E[M_0]; for an exact e-process, sup over α̃, τ and P ∈ H of E_P[·] equals sup_P E_P[M_0]; Ville's inequality is the restriction to data-independent α [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Prop. 11].
- Thm 5 (App. A): Grünwald's Type-I risk safety E[sup_α ℓ(φ(α))] ≤ 1 holds iff sup_α ℓ(φ(α)) is an e-value; with Koning's decision-valued evidence and ℓ non-decreasing lower-semicontinuous it becomes E[ℓ(e_φ)] ≤ 1, not a classical e-value unless ℓ(x) = x [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, App. A, Thm 5].
- Def. 5 and Thm 6 (App. B): a double post-hoc p-value is post-hoc under H and its reciprocal post-hoc under the alternative; for mutually absolutely continuous P_0, P_1 the likelihood ratio dP_0/dP_1 is double post-hoc, and so (stated) are the composite numeraire e-values of Larsson et al. 2025; the constant 1 is also double post-hoc, so this is an extra guarantee not a quality criterion [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Def. 5, Thm 6, Remark 9].
- Def. 6, Thms 7–8 (App. C): a p-process is post-hoc anytime valid iff sup_τ E[1/p_τ] ≤ 1, the reciprocal of an e-process; post-hoc FWER control of (φ_i) is post-hoc validity of the maximum test function sup_i φ_i, i.e. of its p-value; post-hoc expected multiple testing error (FDP with uniform random index) is post-hoc validity of the randomised test E_ι̃ φ_ι̃, i.e. of its p-function [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Def. 6, Thms 7–8].
- Thm 9 (App. D): control of the maximum size distortion for every α̃ is equivalent to φ(α) = 0 on (0, 1), i.e. p ≥ 1 [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Thm 9].
- App. E: for randomised test functions ([0, 1]-valued, càdlàg, non-decreasing in α) the p-function p̃(u) = inf{α : φ̃(α) ≥ u} is the quantile function of φ̃ (a Galois connection); Thm 10: p̃ is post-hoc iff sup_P E_P[sup_u u/p̃(u)] ≤ 1, so 1/p̃ need not be an e-value; Cor. 4: p̃(1) is a post-hoc p-value; Thm 11: p is post-hoc iff u ↦ up is a post-hoc p-function, so Up with U ~ Unif(0, 1] is a.s. smaller than p; Cor. 5: e is an e-value iff α ↦ αe ∧ 1 is a post-hoc test function, improving Ramdas and Manole 2023; Thm 12: harmonic means of post-hoc p-functions are post-hoc; Thm 13: products of independent p-functions are post-hoc under ∏ p̃_i(1)/p̃_i(u) ≤ 1/u; Thm 14: arbitrarily many i.i.d. copies with E[1/p̃(1)] = 1 all multiply post-hoc only if the p-functions are not properly randomised (non-randomised when H_0 is finite) [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Defs 8, Prop. 12, Thms 10–14, Cors 4–5].
- App. G: measurability on information structures (constant on information cells) so suprema of evidence variables are measurable, and an order-theoretic version of Kolmogorov's replacement axiom defining conditional certainty equivalents [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Def. 9, Lemma 3, App. G.2].

## Setting and assumptions
- Null P (composite nulls by sup over P ∈ H, footnote 1 and Remark 6); alternative Q with densities f_P, f_Q only for the optimality results of Section 5 [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Secs 2.1, 5.2].
- Test functions are indicator-valued, non-decreasing in α, with a smallest α at which they jump to 1; randomised test functions are deferred to Appendix E [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Sec. 2.2, App. E].
- The data-dependent level α̃ is any random variable (any function of the data), and the paper assumes α̃ is selected with full knowledge of the data before any external randomisation [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Def. 2, Remark 10].
- The choice to measure size distortion relatively (size/α) rather than by difference is explicit and motivated; the difference leads to harmonic e-values instead [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Remark 2, Example 8].
- Optional stopping appears only in Section 8.3 and Appendix C.1: bounded stopping times for martingales, all stopping times for supermartingales and exact e-processes [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Prop. 11, Def. 6].
- The expected-utility existence conditions are inherited from Koning 2024 [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Sec. 5.2].

## Relation to other sources
- [grunwaldBeyondNeymanPearsonEvalues2024](grunwaldBeyondNeymanPearsonEvalues2024.md): the closest precursor. Koning restates Grünwald's Proposition 1 for binary testing as Theorem 5 (Type-I risk safe iff sup_α ℓ(φ(α)) is an e-value) and lists three differences: Grünwald treats e-values as loss-valued whereas Koning's are decision-valued; Grünwald does not show that e-values are necessary for post-hoc testing nor the p-value duality; and his adoption of the standard non-decision-theoretic e-value definition inside a decision-theoretic framework is suspected as the reason. Koning's framework also covers post-hoc variants that do not use classical e-values (harmonic e-values) [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Sec. 1.1, App. A]. Grünwald's roving α̂ = ℓ/S(y) and Koning's "reject at level p = 1/e" are the same device read from the two sides.
- [grunwaldEposterior2023](grunwaldEposterior2023.md), [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024](grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024.md): the e-posterior's data-dependent α and the "post-hoc loss" arguments are the multi-action analogue; Koning restricts to binary decisions but obtains a converse (necessity).
- [ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025](ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025.md): the monograph's Definition 4.1 (E[sup_α φ_α/α] ≤ 1) and Theorem 4.9 are the same post-hoc validity notion; Koning's Theorem 2 is its binary-test converse via p = 1/e, and Example 5 (E[1/p] = ∞ for an exact p-value) is the "rejecting at level p is not allowed for p-values" statement.
- [ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022](ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022.md): Appendix C.1's post-hoc anytime-valid p-process is the reciprocal of an e-process, and the p-process definition there is adopted [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Def. 6].
- [koningAnytimeValidityFree2026](koningAnytimeValidityFree2026.md): the same author's test induction reinterprets [0, 1]-valued tests as conditional levels; the p-function and Galois-connection machinery of Appendix E is the fixed-sample counterpart of viewing a test as a CDF in α.
- [larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025](larssonNumeraireEvariableReverse2025.md): the numeraire e-variable is cited as a composite double post-hoc p-value [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, App. B].
- [koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022](koolenLogoptimalAnytimevalidEvalues2022.md), [grunwaldSafeTesting2024](grunwaldSafeTesting2024.md): cited for likelihood ratios maximising expected log; Corollary 2 recovers them as log-utility-optimal post-hoc p-values, and Example 9 proposes the geometric mean as a validity notion rather than an optimality target.
- [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021](vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021.md): Props 2–3 and 6–7 are the product and harmonic-mean (arithmetic mean of e-values) mergers written on the p-scale; see [e-merging](../concepts/e-merging.md). Wilson 2019's harmonic mean p-value is noted as "deeply related" [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Sec. 1.1].
- [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021](wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021.md), [xuBringingClosureFalse2025](xuBringingClosureFalse2025.md): earlier observations that e-values give post-hoc valid decisions in multiple testing (also Katsevich and Ramdas 2020, Xu, Wang and Ramdas 2024); Koning says they do not cover necessity or single-hypothesis testing. Xu et al. 2025 and Hartog and Lei 2025 are described as building on earlier versions of this paper [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Sec. 1.1].
- [villeEtudeCritiqueNotion1939](villeEtudeCritiqueNotion1939.md), [howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021](howardTimeuniformNonparametricNonasymptotic2021.md): Prop. 11 turns Ville's inequality into an equality by allowing a data-dependent α; Wang and Ramdas 2025 (extended Ville's inequality) is matched with the non-integrable Markov step.
- Koning 2024 (Continuous testing: unifying tests and e-values, arXiv 2409.05654; not in the bib) supplies the Neyman–Pearson lemma for e-values used in Theorem 3 and the view of level-α tests as {0, 1/α}-valued e-values [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Thm 3, Sec. 7.4]. Hemerik and Koning 2024 apply the post-hoc p-value to multiple "standard" significance thresholds in one literature.

## Concepts touched
- [post-hoc-validity](../concepts/post-hoc-validity.md): primary source for the binary-test characterisation and the necessity of e-values.
- [e-variable](../concepts/e-variable.md): decision-theoretic foundation as reciprocal post-hoc p-value; h-valid, harmonic, geometric and max e-values as generalisations.
- [calibrator](../concepts/calibrator.md): the reciprocal map p = 1/e is the admissible e-to-p calibrator; the paper argues it is a change of scale, not a calibration.
- [e-merging](../concepts/e-merging.md): products and harmonic means of post-hoc p-values; h-mean merging; Theorems 12–14 for randomised p-functions.
- [villes-inequality](../concepts/villes-inequality.md): Markov's equality and Ville's equalities, Prop. 10–11.
- [e-process](../concepts/e-process.md): post-hoc anytime-valid p-processes are reciprocals of e-processes; Ville's equality for exact e-processes.
- [numeraire](../concepts/numeraire.md), [gro-criterion](../concepts/gro-criterion.md): log-utility-optimal post-hoc p-value is f_P/f_Q; numeraire as double post-hoc p-value.
- [test-induction](../concepts/test-induction.md): continuous-testing viewpoint (tests as {0, 1/α}-valued e-values) and p-functions of randomised tests.
- [e-bh](../concepts/e-bh.md): post-hoc FWER and expected multiple testing error in Appendix C.
- post-hoc-p-value (new): the p-value of a post-hoc α valid test function; characterised by E[1/p] ≤ 1, closed under independent products and harmonic means, generalised by p-functions and h-validity, with expected-utility optimality via the Neyman–Pearson lemma for e-values.

## Open questions / gaps
- No unique notion of post-hoc power: the paper settles on expected utility of 1/p under Q, but the choice of U is left to the application [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Sec. 5.1].
- Characterising quasi-arithmetic certainty equivalents on total orders from idempotence, monotonicity, replacement and continuity is left open; so is sequential testing on total orders under information-structure filtrations [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, footnote 2, App. G.2].
- Whether e-values should be redefined as reciprocals of post-hoc p-functions, to make them meaningful for randomised tests, is suggested but not pursued [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, App. E.2].
- Markov's equality is proposed as a route to "concentration equalities" beyond Ville; only the Ville case is worked out [koningPosthocAlphaHypothesis2023, Sec. 8].
- Not in the bib: Koning 2024 (continuous testing), Hemerik and Koning 2024, Hartog and Lei 2025, Ramdas and Manole 2023, Wang and Ramdas 2025, Katsevich and Ramdas 2020, Xu, Wang and Ramdas 2024, Wilson 2019.
