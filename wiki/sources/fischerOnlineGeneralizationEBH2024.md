# An online generalization of the (e-)Benjamini–Hochberg procedure

arXiv 2407.20683v5 (February 2026). Numbering below follows v5: Sections 1–5, Appendices A (boosting details), B (e-TOAD), C (online BH vs LORD), D (online Storey-BH), E (dependence notions), F (simulation setup), G (omitted proofs). R code at github.com/fischer23/online_e-BH.

## Summary
In online multiple testing hypotheses H_1, H_2, … arrive one at a time with p-values or e-values, and the classical setup demands an irrevocable decision on H_t at time t. The paper relaxes this to *online ARC* (accept-to-reject changes) procedures: nested rejection sets R_1 ⊆ R_2 ⊆ … where R_t ⊆ [t] depends only on the first t statistics, so a rejection is never undone but an earlier non-rejection may later become a rejection (Definition 1) [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024]. Within this class the authors define online e-BH and online BH: fix nonnegative weights (γ_t) with Σ γ_t ≤ 1 and at time t reject every i ≤ t with E_i ≥ 1/(k*_t α γ_i), where k*_t = max{k ≤ t : Σ_{j ≤ t} 1{E_j ≥ 1/(kαγ_j)} ≥ k} (Section 2); the p-value version replaces this by P_i ≤ k*_t α γ_i (Section 3). Because k*_t is nondecreasing in t the sets are nested, whereas naively re-running offline (e-)BH on E_1, …, E_t at each t can change its mind in both directions; with γ_i = 1/K for i ≤ K and 0 afterwards the online rules reproduce e-BH and BH exactly at time K, which justifies the names [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024]. The error metric is the SupFDR E[sup_t FDP_t] of Xu and Ramdas 2021, which implies FDR control at every stopping time (StopFDR); Theorem G.3 shows the converse holds up to a factor 1 + log(1/α) via Ville's inequality. The main theorems: online e-BH controls SupFDR ≤ α under arbitrary dependence (Theorem 2.2) and uniformly improves e-LOND (Section 2.2), while online BH controls OnlineFDR ≤ α under PRDS (Proposition 3.1), SupFDR ≤ α(1 + log(1/α)) under PRDN (Theorem 3.4, sharp by Theorem 3.5), SupFDR ≤ α(3.18 + log(1/α)) under WNDN (Theorem 3.7) and SupFDR^K ≤ α ℓ_K under arbitrary dependence with a known horizon K (Theorem 3.9). Along the way the self-consistency proof technique upgrades e-LOND, r-LOND, online BR and TOAD from fixed-time FDR to SupFDR control, and LOND and TOAD (positive-dependence version) to inflated SupFDR control under PRDN and WNDN [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].

## Main results
- **Definition 1 (online ARC).** A nested sequence R_1 ⊆ R_2 ⊆ … with R_t ⊆ [t] measurable in the first t statistics; fully online procedures (no changes) and offline procedures (no rejections until the last step) are both special cases. Reversing rejections is argued to be harmful in A/B testing, platform trials and genomics, while accept-to-reject changes are useful [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Sec. 1.1].
- **Section 1.2 (error metrics).** OnlineFDR = sup_t FDR_t; SupFDR = E[sup_t FDP_t] (Xu and Ramdas 2021); StopFDR = sup_τ FDR_τ over all stopping times; SupFDR^K = E[sup_{t ≤ K} FDP_t]. SupFDR ≤ α ⇒ StopFDR ≤ α, and StopFDR ≤ α ⇒ SupFDR ≤ α(1 + log(1/α)) [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Thm G.3, Cor. G.4].
- **Definition 2 and Proposition 2.1 (weighted self-consistency).** A finite R ⊆ ℕ is self-consistent at level α if E_t ≥ 1/(αγ_t|R|) (or P_t ≤ αγ_t|R|) for every t ∈ R. For arbitrarily dependent e-values in an infinite stream, E[sup_{R ∈ R(α)} FDP(R)] ≤ π_0 α ≤ α with π_0 = Σ_{i ∈ I_0} γ_i; the proof uses 1{x ≥ 1} ≤ x and linearity, then Fatou–Lebesgue for the limit t → ∞ (eq. 1) [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Theorem 2.2.** Online e-BH controls SupFDR ≤ α under arbitrary dependence between the e-values; claimed as the first nontrivial online procedure with SupFDR control under arbitrary dependence [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Sec. 2.1].
- **Section 2.2, Proposition 2.3.** e-LOND rejects H_t iff E_t ≥ 1/(αγ_t(|R_{t-1}| + 1)); its rejection set is contained in that of online e-BH at every t, so online e-BH uniformly improves e-LOND; e-LOND is itself self-consistent, hence controls SupFDR (previously only fixed-time FDR). Figure 1: the power gain is large for weak signals (μ_A = 3.5), smaller for strong signals (μ_A = 4.5) [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Remark 1 (stochastic rounding).** The randomised e-BH/e-LOND trick of Xu and Ramdas 2023 needs a single data-dependent level; online e-BH has an increasing sequence of levels, so rounding at time t with α̂_t = αγ_t(k*_{t-1} + 1) reduces it to randomised e-LOND, and rounding only at the stopping time τ gives a weighted randomised e-BH [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Section 2.3, eq. (2), Proposition 2.4 (boosting).** With the per-hypothesis truncation T_t(x) = Σ_{k ≥ 1} 1{1/(kαγ_t) ≤ x < 1/((k−1)αγ_t)} / (kαγ_t), online e-BH on any nonnegative X_1, X_2, … with E_{H_t}[T_t(X_t)] ≤ 1 controls SupFDR; T_t = T of Wang and Ramdas when γ_t = 1/K. Appendix A.1 handles the infinite sum by a conservative truncation T^{+s} (eq. 3) or by T^{−s} (eq. 4), which must then be plugged in as the e-value; Example 1 (Gaussian likelihood ratio, δ = 3, α = 0.05, γ_t = 0.01) gives b_t = 1.165 (s = 10) or 1.174 (s = 100) with T^{+s}, and 3.071 (s = 10) or 1.73 (s = 100) with T^{−s} [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Appendix A.2, Proposition A.1 (boosting under local dependence, new).** If E_t is independent of E_1, …, E_{t−L_t−1} (lags L_t, Zrnic et al. 2021), the lag-dependent truncation T_t^{L_t}(x) = min(T_t(x), 1/((k*_{t−L_t−1} + 1)αγ_t)) still gives SupFDR control, because nothing is gained from E_t exceeding the level implied by the already-known independent rejections; Example 2 raises b_t to 1.265–2.639 depending on k*_{t−L_t−1} ∈ {2, 10} and the truncation. Remark 6: under local dependence γ_t may even be chosen predictably from E_1, …, E_{t−L_t−1}, and conditional validity suffices [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Appendix A.3, Proposition A.2 (PRDS boosting).** Under the e-value PRDS condition (Definition 6), the weaker requirement sup_k P_{H_t}(X_t ≥ 1/(kαγ_t))/(kαγ_t) ≤ 1 (eq. 5) gives OnlineFDR control only, not SupFDR; the proof uses Lemma 1(ii) of Wang and Ramdas 2022 [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Section 2.4, Proposition 2.5 (p-values through nonincreasing transforms).** Applying online e-BH to ψ_t(P_t) with ψ_t nonincreasing and left-continuous controls SupFDR if Σ_k [ψ_t^{−1}(1/(kαγ_t)) − ψ_t^{−1}(1/((k−1)αγ_t))]/(kαγ_t) ≤ 1 for every t (i), and OnlineFDR under PRDS if sup_k ψ_t^{−1}(1/(kαγ_t))/(kαγ_t) ≤ 1 (ii); ψ_t = 1/x yields online BH under (ii). Remark 2 and Proposition G.1: the online Blanchard–Roquain procedure (Fisher 2022 with shape functions β_t(k) = ∫_0^k x dν(x)) is a special case of (i), hence controls SupFDR; Remark 3 and Proposition G.2: r-LOND is boosted e-LOND, hence controls SupFDR [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Section 3, Proposition 3.1.** Online BH rejects i ≤ t with P_i ≤ k*_t α γ_i. Under PRDS p-values, OnlineFDR ≤ π_0 α ≤ α via Lemma 1(b) of Ramdas, Barber, Wainwright and Jordan 2019. Remark 4: online BH uniformly improves LOND. Remark 5 and Appendix D: online Storey-BH with λ ∈ [α, 1) and π̂_0^t = (max_i γ_i + Σ_i γ_i 1{P_i > λ ∨ i > t})/(1−λ) controls OnlineFDR under independence (Proposition D.1) and compares to SAFFRON as online BH compares to LORD (Figure 5) [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Theorem 3.2 (weighted FDR-linking).** For any dependence and fixed K, E[sup_{R ∈ R(α)} FDP(R)] ≤ π_0 α + π_0 α ∫_{π_0 α}^1 FDR_0(x)/x² dx, where FDR_0 is the FDR of weighted BH restricted to the nulls with weights γ_t/π_0; proved in Appendix G.3 through the weighted Simes p-value, extending Theorems 1–2 of Su 2018 [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Proposition 3.3, Theorem 3.4.** Under PRDN, FDR_0(x) = x, so E[sup FDP] ≤ π_0 α(1 + log(1/(π_0 α))) ≤ α(1 + log(1/α)); online BH controls SupFDR at that level. **Theorem 3.5 (sharpness).** For every ε > 0 there are K_0, α' such that for all α ≤ α', SupFDR ≥ StopFDR > (1−ε)α(1 + log(1/α)) with K_0 i.i.d. uniform null p-values followed by non-nulls with p-values 0; the instance is independent, so no stronger positive-dependence assumption helps (Appendix G.5, Lemma G.5, Figure 6, built on Su 2018 Lemma 2.4 and Theorem 6) [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Proposition 3.6, Theorem 3.7.** Under WNDN (P(∩_{i ∈ A}{P_i ≤ s}) ≤ Π P(P_i ≤ s) for null A), E[sup FDP] ≤ α(3.18 + log(1/α)) following Chi, Ramdas and Wang 2025 Theorem 19 and Proposition 12; the log factor is unimprovable, the constant 3.18 may be. **Proposition 3.8.** LOND and positive-dependence TOAD inherit both inflated SupFDR bounds [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Theorem 3.9.** With a fixed horizon K, online BR at level αℓ_K with β(k) = k/ℓ_K (ℓ_K = Σ_{i ≤ K} 1/i) coincides with online BH at level α, so SupFDR^K ≤ αℓ_K under arbitrary dependence. For an infinite stream online BH controls none of OnlineFDR, StopFDR or SupFDR below 1 under arbitrary dependence, since BH's FDR equals min(αℓ_K, 1) on the Guo and Rao 2008 configuration [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Sec. 3.4].
- **Appendix B, Propositions B.1–B.3 (e-TOAD).** With decision deadlines d_t ≥ t and active set C_t = {i ≤ t : d_i ≥ t}, e-TOAD rejects i with E_i ≥ 1/(αγ_i k*_{min(d_i,t)}); it equals online e-BH when d_t = ∞ and e-LOND when d_t = t, controls SupFDR under arbitrary dependence, and contains Fisher's TOAD (now with hypothesis-specific shape functions, which matters when d_t < ∞) as a calibrated special case, so TOAD controls SupFDR too [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Appendix C (online BH versus LORD).** The online BH levels α_{i,t} = k*_t α γ_i satisfy the LORD budget Σ_{i ≤ t} α_i/(|R_t| ∨ 1) ≤ α (eq. 7) but depend on all rejections up to t, not only on the first i − 1, so online BH is not a LORD instance; both spend the same level budget and differ in weighting (online BH lets every level gain from every rejection; LORD only future ones). Figure 4 with γ_t = q^{t−1}(1−q): LORD wins at q = 0.99, online BH wins at q = 0.999, and online BH at q = 0.999 almost matches LORD at q = 0.99. Online BH's weighting is interpretable (γ_i = 2γ_j means twice the level regardless of rejections) and it has PRDS/PRDN/WNDN guarantees LORD lacks, at the cost of being ARC rather than strictly online [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Theorem G.3.** If an adapted nonnegative process bounded by c ≥ 1 has E[W_τ] ≤ 1 at every stopping time, then E[sup_t W_t] ≤ 1 + log c by Ville's inequality and tail integration; the authors liken it to an adjuster for e-processes or a calibrator for anytime-valid p-values [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Remark 7.** The sharpness construction shows the α(1 + log(1/α)) bound for the worst self-consistent set is attained with i.i.d. null and non-null p-values, but sets along the BH path need adversarially chosen non-nulls as in Su 2018 [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- **Table 3.** Procedures with proven SupFDR control: online e-BH, e-LOND, e-TOAD, online BR, r-LOND and arbitrary-dependence TOAD under arbitrary dependence; online BH, positive-dependence TOAD and LOND under positive and negative dependence at inflated levels; SupLORD only under conditional validity [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Sec. 5].

## Setting and assumptions
- Stream of nulls H_t with p-values P_t (P_{H_t}(P_t ≤ x) ≤ x) or e-values E_t (E_{H_t}[E_t] ≤ 1); I_0 the unknown set of true nulls; weights γ_t ≥ 0 with Σ γ_t ≤ 1, nonrandom unless local dependence permits predictable choice (Remark 6); π_0 = Σ_{i ∈ I_0} γ_i [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Secs 1–2].
- Dependence notions extended to countable index sets in Appendix E: PRDS (Definition 3, conditioning on P_i ≤ s with increasing sets over finite subsets), PRDN (Definition 4, restricted to nulls), WNDN (Definition 5), and PRDS for e-values with decreasing sets and conditioning on E_i ≥ s (Definition 6), which is preserved by nonincreasing transforms of PRDS p-values [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].
- Stopping is fully data-adaptive: SupFDR control is the target for e-values under arbitrary dependence and for p-values under PRDN/WNDN; only OnlineFDR (fixed times) under PRDS for online BH and PRDS-boosted online e-BH; nothing below level 1 for online BH on an infinite arbitrarily dependent stream [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Tables 1–2, Sec. 3.4].
- Simulations (Appendix F): 100 trials of 1000 Gaussian hypotheses, N(0,1) vs N(μ_A,1) with μ_A ∈ {3.5, 4.5}, likelihood-ratio e-values, batch correlation 0.5 within batches of 20 for the e-value experiments, independent statistics for the p-value experiments, γ_t = q^{t−1}(1−q) with q = 0.99 [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024].

## Relation to other sources
- [wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021](wangFalseDiscoveryRate2021.md): the offline e-BH, its self-consistency proof (Proposition 2 there), boosting through the truncation T and PRDS boosting are all extended to weights, infinite streams and stopping times; the weighted e-BH of that paper does not immediately cover infinitely many hypotheses [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Secs 2, 2.3, A.3].
- [xuOnlineMultipleTesting2023](xuOnlineMultipleTesting2023.md): e-LOND is the state of the art for online FDR with arbitrarily dependent e-values; this paper shows it also controls SupFDR and that online e-BH uniformly dominates it (Proposition 2.3, Figure 1), and e-TOAD interpolates between the two through decision deadlines [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Secs 2.2, B].
- [fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024](fischerAdmissibleOnlineClosed2024.md) (in the bib, source page pending): cited for online closed testing and the necessity of e-values for online true-discovery guarantees [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Sec. 4]; the present paper is about FDR, not simultaneous true-discovery bounds.
- [xuBringingClosureFalse2025](xuBringingClosureFalse2025.md): the e-Closure view of FDR control is the offline counterpart of the self-consistency arguments here; closed e-BH improves e-BH at a fixed horizon, whereas this paper improves along the time axis. Combining the two is not addressed.
- [ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025](ramdasHypothesisTestingEvalues2025.md): book treatment of e-BH, compound e-values and e-BH's post-hoc guarantee (Chapter 9) supplies the offline background; the online ARC relaxation is not in the book.
- [vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021](vovkEvaluesCalibrationCombination2021.md) and [vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023](vovkConfidenceDiscoveriesEvalues2023.md): cited as the e-merging and closure-based alternatives to e-BH in the offline setting [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Sec. 4].
- [shaferTestingBettingStrategy2021](shaferTestingBettingStrategy2021.md) and the Ville page: Theorem G.3's StopFDR-to-SupFDR conversion is Ville's inequality applied to the FDP process, in the spirit of adjusters (Shafer, Shen, Vereshchagin and Vovk 2011; Dawid et al. 2011; Choe and Ramdas 2024) and calibrators (Vovk 1993) [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, App. G.4].
- [ignatiadisEvaluesUnnormalizedWeights2022](ignatiadisEvaluesUnnormalizedWeights2022.md): weighted BH with weights γ_t/π_0 appears inside the weighted FDR-linking theorem (Theorem 3.2); the weights here are user-chosen level allocations, not data-driven e-value weights.

## Concepts touched
- [e-BH, compound e-values and multiple testing](../concepts/e-bh.md): online e-BH and online BH as ARC generalisations; weighted self-consistency; boosting under local dependence; SupFDR versus OnlineFDR.
- [Post-hoc validity and data-dependent α](../concepts/post-hoc-validity.md): SupFDR control means FDR holds at any data-dependent stopping time, the time-axis analogue of post-hoc choice.
- [Ville's inequality](../concepts/villes-inequality.md): Theorem G.3 converts stopping-time validity of a bounded process into a bound on its running supremum at cost 1 + log c.
- [Calibrators](../concepts/calibrator.md): BH, BY/BR and r-LOND recovered from online e-BH/e-LOND via nonincreasing transforms ψ_t with Σ_k [ψ_t^{−1}(1/(kαγ_t)) − ψ_t^{−1}(1/((k−1)αγ_t))]/(kαγ_t) ≤ 1 (Proposition 2.5), a per-hypothesis calibration condition.
- [Optional stopping](../concepts/optional-stopping.md): the SupFDR/StopFDR metrics are the multiple-testing counterpart of validity at stopping times.
- online-multiple-testing (new): online ARC procedures, OnlineFDR/SupFDR/StopFDR, the LOND/LORD/SAFFRON/e-LOND/TOAD family and their guarantees under independence, PRDS, PRDN, WNDN, local and arbitrary dependence.

## Open questions / gaps
- Whether the WNDN constant 3.18 can be sharpened for online BH, and whether online BH admits any guarantee under arbitrary dependence beyond the horizon-K bound αℓ_K (it cannot for infinite streams) [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Secs 3.3–3.4].
- PRDS boosting of online e-BH yields OnlineFDR only; SupFDR control under PRDS boosting is open [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, App. A.3].
- Stochastic rounding does not combine with the increasing level sequence of online e-BH except at a fixed time or at the stopping time [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Remark 1].
- Power comparisons against LORD/SAFFRON are only simulated under independence with geometric weights; no theory on when ARC relaxation buys power [fischerOnlineGeneralizationEBH2024, Apps C–D].
- Cited works not in the bib: Xu and Ramdas 2021 (Dynamic algorithms for online multiple testing, SupLORD), Xu and Ramdas 2023 (randomised multiple testing under dependence), Fisher 2022 (TOAD), Zrnic, Ramdas and Jordan 2021, Chi, Ramdas and Wang 2025, Su 2018, Blanchard and Roquain 2008, Shafer, Shen, Vereshchagin and Vovk 2011, Choe and Ramdas 2024 (Combining evidence across filtrations).
