# Time-uniform central limit theory and asymptotic confidence sequences

## Summary
Classical CLT confidence intervals are only asymptotically valid but are universal (same form for every problem up to the influence function) and reach problems, such as doubly robust estimation of causal effects, where no nonasymptotic interval exists; every confidence sequence published before this paper was nonasymptotic and so inherited neither property [waudbysmithTimeuniformCentralLimit2021, Sec. 1]. The paper defines an asymptotic confidence sequence (AsympCS) as a sequence of intervals whose half-widths are almost-surely asymptotically equivalent (ratio → 1) to those of some unknown nonasymptotic (1−α)-confidence sequence for the same estimand (Definition 2.1), and argues that the natural alternatives (lim sup of eventual miscoverage probabilities, convergence in probability) are vacuous or too weak (Sec. 2.1, Remark 1, App. B.3). The construction replaces the CLT by strong invariance principles: Strassen's 1964 coupling writes the centred partial sum as a Gaussian random walk plus o(√(t log log t)) almost surely, and Robbins' 1970 Gaussian mixture boundary, which grows as O(√(t log t)), is applied to the coupled Gaussian walk, so the coupling error is asymptotically negligible (Thm 2.2, App. A.1). An abstract four-condition recipe (strong approximation, boundary for the approximating process, rate domination, almost-sure approximation of the boundary) turns any Gaussian time-uniform boundary into an AsympCS (Thm 2.4); instances cover iterated-logarithm rates (Prop. 2.3), time-varying means under martingale dependence via Strassen 1967 and a Lindeberg-type condition (Prop. 2.5), independent non-identically distributed data under a Lyapunov-type condition (Cor. 2.6), and Robbins' delayed-start boundary (Prop. 2.9). A separate definition of asymptotic time-uniform coverage for sequences of AsympCSs that start later and later is shown to hold sharply, with limit exactly 1−α, once the tuning parameter is matched to the first peeking time (Def. 2.7, Thm 2.8). The second half derives AsympCSs for the average treatment effect using cross-fit AIPW estimators with sequential sample splitting, in randomised experiments (Thm 3.1), observational studies under an o(√(log t / t)) product nuisance rate (Thm 3.2), and for a running average of time-varying individual effects (Thm 3.3), plus a general corollary for asymptotically linear time-uniform estimators and a delta method (Cor. 3.4, Prop. 3.5); simulations and a MIMIC-III sepsis application close the paper (Secs 4–5).

## Main results
- Definition 2.1: (θ̂_t − L_t, θ̂_t + U_t) is a (1−α)-AsympCS for (θ_t) if some nonasymptotic (1−α)-CS (θ̂_t − L*_t, θ̂_t + U*_t) exists, possibly on an enriched probability space, with L*_t/L_t → 1 and U*_t/U_t → 1 almost surely [waudbysmithTimeuniformCentralLimit2021, Sec. 2.1].
- Remark 1 and Prop. B.4: convergence in probability is the wrong notion; time-uniform convergence in probability sup_{k≥t}|X_k| →_p 0 is equivalent to almost-sure convergence, so the a.s. requirement is exactly "approximation holds for all t simultaneously" [Sec. 2.1, App. B.3].
- Theorem 2.2 (Gaussian mixture AsympCS): for i.i.d. data with finite variance, μ̂_t ± σ̂_t √(2(tρ²+1)/(t²ρ²) · log(√(tρ²+1)/α)) is a (1−α)-AsympCS for μ, for any ρ > 0; it needs no MGF, unlike Robbins' sub-Gaussian CS of the same form, and scales with the estimated variance [Sec. 2.2.3, App. A.1].
- Lemma A.1: Strassen's strong approximation gives i.i.d. Gaussians G_i with (1/t)Σ(Y_i − μ) = (σ̂_t/t)ΣG_i + o(√(log log t / t)) a.s.; a Wiener process may replace the Gaussian walk [App. A.1].
- Proof structure of Thm 2.2: exp(λW_t − tλ²/2) mixed over λ ~ N(0, ρ²) gives the closed-form martingale M_t = (tρ²+1)^{−1/2} exp(ρ²W_t²/(2(tρ²+1))); Ville's inequality on M_t yields Robbins' boundary; the triangle inequality plus Lemma A.1 transfers it to the data [App. A.1, Steps 1–3].
- Proposition 2.3: the boundary σ̂_t · 1.7 √((log log(2t) + 0.72 log(10.4/α))/t) is an AsympCS at the LIL-optimal √(log log t / t) rate, obtained by swapping in the stitched boundary of Howard et al.; mixtures are nonetheless preferred because tighter later means looser early [Sec. 2.2.4].
- Theorem 2.4 (abstract recipe): under Conditions G-1 (strong approximation at rate r_t), G-2 (a (1−α)-boundary for the approximating process), G-3 (r_t = o(L̃_t ∧ Ũ_t) a.s.) and G-4 (L̃_t/L_t → 1, Ũ_t/U_t → 1 a.s.), [θ̂_t − L_t, θ̂_t + U_t] is a (1−α)-AsympCS; the proof shifts the boundary by the approximation error [Sec. 2.3, App. A.2].
- Proposition 2.5 (Lindeberg-type martingale AsympCS): for Y_t with conditional means μ_t and variances σ_t², under L-1 (V_t = Σσ_i² → ∞ a.s.), L-2 (Σ_t E[(Y_t−μ_t)² 1{(Y_t−μ_t)² > V_t^κ} | Y_1^{t−1}]/V_t^κ < ∞ a.s. for some κ ∈ (0,1)) and L-3 (σ̂_t²/σ̃_t² → 1 a.s.), the Thm 2.2 formula with σ̂_t² inside the mixture covers the running average conditional mean μ̃_t = (1/t)Σμ_i [Sec. 2.4].
- Lemma A.2: under L-1 and L-2, Strassen 1967 (Thm 4.4, eq. 159) couples Σ(Y_i − μ_i) with Σσ_i G_i at rate o(V_t^{3/8} log V_t), which is o(√(V_t log V_t)) so G-3 holds [App. A.3].
- Lyapunov-type condition Σ_t E[|Y_t − μ_t|^{2+δ} | Y_1^{t−1}]/V_t^{(2+δ)/2} < ∞ a.s. implies L-2 (the text layer's choice of κ in the short proof is garbled) [App. B.5]; it holds if conditional q-th moments are a.s. uniformly bounded for some q > 2 [Sec. 2.4].
- Corollary 2.6 (Lyapunov-type AsympCS): independent non-identically distributed data with L-1, the Lyapunov condition, and (14) [Σ E|Y_i − EY_i|^{2+2β}/V_i^{1+β} < ∞, μ̃_t² = o(V_t) a.s., (1/t)Σ(μ_i − μ̃_t)² = o(σ̃_t²)] give the same AsympCS with the sample variance [Sec. 2.4, App. A.4].
- Definition 2.7 (asymptotic time-uniform coverage): a family (C_t(m))_{t≥m} has asymptotic (1−α) coverage if lim inf_{m→∞} P(∀t ≥ m, μ_t ∈ C_t(m)) ≥ 1−α, sharp if equality; the naive lim sup_m P(∃t ≥ m: μ ∉ C_t) ≤ α is vacuous since it is zero for every nonasymptotic CS at any level [Secs 2.1, 2.5].
- Theorem 2.8: under L-1, L-2 and L-3-η (σ̂_t² − σ̃_t² = o((tσ̃_t²)^η/t) a.s. for some η ∈ (0,1), implied in the i.i.d. case with 2+δ moments by Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund), the AsympCS with ρ_m := √((−2 log α + log(−2 log α) + 1)/(σ̂_m² m log(m ∨ e))) has sharp asymptotic coverage: lim_m P(∀t ≥ m, μ̃_t ∈ C̃_t(m)) = 1−α [Sec. 2.5, App. A.7, Lemmas A.12–A.13].
- Proposition 2.9 (delayed-start AsympCS): from Robbins' P(∀t ≥ m: |G_t| < √(t(a² + log(t/m)))) ≥ 1 − Ψ(a), Ψ(a) = 2(1 − Φ(a) + aφ(a)), the sequence μ̂_t ± σ̂_t √((a² + log(tσ̂_t²/(mσ̂_m²)))/t) for t ≥ m is an AsympCS under L-1 to L-3 and has sharp asymptotic coverage under L-3-η; Robbins–Siegmund gave equality for the Wiener process, and with known variance this recovers their asymptotic result [Sec. 2.6, App. A.9, Lemma A.14].
- Claims B.1–B.2: the fixed-m corollary (22) of Prop. 2.9 is a strict subset of the bound in version 1 of Bibaut, Kallus and Lindon 2022 for every λ > 0 and is identical to their version-2 "running maximum likelihood SPRT" [Sec. 2.6.2, App. B.7].
- Theorem 3.1 (ATE in randomised experiments): with known propensity scores in [δ, 1−δ], cross-fit AIPW with any regression estimators converging in L₂(P) to some (possibly wrong) limit, and E|f̄(Z)|^{2+ε} < ∞, ψ̂_t ± √(var̂_t(f̂)) · (Thm 2.2 boundary) is a (1−α)-AsympCS for ψ [Sec. 3.2, App. A.5.1].
- Theorem 3.2 (ATE in observational studies): additionally requiring Σ_a ‖π̂_t − π‖·‖μ̂^a_t − μ^a‖ = o(√(log t / t)) in L₂(P) and f̂ → the efficient influence function, the same interval is an AsympCS; the nuisance rate is slower than the fixed-n n^{−1/2} but must hold almost surely [Sec. 3.3, App. A.5.2].
- Sequential sample splitting assigns each new subject permanently to a training or evaluation stream with probability 1/2; cross fitting swaps the roles and averages (eqs 23–26, Fig. 3) [Sec. 3.1].
- Theorem 3.3 (running average treatment effect): for independent non-identically distributed triples under Conditions ATE-1 (uniform L₂ convergence of regressions), ATE-2 (average nuisance product error o(√(log t / t))) and Cor. 2.6 applied to influence functions, the cross-fit AIPW interval (29) covers ψ̃_t = (1/t)Σψ_i; sample splitting alone does not suffice here; a martingale version with predictable nuisance plug-ins is Prop. B.5 [Sec. 3.4, App. A.6, B.6].
- Corollary 3.4 and Proposition 3.5: any asymptotically linear time-uniform estimator θ̂_t − θ = (1/t)Σφ(Z_i) + o(√(log t / t)) with var(φ) < ∞ gives an AsympCS with sharp asymptotic coverage after the ρ_m tuning; g(θ̂_t) is asymptotically linear with influence function g'(θ)φ, by the a.s. continuous mapping theorem and the LIL in place of the CLT [Sec. 3.5, App. A.8].
- Appendix B.1: one-sided AsympCSs replace log(√(tρ²+1)/α) by log(1 + √(tρ²+1)/(2α)) via a folded-Gaussian mixture (Props B.1–B.2, Lemma B.3).
- Appendix B.2: the ρ minimising the Thm 2.2 width at time t* is √((−W_{−1}(−α² e^{−1}) − 1)/t*) with the lower Lambert-W branch, approximated by √((−2 log α + log(−2 log α + 1))/t*), close to the fixed-n Cramér–Chernoff choice √(2 log(1/α)/t*).
- Appendix B.9: the ATE width is sharp in three senses: Robbins' mixture CS cannot be uniformly tightened (Ville almost an equality for continuous paths; cf. Howard et al. Sec. 3.6 and Ramdas et al. 2020 on admissibility), the Strassen/KMT coupling rates are unimprovable without further moments, and var(f) is the semiparametric efficiency bound.
- Appendix B.10 (Def. B.6, Prop. B.7): multivariate AsympCS defined through the normalised Lebesgue measure of the symmetric difference ν(C_t △ C*_t)/ν(C*_t) → 0 a.s.; for i.i.d. vectors with 2+δ moments, Einmahl's multivariate strong invariance principle plus Manole–Ramdas or Chugg–Wang–Ramdas Gaussian-vector boundaries give balls of radius √(γ̂_t · 16[2 log(log₂ t + 1) + log(1/α) + d log 5]/t) with γ̂_t the top empirical eigenvalue (58), or a mixture form (59).
- Section 4 (simulations): against Robbins' sub-Gaussian CS and Howard et al.'s empirical-Bernstein CS for bounded data, AsympCSs are tighter and are the only ones whose cumulative miscoverage sharply approaches α; the gap grows as the propensity floor p_min shrinks while influence-function variances stay bounded; Fig. 8 shows miscoverage below α = 0.1 over 10⁵ steps for Uniform and t₃ data once the first peek is at m ≥ 50. Appendix B.4 places Pocock and O'Brien–Fleming repeated CIs between the CLT CI and the AsympCS, with miscoverage slightly above α.
- Section 5: MIMIC-III, 5231 Sepsis-3 patients, effect of capping 24h IV fluids at 6 L on 30-day mortality; stacking-based AIPW AsympCS covers 0 from patient 1000 to 5231; a difference-in-means AsympCS would have declared a negative effect before 1500 patients.

## Setting and assumptions
- Estimand: a mean μ, a running average conditional mean μ̃_t, an ATE ψ, a running average ψ̃_t of individual treatment effects, or any pathwise differentiable functional with finite positive information bound; T any totally ordered infinite set with a minimum, mostly N [waudbysmithTimeuniformCentralLimit2021, Secs 2.1, 3.5].
- Data: i.i.d. with finite variance (Thm 2.2); martingale-dependent with diverging cumulative conditional variance and a Lindeberg-type condition (Prop. 2.5); independent non-identically distributed under Lyapunov-type moments (Cor. 2.6); finite 2+δ absolute moments for sharp coverage (L-3-η). No MGF, boundedness or parametric likelihood anywhere; Bahadur–Savage 1956 is cited as the reason no nonasymptotic CS can exist for means without moment bounds [Sec. 4].
- Guarantee: not a finite-sample one. Validity is almost-sure asymptotic equivalence to an unknown nonasymptotic CS (Def. 2.1); asymptotic coverage is a limit over the first peeking time m, with the boundary tuned to m (Def. 2.7, Thm 2.8). The authors state that limiting behaviour is "a guiding principle rather than a panacea" and that finite-sample behaviour can be arbitrarily bad [Sec. 2.1].
- Optional stopping: the intervals are meant to be monitored continuously and stopped at arbitrary data-dependent times, but the anytime guarantee is itself only asymptotic; the coupled Gaussian process is implicit and lives on an enriched probability space [Secs 1.1, 2.2.2, fn. 4].
- Causal part: consistency, positivity and exchangeability; propensities in [δ, 1−δ]; nuisance estimators built only from the training stream; the observational theorem needs a.s. L₂ product-rate consistency of both nuisances [Secs 3.1–3.3].
- The underlying martingales are the sub-Gaussian exponential martingales of the Gaussian walk and their N(0, ρ²) mixtures (two-sided) or folded-Gaussian mixtures (one-sided); all boundaries are obtained through Ville's inequality on these [App. A.1, A.3, B.1].

## Open questions / gaps
- Finite-sample behaviour of an AsympCS is unconstrained by Definition 2.1; the only quantitative guidance is the simulation evidence that m ≥ 50 suffices for Uniform and t₃ data (Fig. 8) [waudbysmithTimeuniformCentralLimit2021, Secs 2.1, 4].
- Asymptotic coverage (Thm 2.8) is a statement about the first peeking time m → ∞, not about a fixed design; how to choose m in practice is left to the ρ_m tuning rule.
- The approximation rate o(√(log log t / t)) of Strassen could be improved to o(t^{1/q − 1}) under q > 2 moments via KMT, which the paper uses only in the multivariate appendix; whether this yields practically sharper AsympCSs is not explored.
- The martingale AsympCS without sample splitting (Prop. B.5) has "less transparent" conditions; a clean statement is not given.
- Whether the asymptotic anytime-validity here can be turned into a formal notion of approximate optional stopping for non-e-process e-variables, as conjectured in [haoEValuesExponentialFamilies2025], is open; the paper offers confidence sequences and type-I control in the limit, not e-processes.
- The paper's own cross-fit ATE results assume i.i.d. or independent triples; adaptive (bandit-style) treatment assignment is handled only through the martingale Prop. 2.5 route (Remark 3, App. B.6), not in the main theorems.
