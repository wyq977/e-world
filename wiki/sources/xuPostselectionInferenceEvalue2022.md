# Post-selection inference for e-value based confidence intervals

arXiv 2203.12572v4 (February 2024), the version matching the Electronic Journal of Statistics article 18(1), 2024. Numbering below follows v4: Sections 1–8, Appendices A (proofs), B (multiple testing from e-BY), C (weighted e-BY), D (relaxed improvement), E (generalised e-CIs and Chernoff), F (FCR versus other error metrics).

## Summary
A scientist builds marginal (1 − α)-confidence intervals for K parameters, then uses the same data to select a subset S to report; the uncorrected intervals lose coverage, and Benjamini and Yekutieli 2005 proposed controlling the false coverage rate FCR = E[Σ_{i∈S} 1{θ*_i ∉ C_i(α_i)} / (|S| ∨ 1)] by reporting the selected intervals at level α|S|/K, which is valid only under independence or PRDS and a known selection rule, and otherwise at α|S|/(Kℓ_K) with ℓ_K = Σ_{k≤K} 1/k ≈ log K [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Section 1]. The paper isolates the class of e-confidence intervals (e-CIs), C(α) = {θ : E(θ) < 1/α} for a family of e-values E(θ) indexed by the parameter (Definition 2), and proves that the e-BY procedure, which reports each selected e-CI at level α|S|/K, has FCR ≤ α under any dependence between the K data sets and any (even unknown) selection rule, by a three-line Markov argument (Theorem 2). The guarantee is sharp (Theorem 3, stopped Brownian motions) and e-BY is admissible among FCR-controlling CI reporting procedures (Theorem 4). Any CI can be turned into an e-CI through a p-to-e calibrator, C^cal(α) = C(f^{−1}(1/α)) (Theorem 1), and with the specific BY calibrator f^{BY(K,α)} the e-BY procedure on calibrated CIs reproduces the assumption-free BY procedure exactly (Proposition 3, Corollary 2), so e-BY dominates BY whenever e-CIs exist [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022]. Universal inference sets, stopped confidence sequences and Chernoff-type intervals are already e-CIs (Section 2); e-BH, BH and a directional e-BH are derived as special cases (Appendix B); simulations and 263 Twitter A/B experiments show e-BY matching the width of BY-with-assumptions while BY-without-assumptions is far wider (Sections 6–7).

## Main results
- **Definitions 1–2, Proposition 1.** E(θ) an e-value for P_θ = {P : θ(P) = θ} for every θ; the (1 − α)-e-CI is C(α) = {θ ∈ Θ : E(θ) < 1/α} (eq. 1), a valid CI by Markov. Shafer's warranty sets and Vovk–Wang's e-confidence regions are the same object [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Section 2].
- **Proposition 2 (split universal inference e-CI).** E_UI(θ) = L_0(P_1)/argmax_{P_0 ∈ P_θ} L_0(P_0) with P_1 fitted on D_1 is an e-value for P_θ for any fitting rule, so {θ : E_UI(θ) < 1/α} is an e-CI; only likelihoods (or upper bounds) for each null are needed (eq. 2) [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Section 2.1].
- **Definitions 3–4, Section 2.2 (stopped confidence sequences).** A CS is a sequence (C_t(α)) valid at every stopping time, equivalently time-uniform; applying (1) to a family of e-processes gives a CS, and by Ramdas et al. 2020 every admissible anytime-valid CI sequence is an e-CI. Example: the Hoeffding e-process E^θ_t = exp(Σ λ_i(X_i − θ) − Σ λ_i²/8) with predictable λ_i gives C_t(α) = (Σ λ_i X_i ± (log(2/α) + Σ λ_i²/8)) / Σ λ_i, which with constant λ of order √(log(2/α)/n) is the classical Hoeffding CI [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- **Definition 5, Theorem 1 (calibrating CIs to e-CIs).** A calibrator is a nonincreasing, upper semicontinuous f : [0,1] → [0,∞] with ∫_0^1 f ≤ 1, with dual f^{−1}(y) = sup{p : f(p) ≥ y}. If C is nonincreasing and continuous from below (C(α) = ∪_{β>α} C(β)) and C(α) is a (1 − α)-CI, then C^cal(α) = C(f^{−1}(1/α)) is a (1 − α)-e-CI. Proof: the dual p-value p^dual(θ) = inf{α : θ ∉ C(α)} is a p-value for P_θ and E^cal(θ) = f(p^dual(θ)) is the e-value family (Appendix A.1) [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- **Definition 6, Theorem 2 (e-BY).** Set α_i = α|S|/K for i ∈ S. If C_i(α) is a (1 − α)-e-CI for each i and α, then FCR ≤ α for any dependence among X_1, …, X_K and any selection rule S. Proof: 1{θ*_i ∉ C_i(α|S|/K)} = 1{E_i(θ*_i) α|S|/K > 1} ≤ E_i(θ*_i) α|S|/K, sum, divide by |S| ∨ 1, take expectations [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Section 3].
- **Corollary 1 (post-hoc α).** E[sup_{α'∈(0,1]} FCP(α')/α'] ≤ 1 for e-BY, implying FCR control at every fixed α; the same holds for the FDP of e-BH, and for a single e-value reduces to E[sup_{α'} 1{E ≥ 1/α'}/α'] ≤ 1 (Wang and Ramdas 2022, Lemma 1), of which Grünwald's post-hoc Type-I safety is a rephrasing [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- **Definition 7, Fact 1 (BY), eq. (4).** BY sets α_i = α R_i^min/K under independence or PRDS when R_i^min = min{|S(X_{i→x})| : x, i ∈ S(X_{i→x})} is known and exceeds |S|/ℓ_K, else α|S|/(Kℓ_K); R_i^min needs the counterfactual behaviour of S on altered data and is 'often impractical' to compute [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Section 3.1].
- **Proposition 3, Corollary 2 (BY as a special case of e-BY).** The BY calibrator f^{BY(K,α)}(p) = (K/α) (⌈Kℓ_K p/α⌉ ∨ 1)^{−1} 1{p ≤ α/ℓ_K}, a step function equal to K/(αk) on the k-th interval of length α/(Kℓ_K), is an upper semicontinuous calibrator with ∫f = (1/ℓ_K) Σ_{k≤K} 1/k = 1. Since f^{−1}(K/(α|S|)) = α|S|/(Kℓ_K), e-BY on f^{BY}-calibrated CIs outputs exactly the assumption-free BY intervals C_i(α|S|/(Kℓ_K)) for every |S|. The calibrator is exact (E[E(θ*)] = 1 for exact CIs) and conjectured admissible; it is recommended as the default (Remark 2) [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Section 3.2].
- **Theorem 3 (sharpness).** Independent Brownian motions B^i with drift θ*_i, E^i_t(θ) = (1 + B^i_t − θt) stopped at 0; step 1 stops at hitting c − 1 or −1 with c ∈ [1, 1/α) and selects i if the e-process E^i(0) reaches c; step 2 continues selected experiments to level d = K/(α|S|) > c. With the one-sided e-CIs (5), lim_{θ*↑0} FCR = α; FCR ≤ α monotonically for θ* ≤ 0 and independence is only used between step-1 paths and step-2 increments (Appendix A.2, Remark 5) [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Section 4].
- **Lemma 1, Lemma 2 (what e-CIs look like).** A set function C : [0,1] → 2^Θ is a possible realisation of an e-CI constructor iff it is nonincreasing and continuous from below, so 'we cannot exclude the possibility that it is an e-CI just by looking at the CI'; the realising e-value family must equal e(θ) = sup{1/α : θ ∉ C(α)} wherever e(θ) ≥ 1 (eqs 10–11) [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Section 5.1].
- **Definitions 8–11, Theorem 4 (admissibility).** A CI reporting procedure Φ(S, C) outputs ∅ for i ∉ S and depends only on (C_i)_{i∈S}; Φ dominates Φ' if Φ(S,C)_i ⊆ Φ'(S,C)_i always, strictly if proper on some strictly decreasing input. e-BY at level α ∈ (0,1) is admissible among procedures with FCR ≤ α; the proof builds e-values whose FCR under any strict improver exceeds α by ℓ*/(|S|d*) (Appendix A.4). Condition (b) is essential (Remark 6) and the construction re-proves sharpness (Remark 7) [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Section 5.2].
- **Section 6.1 (bounded means).** n = 100 draws of truncated normals on [−1,1], α = 0.1, select i if the Hoeffding p-value 2 exp(−nμ̂_i²/2) ∧ 1 < α; BY interval μ̂ ± √(2 log(2/α)/n), e-CI the generalised Hoeffding interval of Appendix E. For K = 200–1000, e-BY and BY-with-assumptions (independence, R_i^min = |S|) have nearly identical average width (about 1.40–1.45) while BY-without-assumptions is about 1.55–1.60; all FCRs below 0.003 (Figure 3) [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- **Section 6.2 (stopped Brownian motion).** K ∈ {10, 30, 100, 300, 1000}, common drift θ* ∈ {−0.1, −0.01, −0.001}, α = 0.05, c = 2, time step 10^{−5}; neither independent nor PRDS, so BY-wA has no guarantee but is empirically valid; e-BY and BY-wA have FCR approaching α as θ* ↑ 0 and far larger lower endpoints than BY-woA, whose FCR is near 0 (Figure 4) [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- **Section 7 (Twitter A/B tests).** 263 experiments over 1.5 years, each at least two weeks, 15 metrics tracked daily (K = 30, control and treatment separately), selection rule a simplified shipping criterion; e-CIs are the asymptotic CS μ̂_t ± 1.7 σ̂_t √((2 log log(2t) + 0.72 log(5.2/α))/t) of Waudby-Smith et al. 2023 with about 10^6 users on day one, BY uses the running intersection. At α = 0.1 e-BY justifies shipping in 127 experiments versus 122 for BY (a superset), in 1.2 days on average versus 1.5 (Figure 5) [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- **Appendix B (Figure 6).** Fact 4: BH is BY with C_i(α) = Θ_i ∖ Θ_i^0 if p_i ≤ α, else Θ_i. Corollary 3: any self-consistent e-procedure (e_i ≥ K/(α|R|) on R) controls FDR via e-BY with E_i^ind(θ) = e_i 1{θ ∈ Θ_i^0}, so Theorem 2 implies e-BH's FDR theorem but not conversely. Corollary 4: with two-sided e-values e_i = (e_i^+ ∨ e_i^−)/2, e_i^+ = (e_i^−)^{−1}, e-BH plus direction −1 iff e_i^+ ≥ 2K/(α|R_eBH|) controls the directional FDR (mixed directional FDR of BY) at α [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- **Theorem 5 (weighted e-BY, Appendix C).** Weights w_i ≥ 0 with Σ w_i ≤ K and α_i = w_i α|S|/K give FCR ≤ α; also admissible; Ignatiadis, Wang and Ramdas 2023 show the weights may themselves be e-values [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- **Proposition 4 (Appendix D).** If condition (b) is dropped and inputs may be non-strictly decreasing, reporting e-BY at level λα on the event γ(S,C) = (1 + β(S,C))/κ(S,C) ≥ λ still has FCR ≤ α and strictly dominates e-BY; 'not very useful' in practice [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- **Appendix E, Proposition 5 (generalised e-CIs).** C^{α'}(α) = {θ : E(θ, α') < 1/α} fixes a tuning level α'; the Hoeffding version μ̂ ± √(log(2/α)/(2n)) · (log(2/α) + log(2/α'))/(2√(log(2/α') log(2/α))) with α' = α is at least as tight at level α|S|/K as the classical Hoeffding CI at α|S|/(Kℓ_K) whenever |S| ≥ K / exp(2√(log(2/α) log ℓ_K)), a proportion that shrinks as K grows [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- **Appendix F.** Conditional coverage given selection is impossible without knowing the selection rule (Gaussian toy: select if the interval is positive, conditional coverage 0 when θ* ≤ 0); the joint bound P(θ*_i ∉ C_i(α_i), i ∈ S) ≤ α_i motivates FCR; simultaneous-over-selected control (Benjamini, Hechtlinger and Stark 2019) is strictly stronger and for arbitrary selection needs Bonferroni α/K [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].

## Setting and assumptions
- Universe of distributions P, functionals θ_i : P → Θ_i (not necessarily bijective, e.g. medians), data X = (X_1, …, X_K) ∼ P*, marginal CI constructors C_i(α) with P(θ*_i ∉ C_i(α)) ≤ α for every α ∈ [0,1]; selection S = S(X) ⊆ [K] by an arbitrary, possibly unknown and iterative data-dependent rule; FCP uses |S| ∨ 1 in the denominator [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Section 1].
- e-BY needs only that each C_i be an e-CI (strict inequality E(θ) < 1/α matters for admissibility, Section 5.1); no assumption on the joint law of (X_i) or on S; the level α is fixed in advance but Corollary 1 licenses a data-dependent α [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- BY needs independence or PRDS (Benjamini and Yekutieli 2001) for the α|S|/K level, plus R_i^min; otherwise pays ℓ_K; Figure 2 and Table 1 give the decision flowchart: BY only when both dependence and selection are controlled and the CIs are not e-CIs [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].
- Sequential settings: e-CIs at stopping times, continuously monitored confidence sequences and bandit sampling are covered because stopped e-processes are e-values; A/B tests have dependent metrics from the same users and sequential enrolment [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, Sections 2.2, 7].
- Calibration applies to nonincreasing CI constructors continuous from below; any nonincreasing C can be replaced by its limit from below, which is no wider and still valid [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022, after Corollary 2].
- The Twitter CS is asymptotic, not exact, justified by the sample size (Section 7); the sharpness example is 'stylized' and adversarial, with θ* just below 0 (Remark 4) [xuPostselectionInferenceEvalue2022].

## Open questions / gaps
- Whether the BY calibrator is admissible among CI calibrators, and whether it is the only calibrator whose e-BY output is never worse than BY (Remark 2, conjectured).
- In which settings the tightest CIs are e-CIs: Gaussian-type exact CIs are not, while composite and sequential problems often admit only e-CIs (Section 8).
- Proposition 4's improvement lacks a proof that a small λ > 1 preserves FCR when condition (b) is relaxed on constant-interval inputs (Appendix D).
- The sharpness bound is approached only when θ* ↑ 0 from below under an adversarial two-stage design; typical FCR of e-BY is unquantified (Remark 4).
- Works cited here and not in the bib: Benjamini and Yekutieli 2005; Waudby-Smith and Ramdas 2023; Waudby-Smith et al. 2023; Howard et al. 2020; Ignatiadis, Wang and Ramdas 2023; Weinstein and Ramdas 2020; Benjamini, Hechtlinger and Stark 2019; Vovk 1993.
