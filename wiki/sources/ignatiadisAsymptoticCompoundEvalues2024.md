# Asymptotic and compound e-values: multiple testing and empirical Bayes

## Summary
Compound e-values, K nonnegative statistics with Σ_{k ∈ N(P)} E_P[E_k] ≤ K for every P, had been used as a technical device since the e-BH paper and renamed "generalized" or "relaxed" e-values by several authors; this paper makes them, and their approximate and asymptotic relaxations, formal objects (Definitions 2.1, 3.1, 3.5, 3.13, 3.18) and proves that they are exactly the right currency for FDR control [ignatiadisAsymptoticCompoundEvalues2024]. The universality result (Theorem 4.4) states that any procedure controlling FDR at a known level α is reproduced by e-BH applied to the compound e-values E_k = K·1{k ∈ D}/(α(R_D ∨ 1)), tight if the procedure is admissible, so searching over compound e-values loses nothing; since compound e-values are closed under convex combination this yields a general recipe for combining and derandomizing FDR procedures (Appendix C.1). The second half connects the compound constraint to Robbins' compound decision theory: the log-optimal simple separable compound e-value for point nulls P_k° against point alternatives Q_k° is the ratio of the two mixture densities Σ_j q_j°/Σ_j p_j° (Theorem 5.2), precisely Storey's optimal discovery procedure (ODP) statistic. For composite nulls with nuisance parameters the paper proposes localized universal inference (minimise the null likelihood over a confidence set) and compound universal inference (minimise over a localization of the empirical nuisance distribution), both giving (0,δ)-approximate (compound) e-values (Propositions 5.5, 5.6). In the simultaneous t-test model with heterogeneous variances, plugging the nonparametric MLE of the variance distribution into the ODP Bayes factor gives strongly asymptotic compound e-values as K → ∞ with n ≥ 3 fixed (Theorem 6.3), the first type-I guarantee for a data-driven ODP, and in simulations with K = 2000 the method matches the oracle that knows the empirical variance distribution and dominates t-test e-values, universal inference and CUI (Figure 1) [ignatiadisAsymptoticCompoundEvalues2024].

## Main results

### Compound p- and e-values (Section 2)
- Definition 2.1: compound p-variables satisfy Σ_{k: P ∈ P_k} P(P_k ≤ t) ≤ Kt; compound e-variables satisfy Σ_{k: P ∈ P_k} E_P[E_k] ≤ K; tight if the supremum over P equals K. No dependence restriction. Weighted p- or e-values with Σ w_k ≤ K are the first example (Example 2.2); the compound class is strictly larger because the supremum sits outside the sum [ignatiadisAsymptoticCompoundEvalues2024].
- Theorem 2.3: any p-to-e calibrator h maps compound p-variables to compound e-variables (proof via a randomly chosen null index L ∼ Unif(N(P)) and a uniform U, Appendix E); (1/E_k) ∧ 1 goes the other way by Markov.
- Examples 2.4–2.6: P_k/E_k are compound p-variables when independent p-values are divided by compound e-values; convex combinations Σ_ℓ w_ℓ E_k^{(ℓ)} are compound; multiplying by conditionally valid follow-up e-values F_j (F_j = 1 off the selected set) keeps compoundness.
- Terminology: the term appeared first in Ignatiadis, Wang and Ramdas 2024 (Biometrika) with |N(P)| in place of K; "generalized e-values" (Banerjee, Gang, He 2023; Bashari et al. 2023; Zhao, Sun 2024; Lee, Ren 2024) and "relaxed e-values" (Ren, Barber 2024; Gablenz, Sabatti 2024) are the same object; Habiger and Peña 2014's "compound p-values" mean non-separable p-values and are a different notion (Appendix A).

### Approximate and asymptotic e-values (Section 3)
- Definition 3.1: an (ε,δ)-approximate e-variable has E_P[E ∧ t] ≤ 1 + ε_P + δ_P t for all t ≥ 0; approximate p-variable: P(P ≤ t) ≤ (1 + ε_P)t + δ_P. (ε,0) is Wang and Ramdas's 1 + ε inflation; δ = 1 is vacuous.
- Proposition 3.2: for atomless P this is equivalent to the existence of an event A with P(A) ≥ 1 − δ_P and E_P[E 1_A] ≤ 1 + ε_P (the truncation ∧t separates benign multiplicative error from tail events that can make E[E] = ∞). The implication (E1) ⇒ (E2) fails on discrete spaces without external randomization.
- Theorem 3.3 and Proposition 3.4: calibration preserves (ε,δ)-approximation in both directions; an (ε,δ)-approximate variable is (0, δ′)-approximate with δ′ = (ε + δ)/(1 + ε) < ε + δ.
- Definition 3.5: a sequence is asymptotic if ε_n(P), δ_n(P) → 0 for each P; strongly asymptotic if δ_n(P) = 0 eventually; uniformly (strongly) asymptotic if the limits hold in sup over P.
- Proposition 3.6: uniformly strongly asymptotic iff limsup_n sup_P E_P[E^{(n)}] ≤ 1; Proposition 3.7: asymptotic plus uniformly integrable implies strongly asymptotic; Proposition 3.8: convergence in distribution to an e-variable (p-variable) under each P suffices (the Gasparin–Wang–Ramdas 2025 definition is the special case, Remark 3.9); Proposition 3.10: asymptotic p-variables iff limsup_n P(P^{(n)} ≤ t) ≤ t.
- Example 3.11: if Z^{(n)} ⇝ Z with continuous cdf F, then F(Z^{(n)}) is an asymptotic p-variable and h(Z^{(n)}) is an asymptotic e-variable for any monotone or upper semicontinuous h with E[h(Z)] ≤ 1, strongly asymptotic if h is bounded; the p-value half is textbook, the e-value half is new.
- Definition 3.13: (ε,δ)-approximate compound e-variables: for each P an event A with P(A) ≥ 1 − δ_P and Σ_{k ∈ N(P)} E_P[E_k 1_A] ≤ K(1 + ε_P); Proposition 3.15 gives the equivalent truncated-expectation form E_P[(Σ_{k∈N(P)} E_k) ∧ t] ≤ K(1 + ε_P) + δ_P t. Lee and Ren 2024's Monte-Carlo-approximated e-values are (0,δ)-approximate compound e-values.
- Proposition 3.14: per-hypothesis (ε_k,δ_k)-approximate variables are (ε,δ)-approximate compound with ε = K^{-1} Σ_{k∈N} ε_k and δ = Σ_{k∈N} δ_k (the δ's add); Proposition 3.16: calibration with a common calibrator preserves approximate compoundness.
- Definition 3.17 and Appendix B: the weaker *approximate notion Σ_{k∈N} E_P[E_k ∧ t] ≤ K(1 + ε_P + δ_P t) (used implicitly by Ignatiadis and Sen 2025); it does not give approximate FDR control by itself, but Proposition B.3 gives FDR ≤ α(1 + ε) + inf_κ {δ/κ + P(R_D < Kκ)}, so e-BH is asymptotically valid when it makes order-K rejections.
- Definition 3.18: asymptotic compound e-variables along a triangular array indexed by m with n = n(m), K = K(m), max{n,K} → ∞, covering n → ∞ with K fixed, K → ∞ with n fixed (empirical Bayes), and both.

### Universality of e-BH (Section 4, Appendix C)
- Definition 4.1: (ε,δ)-approximate FDR control, E_P[(F_D/R_D) 1_A] ≤ α(1 + ε_P) on an event of probability ≥ 1 − δ_P, which implies FDR ≤ α(1 + ε) + δ, and conversely that bound gives (ε, δ/α)-approximate control; asymptotic FDR control defined along the array.
- Theorem 4.3: e-BH (k*_e = max{k : kE_[k]/K ≥ 1/α}) on compound e-values controls FDR at α; on (ε,δ)-approximate compound e-values it has (ε,δ)-approximate FDR control, hence FDR ≤ α(1 + ε) + δ; on asymptotic compound e-values it has asymptotic FDR control. No dependence assumption; extends the (ε,0) case of Wang and Ramdas.
- Theorem 4.4 (universality): for any procedure D with FDR ≤ α under P, E_k = K V_k/(α(R_D ∨ 1)) are compound e-variables (the sum over nulls equals (K/α)·FDR_D ≤ K) and e-BH on them yields k*_e = R_D, i.e. the same discoveries; if D is admissible (no FDR-α procedure strictly contains it), rescaling by K/K* with K* = sup_P Σ_{k∈N} E_P[E_k] makes them tight (Appendix G). The paper notes that no such statement is known for BH or any other procedure.
- Appendix C.1 (combination and derandomization): run L FDR procedures on subsets S_ℓ at levels α_ℓ, form their implied compound e-values (E_k^{(ℓ)} = 1 off S_ℓ), average with weights w_ℓ (random weights allowed if independent of the e-values with Σ E[w_ℓ] ≤ 1), apply e-BH at α; the α_ℓ do not matter. Recovers Ren and Barber's derandomized knockoffs and Banerjee et al.'s meta-analysis of reported discovery sets, and newly derandomizes the randomized e-BH variants (R1-, R2-, R-, U-e-BH) of Xu and Ramdas 2024 into a procedure that still differs from e-BH.
- Appendix C.2: for p-BH with weights W_k = E_k, the PRDS argument gives FDR ≤ sup_P (α/K) Σ_{k∈N} E_P[W_k], so compound e-values are exactly the random weights under which ep-BH (Ignatiadis, Wang, Ramdas 2024) controls FDR.
- Appendix C.3, Proposition C.2: e-weighted p-merging F(p,E) = inf{α : K^{-1} Σ E_k f_k(p_k/α) ≥ 1} is a valid p-merging function under the global null for compound E independent of P, with f_k allowed to be negative on (1,∞); f(x) = 2 − 2x gives an e-weighted "twice the mean", f(x) = −log x an e-weighted exp(1)·geometric mean.
- Appendix C.4, Proposition C.3: BY on (asymptotic) compound p-values controls FDR (asymptotically) under arbitrary dependence, reproved by calibrating with T(α/(ℓ_K p))/α and applying Theorems 2.3 and 4.3; Armstrong 2022 showed BH itself can fail on independent compound p-values.

### Compound decisions and optimal discovery compound e-values (Section 5, Appendix D)
- Definition 5.1: separable (E_k is X_k-measurable) and simple separable (E_k = f(X_k) with one f). Section 5.1 recalls Robbins 1951: the fundamental theorem of compound decisions equates the compound risk of a simple separable rule under fixed µ with the Bayes risk under the empirical prior K^{-1} Σ δ_{µ_k} (eq. 8), which is why the paper uses the word "compound".
- Theorem 5.2: among simple separable s with s(X_1), …, s(X_K) compound e-values, K^{-1} Σ_k E_{Q_k°}[log s(X_k)] is maximised by s(x) = Σ_j q_j°(x)/Σ_j p_j°(x); the proof reduces to Kelly's single-e-value problem for the mixtures P° = Σ P_k°/K and Q° = Σ Q_k°/K. The optimal value is KL(Q°‖P°) ≤ K^{-1} Σ KL(Q_k°‖P_k°), so per-hypothesis likelihood ratios are better when the nulls are known; the compound version wins when K is large and the P_k° must be learned.
- Proposition 5.3 (Storey 2007): thresholding s maximises expected true discoveries among simple separable procedures with the same expected false discoveries, hence the name "optimal discovery compound e-values". Proposition 5.4: every tight simple separable compound e-value for point nulls has the form (10), WLOG with a common q°.
- Theorem D.1: for any utility U, the optimal simple separable compound e-value is the solution of the single-hypothesis problem for (P°, Q°); worked out for Koning 2025's generalized-mean utilities U_h(s) = (s^h − 1)/h, giving (s^ODP)^{1/(1−h)} normalised by its P°-mean, with a clipped variant.
- Section 5.3: universal inference e-value q°(X_k)/p°_{ψ̂_k}(X_k) with ψ̂_k the null MLE (eq. 11); localized UI restricts the minimisation to a (1 − δ)-confidence set Ψ̂_k(δ) and is a (0,δ)-approximate e-value (Proposition 5.5; the Berger–Boos device for e-values); with a simultaneous confidence set the LUI e-values are (0,δ)-approximate compound. Compound UI (eq. 13) minimises the mixture null ∫ p°_ψ(X_k) dG(ψ) over a (1 − δ)-localization Ĝ(δ) of the empirical nuisance distribution G(ψ) = K^{-1} Σ δ_{ψ_k} and gives (0,δ)-approximate compound e-values (Proposition 5.6).

### Simultaneous t-tests (Section 6) and simulations (Section 7)
- Model: K groups of n iid observations, null µ_k = 0 with unknown variance σ_k² (eq. 15); under normality with σ_k² ∈ [σ̲², σ̄²] (eq. 17), n ≥ 3 fixed, K → ∞.
- Proposition 6.1: the Kolmogorov–Smirnov band Ĝ(δ) = {G : sup_t |K^{-1} Σ 1{σ̂_k² ≤ t} − F_G(t)| ≤ √((1 + log(2/δ))/(2K))} around the scaled-χ² mixture cdf contains G(σ²) with probability ≥ 1 − δ (DKW with Massart's constant and Bretagnolle's non-identically-distributed argument), so CUI is computable by a linear program over a grid of B variances (Remark 6.2).
- Theorem 6.3: with Ĝ the NPMLE of G over the σ̂_k² (Robbins 1950, Kiefer–Wolfowitz 1956, computed as in Koenker–Mizera 2014) and Ĥ any estimate of the effect distribution on a bounded interval converging a.s. to some H*, the plug-in Bayes factors E_k = ∫ p_{λ,σ²}(X_k) d(Ĥ ⊗ Ĝ)/∫ p_{σ²}(X_k) dĜ are strongly asymptotic compound e-variables for n ≥ 3 fixed and K → ∞; a frequentist statement with σ² fixed, H* need not be the truth. Combined with Theorem 4.3 this is the first type-I guarantee for a data-driven ODP (earlier implementations: Storey, Dai, Leek 2007; Guindani et al. 2009; Woo et al. 2011; Noma and Matsui 2012, 2013).
- Theorem 6.5 (no normality, K = 1, n → ∞): E^{(n)} = exp(λ√n X̄/S − λ²/2) and its two-sided mixture are strongly asymptotic e-variables for the null E[X] = 0, var ∈ (0,∞) (asymptotic if σ̂ replaces S), grow to infinity under alternatives with finite variance, and so does any compactly supported λ-mixture; the authors know no nonasymptotic e-variable for this null other than constants.
- Proposition 6.6: E_k^{(n)} = K (S_k^{(n)})²/Σ_j (σ̂_j^{(n)})² (the Biometrika paper's construction, now without normality) are asymptotic compound e-variables under a 2(1+δ)-moment bound and strongly asymptotic under normality, with max{n,K} → ∞ in any combination.
- Section 7: K = 2000, 200 non-nulls at λ = ξ/√n, ξ ∈ [2,6], n ∈ {5,10}, σ_k² = 1 or Unif[0.5,2], e-BH at α = 0.1, 200 replications, B = 600 log-spaced grid, σ̲² = 10⁻³, σ̄² = 10³. All methods control FDR; EB is indistinguishable from EB-Oracle (knows G(σ²)), below z-Oracle (knows every σ_k²) only when variances vary, and far above CUI, which in turn beats the scale-invariant t-test e-value of Wang and Ramdas 2025 and universal inference (Figure 1). Code: github.com/nignatiadis/compound-evalues-paper.

## Setting and assumptions
- Fixed K hypotheses given by sets P_k, null index set N(P), a universe P of distributions (WLOG P ⊆ ∪ P_k); arbitrary dependence throughout [ignatiadisAsymptoticCompoundEvalues2024].
- Atomless P (a uniform variable can be simulated) is assumed in Sections 3.3–4 to state approximate notions through events A; external randomization substitutes otherwise.
- No sequential element and no optional stopping; Example 2.6 is the only two-stage construction (follow-up e-values conditionally valid given the first stage).
- Section 5 needs sequence models (data split into X_k, each hypothesis about the k-th marginal) with point nulls dominated by a common ν, or composite nulls parametrised by nuisance ψ_k with a common alternative density q°; Section 6 additionally needs normality, variance bounds and n ≥ 3 for Theorem 6.3, or 2(1+δ) moments for Proposition 6.6.
- Admissibility in Theorem 4.4 is set inclusion among FDR-α procedures.

## Open questions / gaps
- Universality for BH: Theorem 4.4 represents the discoveries of any FDR procedure, but no analogous statement is known for BH or any p-value procedure as a map from p-values (Section 4).
- Tightness beyond admissibility: Appendix G rescales by K/K*, which requires knowing K* = sup_P Σ_{k∈N} E_P[E_k]; no constructive route to tight compound e-values for a general FDR procedure.
- Power of UI versus LUI/CUI: UI and CUI in the simulation estimate the numerator from the data and are "not fully justified" (footnote 9); a clean finite-sample version of CUI with data-driven alternatives is open.
- Nonasymptotic e-values for the mean-zero, finite-variance null of Section 6.2.1 are not known beyond constants (Theorem 6.5 discussion); cf. the bounded or sub-Gaussian nulls elsewhere in the wiki.
- Rates: Theorem 6.3 is a limit statement (ε_K → 0) with no rate for how fast the NPMLE plug-in reaches compoundness; the e-Closure paper's Remark 9 frames the resulting error-rate convergence as E[e_N] → 1.
- The *approximate (Definition 3.17) notion only gives FDR control in a many-rejections regime (Proposition B.3); whether Ignatiadis and Sen 2025's procedures satisfy the stronger Definition 3.13 is not settled here.
- Theorem D.1 transfers Koning 2025's utility-optimal e-values to the compound setting but gives no data-driven version.
