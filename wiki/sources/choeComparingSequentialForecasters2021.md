## Summary
Two forecasters issue probability forecasts p_t, q_t for a sequence of outcomes y_t, Reality chooses the conditional law r_t of y_t possibly after seeing both forecasts, and a statistician wants to know which forecaster has done better according to a scoring rule S, without assuming anything about how forecasts or outcomes were generated [choeComparingSequentialForecasters2021, Game 1, Sec. 4.1]. The estimand is the time-varying average score differential Δ_t = t^{-1} Σ_{i≤t} E_{i−1}[S(p_i,y_i) − S(q_i,y_i)], whose empirical version Δ̂_t makes S_t = t(Δ̂_t − Δ_t) a martingale [eqs 8–10]. The paper turns the sub-ψ machinery of Howard et al. into confidence sequences for Δ_t: a Hoeffding-style sequence under conditional sub-Gaussianity [Thm 1] and the main empirical-Bernstein sequence Δ̂_t ± u(V̂_t)/t with V̂_t = Σ(δ̂_i − γ_i)² for any predictable centring γ_i, valid for any bounded score [Thm 2]. The exponential test supermartingale behind Theorem 2 is lower-bounded, under the weak null H_0^w : Δ_t ≤ 0 for all t, by the empirical process exp{λ Σ δ̂_i − ψ_E(λ) V̂_t}, which is therefore an e-process; its gamma-exponential mixture has a closed form [Thm 3, Prop. 3]. The weak null ("no better on average") is contrasted with the strong null δ_t ≤ 0 for all t of Henzi and Ziegel, which is easier to reject and whose rejection says only that one forecaster was better at some time [Sec. 4.4]. Applications: ten seasons of Major League Baseball (T = 25,165), where Vegas closing odds marginally beat FiveThirtyEight (95% EB CS for Δ_T is (−0.00265, −0.00062), e-value 2979 against "Vegas no better"), and ECMWF precipitation post-processing at four airports, where the weak-null e-processes are large only where Henzi–Ziegel's strong-null e-processes are also large but not conversely [Secs 5.2–5.3]. Extensions handle unbounded scores through asymptotic confidence sequences and the Winkler score, lag-h forecasts through sequential e-values merged into p-processes and calibrated back to e-processes, and predictable subsequences and bounds [Apps C–F].

## Main results

### Setting (Sections 3–4.2)
- **Game 1**: forecasters move (in any order, with any side information), Reality picks r_t ∈ Δ(Y) possibly adversarially after seeing p_t and q_t, y_t ~ r_t is revealed. Observable filtration F (forecasts and outcomes) is nested in the oracle game filtration G in which (p_t), (q_t), (r_t) are predictable and (y_t) adapted; all expectations E_{t−1} are conditional on G_{t−1} [choeComparingSequentialForecasters2021, Secs 4.1–4.2]. The setup is prequential: only forecasts actually made and outcomes actually realised are judged [Sec. 4.1, citing Dawid 1984].
- Scoring rules are positively oriented; examples Brier 1 − (p−y)², spherical, logarithmic, zero-one; pointwise differentials δ̂_i = S(p_i,y_i) − S(q_i,y_i), δ_i = E_{i−1}[δ̂_i]; S_t = Σ(δ̂_i − δ_i) = t(Δ̂_t − Δ_t) is a G-martingale [Sec. 3.2, eqs 8–10].
- **Table 2** places the method against Diebold–Mariano, Giacomini–White, Lai–Gross–Shen and Henzi–Ziegel: only the last two are anytime-valid, nonasymptotic and distribution-free, and only this paper tests the weak null and gives a confidence sequence.

### Confidence sequences (Section 4.3)
- Sub-ψ framework: (S_t) is sub-ψ with variance process (V̂_t) if L_t(λ) = exp{λS_t − ψ(λ)V̂_t} is a test supermartingale for each λ ∈ [0, λ_max); used with ψ_{N,c}(λ) = c²λ²/2 and ψ_{E,c}(λ) = c^{−2}(−log(1−cλ) − cλ) on [0, 1/c); a sub-ψ uniform boundary u satisfies P(∀t : S_t ≤ u(V̂_t)) ≥ 1 − α [Sec. 4.3.1, eqs 11–12].
- **Theorem 1 (Hoeffding-style CS)**: if δ̂_i is conditionally c-sub-Gaussian, C^H_t = Δ̂_t ± u(t)/t is a (1−α)-CS for Δ_t for any sub-Gaussian boundary u_{α/2,c} (or two-sided u_{α,c}); V̂_t = t [eq. 14].
- **Theorem 2 (empirical-Bernstein CS, main result)**: if |δ̂_i| ≤ c/2 and (γ_i) is any [−c/2, c/2]-valued predictable sequence, C^{EB}_t = Δ̂_t ± u(V̂_t)/t with V̂_t = Σ_{i≤t}(δ̂_i − γ_i)² is a (1−α)-CS for any sub-exponential boundary u_{α/2,c}; default γ_i = Δ̂_{i−1}; an extension of Theorem 4 of Howard et al. 2021 [eq. 15, proof App. A.2]. Covers Brier, spherical, zero-one and ε-truncated logarithmic scores (the last improper but still valid) and bounded continuous outcomes [Sec. 4.3.2–4.3.3].
- **Proposition 1 (App. A.1)**: for any process X_i with conditional means μ_i, predictable (γ_i) and predictable positive (c_i) with X_i − γ_i ≥ −c_i, L_t(λ) = Π exp{λ(X_i − μ_i) − ψ_{E,c_i}(λ)(X_i − γ_i)²} is a test supermartingale for λ ∈ [0, 1/sup c_i); proof via Fan–Grama–Liu exp{λξ − ψ_E(λ)ξ²} ≤ 1 + λξ for ξ ≥ −c, then 1 − x ≤ e^{−x}. Extends Lemma 3(e) of Howard et al. 2020 with predictable bounds [eqs 27–32].
- **Boundaries (Sec. 4.3.4, Table 3)**: conjugate-mixture boundary u^{CM}_α(v) = sup{s : m(s,v) < 1/α} with m(s,v) = ∫exp{λs − ψ(λ)v}dF(λ) (Lemma 2 of Howard et al. 2021), Robbins' two-sided normal mixture √((v+ρ)log((v+ρ)/(α²ρ))) for Theorem 1, gamma-exponential mixture for Theorem 2 via a root finder; polynomial stitching as a closed-form alternative (eq. 53 gives the explicit 95% EB CS with |δ̂_i| ≤ 1, LIL rate √(v log log v)). The CM boundary grows as √(v log v) yet is tighter in practice and unimprovable in the sub-Gaussian case [eqs 16–17, App. B.2]. The mixture hyperparameter ρ is tied to the intrinsic time v_opt at which width is optimised via ρ = −v_opt(W_{−1}(−α²/e) + 1); default v_opt = 10 (100 for MLB) from an IID Beta(30,10) − Beta(10,30) tuning study [App. B.1, App. I.4, Fig. 14].

### E-processes and p-processes (Section 4.4)
- **Weak vs strong null**: H_0^w(p,q): Δ_t ≤ 0 ∀t (composite, all joint laws on G with that property) versus Henzi–Ziegel's strong H_0^s(p,q): δ_t ≤ 0 ∀t; H_0^s ⊂ H_0^w. Example (22): δ_t = +0.1 every k-th step, −1 otherwise, falsifies the strong null although q is clearly better [eqs 18, 21–22].
- **Corollary 1**: reject H_0^w(p,q) when L_t > 0 and H_0^w(q,p) when U_t < 0 from a (1−α)-CS; the two one-sided tests each have level α/2 and sum to at most α; a Robbins power-one stopping rule [eqs 19–20]. Testing both is not the same as testing Δ_t = 0 ∀t.
- **Theorem 3 (sub-exponential e-process for H_0^w)**: under the conditions of Theorem 2, E_t(λ) = exp{λ Σ_{i≤t} δ̂_i − ψ_{E,c}(λ)V̂_t} is an e-process for H_0^w(p,q) for each λ ∈ [0,1/c), and so is any mixture E^mix_t = ∫E_t(λ)dF(λ). Proof: under the null exp{−λΣδ_i} ≥ 1, so E_t(λ) ≤ L_t(λ), the test supermartingale of Proposition 1; domination by a P-dependent test supermartingale is equivalent to being an e-process [eq. 24, App. A.3, citing Corollary 22 of ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022]. Proposition 2 is the one-sided generalisation needing only δ̂_i ≥ −c/2 and γ_i truncated above [eq. 38].
- **Proposition 3 (App. B.1)**: closed form of the gamma-exponential mixture m(s,v) with reparametrised Gamma density f_ρ(λ) ∝ (1−λ)^{ρ−1}e^{−ρ(1−λ)} on [0,1/c): an incomplete-gamma expression when cs + v + ρ > 0, and m(s,v) ≤ C(ρ)e^{−ρ}/(v+ρ) ≤ 1 otherwise (c = 1 scaling), so the mixture e-process is below one whenever the summed differentials strongly support the null [eqs 41–44].
- **P-processes**: p_t = 1/sup_{i≤t}E_i is a p-process (P(p_τ ≤ α) ≤ α at any stopping time); alternatively the smallest α for which the (1−α)-CS excludes zero [eqs 25–26]. The e-process crossing 2/α coincides with the CS excluding zero at level α [Fig. 1, Fig. 3].

### Experiments (Section 5, App. H–I)
- Simulations with a non-IID Reality with sharp changepoints (T = 10^4, five regimes): EB CS tighter than Hoeffding, slightly looser than the asymptotic CS, cumulative miscoverage exactly zero for the nonasymptotic CSs while the fixed-time CI of Lai et al. 2011 miscovers increasingly; the e-process for Δ_t ≤ 0 stays below 1, grows during t ∈ (2000, 6000) and recedes afterwards [Figs 3–4]. Diebold–Mariano and Giacomini–White p-values exceed cumulative type-I error 0.05 after roughly 100 and 1000 steps even with their assumptions met; the paper's and Henzi–Ziegel's p-processes need comparable sample sizes to detect a late changepoint [App. H.2, Figs 7–8].
- **MLB 2010–2019** (T = 25,165; forecasters 538, vegas, constant, laplace, k29): all other forecasters lose to vegas under Brier (e-values > 10^4 except 538 at 2979) and under the Winkler-logarithmic skill score with one-sided upper bounds [Table 4, Fig. 11].
- **Weather** (ECMWF 24-hour precipitation 2007–2017, Brussels, Frankfurt, London, Zurich; IDR vs HCLR vs HCLR_; 90% CS, threshold 2/α = 20): IDR beats both HCLR variants in Brussels, HCLR beats HCLR_ in Frankfurt and Zurich; strong-null evidence is present wherever weak-null evidence is but not conversely (IDR vs HCLR_ in Frankfurt has only strong-null evidence) [Fig. 5, Sec. 5.3].

### Extensions (Appendices C–G)
- **Asymptotic CS** of Waudby-Smith et al. 2021 adapted to Δ_t under Lyapunov-type conditions (a.s. convergent average conditional variance, q > 2 moments, consistent variance estimate): Δ̂_t ± √(2(tσ̂²_tρ² + 1)/(t²ρ²) log(√(tσ̂²_tρ² + 1)/α)), approximation rate o(√(V_t log V_t)/t) [eq. 55].
- **Winkler score** w(p,q,y) = (S(p,y) − S(q,y))/(S(p,1(p>q)) − S(q,1(p>q))), equal to 1 when y = 1(p > q) and ≤ 0 otherwise for proper S (Lemma 1), hence bounded above by 1 but unbounded below; **Proposition 4** gives a one-sided EB CS (−∞, Ŵ_t + u_α(V̂_t)/t] ∩ (−∞, 1] and an e-process exp{−λtŴ_t − ψ_{E,2}(λ)V̂_t} for H_0^{ww,≥}: W_t ≥ 0 ∀t, valid for any proper score including the logarithmic; Corollary 2 is the two-sided version when the baseline is bounded away from 0 and 1 (c = 2/q_0 for Brier) [eqs 57–66]. The Winkler increment is exactly the increment in Henzi–Ziegel's product e-process, so that e-process bets on relative skill [App. H.1, eq. 115].
- **Lag-h forecasts**: the naive E_t(λ) is not even adapted; the analogue satisfies only E_{t−h}[E_t] ≤ 1 (a sequential e-value at lag h, Arnold–Henzi–Ziegel 2023). **Proposition 5**: for the period-wise weak null H_0^{pw}(h) (Δ^{[k]}_t ≤ 0 for each residue class k), the average of the h sub-filtration processes satisfies E[Ē_{τ+h−1}] ≤ 1, the harmonic-mean merge p^{pw}_t = h e log h / Σ_k (1/p^{[k]}_t) is a p-process, and f(p^{pw}_t) with the calibrator f(p) = (1 − p + p log p)/(p(log p)²) is an e-process. **Proposition 6**: for the lag-h weak null, use the minimum over k for e and the maximum for p [eqs 68–95]. Table 5: on the weather data with lags 1–5, Ē^w is very conservative, Ē^{pw} remains powerful, and Ē^s is generally large whenever Ē^{pw} is.
- **Predictable subsequences and bounds** (App. F): restricting to times with ξ_t = 1 (predictable) keeps both CS and e-process valid [eqs 96–102]; with predictable bounds |δ̂_i| ≤ c_i/2 the e-process Π exp{λδ̂_i − ψ_{E,c_i}(λ)(δ̂_i − γ_i)²} is valid because ψ_{E,c}(λ) is nondecreasing and convex in c (Lemma 2, **Proposition 7**) [eqs 108–111].
- **Other outcome types** (App. G, Table 6): categorical outcomes with C-dimensional Brier, continuous outcomes with interval score or CRPS, all under the bounded-differential class P(c) [eq. 114].

## Setting and assumptions
- Null: composite weak null H_0^w(p,q): Δ_t ≤ 0 for all t (all joint laws on the game filtration with this property); strong null δ_t ≤ 0 for all t discussed for contrast [choeComparingSequentialForecasters2021, eqs 18, 21].
- Data model: none beyond bounded (Theorems 2–3) or conditionally sub-Gaussian (Theorem 1) pointwise score differentials; Reality may be adversarial and non-stationary; forecasts may use arbitrary side information [Sec. 4.1]. Lagged forecasts need the sub-filtration construction of App. E.
- Optional stopping: all guarantees are time-uniform, hence valid at arbitrary stopping times, with continuous monitoring or a single look [eqs 2–3, Cor. 1]; power-one stopping rules only stop on rejection.
- Conclusions are descriptive, not predictive: a large e-value says p has done better than q up to τ on average; extrapolation needs extra assumptions, which the authors leave to the practitioner [Sec. 6].

## Open questions / gaps
- A tight confidence sequence for the lag-h average score differential Δ^{(h)}_t and a more powerful lag-h e-process are stated as open; the weak-null lag-h e-process is very conservative in practice [choeComparingSequentialForecasters2021, Sec. 6, App. E, Table 5].
- Powerful choices of the mixture F in the lagged scenario, and how aggressively to bet, are left open [App. E].
- A fully general anytime-valid procedure for unbounded outcome domains and unbounded scoring rules is open; the Winkler route does not extend to categorical outcomes [App. G].
- Extrapolation from past to future performance requires distributional assumptions the method deliberately avoids [Sec. 6].
- The IID tuning study picks v_opt = 10 by inspection; no principled selection rule is given [App. I.4].
