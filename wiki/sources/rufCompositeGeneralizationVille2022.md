# A composite generalization of Ville's martingale theorem using e-processes

arXiv 2203.04485v3, 21 pages, typeset in EJP style (the header still carries the journal's placeholder volume and dates); the published version is Electronic Journal of Probability 2023. Cited by other sources in this wiki as "Ruf et al. 2022" or "Ruf, Larsson, Koolen and Ramdas 2022".

## Summary
Ville's theorem says that, on the filtered space of coin flips, an event A has P(A) = 0 if and only if some nonnegative P-martingale started at one diverges to infinity on A; the paper replaces the single P by an arbitrary family P of laws on a general filtered space and asks what must replace "null set" and "martingale" [rufCompositeGeneralizationVille2022, Sec. 1]. The answers are, respectively, a new outer measure ν(A) = inf_{τ : A ⊆ {τ<∞}} sup_{P∈P} P(τ < ∞), called the inverse capital measure, which is in general strictly larger than the "maximum likelihood" outer measure µ(A) = sup_P P(A) (polar sets are not enough), and the e-process, a nonnegative adapted process with E_P[E_τ] ≤ 1 for every P ∈ P and stopping time τ [Sec. 2, eq. 1.2]. The composite Ville theorem (Theorem 3.1) states that ν(A) = 0 iff some P-e-process tends to infinity on A, with an explicit witness E_t = Σ_n 1{τ_n ≤ t} built from stopping times τ_n with sup_P P(τ_n < ∞) ≤ 2^{−n}; the positive-measure version (Theorem 3.7, Corollary 3.10) identifies 1/ν(A) with the supremal level an e-process started at one can reach on A, and Example 3.9 shows that, unlike the singleton case, the supremum need not be attained [Sec. 3]. To exhibit a non-trivial ν-null event the paper proves an L¹-type line-crossing inequality for i.i.d. sums with only a first moment (Theorem 4.1, an analogue of the L² Dubins–Savage inequality) and derives a composite strong law of large numbers: for any family of i.i.d. laws with a centred uniform-integrability condition, the divergence event of the running mean has ν = 0, so an explicit e-process explodes on every erratic sequence and a level-α sequential test for "the empirical mean converges" exists (Theorem 4.3, Remark 4.4) [Sec. 4]. Finally, whenever the family contains all i.i.d. Bernoulli laws, the only nonnegative composite martingales are constants and the only supermartingales are decreasing, so no (super)martingale can witness the composite SLLN while an e-process does; this is the paper's evidence that the e-process is the organic composite counterpart of Ville's martingale [Sec. 4.3, Sec. 5].

## Main results
- **Definition of ν (eq. 2.1)**: ν(A) = inf over stopping times τ with A ⊆ {τ < ∞} of sup_{P∈P} P(τ < ∞); for α > ν(A) there is a level-α sequential test for the null P that is guaranteed to reject when A occurs [rufCompositeGeneralizationVille2022, Sec. 2].
- **Lemma 2.1**: ν is monotone, countably subadditive, and equals P(A) when P = {P} (proof of the singleton case via a Carathéodory cover by finite-time events); hence ν is an outer measure. µ(A) = sup_P P(A) ≤ ν(A), with equality for finite-time events {τ < ∞} and for constant filtrations; larger filtrations make ν smaller and admit more e-processes (Remark 2.3).
- **Example 2.2**: on binary sequences with P_n = ½δ_{0^∞} + ½δ_{0^n 1 0^∞}, the all-zeros event has µ(A) = ½ but ν(A) = 1, while ν(A^c) = ½; ν-null sets can be strictly fewer than polar sets.
- **Remark 2.4**: submodularity of µ is inherited by ν (then the Choquet integral ∫ f dν = max over finitely additive P ≤ ν); µ itself need not be submodular (four-point counterexample).
- **Proposition 2.6 (truncated Cauchy)**: for P = {law of Cauchy truncated at ±a : a > 0}, the divergence event A_div of the running mean has µ(A_div) = 0 (each P_a has a mean, so the SLLN holds) but ν(A_div) = 1 = ν(A_div^c): every stopping time that catches divergence is fooled, uniformly over a, by the untruncated Cauchy law.
- **Theorem 3.1 (composite Ville's theorem)**: ν(A) = 0 iff there is a P-e-process (E_t) with lim_t E_t = ∞ on A. Forward direction: E_t = Σ_n 1{τ_n ≤ t} with sup_P P(τ_n < ∞) ≤ 2^{−n}, so E_P[E_τ] ≤ Σ_n P(τ_n < ∞) ≤ 1; converse: τ = inf{t : E_t ≥ a} gives P(τ < ∞) ≤ (1/a) E_P[E_τ] ≤ 1/a.
- **Remark 3.2**: in the singleton case the witness can be made a P-martingale, E_t = Σ_n P(τ_n < ∞ | F_t), provided versions equal to one identically (not just a.s.) on {τ_n ≤ t} are chosen; this recovers Ville [villeEtudeCritiqueNotion1939] and Shafer–Vovk 2001 Ch. 8.5.
- **Remark 3.3**: the theorem is false with µ in place of ν (Proposition 2.6 gives the counterexample). **Remark 3.4**: nonnegative P-supermartingales do not suffice in general; when P is mutually locally absolutely continuous and fork-convex the witness can be made a supermartingale via composite Snell envelopes (Ramdas, Ruf, Larsson and Koolen 2021, Sec. 3). **Remark 3.5**: countable unions of ν-null sets are witnessed by mixtures Σ w_n E^n. **Remark 3.6**: an e-process with sup_t E_t = ∞ on A can be converted into one with lim_t E_t = ∞ on A by the one-jump construction at crossing times of 2^n.
- **Section 3.2 (sub-Gaussian witnesses)**: for conditionally 1-sub-Gaussian zero-mean laws, the Robbins–Siegmund normal-mixture supermartingale M_t = exp(t² X̄_t²/(2(t+1)))/√(t+1) is unbounded on {X̄_t ↛ 0}, so ν of that event is zero (Case 1); for conditional means ≤ 0, the closed-form lower bound N_t = 2 exp(t²X̄_t²/(t+1)) Φ(2tX̄_t/√(t+1))/√(t+1) of the one-sided mixture is an e-process but not a supermartingale and is unbounded on {lim sup X̄_t > 0} (Case 2); for cumulative means Σ_{s≤t} a_s ≤ 0 (a class P† strictly larger than Case 2) exp(λΣX_i − λ²t/2) is no longer a supermartingale but is an e-process for λ ≥ 0, dominated for each P by exp(λΣ(X_s − a_s) − λ²t/2), so N_t still witnesses ν({lim sup X̄_t > 0}) = 0 (Case 3).
- **Theorem 3.7 (positive measure)**: (1) for every ε > 0 there is an e-process with lim_t E_t = 1/(ν(A) + ε) on A, namely the one-jump process 1{τ_ε ≤ t}/(ν(A) + ε); (2) if an e-process has sup_t E_t ≥ c on A then ν(A) ≤ 1/c. **Remark 3.8**: for the truncated Cauchy family neither divergence nor convergence of the mean can be refuted with uniform power.
- **Example 3.9 (no single optimal witness)**: a two-element family {P_1, P_2} on coin flips (first flip fair; thereafter i.i.d. fair or with success probabilities 2^{−t}, depending on X_1, with the roles swapped between P_1 and P_2) and A = {X_t = 1 infinitely often} has ν(A) = ½, yet any e-process with sup_t E_t ≥ c on A has c < 2; the Doob martingale P(A | F_t)/P(A), which attains 1/ν(A) by Lévy's zero-one law in the singleton case, has no composite analogue.
- **Corollary 3.10 and Lemma 3.11 (pricing)**: ν(A) = inf{c ∈ (0,1] : lim inf_t cE_t ≥ 1 on A} = inf{c : sup_t cE_t ≥ 1 on A} over all P-e-processes (composite analogue of Shafer–Vovk 2001 Prop. 8.13), and ν(A) = inf over nonnegative processes M with sup_t M_t ≥ 1_A of sup_P sup_{ρ∈T} E_P[M_ρ], the smallest initial capital that super-replicates 1_A; hence the name (one could also say "initial capital measure") [Sec. 3.5].
- **Theorem 4.1 (L¹ line-crossing inequality)**: for i.i.d. X_t with E|X_1| < ∞, E X_1 = 0 and tail function r(K) = E[|X_1| 1{|X_1| > K}], for all ε, γ > 0 and K ≥ 1, P(sup_t |Σ_{s≤t} X_s|/(γ + t) > ε + r(K)) ≤ (8K²/(γε²) + 16/ε² + 2) r(K); proof splits X_s into a bounded part (Dubins–Savage), a middle part with |X_s| ∈ (K, s−1] (Kronecker-type summation by parts plus Kolmogorov's maximal inequality) and a tail part (union bound, Σ_n P(ξ > n−1) ≤ P(ξ > 0) + Eξ). **Corollary 4.2**: with K = γ^{1/3}, P(sup_t |Σ X_s|/(γ+t) > 2ε) ≤ (γ^{−1/3} + 8/ε² + 16/ε² + 2) r(γ^{1/3}) and P(|X̄_k| > ε) ≤ (128/ε²) k^{−1/3} + (256/ε² + 2) r(k^{1/3}) for k with r(k^{1/3}) ≤ ε. Constants are not optimised.
- **Theorem 4.3 (composite SLLN)**: if P is a family of i.i.d. laws with E_P|X_1| < ∞ and the centred uniform-integrability condition lim_{K→∞} sup_P E_P[|X_1 − E_P X_1| 1{|X_1 − E_P X_1| > K}] = 0 (eq. 4.11), then ν(A_div) = 0 for A_div = {lim_t X̄_t does not exist}. Proof: A_div ⊆ ∪_n A_n with A_n = {lim sup X̄ − lim inf X̄ > 2/n}, A_n ⊆ {τ_{k,n} < ∞} with τ_{k,n} = inf{t : |X̄_{k+t} − X̄_k| > 1/n}, and sup_P P(τ_{k,n} < ∞) ≤ (4352n² + 4) k^{−1/3} + sup_P E_P[|X_1 − E_P X_1| 1{… > k^{1/3}}] → 0 by Corollary 4.2. Classical SLLNs give µ(A_div) = 0 ("convergence occurs"); ν(A_div) = 0 adds "divergence is detectable in finite time with uniform type-I error".
- Condition (4.11) holds for location families of any density with finite first moment and is equivalent to ordinary uniform integrability of the centred family (de la Vallée-Poussin); the truncated Cauchy family violates it and has ν(A_div) = 1, so some such condition is necessary, although the exact necessary condition is left open.
- **Remark 4.4**: the explicit witness is E_t = Σ_n 1{τ_{k_n,n} ≤ t} with k_n ≫ n⁶ 2^{3n} chosen so that the bound above is ≤ 2^{−n}; a game-theoretic composite SLLN for unbounded observations with only a first moment, in contrast with the bounded-observation pointwise SLLNs of Shafer–Vovk 2001 and Kumon–Takemura 2008. **Remark 4.5**: Chung's 1951 uniform SLLN (Shorack Thm 12.1) can replace Theorem 4.1 in the proof, and (4.11) already implies Chung's hypothesis after centring.
- **Section 4.3 (insufficiency of (super)martingales)**: for P^{01} = {Ber(p)^∞ : p ∈ [0,1]} the only nonnegative P^{01}-martingales are constants and the only nonnegative supermartingales are decreasing (Ramdas, Ruf, Larsson and Koolen 2021); since A_div contains binary sequences, for any P ⊇ P^{01} on R^N no nonnegative P-(super)martingale can grow to infinity on A_div, while the e-process of Remark 4.4 does. E-processes are therefore "not only sufficient but also (in some sense) necessary" for the composite SLLN.
- **Section 5**: e-processes are "an equally fundamental concept for dealing with composite nulls (as martingales are for point nulls)"; future work: composite game-theoretic laws of the iterated logarithm (Miyabe–Takemura 2013, Sasai–Miyabe–Takemura 2019); closing quotation from Ville's 1984–85 recollections (Crépel 2009) on defining martingales that tend to infinity on null sets.

## Setting and assumptions
- Filtered measurable space (Ω, F, (F_t)_{t∈N_0}) with F = σ(∪_t F_t); P an arbitrary set of probability measures on F; T the set of all stopping times, including infinite ones. No dominating measure, no i.i.d. structure and no measurability of A are assumed for Theorems 3.1 and 3.7 (A ⊆ Ω is arbitrary). ν depends on the filtration as well as on P (Remark 2.3); the filtration is the natural one in all examples.
- E-process definition: nonnegative, adapted, E_P[E_τ] ≤ 1 for every P ∈ P and every stopping time τ, with E_∞ = lim sup_t E_t (eq. 1.2); the equivalent "dominated by a P-martingale started at one for each P" form is quoted from [ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022], and Ville's inequality for e-processes follows from it. Witnesses are required to explode everywhere on A, not almost everywhere, which is why versions of conditional probabilities matter in Remark 3.2.
- Optional stopping is built in: ν(A) is phrased through stopping times, the sequential test "reject at τ" has level sup_P P(τ < ∞), and the e-process tests 1{E_t ≥ 1/α} are level-α sequential tests (Sec. 2, Sec. 4.3). No alternative hypothesis and no optimality criterion against alternatives appear; "power" means explosion on A.
- Section 3.2 uses conditionally σ-sub-Gaussian increments with predictable conditional means (zero, nonpositive, or nonpositive in cumulative sum). Theorem 4.1 needs i.i.d. increments with a finite first moment only; Theorem 4.3 needs i.i.d. laws under each P plus the centred uniform-integrability condition (4.11), which fails for the truncated Cauchy family.
- Witness e-processes of Theorems 3.1 and 3.7 are nondecreasing step functions in time, so they are e-processes but typically neither martingales nor supermartingales; the sub-Gaussian lower bound N_t of Section 3.2 is likewise an e-process dominated by a supermartingale.

## Relation to other sources
- [villeEtudeCritiqueNotion1939](villeEtudeCritiqueNotion1939.md): the theorem being generalised (Chapter IV Theorem 1: every null set is detected by a martingale) and the inequality (1.1) proved in the same thesis; Remark 3.2 shows the composite witness collapses to a P-martingale in the singleton case with the same care over versions that Ville and Shafer–Vovk 2001 Ch. 8.5 take; the paper closes with Ville's own description of his programme.
- [ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022](ramdasAdmissibleAnytimevalidSequential2022.md): supplies the e-process definition and its equivalence to domination by a P-martingale for each P (Lemma 6 there, cited as [17]); that paper proves e-processes are infima of pointwise martingales and admissible ones must be so, this paper proves that the composite Ville theorem, the composite analogue of Lévy's zero-one law (Example 3.9), and the composite SLLN force e-processes rather than supermartingales. The two papers are complementary: admissibility characterises the best e-process for a given null; the composite Ville theorem characterises which events any e-process can detect at all, via ν. The sub-Gaussian trichotomy of Section 3.2 (zero mean, nonpositive mean, nonpositive cumulative mean) is the same trichotomy as Section 5.2 of the admissibility paper, where exp(ΣX_i − t/2) is additionally shown admissible.
- [ramdasTestingExchangeabilityForkconvexity2021](ramdasTestingExchangeabilityForkconvexity2021.md) (testing exchangeability, fork-convexity; ingested in the same batch): source of the facts that nonnegative P^{01}-martingales are constant and supermartingales decreasing (Sec. 4.3), of the fork-convexity condition under which supermartingale witnesses exist (Remark 3.4), and of the parallel-games interpretation of e-processes (Sec. 3.5).
- [ramdasGametheoreticStatisticsSafe2023](ramdasGametheoreticStatisticsSafe2023.md): the survey states Ville's theorem and cites this paper for the composite version requiring e-processes (Sec. 2.5) and for the existence hierarchy of martingales, supermartingales and e-processes (Sec. 5, Sec. 8.2.1); this paper in turn cites the survey for the SAVI programme.

## Concepts touched
- [Ville's inequality](../concepts/villes-inequality.md): Ville's theorem as distinct from Ville's inequality; the composite theorem (Theorem 3.1), its positive-measure form (Theorem 3.7) and the failure of attainment at 1/ν(A) (Example 3.9); Ville's inequality for e-processes as a consequence of martingale domination (Sec. 1).
- [E-process](../concepts/e-process.md): the composite Ville theorem as an independent justification of the e-process concept (Theorem 3.1, Sec. 5); explicit nondecreasing step-function e-processes as witnesses (eq. 3.1, Remarks 3.5–3.6); nonpositive-cumulative-mean sub-Gaussian e-processes that are not supermartingales (Sec. 3.2, Case 3); the composite SLLN e-process that no (super)martingale can replace (Remark 4.4, Sec. 4.3); parallel-games interpretation (Sec. 3.5).
- [Test martingale](../concepts/test-martingale.md): singleton witnesses are martingales Σ_n P(τ_n < ∞ | F_t) (Remark 3.2); for families containing all i.i.d. Bernoulli laws the only nonnegative composite martingales are constants and the only supermartingales decreasing (Sec. 4.3); fork-convexity as the condition for supermartingale witnesses (Remark 3.4).
- [Confidence sequence boundaries](../concepts/confidence-sequence-boundaries.md): the normal-mixture supermartingale and the closed-form one-sided mixture lower bound N_t as explosion witnesses for sub-Gaussian means, with the trichotomy of null classes (Sec. 3.2).
- [Testing by betting](../concepts/testing-by-betting.md): ν as the cheapest initial capital that super-replicates 1_A and 1/ν(A) as the maximal wealth an e-process can reach on A (Corollary 3.10, Lemma 3.11); the parallel-games wealth-minimum interpretation (Sec. 3.5).
- [Optional stopping](../concepts/optional-stopping.md): ν defined through stopping times; e-process tests as level-α sequential tests with guaranteed detection (Sec. 2, Sec. 4.3); filtration dependence of ν (Remark 2.3).
- [Martingale history](../concepts/martingale-history.md): Ville's theorem on coin flips and its role in the LIL; Doob's attribution of the inequality to Ville; Ville's switch of "martingale" from strategy to wealth (Mazliak–Shafer 2022); Ville's 1984–85 recollections (Sec. 1, Sec. 5).
- inverse-capital-measure (new): the outer measure ν(A) = inf_{τ : A ⊆ {τ<∞}} sup_P P(τ < ∞), its properties (Lemma 2.1, Remarks 2.3–2.4), the gap with µ = sup_P P(·) (Example 2.2, Proposition 2.6), and the pricing representations (Corollary 3.10, Lemma 3.11).
- composite-strong-law (new): the L¹ line-crossing inequality (Theorem 4.1, Corollary 4.2), the composite SLLN under centred uniform integrability (Theorem 4.3), its explicit witness (Remark 4.4), Chung's uniform SLLN as an alternative route (Remark 4.5), the truncated Cauchy family as the boundary case (Proposition 2.6), and the insufficiency of (super)martingales (Sec. 4.3).

## Open questions / gaps
- Exact necessary condition on P for ν(A_div) = 0 in the composite SLLN; (4.11) is sufficient and the truncated Cauchy family shows some restriction is needed (Sec. 4.2).
- Constants in the L¹ line-crossing inequality (Theorem 4.1) are not optimised; the paper suggests improving them is of independent interest.
- Relation between the set P* of finitely additive measures dominated by ν, the family P, and the filtration when ν is submodular (Remark 2.4, raised by a referee).
- Composite game-theoretic laws of the iterated logarithm extending Miyabe–Takemura and Sasai–Miyabe–Takemura (Sec. 5).
- Under what conditions beyond fork-convexity with mutual local absolute continuity can the witness of Theorem 3.1 be a supermartingale (Remark 3.4); the composite Snell-envelope machinery is deferred to the exchangeability paper.
