# On the existence of powerful p-values and e-values for composite hypotheses

## Summary
The paper asks four existence questions for a composite null P and alternative Q on a Polish space: when is there an exact p-variable (uniform under every P ∈ P, stochastically smaller than uniform under every Q ∈ Q), an exact [e-variable](../concepts/e-variable.md) (E_P[X] = 1 for all P ∈ P, E_Q[log X] > 0 or at least E_Q[X] > 1 for all Q ∈ Q), and their non-exact counterparts (P(X ≤ α) ≤ α, E_P[X] ≤ 1) [zhangExistencePowerfulPvalues2023, Sec. 1]. For P and Q convex polytopes in the space of probability measures and under a joint non-atomicity assumption (JA), equivalent to allowing an external uniform, the answer is clean: exact and pivotal powerful p/e-variables exist iff Span P ∩ Conv Q = ∅, and powerful (non-exact) ones iff Conv P ∩ Conv Q = ∅; the exact-e-variable equivalences hold even without (JA) [Thms 3.1, 3.4, 6.1, 6.2, Cor. 6.4]. The proof technique is simultaneous optimal transport (Wang and Zhang 2023): one looks for a single measurable map that sends every null distribution to the same law F (typically uniform) while sending the alternative to a different law G, which by Strassen's theorem reduces to a convex-order comparison of the law of the Radon–Nikodym vector (dP_1/dQ, ..., dP_L/dQ) under Q with a measure supported on the diagonal [Props 2.3, 3.3]. The same picture turns the problem of maximising [e-power](../concepts/e-power.md) among pivotal exact e-variables into finding the maximum element in convex order of a set M_γ of diagonal measures, which leads to the SHINE construction (Separating Hyperplanes Iteration for Nontrivial and Exact e/p-variables), an iterative bisection of γ by hyperplanes through diagonal barycenters whose output is a nonnegative martingale converging to a maximal element, with exponential convergence of e-power under moment conditions [Thms 4.2, 4.4, 4.7, 5.3, 5.5]. Section 7 applies the results to composite [test martingales](../concepts/test-martingale.md): disjoint convex hulls of the marginal classes give a test supermartingale of power one, a span condition gives a genuine test martingale of power one, and when a single observation cannot separate Q from Span P a batch of k observations can, which is the paper's general explanation of why some nulls admit nontrivial test martingales only in a coarsened filtration [Thm 4.9, Cor. 7.1, Remark 7.2].

## Main results
- Terminology (Sec. 1): X is pivotal for P if its law is the same under all P ∈ P; an e-variable is exact if E_P[X] = 1 for all P ∈ P, nontrivial for Q if E_Q[X] > 1 for all Q ∈ Q, and has nontrivial e-power if E_Q[log X] > 0 for all Q ∈ Q; nontrivial e-power implies nontrivial since x − 1 ≥ log x. An exact p-variable is always pivotal; an exact e-variable need not be [zhangExistencePowerfulPvalues2023].
- Proposition 2.3 (from Wang and Zhang 2023, Shen et al. 2019): a simultaneous transport plan from (μ_1, ..., μ_d) to (ν_1, ..., ν_d) exists iff the law of the Radon–Nikodym vector of μ dominates that of ν in convex order; under joint non-atomicity the same holds for transport maps. Proposition 2.4 (MOT–SOT parity): the plan is realised by a backward martingale coupling of the Radon–Nikodym vectors that is Monge.
- Theorem 3.1 (finite null {P_1, ..., P_L}, simple alternative Q, (JA)): equivalent are (a) an exact (hence pivotal) nontrivial p-variable exists; (b) a pivotal, exact, bounded e-variable with nontrivial e-power exists; (c) an exact e-variable nontrivial for Q exists; (d) some X is pivotal for P with a different atomless law under Q; (e) Q ∉ Span(P_1, ..., P_L). Proposition 3.3 is the transport core: Q ∉ Span P iff T((P_1, ..., P_L, Q), (F, ..., F, G)) ≠ ∅ for some G ≠ F, and F may be taken uniform.
- Theorem 3.4 (same setting): a nontrivial p-variable exists iff a bounded e-variable with nontrivial e-power exists iff an e-variable nontrivial for Q exists iff Q ∉ Conv(P_1, ..., P_L). Remark 3.5: (c) ⇔ (d)/(e) in both theorems hold without (JA) (Prop. A.7, averaging out the external uniform).
- Example 3.6: P = {Ber(0.1), Ber(0.2)}, Q = Ber(0.3): Q ∈ Span P \ Conv P, so a nontrivial e-variable exists but no exact one. P = {N(−1,1), N(1,1)}, Q = N(0,1): a pivotal exact nontrivial e-variable exists. Remark 3.7: for finite sample spaces the exact e-variable is 1 + Y with Y the component of Q orthogonal to Span P; for infinite spaces this Hilbert-space trick fails and transport is needed.
- Theorem 4.2 ((JA), (AC): P_i ≪ Q): the maximiser of E_Q[log X] over pivotal exact e-variables can be taken of likelihood-ratio form X = (dG/dF)(Y) with Y a simultaneous transport map sending P to F and Q to G; so the problem (3) equals problem (7): maximise E_G[−log(dF/dG)] subject to (dF/dG, ..., dF/dG)|_G ⪯_cx γ := (dP_1/dQ, ..., dP_L/dQ)|_Q. With L = 1 this is Gibbs' inequality and the likelihood ratio (Example 4.1).
- Proposition 4.3 / Theorem 4.4 (assumption (N): γ gives no mass to hyperplanes): at each x ≥ 0 there is a separating hyperplane through x·1 splitting γ into μ_x and γ − μ_x with barycenters on the diagonal; the set M_γ of diagonal measures below γ in convex order has a unique maximum element iff x ↦ μ_x is monotone. Theorem 4.7 (L = 2): if γ is supported on the boundary of a convex set Γ ⊆ R², the maximum exists and μ([0,x]²) = μ_x(R²). Example 4.17 shows the maximum can fail to exist.
- Example 4.12: P = {N(−1,1), N(1,1)}, Q = N(0,1): γ lies on the hyperbola x_1 x_2 = 1/e; the most powerful pivotal exact e-variable is X = e^{1/2}/cosh(Z), a function of |Z|, with e-power ≈ 0.125 (closed form (9)). Example 4.15: three Gaussians with means at the vertices of an equilateral triangle around 0: the optimal pivotal e-variable is 3/(e^{h(Z)}) with h a sum of three exponentials, not a function of ‖Z‖. Example 4.16 (with atoms): the pivotal optimum has e-power ≈ 0.047 while dropping pivotality raises it to ≈ 0.07.
- Theorem 4.9 (batching): for distinct P_1, ..., P_L on a Euclidean space and Q ∉ P there is k ≥ 1 with Q^k ∉ Span P^k; if (AC) holds and the P_i are linearly independent then k ≤ 2. Relies on Lemma C.3: distinct measures have linearly independent k-fold products for some k. Example 4.10: the Bernoulli triple above admits an exact nontrivial e-variable on pairs, X ≈ (1.009, 0.939, 0.939, 1.338).
- Proposition 4.11: the maximal pivotal exact e-power ℓ_n with n iid points is superadditive, so ℓ_n/n converges to a positive limit ≤ min_{P ∈ P} E_Q[log(dQ/dP)]; whether the limit equals this bound is open; it does in Example 4.13 (n-fold Gaussians, E_n = e^{n/2}/cosh(ΣZ_i) versus the product E*_n = Π e^{1/2}/cosh(Z_i), which is a martingale under P_1 and P_2 while E_n is not; E_Q[log E_n]/n → 1/2 and E_Q[log E*_n]/n ≈ 0.125).
- Section 5 SHINE: start from δ_1 and γ; at each step split every sub-measure by its separating hyperplane and replace it by Dirac masses at the two barycenters; the first coordinates form the SHINE martingale {X_s}, converging a.s. Theorem 5.3: μ^(s) increases strictly in convex order while progress is possible and, under (N), converges to a maximal element of M_γ, to the maximum when one exists. Section 5.2 recovers the e-variable explicitly via (13); Example 5.4: one step gives X = 1.403 or 0.574 with e-power ≈ 0.089 against the optimum 0.12543. Theorem 5.5 / Lemma 5.6: if E_Q[(dP_j/dQ)^{2+ε}] < ∞ and E_Q[(dP_j'/dQ)^{−2}] < ∞ then the e-power gap after k steps is ≤ C r^k, with r < 0.827 for bounded ratios (separated-tree martingales). Section 5.4: simulations with N = 2 × 10^4 Monte Carlo samples; two data points give e-power ≈ 0.358 > 2 × 0.125; dimensions above two are left open because separating hyperplanes are hard to find.
- Theorem 6.1 (finite P and Q, (JA)): exact nontrivial p-variable ⇔ pivotal exact bounded e-variable with nontrivial e-power ⇔ exact e-variable nontrivial for Q ⇔ Span(P_1, ..., P_L) ∩ Conv(Q_1, ..., Q_M) = ∅; (c) ⇔ (e) without (JA). Theorem 6.2: nontrivial p-variable ⇔ bounded e-variable with nontrivial e-power ⇔ nontrivial e-variable ⇔ Conv P ∩ Conv Q = ∅, with (c) ⇔ (d) needing neither (JA) nor finiteness (Kraft's theorem, Remark 6.3). Corollary 6.4 passes to convex polytopes via their vertices (Prop. A.1); Corollary 6.5 and Proposition A.2: for finite Q, nontrivial and nontrivial-e-power existence coincide, by shrinking (1 − b) + bX.
- Example 6.6: P = {Ber(q_1)}, Q = {Ber(p): q_2 ≤ p ≤ 1}, exact nontrivial e-variable exists; it does not if P = {Ber(p): 0 ≤ p ≤ q_1}.
- Theorem 6.7 (infinite P, Q with common reference measure R): an exact bounded e-variable with inf_{Q ∈ Q} E_Q[log X] > 0 exists iff 0 ∉ closure(Span P + Conv Q) in total variation; if Q is tight this is Span P ∩ Conv Q = ∅ (Hahn–Banach in L¹(R)/Span P). Conjecture 6.8: the same characterises pivotal exact e-variables under (JA).
- Proposition 6.9: for P the translates of any infinitely divisible density on R^d and any Q with a density, no exact e-variable nontrivial for Q exists (Wiener's Tauberian theorem: translates span a dense subspace of L¹); compare Gangrade, Rinaldo and Ramdas 2023, who show no e-variable with nontrivial e-power exists, even non-exact, for the class of all Gaussians.
- Example 6.10: for symmetric P with no atom at 0 and Q(R_+) > 1/2, X = 3/2 on ω > 0 and 1/2 on ω < 0 is pivotal and exact, and with an external uniform a nontrivial exact p-value exists.
- Corollary 7.1 (iid data, marginal classes P_mar, Q_mar with a common reference measure): Conv P_mar ∩ Conv Q_mar = ∅ gives a test supermartingale for P with power one against Q (E_Q[log M_t] → ∞); 0 ∉ closure(Span P_mar + Conv Q_mar) gives a test martingale with power one; both by multiplying one-step e-values. The martingale half is claimed as new. The converse is conjectured (e-processes using past data are not ruled out). Remark 7.2: if Q ∈ Span P_mar \ P_mar with P_mar a finite convex hull, no nontrivial test martingale exists in the data filtration but one exists in the coarser filtration obtained by batching observations (Thm 4.9).
- Appendix A: Proposition A.5 calibrates an exact nontrivial p-variable X into the pivotal exact bounded e-variable 2 − 2X; Proposition A.6 gives the trivial necessary directions; Example A.4: P = {N(0,1)}, Q = {N(μ,1): μ > 0}: a nontrivial bounded e-variable exists but none with nontrivial e-power uniformly over Q, since Z_μ → Z_0 in total variation.

## Setting and assumptions
- One observation Z (possibly a batch of n points) from a Polish space; P and Q are finite sets or convex polytopes of probability measures, generalised in Section 6.2 to infinite sets with a common reference measure. No parametric structure, no Wilks-type regularity [zhangExistencePowerfulPvalues2023, Sec. 1, Remark 1.1].
- (JA) joint non-atomicity: an atomless random variable independent of the Radon–Nikodym derivatives exists; equivalent to allowing external randomisation, as in resampling or data splitting. It is needed for the p-variable and pivotality statements but not for the exact-e-variable equivalences [Def. 2.2, Remark 3.5, Prop. A.7].
- (AC) P_i ≪ Q, and (N) γ charges no hyperplane, are technical assumptions for the e-power maximisation and SHINE in Sections 4–5; (N) is expected to be removable (Remarks 4.6, 4.8).
- Fixed-sample throughout Sections 1–6; Section 7 is sequential with iid data and the question is existence of test (super)martingales in the data filtration or a coarser one, without optional stopping analysis.

## Concepts touched
- [e-variable](../concepts/e-variable.md) — exact, pivotal and nontrivial e-variables as distinct properties; existence characterised by span and convex hull (Thms 3.1, 3.4, 6.1, 6.2); non-existence for translation families (Prop. 6.9).
- [e-power](../concepts/e-power.md) — the paper is the first to use Vovk and Wang's term in print for E_Q[log X] (Sec. 4); nontrivial versus nontrivial e-power and the shrinking lemma (Prop. A.2); maximisation under pivotality and exactness (Thms 4.2, 4.4, 4.7); superadditivity in n and the open limit question (Prop. 4.11); uniform e-power over infinite Q (Thm 6.7, Example A.4).
- [gro-criterion](../concepts/gro-criterion.md) — log-optimality under the extra constraints of exactness and pivotality is still attained by a likelihood ratio, now against a probability measure (Thm 4.2); numerical cost of pivotality (Example 4.16).
- [numeraire](../concepts/numeraire.md) — the finite-null attainability statement that the numeraire paper's Remark 3.10 extends; the span condition is the exact-e-variable analogue of lying outside the effective null.
- [test-martingale](../concepts/test-martingale.md) — existence of composite test (super)martingales of power one from one-step e-values (Cor. 7.1); the product-of-pivotal-e-values martingale E*_n versus the non-martingale batch e-variable E_n (Example 4.13).
- [e-process](../concepts/e-process.md) — the conjectured converse of Cor. 7.1 is blocked exactly by e-processes that use past data (Sec. 7).
- [universal-inference](../concepts/universal-inference.md) — contrasted in the introduction as the regularity-free alternative to Wilks.
- [calibrator](../concepts/calibrator.md) — 2 − 2X turns an exact nontrivial p-variable into a pivotal exact bounded e-variable (Prop. A.5).
- [optional-continuation](../concepts/optional-continuation.md) — Corollary 7.1 builds the (super)martingale by multiplying independent one-step e-values.
- filtration-coarsening (new) — the systematic account of when nontrivial p/e-variables or test martingales exist only in a reduced σ-algebra, realised by a simultaneous transport map that collapses the null to one law (Props 2.3, 3.3, Thm 4.9, Example 4.12, Remark 7.2).
- simultaneous-optimal-transport (new) — the tool: transport plans and maps sending a tuple of measures simultaneously, existence via convex order of Radon–Nikodym vectors and Strassen's theorem, the MOT–SOT parity, and the separating-hyperplane SHINE iteration (Sec. 2, Props 2.3–2.4, 4.3, Thms 5.3, 5.5).

## Open questions / gaps
- Conjecture 6.8: for infinite P, Q under (JA), pivotal exact e-variables with uniformly positive e-power exist iff 0 ∉ closure(Span P + Conv Q); pivotal p-variables for infinite classes are beyond the paper's techniques (Sec. 6.2).
- Whether ℓ_n/n → min_{P ∈ P} E_Q[log(dQ/dP)] in general (Sec. 4.4, footnote 8), and an efficient algorithm for the best e-power in high dimensions.
- Extending Theorem 4.7 and SHINE to composite alternatives, to L > 2 (finding separating hyperplanes), and to γ with atoms where (N) fails (Secs 5.1, 5.4, 6.1).
- Converse of Corollary 7.1: does a power-one test martingale force Span P_mar ∩ Q_mar = ∅ (Sec. 7)?
- Not in the bib: Wang and Zhang 2023 (simultaneous optimal transport); Vovk and Wang 2024 (e-tests of symmetry); Gangrade, Rinaldo and Ramdas 2023; Kraft 1955; Hoeffding and Wolfowitz 1958; Vovk 2021; Shafer, Shen, Vereshchagin and Vovk 2011; Nutz, Wang and Zhang 2022; Shen et al. 2019.
