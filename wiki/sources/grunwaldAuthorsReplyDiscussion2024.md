# Authors' reply to the discussion of 'Safe testing'

The reply closes the JRSSB discussion of [grunwaldSafeTesting2024](grunwaldSafeTesting2024.md) (read before the RSS on 24 January 2024). That page already carries a one-paragraph digest of the discussion and reply; this page goes through the reply itself. The authors group the comments into four themes, Sections 1-4 (GRO(W) limitations and alternatives; GRO(W) and power; point vs smooth vs no priors; foundations), followed by Section 5 (miscellaneous). Not every discussant is answered: paraphrasing Diaconis and Freedman 1986, "silence is consent" [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, intro]. In particular Mulder (fractional Bayes factors), ter Schure (cumulative power plots), Xu, Wen and Dowe receive no direct reply.

## Summary
The reply's main technical move is to accept the generalisations of Theorem 1 produced since the paper was written: Lardy, Grünwald and Harremoës 2023 replace KL by "description gain", and Larsson, Ramdas and Ruf 2024 define the numeraire e-variable, which exists with no regularity conditions and coincides with GRO/RIPr whenever Theorem 1 applies; the authors read this as saying their Theorem 1 and the nonparametric betting e-processes of Waudby-Smith and Ramdas, Honda and Takemura 2010 and Agrawal et al. 2021 are "really the same idea" [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 1]. What remains open is the case of composite alternatives (where Example 8 of the main paper shows a coarser filtration may be needed) and of alternatives that charge events null under the whole convex hull of H0 (Vovk's infinite exchangeable sequences, where the numeraire has e-power +infinity and says nothing useful). On power, the authors answer Greenland's chi-square challenge with likelihood-ratio e-variables (Proposition 3 of the main paper gives E_{delta*} as GROW under a minimum effect size) and, for the one-shot case without a minimum effect, with Johnson's UMP Bayes factor, rejecting at p <= 0.007 against p <= 0.0007 for a noninformative prior. On priors, they concede Ly's point that GROW point priors prevent a test and an anytime-valid CI from being both well-behaved and consistent, and present REGROW (Sections 4.1-4.3 of the main paper) and the nonlocal priors of Johnson and Rossell 2010 as the way out. On foundations they agree with Shafer that they "conceded too much" on sample size, agree with Hennig and Pawel and Held that "safe" was not a good name, and insist the paper's target is optional continuation across studies, not interim analysis. Each section ends with research directions the group is pursuing.

## Main results
The reply proves nothing new; its content is a set of positions, concessions and worked numbers. Listed by section of the reply, with the discussant answered and the cross-reference into the main paper.

### Section 1: GRO(W) construction, limitations, extensions, alternatives
- **Regularity (Vovk; Larsson, Ramdas and Ruf).** Theorem 1 of the main paper needs common densities and D(Q || P_W) < infinity for some prior W on the null; all four versions of the theorem need analogous conditions, which are "quite strong" nonparametrically and can trivialise GRO. The authors accept the numeraire as the general form: for a singleton alternative Q that charges no event null under every element of conv(H0), GRO/numeraire is now "essentially fully general" [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 1]. See [numeraire](../concepts/numeraire.md).
- **Changing the filtration (Tian and Yu; Vovk).** Two failure modes remain: (a) composite alternatives under GROW/REGROW, where Example 8 of the main paper shows one must move to a coarser filtration to get the optimal e-variable (cf. the t-test's sigma(V)); when coarsening increases worst-case growth or power is only partly understood (Pérez-Ortiz et al. 2024, Choe and Ramdas 2024). (b) Y an infinite sequence with an exchangeability null: condition (b) fails for every Q outside the null (Ramdas et al. 2022), the numeraire exists but has expected log growth infinity (Larsson et al. 2024, Theorem 2.5), so one must restrict e-processes to a coarsened filtration as Vovk's conformal test martingales do, after which e-power becomes a natural optimality notion again. Speculation: "design by filtration change" may be unifiable with design by growth optimality and information projection [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 1].
- **Appropriateness of e-power (Wang).** The authors agree e-power is a "sore point": the paper defines and optimises it for single e-variables although its meaning comes from optional continuation. Wang's Pareto-type examples (e-power -infinity yet the e-variable almost surely grows large and cannot hit zero) have reciprocals showing the opposite (e-power +infinity yet a large probability of being tiny). Directions: concave utilities f(E) other than log, arising from Rényi projections in place of the RIPr (Lardy et al. 2023, Larsson et al. 2024), with a monetary interpretation via Soklakov 2020; data-dependent loss functions (Grünwald 2022). They nonetheless predict GRO and the RIPr will stay "the default notion of e-power, with properly acknowledged caveats" (quoting Wang) [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 1]. See [e-power](../concepts/e-power.md).
- **Payout E vs log E (Hennig).** Dismissed: in the betting game the payout is E, and the paper already explains why optimising E_Q[E] directly is bad under optional continuation.
- **Monetary interpretation of p-values (Hennig; Shafer).** Lottery-ticket argument: tickets T_r paying 1/r if p <= r each cost 1 euro under H0; reporting p amounts to claiming the gain of the ticket T_p one did not buy in advance, i.e. "cheating". This is Shafer's point that p-to-e calibration makes p-values honest. Concession: Shafer wrote that the authors "concede too much" in saying e-tests need more data; the authors agree, clarifying that "requires more data" referred only to a test outputting a bare accept/reject decision without a continuous evidence measure [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 1]. See [calibrator](../concepts/calibrator.md).

### Section 2: GRO(W) and power
- **Greenland's chi-square example.** Y ~ chi-square with k degrees of freedom (k = 1 in the worked case), observed y = 3.84 (p = 0.05). Greenland's two naive e-variables reject (E >= 20) only at p <= 0.0000077. The authors' answer: under the alternative Y is noncentral chi-square with noncentrality delta, so E_delta(y) = p_delta(y)/p_0(y) and mixtures E_W are e-variables (simple null); noncentral chi-square is Pólya type infinity (Karlin 1956), hence MLR, so every such E is monotone in p. (i) With a minimum effect delta*, Proposition 3 of the main paper (Section 3.1) makes E_{delta*} the GROW e-variable; the reply's Figure 1 plots E_delta(3.84) against delta. (ii) Without one, the REGROW prescription of Sections 4.1-4.3 applies; the exact REGROW prior is hard, so they use the "exponentiated Cauchy" prior w(delta) = 1/(pi delta (1 + log^2 delta)), giving E = 1.81 at p = 0.05 and E >= 20 iff y >= 11.52, i.e. p <= 0.0007. (iii) For a one-shot sample-size-1 test at known alpha, REGROW is "not the method of choice": use Johnson's 2013 UMP Bayes factor, an alpha-dependent point prior; here delta* = 7.378 and E >= 20 iff y >= delta*, i.e. p <= 0.007, a factor 10 better, and the best possible among likelihood-ratio e-variables; type-I control under OC is kept at every other threshold. Direction: UMP Bayes factors as priors for powerful e-variables in general [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 2].
- **Exact vs asymptotic growth optimality (Martin; Tian and Yu).** Dixit and Martin 2023 (predictive recursion + universal inference) match GRO to first order. The reply: for d x d multivariate Gaussian parameters UI and GRO agree in e-power up to sublinear terms, but when n is not much larger than d the gap in both classical power and e-power "can be extreme" (Tse and Davison 2022, Spector, Candès and Lei 2023, own experiments; a theoretical exponential-family comparison is in progress). The loss is attributed to UI, not to predictive recursion, which they like as an alternative to Robbins' mixtures or plug-in [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 2]. See [universal-inference](../concepts/universal-inference.md).
- **Restricted stopping-time sets S (Martin).** Agreed that NP tests manually adjusted for a pre-specified S (alpha-spending being one instance) beat e-processes designed for all stopping times in classical power; but this handles restricted optional stopping within a study, not optional continuation across studies, which "was our main target". Also e-values permit valid risk assessment of decisions with post-hoc loss functions (Grünwald 2022, 2023), not easily reproduced by adjustment [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 2]. See [post-hoc-validity](../concepts/post-hoc-validity.md).

### Section 3: point vs smooth vs no priors
- **Objective Bayes and the least favourable prior (Bon and Robert).** Pointer to Pérez-Ortiz et al. 2024, Section 5.2: the right-Haar prior's non-uniqueness disappears for e-posteriors (Grünwald 2023). The GRO variant closest to reference analysis is REGROW (Sections 4.1-4.3), which for simple nulls recommends Jeffreys' prior asymptotically and generally does not give point masses; concession: no efficient general construction of the REGROW/RIPr pair is known. Collaboration picture: objective Bayesians using REGROW priors and frequentists using GROW point priors may multiply their e-values for the same null on different data [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 3].
- **Point-mass RIPr worry (Bon and Robert).** For a unit-variance Gaussian alternative with N(0, .) prior against the null mu in (-a, a), the discussants expect W0 to sit on the endpoints. The authors doubt it: in similar k-sample settings (Hao et al. 2023) the mass spreads symmetrically over many points or a continuum; concentration on one point happens only if the alternative prior's mean lies outside [-a, a]. Precise multivariate Gaussian e-variables are "ongoing research" ([grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024](grunwaldOptimalEValuesExponential2024.md)) [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 3]. See [reverse-information-projection](../concepts/reverse-information-projection.md).
- **Empirical Bayes to "empirical E" (Rizzelli).** Data-optimised E_{gamma-hat} is not an e-variable; the authors decline approximate guarantees but propose renormalising E'(Y) = E_{gamma-hat}(Y) / sup_{P in H0} E_P[E_{gamma-hat}], which for simple nulls turns GROW's redundancy into regret (cf. Orabona and Jun 2024); "currently exploring" [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 3]. See [mixture-and-plug-in-methods](../concepts/mixture-and-plug-in-methods.md).
- **GROW vs shrinking AV confidence intervals (Ly).** With a point prior at the minimal relevant delta, delta never leaves the anytime-valid CI. One wants (a) CI width -> 0 and (b) test rejects iff 0 is outside the CI; for GROW-optimal tests (a) and (b) are incompatible. Concession: "we did not realize this to be the case when we developed the GROW criterion". REGROW yields full-support priors (asymptotically Jeffreys for simple nulls) and avoids the issue, but forbids an informed effect-size guess; nonlocal priors (Johnson and Rossell 2010) may give "(almost) the best of both worlds" [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 3]. See [confidence-sets-and-sequences](../concepts/confidence-sets-and-sequences.md), [gro-criterion](../concepts/gro-criterion.md).

### Section 4: foundational aspects
- **Logical coherence (Bickel) and the likelihood principle (Pace and Salvan).** Agreed there is a power-coherence trade-off (UI e-variables are coherent in the sense of Hansen and Rice 2023, GRO ones are not), but the GRO e-variable for Theta_0 remains a valid e-variable for every Theta_0' subset of Theta_0, so a single statistician who keeps the e-variable when shrinking the null is coherent; incoherence appears only when GRO is re-applied to design a new e-variable for the subset. Bickel's conclusion is called "overly pessimistic", Pace and Salvan's claim that likelihood e-values obey the strong likelihood principle "overly optimistic"; both depend on definitions; foundations are future work [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 4].
- **Testing vs estimation (Pawel and Held).** Sympathy for anti-"nullistic" CIs and e-value curves over the parameter (as in Grünwald 2022, 2023), but testing must be in order first.
- **Games vs measures (Shafer).** Agreement that optional ("free") continuation is best treated game-theoretically; measure-theoretic habits make such a treatment hard to follow, "including us".
- **Statistics meets probability (Wang; Srakar).** Endorsed; extensions to general stochastic processes called promising.
- **Is the method safe? (Pawel and Held; Hennig; Greenland).** Interim analyses threatening blinding miss the point: the claim is about optional continuation across studies; within-study interim analysis is a bonus that is not always available (main paper Section 5). Concession: "'safe' may not be such a good name" since it suggests safety in every sense. Robustness to outliers via (1 - gamma)E + gamma (keeping a fraction gamma in one's pocket), with gamma learnable by Robbins' mixtures; e-hacking is acknowledged as a concern (cf. [ramdasGametheoreticStatisticsSafe2023](ramdasGametheoreticStatisticsSafe2023.md)) [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 4]. See [optional-continuation](../concepts/optional-continuation.md).

### Section 5: miscellaneous
- **Minimum-e-value estimator (Cattaneo).** arg min{delta : E_delta(Y) <= 1/alpha} as an effect-size estimator and its behaviour under OC: "intriguing", not studied.
- **Overloading of "e-value" (Dickhaus; Greenland).** Regretted but irreversible.
- **Misspecification (Pawel and Held; Dey, Martin and Williams).** Options: nonparametric e-values (Ramdas et al. 2023), or Gibbs-posterior/learning-rate analogues (Grünwald 1999; Grünwald and Mehta 2020 connect both to nonnegative supermartingales). Dey, Martin and Williams' empirical-risk generalisation of UI is welcomed and "might call for a corresponding notion of growth optimality" [grunwaldAuthorsReplyDiscussion2024, Sec. 5].
- **Threshold (1 - alpha)/alpha under likelihood-ratio symmetry (Pace and Salvan).** Agreed; Berger, Brown and Wolpert 1994 was "a major source of inspiration". Partial answer in Grünwald 2023, Section 3.2, eq. 31: a special e-variable reaches 1/alpha exactly when a standard likelihood-ratio e-variable reaches (1 - alpha)/alpha; it does not extend to an e-process, so it supports OC but not OS.
- **Tian and Yu's list.** Applications: [terschureAnytimevalidLogrankTest2024](terschureAnytimevalidLogrankTest2024.md), [turnerGenericEvariablesExact2024](turnerGenericEvariablesExact2024.md); regression: Pérez-Ortiz et al. 2024; nonparametric e-processes as variants or strict generalisations of GRO (Larsson et al. 2024); admissibility characterised by Ramdas, Ruf, Larsson and Koolen 2020.
- **Chai.** 0.8 always means power; OC arises automatically in meta-analysis (ter Schure and Grünwald 2022, ALL-IN), OS depends on context; Held's sceptical p-value is not designed for more than two continuations.

## Setting and assumptions
- Same objects as the main paper: e-variables for composite nulls, GRO/GROW/REGROW optimality, RIPr priors, optional continuation at the study level and optional stopping within a study. All numerical claims are for the 1-degree-of-freedom central vs noncentral chi-square problem with simple null and alpha = 0.05 (threshold 20).
- The UMP Bayes factor claim relies on a monotone likelihood ratio family; the "best possible" statement is restricted to e-variables of likelihood-ratio form p_W / p_0.
- The coherence argument assumes the alternative Q is held fixed when the null is shrunk.
- Dates: the text layer is dated 14 January 2025, after the November 2024 issue; the published reply occupies pp. 1163-1171.

## Open questions / gaps
- Research directions the authors list as ongoing or future work: (1) when filtration coarsening increases worst-case growth, and whether design by filtration change unifies with design by information projection (Sec. 1); (2) Rényi projections and non-log concave utilities with a monetary reading (Sec. 1); (3) UMP Bayes factors as alpha-dependent priors for powerful e-variables in general (Sec. 2); (4) theoretical nonasymptotic comparison of GRO and UI for exponential-family nulls (Sec. 2); (5) exact multivariate Gaussian e-variables (Sec. 3); (6) efficient computation of REGROW and RIPr priors (Sec. 3); (7) renormalised "empirical e" variables (Sec. 3); (8) nonlocal priors as a GROW/REGROW compromise (Sec. 3); (9) logical coherence and the likelihood principle (Sec. 4); (10) Cattaneo's minimum-e-value estimator under OC (Sec. 5); (11) Gibbs-posterior/empirical-risk growth optimality (Sec. 5); (12) an e-process version of the (1 - alpha)/alpha device (Sec. 5); (13) sceptical p-value vs e-values (Sec. 5).
- Not addressed in the reply although raised: Mulder's numerical finding that fractional Bayes factors satisfy the e-constraint for the t-test; ter Schure's cumulative power plots; Dickhaus's Bayes-factor calibration and multiple-testing points; Xu's f-divergence view.
