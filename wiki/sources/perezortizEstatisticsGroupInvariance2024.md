# E-statistics, group invariance and anytime-valid testing

Annals of Statistics 52(4), 1410–1432 (2024); received October 2023, revised April 2024; arXiv 2208.07610 (first version August 2022). Proofs of Theorem 4, Propositions 6 and 7 and the main Theorem 2 are in Section 6; Lemmas 2–4, the sufficiency-and-invariance discussion (S1), the non-uniqueness discussion (S2), the anytime-validity details and the counterexample stopping time (S3) and the Wishart computation (S4) are in the online supplement, which is not part of the ingested text.

## Summary
The paper studies composite testing problems H0: δ(θ) = δ0 vs H1: δ(θ) = δ1 in which a group G acts freely on data and parameters, the model is invariant, and the parameter of interest δ is a maximal invariant of the parameter (prototype: the one-sample t-test with δ = μ/σ under the scale group (R+, ·)) [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 1–2.2]. Reparametrising each orbit by G turns the problem into H0: X^n ~ P_g vs H1: X^n ~ Q_g, g ∈ G (eq. 4), and the classical invariant test statistic is the likelihood ratio T^{M_n} = q^{M_n}/p^{M_n} of a maximally invariant statistic M_n, which by Wijsman's representation theorem equals the ratio of right-Haar integrals ∫ q_g dρ(g) / ∫ p_g dρ(g), a Bayes factor with the (usually improper) right Haar prior on both hypotheses (eq. 5) [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 2.2]. The main result (Theorem 2 with Corollary 3) is that when G is amenable and mild topological and moment conditions hold, inf over all pairs of priors on G of KL(Q_{Π1} ‖ P_{Π0}) equals KL(Q^{M_n} ‖ P^{M_n}), so by Theorem 1 of Safe Testing T^{M_n} is GROW among all e-statistics, invariant or not; the authors call this an anytime-valid analogue of the Hunt–Stein theorem [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 1, 2.5, 3.1]. Theorem 4 shows that in the invariant setting the oracle growth sup_T E_{Q_g}[ln T] does not depend on g, so GROW and relatively GROW (REGROW) coincide (Corollary 5), unlike for contingency tables [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 3.1]. Proposition 6 shows (T^{M_n})_n is a nonnegative martingale in the filtration generated by the maximal invariants, which licenses optional stopping and continuation for stopping times adapted to that coarser filtration but not for all stopping times in the data filtration [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 3.2]. Section 3.3 extends everything to composite δ-hypotheses, with or without a proper prior on δ (Proposition 7, Corollaries 8–9), which gives the safe t-test and the Bayesian t-test with a moment-bearing prior; Section 4 works out the lower-triangular-group alternative to Hotelling's T² and the test of a single regression coefficient; Section 5 discusses non-amenable groups and why the right-Haar non-uniqueness objections to objective Bayes do not arise [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].

## Main results
- **Setting (Sec. 2.2, eqs 1, 3–4)**: G acts freely on X and Θ; invariance P_θ{X ∈ B} = P_{gθ}{X ∈ gB}; m is invariant if constant on orbits and maximally invariant if it indexes orbits; δ a maximally invariant parameter; orbits reparametrised by G after fixing θ0 ∈ δ^{-1}(δ0), θ1 ∈ δ^{-1}(δ1), with results insensitive to these choices [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **Wijsman's representation (eq. 5)**: under a continuous proper action, G σ-compact locally compact, and densities w.r.t. a relatively left-invariant measure, T^{M_n} = ∫_G q_g(X^n) dρ(g) / ∫_G p_g(X^n) dρ(g) with ρ the right Haar measure (Andersson 1982; Eaton 1989, Thm 5.9); hence T^{M_n} does not depend on which maximal invariant is used, and by Stein's result (Hall, Wijsman and Ghosh 1965; supplement S1) it is the same whether one takes a maximal invariant of the data or of a sufficient statistic [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 2.2].
- **Amenability (Sec. 2.2)**: G is amenable if there is a sequence of almost-right-invariant probability measures Π_k, |Π_k(B) − Π_k(Bg)| → 0; includes finite, compact, commutative and solvable groups; the free group on two generators and GL(d) are not amenable [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **Example 1 (t-test, eq. 6)**: N(μ, σ), G = (R+, ·), δ = μ/σ, maximal invariant M_n = (X1/|X1|, …, Xn/|X1|), dρ(σ) = dσ/σ; T^{M_n} is the ratio of 1/σ-mixed Gaussian likelihoods and equals the likelihood ratio of the t-statistic [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **E-statistic and its two properties (Sec. 2.3, eq. 7)**: T_n ≥ 0 with sup_g E_{P_g}[T_n] ≤ 1; Markov gives level α; products with a data-dependently chosen second e-statistic remain e-statistics (optional continuation); nonnegative martingales additionally allow optional stopping via Ville's inequality, with the caveat that stopping times in the data filtration need not be stopping times in a coarser filtration [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **GROW and relatively GROW (Sec. 2.4, eqs 8, 10)**: GROW maximises inf_g E_{Q_g}[ln T_n]; relatively GROW maximises inf_g {E_{Q_g}[ln T_n] − sup_{T'} E_{Q_g}[ln T'_n]}; power is rejected as a criterion because it needs a known stopping rule and a power-optimal e-statistic takes value zero with positive probability [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **Theorem 1 (restated from Safe Testing, Thm 1 of Sec. 4.3)**: if inf over priors of KL(Q_{Π1} ‖ P_{Π0}) is attained at the level of some statistic V_n = v_n(X^n) (eq. 9), the GROW value is that KL and the essentially unique GROW e-statistic is the ratio of V_n-Bayes marginals under the minimising priors; allowing V_n = M_n covers non-compact G where the infimum on the data is not attained [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 2.4].
- **Finite-group warm-up (Sec. 3.1, eqs 11–13)**: ρ = uniform, T^{M_n} is the ratio of uniform mixtures, and the information processing inequality KL(Q_{Π1} ‖ P_{Π0}) ≥ KL(Q^{M_n} ‖ P^{M_n}) with equality for uniform priors gives GROW directly; this fails for non-compact G because ρ is finite iff G is compact [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **Assumption 1**: G and X^n Polish and locally compact; the action free, continuous and proper; invariant models with densities w.r.t. a common relatively left-invariant measure with multiplier χ and a single common support; these ensure Wijsman's theorem and Bondar's (1976) bimeasurable bijection X^n ≅ subset of G × X^n/G [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 3.1].
- **Theorem 2 (main)**: for M_n maximally invariant, G amenable, Assumption 1, and (1+ε)-th absolute moments of ln q_1/p_1 under Q_1 and of ln q^{M_n}/p^{M_n} under Q^{M_n} finite (eq. 14): inf_{Π0, Π1} KL(Q_{Π1} ‖ P_{Π0}) = KL(Q^{M_n} ‖ P^{M_n}) [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **Corollary 3**: under Theorem 2, T^{M_n} for any maximal invariant (of the data or of a sufficient statistic) is a GROW e-statistic for (4) [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **Theorem 4**: under Assumption 1(3) and finiteness of some KL(Q_g, P_h) for each g, the map g ↦ sup_{T e-stat} E_{Q_g}[ln T_n] is constant, so GROW ⇔ relatively GROW for (4); proof via Safe Testing's simple-alternative Theorem 1, sup_T E_{Q_g}[ln T] = inf_{Π0} KL(Q_g ‖ P_{Π0}), and a translation of the prior by gh^{-1} (eq. 25) [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 6.1].
- **Corollary 5**: T^{M_n} is both GROW and relatively GROW; for the t-test, G = (R+, ·) is amenable (Uniform([−n, n]) on the log scale is almost right invariant), so the t-statistic likelihood ratio (6) is GROW and REGROW [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Example 1 continued].
- **Proposition 6 (anytime validity)**: for a sequence of maximal invariants M_n, (T^{M_n})_n is a nonnegative martingale w.r.t. (σ(M_1, …, M_n))_n under every null element; key step: M_{n−1} is a function of M_n, so T^{M_n} equals the likelihood ratio of (M_1, …, M_n). Hence T^{M_τ} is an e-statistic for every stopping time τ of the invariant filtration, including "stop when T^{M_n} ≥ 1/α", but supplement S3.1 gives a t-test stopping time τ' adapted only to the data filtration for which T^{M_{τ'}} is not an e-statistic [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 3.2].
- **Proposition 7 (composite δ, eqs 15–18)**: if the KL minimisation for the invariance-reduced problem H0: M_n ~ P^{M_n}_δ, δ ∈ Δ0 vs H1: δ ∈ Δ1 is attained by priors Π0*, Π1* on Δ0, Δ1, and the δ-mixed models satisfy Theorem 2, then the full-problem inf over priors on G × Δ_j equals that minimum; proof by information processing plus Theorem 2 on the restricted prior class [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Sec. 3.3, 6.1].
- **Corollary 8**: ∫ q^{M_n}_δ dΠ1*(δ) / ∫ p^{M_n}_δ dΠ0*(δ) is GROW and relatively GROW for the composite problem (16). Example: one-sided t-test H0: δ ≤ δ0 vs H1: δ ≥ δ1 with δ0 < δ1; monotone likelihood ratio of the t density plus Proposition 3 of Safe Testing puts Π0*, Π1* at δ0, δ1, so p^{M_n}_{δ1}/p^{M_n}_{δ0} is GROW among all e-statistics of the raw data, not only scale-invariant ones [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **Corollary 9 (eq. 19)**: for given proper priors Π̃0, Π̃1 on Δ0, Δ1 whose mixed models satisfy Corollary 3, the Bayes factor ∫ q_δ(m_n) dΠ̃1 / ∫ p_δ(m_n) dΠ̃0 is GROW and relatively GROW for testing the mixed models. Jeffreys'/Rouder's Bayesian t-test (Cauchy prior on δ1, point mass at 0) is an e-statistic but condition (14) cannot be verified because the Cauchy has no moments; any prior with a finite (2+ε)-th moment (e.g. Gaussian) satisfies (14) by Lemma 1 with d = 1, and the Bayes factor is then relatively GROW [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024, Example 1 continued].
- **Section 4.1 (lower triangular group)**: X_i ~ N_d(μ, Σ), Σ = ΛΛ' with Λ ∈ LT+(d) (amenable), H0: Λ^{-1}μ = δ0 vs H1: Λ^{-1}μ = δ1; action proper on rank-d data for n ≥ d + 1, Lebesgue multiplier χ(L) = |det L|^n, Assumption 1 holds; invariantly sufficient maximal invariant M_{S,n} = √(n/(n−1)) L_n^{-1} X̄_n with V̄_n = L_n L_n'; related to the Roy–Bargmann step-down procedure. **Lemma 1 (eq. 21)**: for δ0 = 0, T^{M_{S,n}} = e^{−n‖δ1‖²/2} ∫ e^{n⟨δ1, T A_n^{-1} M_{S,n}⟩} dP_{n,I}(T) with I + M M' = A_n A_n' and nTT' ~ Wishart(n, I) [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **Section 4.2 (linear regression)**: Y_i = γ X_i + β'Z_i + σ ε_i, H0: γ/σ = δ0 vs H1: γ/σ = δ1; group G = R+ × R^d acting by (c, v): (X, Y, Z) ↦ (X, cY + v'Z, Z) (Eaton 1989; Kariya 1980), amenable, Assumption 1 holds; maximal invariant M_n = (A_n'Y^n/‖A_n'Y^n‖, X^n, Z^n) with A_n an orthonormal basis of the orthogonal complement of col(Z_n); with the (X, Z) mechanism common to both hypotheses, the likelihood ratio is computed from the Bhowmik–King (2007) density of the unit vector U^n in terms of 1F1 confluent hypergeometric functions with k = n − d; extends to semilinear effects of X [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **Section 5.1 (amenability not necessary)**: Hotelling's T² corresponds to GL(d), non-amenable, with H1 the sphere ‖Λ^{-1}μ‖² = γ; by Giri, Kiefer and Stein (1963) for d = 2, n = 3 (and Shalaevskii 1971 for d = 2, any n) the T² likelihood ratio is a proper-prior mixture of (21), so by Proposition 7 it is GROW; whether a counterexample to Theorem 2 exists for non-amenable groups is open [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **Section 5.2 (no non-uniqueness)**: when the same hypotheses are invariant under several groups, the GROW e-statistic is unique as soon as Theorem 2 applies to one of them (supplement S2); the Sun–Berger (2007) non-uniqueness concerns the Bayes predictive/posterior for a single model, a ratio of marginals at sample sizes n and n−1, whereas the GROW e-statistic is a ratio of marginals for two models at the same n; replacing the posterior by the e-posterior (Grünwald 2023) removes the issue there too [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].
- **Proof of Theorem 2 (Sec. 6.2)**: data rewritten as (H, M) ∈ G × X^n/G via Bondar's map with μ = λ × β (left Haar times a quotient measure); chain rule (28) KL(Q_Π ‖ P_Π) = KL(Q^M ‖ P^M) + ∫ KL(Q^{H|m}_Π ‖ P^{H|m}_Π) dQ^M; the second term is driven to zero by priors Π_i = ρ restricted to compact C_i = J_i K_i L_i with ρ(J_i)/ρ(J_i K_i L_i) → 1 (Lemma 2, Bondar–Milnes Følner-type amenability), split into an inner term bounded by −ln P_1{H ∈ L_i | M = m} (Lemma 3) and an outer term bounded by Hölder with exponent p = 1+ε using Lemma 4 and condition (14) (eqs 31–33) [perezortizEstatisticsGroupInvariance2024].

## Setting and assumptions
- Null and alternative: orbits {P_g}_{g∈G} and {Q_g}_{g∈G} of an invariant parametric model; composite only through the group (Sec. 2–3.2), composite also in δ in Sec. 3.3; i.i.d. observations X^n but the group acts on the whole sample.
- Data model: parametric, dominated, with the topological Assumption 1 (Polish locally compact G and X^n, free continuous proper action, relatively left-invariant dominating measure, common support); plus amenability of G and the (1+ε) log-likelihood-ratio moment condition (14).
- Optional continuation: allowed for any e-statistic at fixed n; optional stopping and continuation with data-dependent sample sizes: allowed for stopping times adapted to the invariant filtration (Proposition 6), explicitly not for all stopping times of the data filtration (supplement S3.1 counterexample).
- Anytime-valid testing is distinguished from Wald-style sequential testing with pre-fixed stopping rules, where power remains meaningful (Sec. 1, 2.5, supplement S3).
- Priors on δ (Corollary 9) must be proper; moment conditions on the prior are needed to verify (14).

## Open questions / gaps
- Is amenability necessary for Theorem 2, or can it be weakened; is there a counterexample for a non-amenable group? Hotelling's T² (GL(d)) is GROW only in the d = 2 cases via Giri–Kiefer–Stein and Shalaevskii (Sec. 5.1).
- Jeffreys' Cauchy-prior Bayesian t-test: an e-statistic, but relative GROW cannot be verified since condition (14) fails (below Corollary 9); same open point as in Safe Testing Sec. 4.3.
- Optional stopping only holds for stopping times of the invariant filtration; characterising which data-filtration stopping times break T^{M_τ} beyond the S3.1 example is not pursued.
- Composite-δ results (Proposition 7) presuppose that the invariance-reduced KL minimisation is attained; no general existence result is given.
- The regression example gives the likelihood ratio through a hypergeometric integral but no computation or power study; no simulations anywhere in the paper.
