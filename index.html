2026

  • Anytime validity is free: Inducing sequential tests. Nick W. Koning and Sam van Meer.
    Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B: Statistical Methodology, pp. qkag050, 2026
     Any level-α test at a fixed sample size N can be turned into an anytime-valid sequential test that coincides with it at time N, by tracking the null-conditional expectation of the test; a [0, 1]-valued test outcome then serves as a conditional significance level for whatever comes next, so optional stopping and continuation need no explicit e-values. PDF DOI

2025

  • The numeraire e-variable and reverse information projection. Martin Larsson, Aaditya Ramdas and Johannes Ruf.
    The Annals of Statistics, 53 (3), pp. 1015–1043, 2025
    With no assumptions at all on a composite null and a point alternative there is a unique strictly positive e-variable, the numeraire, that is log-optimal and is a likelihood ratio dQ/dP* against a sub-probability measure P* in the effective null; P* is the reverse information projection whenever the classical one exists, strong duality holds, universal inference is dominated, and nonparametric nulls without a reference measure become tractable. arXiv DOI

  • Hypothesis testing with E-values. Aaditya Ramdas and Ruodu Wang.
    Foundations and Trends in Statistics, 1 (1-2), pp. 1–390, 2025
    The reference monograph on e-values: validity via Markov and Ville, e-power and log-optimality, calibrators, universal inference, the numeraire and reverse information projection, e-processes, merging, e-BH, and e-confidence intervals, in sixteen chapters with proofs. PDF arXiv DOI

2024

  • Authors’ reply to the discussion of “Safe testing”. Peter D. Grünwald, Rianne de Heide and Wouter M. Koolen.
    Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B: Statistical Methodology, 86 (5), pp. 1163–1171, 2024
    The authors’ answer to twenty discussants of Safe Testing: they endorse the numeraire as the assumption-free form of their Theorem 1, concede that GROW point priors clash with shrinking confidence intervals and that ‘safe’ was an unlucky name, take back the ‘e-values need more data’ line, and show on Greenland’s chi-square example that a UMP Bayes factor rejects at p <= 0.007. DOI

  • Beyond Neyman–Pearson: E-values enable hypothesis testing with a data-driven alpha. Peter D. Grünwald.
    Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 121 (39), pp. e2302098121, 2024
    Why report an e-value instead of a p-value next to the accept/reject decision: in a generalised Neyman–Pearson setting where the loss function is chosen after the data, only e-value-based rules keep Type-I risk under control, and the same holds for confidence intervals via the e-posterior. PDF arXiv DOI

  • Safe testing. Peter D. Grünwald, Rianne de Heide and Wouter M. Koolen.
    Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B: Statistical Methodology, 86 (5), pp. 1091–1128, 2024
    The paper that put e-values and growth-rate optimality on the map: GRO e-variables are Bayes factors with a reverse-information-projection prior on the null; read before the RSS on January 24, 2024 (video), with discussion and authors’ reply. PDF arXiv DOI video

  • E-statistics, group invariance and anytime-valid testing. Muriel Felipe Pérez-Ortiz, Tyron Lardy, Rianne de Heide and Peter D. Grünwald.
    The Annals of Statistics, 52 (4), pp. 1410–1432, 2024
    The anytime-valid Hunt–Stein theorem: for testing problems invariant under an amenable group, the likelihood ratio of a maximally invariant statistic (equivalently the right-Haar Bayes factor) is the GROW and relatively GROW e-statistic among all e-statistics, and it is a test martingale in the invariant filtration; this is the result behind the safe t-test. PDF arXiv DOI

  • The anytime-valid logrank test: Error control under continuous monitoring with unlimited horizon. Judith ter Schure, Muriel Felipe Pérez-Ortiz, Alexander Ly and Peter D. Grünwald.
    The New England Journal of Statistics in Data Science, 2 (2), pp. 190–214, 2024
    The logrank test rebuilt as a test martingale: the ratio of Cox partial likelihoods is an exact e-process under proportional hazards, so survival trials can be monitored continuously, extended indefinitely and pooled into a live meta-analysis without losing type-I error control. PDF arXiv DOI

  • Generic E-variables for exact sequential k-sample tests that allow for optional stopping. Rosanne J. Turner, Alexander Ly and Peter D. Grünwald.
    Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference, 230, pp. 106116, 2024
    Builds exact, anytime-valid k-sample tests from a generic block e-variable whose denominator is a product of per-outcome mixtures of the null, proves it is GRO whenever the single-outcome model is convex (so for 2x2 tables), and shows by simulation that with a Beta(0.18, 0.18) prior it matches Fisher’s exact test in sample size while allowing optional stopping. PDF arXiv DOI

2023

  • The e-posterior. Peter D. Grünwald.
    Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A, 381 (2247), pp. 20220146, 2023
    The reciprocal of an e-variable, read as a posterior over the parameter: it can be combined with any loss function chosen after the data and gives frequentist risk bounds that get loose rather than wrong when the e-collection is chosen badly, which is what makes e-posterior minimax decisions safer than Bayesian ones. PDF arXiv DOI

  • Game-theoretic statistics and safe anytime-valid inference. Aaditya Ramdas, Peter D. Grünwald, Vladimir Vovk and Glenn Shafer.
    Statistical Science, 38 (4), pp. 576–601, 2023
    The four-author Statistical Science survey that fixed the vocabulary of safe anytime-valid inference (e-value, test martingale, e-process, confidence sequence, testing by betting) and catalogued the composite-null constructions, from universal inference and the RIPr to nonparametric supermartingales and e-detectors, ending with a list of open problems. arXiv DOI

  • Exact anytime-valid confidence intervals for contingency tables and beyond. Rosanne J. Turner and Peter D. Grünwald.
    Statistics & Probability Letters, 198, pp. 109835, 2023
    A short note that turns the two-sample 2x2 e-variables into exact anytime-valid confidence sequences for risk difference, relative risk and odds ratio by projecting the alternative onto a convex null and inverting the resulting test over all effect sizes. PDF arXiv DOI

  • Confidence and discoveries with e-values. Vladimir Vovk and Ruodu Wang.
    Statistical Science, 38 (2), pp. 329–354, 2023
    Turns e-values into confidence regions and into simultaneous lower confidence bounds on the number of true discoveries, valid under arbitrary dependence, with an O(K^2) algorithm for the arithmetic-mean discovery matrix that beats closed-testing p-value bounds in simulations and on the BRCA gene-expression data. PDF arXiv DOI

2022

  • Log-optimal anytime-valid E-values. Wouter M. Koolen and Peter D. Grünwald.
    International Journal of Approximate Reasoning, 141, pp. 69–82, 2022
     Extends the GRO criterion from fixed sample size to optional stopping: the log-optimal anytime-valid e-value against a composite null is characterised by a reverse information projection over mixtures of stopped null distributions, with examples where it is a plain likelihood ratio and a numerical example where it is not even a supermartingale. PDF DOI

2021

  • Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences. Steven R. Howard, Aaditya Ramdas, Jon McAuliffe and Jasjeet Sekhon.
    The Annals of Statistics, 49 (2), 2021
    The toolkit paper for confidence sequences: four recipes (stitching, conjugate mixtures, discrete mixtures, inverted stitching) that turn any sub-psi supermartingale into a curved anytime-valid boundary, with an empirical-Bernstein bound, matrix LIL and A/B-testing applications. PDF arXiv DOI

  • Testing by betting: A strategy for statistical and scientific communication. Glenn Shafer.
    Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series A: Statistics in Society, 184 (2), pp. 407–431, 2021
    The manifesto for reporting a test as the outcome of a bet: betting scores are likelihood ratios, the implied target replaces power, (1/alpha)-warranties replace confidence, and the discussion is where betting scores, e-values and Bayes factors are first set side by side; read before the RSS on September 9, 2020. PDF arXiv DOI

  • E-values: Calibration, combination, and applications. Vladimir Vovk and Ruodu Wang.
    The Annals of Statistics, 49 (3), 2021
    The paper that fixed the name and definition of e-values and showed why they are easy to combine: average arbitrarily dependent e-values, multiply independent or sequential ones, and build multiple-testing procedures from these two rules. PDF arXiv DOI

2020

  • Universal inference. Larry Wasserman, Aaditya Ramdas and Sivaraman Balakrishnan.
    Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 117 (29), pp. 16880–16890, 2020
     The split likelihood-ratio test: an e-value built from any estimator on half the data and the null MLE on the other half, valid in finite samples for any model, with a running-MLE e-process version. PDF arXiv DOI

1939

  • Étude critique de la notion de collectif. Jean Ville.
    1939
     Ville’s doctoral thesis: the critique of von Mises–Wald collectives that introduced the martingale as a gambling system and proved P(sup_n s_n ≥ λ) ≤ 1/λ, the inequality behind every test martingale and e-process. PDF

Preprints

  • Testing maximum entropy models with e-values. Francesca Giuffrida, Diego Garlaschelli and Peter D. Grünwald.
    2025
    Builds growth-optimal e-values for testing one maximum-entropy (exponential random graph type) model against another: exact in closed form when both are microcanonical, and a provably valid, near-optimal stand-in for the intractable canonical case, worked out for 2 x k contingency tables, stochastic block models and bipartite configuration models. arXiv DOI

  • A uniform improvement of the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure via e-closure. Jelle Goeman.
    2026
    Closed BH never rejects fewer hypotheses than Benjamini–Hochberg, often rejects many more, stays valid under PRDS, and is built entirely from the e-Closure principle: a family of e-values indexed by subsets of hypotheses. PDF arXiv DOI

  • Optimal E-Values for exponential families: The simple case. Peter D. Grünwald, Tyron Lardy, Yunda Hao, Shaul K. Bar-Lev and Martijn de Jong.
    2024
    Gives one checkable condition, a covariance (Loewner) ordering between the null family and the family generated by the alternative, under which the growth-optimal e-variable for a composite exponential family null is just a likelihood ratio against a single null element. arXiv DOI

  • E-Values for exponential families: The general case. Yunda Hao and Peter D. Grünwald.
    2025
    Derives and compares the RIPr-optimal, conditional, universal-inference and sequential plug-in e-variables for exponential family nulls, including the ‘anti-simple’ case where the optimal e-variable is not a simple likelihood ratio, and shows the conditional e-variable loses only o(1) e-power against the optimum while UI loses (d/2) log n. arXiv DOI

  • A generalisation of Ville’s inequality to monotonic lower bounds and thresholds. Wouter M. Koolen, Muriel Felipe Pérez-Ortiz and Tyron Lardy.
    2025
    Ville’s inequality with a time-decreasing floor and a time-increasing threshold: an exact crossing-probability formula, tight via a ‘floor-hugging’ martingale, used to turn an improper-mixture supermartingale into a clean finite-time law of the iterated logarithm. PDF arXiv DOI

  • Admissible anytime-valid sequential inference must rely on nonnegative martingales. Aaditya Ramdas, Johannes Ruf, Martin Larsson and Wouter M. Koolen.
    2022
    The foundations paper for e-processes, p-processes, sequential tests and confidence sequences: it defines all four, proves they are equivalent to being dominated by nonnegative martingales, and shows that any admissible construction of any of them must, explicitly or implicitly, be built from such martingales. PDF arXiv DOI

  • Non-partitioned e-detectors for nonparametric sequential change detection. Aytijhya Saha and Aaditya Ramdas.
    2026
    Change detection when pre- and post-change laws both live in one unknown class: sum point-null e-processes started at every time, take the infimum over candidate no-change laws, and the aggregation weights decide whether you get average-run-length or false-alarm-probability control, with first-order optimal delay log(1/alpha)/KL. PDF arXiv DOI

  • E-detectors: A nonparametric framework for sequential change detection. Jaehyeok Shin, Aaditya Ramdas and Alessandro Rinaldo.
    2023
    The paper that defines e-detectors: sum (or max) e-processes restarted at every time step, stop at 1/α, and the average run length to a false alarm is at least 1/α for any composite, nonparametric, non-i.i.d. pre-change class, with explicit log(1/α)/KL-rate delay bounds for exponential mixtures. PDF arXiv DOI

  • False discovery rate control with e-values. Ruodu Wang and Aaditya Ramdas.
    2021
    The paper that introduced e-BH: run Benjamini–Hochberg on e-values and the false discovery rate is controlled under any dependence with no log K correction, plus boosting when null distributions are known, and BH and BY recovered as calibrated special cases. PDF arXiv DOI

  • Bringing closure to false discovery rate control: A general principle for multiple testing. Ziyu Xu, Aldo Solari, Lasse Fischer, Rianne de Heide, Aaditya Ramdas and Jelle Goeman.
    2025
    Every multiple testing method that controls an expected loss (FDR, FWER, PFER, FDX, …) is a closed testing procedure driven by e-values for intersection hypotheses, and reverse-engineering the implicit e-values yields strictly more powerful versions of e-BH, Benjamini–Yekutieli and Su’s procedure with free post-hoc choice of rejected set, error metric and even level. PDF arXiv DOI
